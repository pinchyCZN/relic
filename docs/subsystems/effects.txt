PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                 BEAMS, SPARKS AND GLOW: THE NON-MESH OBJECTS


Abstract

   The scene list holds more than meshes. Space_BuildObjects fills
   twenty-one slots with Fspacedata geometry and thirteen more with
   objects that carry a TYPE at +0x2c and are drawn by something
   other than the polygon path.

   The important finding is that the ship's lightning is not a
   texture, a sprite, or canned geometry. It is generated every frame
   from two sine lookups about a straight line, scaled by that line's
   own length, and stroked as nine anti-aliased segments with a glow
   blob at each node.

   The second finding is about the pixel. Every one of these effects
   writes BYTE +2 OF THE FRAMEBUFFER AND NOTHING ELSE, additively,
   through a saturating lookup, and the HAM8 encode tables then fade
   the pixel's three real channels toward a bright base colour by
   that amount. It is a 6-bit light accumulator, not a colour.

1.  The object types

   maybe_Scene_TransformAndDraw reads the type longword at +0x2c and
   branches [verified]:

   +------+----------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Type | Drawn by             | What it is                          |
   +======+======================+=====================================+
   | 1    | 0x002d4452           | one point, perspective-scaled       |
   |      |                      | sprite - section 4                  |
   +------+----------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | 2    | Beam_Draw 0x002ca800 | a lightning bolt between two        |
   |      |                      | points, plus sparks - section 2     |
   +------+----------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | 3    | Draw_GlowBlob        | the ball of light - ten blobs on a  |
   |      | 0x002cefa4, ten times| sphere, section 5.1                 |
   +------+----------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | 4    | 0x002cf19e           | unread                              |
   +------+----------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | else | Scene_TransformProject + Poly_DrawFaceList - a mesh        |
   +------+----------------------+-------------------------------------+

   THE SHIP'S NON-MESH OBJECTS COME IN THREES [verified], which is
   the same threefold symmetry as the three untextured mesh spikes at
   objects 5, 6 and 7, and not the sixfold symmetry of the struts:

       three  type 2, strength from g_BeamPhaseA
       three  type 1, sizes from g_SpriteSizeTableA
       three  type 1, sizes from g_SpriteSizeTableB
       three  type 2, strength from g_BeamPhaseB
       one    type 3

   The slot indices are deliberately left out: Spike_BuildRing
   builds seven instances inside Space_BuildObjects, so every index
   after the master spike shifts by seven.

   THE TWO BEAM GROUPS ARE A CHAIN, NOT A CHOICE [verified]. Each
   beam's geometry is a count longword of 1 and two 16.16
   object-space vertices, and in cylindrical terms:

   +-------------------------+-----------------+------------------+
   | Group                   | From            | To               |
   +=========================+=================+==================+
   | g_BeamGeomHullToSpike   | r=1.016         | r=0.664          |
   | 0x002ee8a0, phase A     | z=-0.664        | z=+0.469         |
   +-------------------------+-----------------+------------------+
   | g_BeamGeomSpikeToOrb    | r=0.664         | r=0, z=+0.781    |
   | 0x002ee8f4, phase B     | z=+0.469        | = g_OrbWorldPos  |
   +-------------------------+-----------------+------------------+

   each at 0, +120 and -120 degrees. The first group's end points
   ARE the second group's start points, so the lightning walks from
   the big disc's perimeter up to three points above it - the three
   flat wedges at objects 5, 6 and 7 - and then inward to the orb.

   Space_RunPart arms phase B only after phase A has reached full,
   so the hull-to-spike arcs strike first and the spike-to-orb arcs
   follow.

   NOTHING MOVES THE ORB INTO THE CLAW'S GRIP. g_OrbWorldPos is
   fixed at (0, 0, 0.781) and the beams are authored to end there.

   The three sprites at g_SpriteGeomHull sit on the first group's
   start points and the three at g_SpriteGeomSpike on its end
   points, so both ends of every arc carry a glow.

   sim/lightning.py carries all six as BEAMS_A and BEAMS_B, and
   sim/run.py draws them on the ship page with the v key.

2.  Type 2: the beam

   Beam_Draw (0x002ca800) gets the object's first two PROJECTED
   vertices from the caller and a strength in D7 [verified]:

       A6 = (0x48,A0)              ; this object's particle array
       Particle_UpdateAndDraw
       if D7 < 0x20:  Beam_DrawGrowing ; rts
       Beam_BuildBoltPath          ; 8 points into g_BoltPath
       8 times: Draw_LineAA to the next point, make it current
       one more Draw_LineAA back to the endpoint

   D7 IS THE BEAM'S STRENGTH and it is read from the longword the
   object's +0x34 points at - g_BeamPhaseA or g_BeamPhaseB. Below
   0x20 the beam is still growing.

   Beam_DrawGrowing (0x002ca9a0) is that growing phase:

       D7 -= 0x10 ; if < 0 return
       interpolate the endpoint to D7/16 along the line
       spawn a particle there at a randomised size
       blit

   SO THE BEAM EXTENDS FROM ITS ORIGIN. D7 runs 0x10 to 0x20 over the
   growing phase and the far end walks out to full length, shedding
   sparks. Below 0x10 nothing is drawn at all, which is what the
   negative start value of g_BeamPhaseA buys - a delay.

3.  The bolt path

   Beam_BuildBoltPath (0x002ca9e4) is the whole of the lightning
   [verified]:

       g_BoltLength = sqrt((dx)^2 + (dy)^2)
       D6 = (g_BoltSeed & 0x3f) << 7
       8 times:
           p = lerp(start, end, D6>>8)
           p.x += (sin[(D6>>1 + g_BoltPhase) & 0x1fff] >> 4)
                    * g_BoltLength >> 8
           p.y += (sin[(D6>>3 + g_BoltPhase) & 0x1fff] >> 3)
                    * g_BoltLength >> 8
           store p ; Draw_GlowBlob at p
           D6 += 0x2000

   TWO SINES AT DIFFERENT RATES. The x offset steps the table at half
   the rate of the walk along the line and the y offset at an eighth,
   so the two never come back into step and the result reads as
   irregular rather than as a wave. There is no random number
   generator in this routine at all.

   SCALED BY THE LINE'S OWN LENGTH, so a bolt is the same shape near
   and far rather than becoming a straight line at distance.

   g_BoltPhase shifts both sines every frame - that is the crackle.
   g_BoltSeed picks where the walk starts, so two bolts drawn in the
   same frame do not trace the same path.

   THE NODE RADIUS TAPERS ALONG THE BOLT:

       r = max(4, abs(((0x10000 - d6/2) >> 11) * length >> 9))

   d6 climbs from 0 to 0x10000 across the eight nodes, so the radius
   falls - about 7 pixels down to its floor of 4 on a 120-pixel bolt.
   That taper is why a bolt reads as a line with a halo rather than
   as a row of lamps, and it is the argument Draw_GlowBlob takes.

4.  Type 1: the scaled sprite

   One vertex, rotated and perspective-divided like any other point,
   then [verified]:

       A1 = (0x30,A0)              ; an 8-entry size table
       D1 = g_SpriteAnimCounter
       g_SpriteAnimCounter += 1
       D1 &= 7
       D1 = A1[D1]                 ; pick a size
       D1 = (D1 << 11) / depth     ; perspective
       clamp to 0x7f
       blit

   THE COUNTER IS INCREMENTED ONCE PER SPRITE, NOT ONCE PER FRAME, and
   it is a single global shared by every type-1 object in the scene.
   So three sprites drawn in one frame take three CONSECUTIVE entries
   of the table, and the group's pattern rotates by one each frame.
   That is a cheap way to make a cluster flicker out of step, and it
   means the effect depends on the draw order.

   Two tables exist - g_SpriteSizeTableA at 0x002ee9a8 and
   g_SpriteSizeTableB at 0x002ee9c8 - one per group of three.

5.  The ball of light

   The scene's single type-3 object is drawn by the branch at
   0x002d2b3c and has two forms, chosen by g_OrbNearMode [verified].

   Far, with the flag clear:

       D7 = (0x002d2bf8) ; D6 = (0x002d2bfc)   ; running angles
       TEN times:
           a vector of length 0x2800 on the x axis
           rotated by D7 and D6 -> a point on a SPHERE
           plus the object's world position
           project, Draw_GlowBlob size 0x1e with g_RampBolt
           D7 += 0x333 ; D6 += 0x514

   SO THE ORB IS TEN GLOWING POINTS ON A SPHERE, NOT A MESH. Nothing
   in Fspacedata is spherical and nothing needs to be. The two
   angles step by different amounts, so the ten points trace a
   spherical spiral rather than a ring, and the stored pair advances
   per frame, so the ball swirls.

   Near, with the flag set, it takes a second form: project the
   position, and if the depth is past 0x1f4 and the projected size
   under 0x50, store the position and call 0x002d1fdc and
   0x002cdc3c. That path is unread.

6.  Face mode 1 is translucent, not flat

   Three of the ship's objects - 5, 6 and 7, the wedges the
   hull-to-spike lightning runs along - go through Uv_WriteFlatTable
   rather than a projection. It sets every face's mode word to 1 and
   copies six literal longwords from g_FlatUvTable, which are the
   SAME triangle wound three ways: (0x7f,0), (0,0), (0x40,0x7f).

   MODE 1 IS NOT UNTEXTURED. Poly_DrawFaceList sends it to
   Rast_AdditivePoly (0x002cb3aa), whose span loop is [verified]:

       D4w  = the interpolated v, high half
       D4b  = the interpolated u, low byte
       D4w += g_FlatScroll ; &= 0x7fff
       D0b  = g_pFlatPattern[D4w]
       D4w += g_FlatScroll ; &= 0x7fff
       D0b += g_pFlatPattern[0x7f + D4w]
       D4   = g_pFlatPalette[D0w * 4]
       add.l D4,(A5)+

   THE BLEND IS ONE 32-BIT ADD. R, G, the light accumulator and B
   are added in a single instruction, so translucency costs nothing
   beyond the lookup. It works because every channel is 6-bit and
   the palette is pre-divided, so no byte carries into its
   neighbour.

   TWO SAMPLES, BOTH SCROLLED, so the wedge carries a moving
   interference pattern rather than a static wash. FUN_002d5c66
   decrements g_FlatScroll by 0x100 or 0x200 a frame - the same
   routine that drives the beam phases.

   NO BACKFACE TEST. Poly_DrawFaceList's mode-1 branch jumps
   straight to the rasteriser once the three points are on screen. A
   translucent surface has no back.

   THE QUARTERING IS THE TRANSLUCENCY. Flat_ConditionTables
   (0x002cff42) runs once per load and shifts both tables right by
   two - the palette after masking 0xf8f8f8f8 so the shift cannot
   carry between channels. Several wedges can therefore overlap
   before a channel saturates. It is not idempotent, which is what
   its guard is for.

   The sizes are the contract: the pattern is exactly 0x8000+1
   bytes, matching the 0x7fff mask, and the palette 0xc0+1
   longwords, just past the 0x7e two quartered pattern bytes can sum
   to.

   SO THE WEDGES ARE THE GLOW THE LIGHTNING SITS INSIDE, not solid
   geometry. sim/ draws them as flat grey and says so, because
   g_pFlatPattern and g_pFlatPalette are loaded tables and which
   chunk feeds them is not settled - see Open questions.

7.  Draw_Flare, and the rays

   Draw_Flare (0x002d4452) is THE MOST USED DRAWING ROUTINE IN THE
   DEMO - seventeen distinct callers - and it is Draw_GlowBlob's
   bigger sibling [verified]:

       per pixel:
           w   = g_PolarTable[(dy << 8) | dx]
           d   = w low byte           ; the distance
           a   = w high byte          ; the ANGLE
           v   = g_BlobFalloff[radius << 8 | d]
           a  += g_FlareRayPhase ; v -= rays[a]
           a  += g_FlareRayPhase ; v -= rays[a]
           add.l colour_ramp[v], (pixel)

   TWO THINGS DIFFER FROM Draw_GlowBlob AND BOTH MATTER.

   THE ANGLE TERM. Draw_GlowBlob uses only the polar table's low
   byte, the distance. This also uses the high byte, the angle, to
   subtract a per-angle amount - which is what carves rays out of an
   otherwise round glow. That one table serves both is section 8.

   IT IS IN COLOUR. Draw_GlowBlob writes byte +2 and nothing else.
   This adds a whole longword - R, G, light and B in one add.l. So
   the demo's monochrome glows go through Draw_GlowBlob and its
   coloured ones through here.

   7.1  The ray tables

   Ray_BuildTables (0x002ca04c) makes three, each 256 words indexed
   by the angle byte, ALL GENERATED WITH THE DEMO'S OWN LCG - so
   they differ every run [verified]:

   +---------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | g_pRaySpikes  | 20 spikes at random angles, each a peak of |
   |               | height 0..7 with a 5-wide taper            |
   +---------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | g_pRayArcs    | 20 arcs of random height and 20-35 degrees |
   |               | width, then smoothed and NEGATED           |
   +---------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | g_pRayStreak  | two rays 180 degrees apart, hand written,  |
   |               | 6/4/4/2/2 - an anamorphic streak           |
   +---------------+--------------------------------------------+

   THE NEGATION FLIPS THE SIGN AT THE CALL SITE. Draw_Flare
   SUBTRACTS the ray value, so spikes DARKEN along their angles and
   arcs BRIGHTEN along theirs. One routine, two opposite characters,
   chosen by which table the caller passes.

   The streak table is never cleared - ten entries are written and
   the rest rely on the allocation being zeroed - and its loop
   counter is loaded and never used.

   7.2  The colour ramps

   Ramp_BuildColour (0x002c9eac) makes 256 longwords with the same
   inverse-square falloff the monochrome ramps use, tinted by a
   triple and clamped to 0x3f a channel. Ramp_BuildAll makes six:

       g_ColRampAmber        3f 2f 20   0x780000
       g_ColRampMagenta      3f 2f 3f   0x500000
       g_ColRampMagentaWide  3f 2f 3f   0x6e0000
       g_ColRampPale         7f 7f af   0x960000
       g_ColRampWarm2        3f 34 2d   0x500000
       g_ColRampWarm         3f 34 2d   0x500000

   THE ENTRY IS BUILT IN THE FRAMEBUFFER'S OWN PIXEL LAYOUT - R, G,
   a ZERO where the light byte goes, then B - which is what lets one
   add.l put it in without carrying between channels. It is also
   independent confirmation of that layout, arrived at from the
   other end.

   The two values above 0x3f clamp, so g_ColRampPale saturates to
   white sooner rather than being a brighter colour.

   7.3  The span is the circle, not the box

   Every blob and flare blitter reads a row half-width from
   g_pSpanHalfWidth so a pixel outside the circle is never touched.
   THAT IS NOT AN OPTIMISATION for Draw_Flare: g_BlobFalloff returns
   255 outside the radius and a colour ramp's entry 255 is dim but
   NOT zero, so walking the bounding box lays a faint square haze
   around every flare.

   src/gfx.c computes it as isqrt(r^2 - dy^2) instead - the same
   circle the table holds, and exact at any scale where a tabled
   half-width would quantise.

8.  Particles

   Particle_UpdateAndDraw (0x002ca962) walks FIFTY 8-byte records
   from the pointer at the object's +0x48 [verified]:

       if (A6).w <= 0: skip            ; dead
       (A6).w -= 2                     ; lifetime
       draw a glow blob at (0x2,A6),(0x4,A6) of size (0x6,A6)

   THE PARTICLES DO NOT MOVE. The record is {life, x, y, size} and
   there is no velocity in it and nothing integrates one. A spark is
   struck at a point and fades where it was struck. Anything that
   looks like motion comes from where new sparks are spawned, which
   for a beam is the advancing endpoint.

   The lifetime ticks down by 2, so a spark lives at most half as
   many frames as its initial value.

9.  The glow blob, and byte +2

   Draw_GlowBlob (0x002cefa4) takes a centre and a radius, rejects
   against 160 x 100 - the framebuffer, subsystems/renderer.txt -
   and then per pixel [verified]:

       v = fb[+2] + ramp[falloff]      ; ramp comes from the caller
       fb[+2] = lut[v]                 ; saturating, at 0x002f0e50

   IT WRITES BYTE +2 AND NOTHING ELSE. So does Draw_LineAA. Textures
   leave that byte at zero, so a texture's zero is the absence of
   light rather than a missing colour channel.

   THE LIGHT BYTE IS 6 BITS. Ramp_BuildSaturate (0x002cff1e) builds
   g_LightSatLut as min(i, 0x3f) and Draw_GlowBlob passes every sum
   through it. Draw_LineAA does NOT - it sets the core pixel to 0x3f
   outright and adds 0x0f to either neighbour with a raw byte add, so
   crossing lines reach 0x5d. Ham8_BuildTables builds 128 light
   levels, so that still lands inside the table.

   WHAT THE BYTE DOES is decided in Ham8_BuildTables (0x002ca456),
   not in any per-pixel code [verified]:

       d5  = max(0, 0xff - 4*light)
       out = min(0x3f00, base*256 + (c - base)*d5) >> 8

   and all three of its output tables are indexed by a colour channel
   PAIRED WITH THE LIGHT BYTE - (light, B), (G, light) and (R, light).

   SO LIGHT FADES THE PIXEL TOWARD A BRIGHT BASE COLOUR. It is not
   added as a fourth channel, not a separate bitplane, and costs
   nothing at run time: the blend is baked into the encode tables and
   the per-pixel work is the lookup the chunky-to-planar pass was
   doing anyway. Light 0 leaves the texture's own colour; light 64
   and over is the base colour outright.

   Space_RunPart passes (0x3f, 0x4f, 0x6f) and later (0x5f, 0x5f,
   0x7f). Every channel clamps at 0x3f, so a fully lit pixel is white
   whichever way the three map onto R, G and B; the values above 0x3f
   only make that channel reach white sooner, giving a blue-white
   glow.

   THE SHAPE AND THE BRIGHTNESS COME FROM DIFFERENT TABLES.
   g_PolarTable (0x0029f97a) is 65536 words indexed by a signed
   (dy << 8) | dx; its LOW byte is the distance from the centre and
   its HIGH byte is the angle Uv_AtanLookup wants, so one 128K table
   serves the UV generator and every glow blob. g_BlobFalloff
   (0x002bf97a) turns (radius, distance) into a ramp index, and the
   caller's ramp in A4 turns that into light:

   +------------------+---------------------------+----------------+
   | Ramp             | Built by                  | Used for       |
   +==================+===========================+================+
   | g_RampBolt       | Ramp_BuildLinear          | bolt nodes     |
   |                  | min(0x3f, (0xff-i) >> 1)  |                |
   +------------------+---------------------------+----------------+
   | g_RampSpark      | Ramp_BuildInverseSquare   | sparks         |
   |                  | D3 = 0x12c                |                |
   +------------------+---------------------------+----------------+
   | g_RampBeamHead   | Ramp_BuildInverseSquare   | the growing    |
   |                  | D3 = 0x96                 | beam's head    |
   +------------------+---------------------------+----------------+
   | g_RampBoltHalf   | g_RampBolt, each entry    | unread caller  |
   |                  | shifted right one         |                |
   +------------------+---------------------------+----------------+

   sim/lightning.py transcribes all of it and sim/run.py draws it on
   the l page.

10. What cues them

   Space_RunPart (0x002cc590) is the space part's sequencer, and the
   first part sequencer found in the demo. It sets a state, renders,
   and waits on a flag at 0x00237230, with every wait also testing
   0x00237232 and bailing out - an abort, almost certainly a key.

   THE BEAM CUES ARE WAITS ON A COUNTER [verified]:

       g_BeamPhaseA = -0x32 ; wait until g_BeamPhaseA >= 0x20
       ...
       g_BeamPhaseB =  0x00 ; wait until g_BeamPhaseB >= 0x20

   Space_RunPart never increments them. FUN_002d5c66 does, by 1 a
   frame. So the part arms a beam and blocks until it is at full
   strength: 82 frames for the first, 32 for the second, about 1.6
   and 0.6 seconds at PAL.

   The first beam's -0x32 start means 50 frames pass before D7 even
   reaches the 0x10 at which Beam_DrawGrowing draws anything.

11. The starfield is a direction lookup, not a scroll

   Scene_DrawTexturedSky (0x002d2db2) draws the sky a pixel at a time
   out of a 256 x 256 longword table of texture offsets, indexed by an
   interpolated screen DIRECTION [verified]. It is not a two-axis
   scroll of a bitmap, and the difference is a whole axis.

   Three screen corners are rotated by the latched head angle through
   0x002d4132, a plain 14-bit rotation:

       c1 = rot(-0x5000, -0x3200)
       c2 = rot( 0x5000,  0x3200)
       c3 = rot( 0x5000, -0x3200)        the walk starts here

   (c1 - c3) / 160 is the per-column step and (c2 - c3) / 100 the
   per-row one. Each pixel's 16-bit index takes its high byte from one
   accumulator and its low byte from the other, reads the table, adds
   a single offset and masks to 0x3ffff - which is Fplasma's size.

   THE TWO SKY ANGLES ONLY SUPPLY THAT ONE OFFSET:

       low byte   (g_CameraAngleLatched  * 2) >> 16
       the rest   (g_CameraAngle2Latched * 2) >> 8,  times 4

   so orientation and pan are separate mechanisms, and a
   reconstruction that collapses them into one 2D offset gets two axes
   right and loses the third.

   THE HEAD ANGLE IS THAT THIRD AXIS, AND IT IS WHY THE ERROR HIDES.
   maybe_Camera_MoveRotated accumulates it as

       head += (yaw delta * sin(-pitch)) >> 6

   so it barely moves while the camera is level and swings hard once
   the camera is pitched. Fixed screen axes therefore look right for
   the first half of the window scene and send the stars off on a path
   of their own once the camera swings under the hull.

   IT IS INHERITED BETWEEN SCENES, AND ONE SCENE EARNS ALL OF IT.
   g_HeadAngle reads zero through the sun scene, through the lit
   planet and at the approach's first frame, and 0x582e3 by the
   approach's last [observed, on hardware at four frames spanning
   the two scenes] - so the whole angle accumulates inside 2.4-2.5,
   where
   Anim_SceneTick starts calling Camera_TrackTarget once
   g_ApproachRange reaches zero and the camera swings after the ship.
   Nothing resets it afterwards: maybe_Space_RunWindowScene seeds its
   own two parameters and leaves the head alone, so mode 5 opens on
   0x5838d - 1411 of 8192 once shifted, about 62 degrees - and drifts
   -83 a tick from there.

   SO src/part_sun.c AND src/part_approach.c ARE RIGHT TO PASS ZERO,
   src/part_portal.c is right to start from zero and accumulate, and
   only src/part_window.c needs the constant seeded, because the port
   runs its parts standalone and cannot inherit it.

   THE D6 EACH CALLER LOADS IS DEAD. 0x002d4132 overwrites D6 with the
   cosine before reading it, so the (0x002e0bb6) fetch ahead of every
   call feeds nothing.

12. Open questions

   o What is type 4 (0x002cf19e)?
   o WHICH CHUNK FEEDS g_pFlatPattern AND g_pFlatPalette? They are
     pointers at 0x002e4834 and 0x002e4838, four and five entries
     past the end of Fspacedata's 21 object triples in the table
     Space_LoadAssets fills from 0x002e472c. No relic.dat chunk is
     32769 bytes, so they are sub-blocks of a larger one. Until this
     is settled the wedges cannot be coloured without inventing a
     palette.
   o WHICH OF Ham8_BuildTables' THREE BASE VALUES IS RED? The
     packing before Ham8_TransposeLongword has not been unpicked. It
     only changes intermediate light levels - a fully lit pixel is
     white either way.
   o What writes the particle arrays besides Beam_DrawGrowing? Fifty
     slots per object is a lot for one spawner.
   o Nothing yet ties the three type-2 beam objects to the three
     untextured mesh spikes at objects 5, 6 and 7, beyond both being
     threefold. The beams' endpoints come from their own two-vertex
     geometry, which has not been dumped.
   o The sprite size tables at 0x002ee9a8 and 0x002ee9c8 have not
     been read out.
