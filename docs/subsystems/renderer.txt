PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                            THE RENDERER


Abstract

   RELIC draws into a CHUNKY 32-BIT framebuffer 160 pixels wide, not
   into a planar Amiga bitmap. Textured polygons are filled by a
   two-phase scanline rasteriser whose inner loop is nine instructions
   long and contains no multiply, no divide and no depth test.

   The texture index is built by merging two registers rather than
   computing v*256+u, which is what fixes the texture size at 256x256
   and the texel at 4 bytes - independently of the asset data.

   This answers how a textured triangle is drawn. Ham8_EncodeFrame
   then consumes this exact buffer and turns it into a HAM8 screen -
   subsystems/display.txt section 8.

1.  The framebuffer

   +------------------+--------------+------------------------------------+
   | Property         | Value        | Evidence                           |
   +==================+==============+====================================+
   | bytes per pixel  |            4 | lea (0,A2,D0w*0x4),A3 scales x by  |
   |                  |              | four; the store is a move.l        |
   +------------------+--------------+------------------------------------+
   | bytes per row    | 0x280 = 640  | move.l #0x280,D7 then adda.l D7,A2 |
   |                  |              | to step one scanline               |
   +------------------+--------------+------------------------------------+
   | pixels per row   |          160 | 640 / 4                            |
   +------------------+--------------+------------------------------------+
   | rows             |          100 | Warp_FullscreenAffine divides its  |
   |                  |              | spans by 0xa0 and 0x64 and its dbf |
   |                  |              | counters are 0x9f and 0x63         |
   +------------------+--------------+------------------------------------+
   | base pointer     | 0x002f101c   | g_pFrameBuffer, about 58 readers   |
   +------------------+--------------+------------------------------------+

   All [verified].

   160 x 100 AT FOUR BYTES IS 64,000 BYTES, and it is chunky, so it is
   not something the Amiga display hardware can show directly. Two
   independent routines agree on the size: the span filler's row stride
   and the warp's divisors.

   That size is also what the readme describes from the other end. It
   says snapshots are 640 x 98 and asks that they be scaled to 160 x 98
   "so they are displayed in the correct aspect ratio" - overview.txt
   section 5. A 160-pixel-wide render matches the 160 in that
   instruction, and the demo's "2x2 mode" is the pixel doubling that
   gets it onto a 320-wide display.

   THE PIXEL IS THE SAME FOUR BYTES AS A TEXEL. The span filler copies
   a texel to the framebuffer with one move.l and no conversion, so the
   framebuffer holds (R, G, pad, B) with six-bit channels exactly as
   formats/textures.txt describes. Render target and texture share one
   format, which is why no sampler-side conversion exists.

2.  The textured span filler

   Rast_FillSpansTextured, at 0x002cabce. Phase two of a two-phase
   fill: an edge walker emits one span record per scanline into a
   list, then this consumes the list.

   IT IS NOT INSIDE Rast_TexturedPoly (0x002caef6) - it sits BEFORE
   it - and nothing references it, so its caller is reached
   indirectly, through one of the fetch variants below. That is why
   the setup of D1, D3, D4 and D5 is not established: see section 3.

   Inputs:

    A1   -> the span list          A6  -> one past its end
    A0   texture base, loaded from g_pTextureBase
    A2   += g_pFrameBuffer
    D1   du/dx                     D5  dv/dx

   The span record is 16 bytes, read in one movem:

    movem.l (A1)+, { D0 D2 D3 D4 }

    D0   x_start, in the HIGH word - swapped before use
    D2   x_end,   in the HIGH word
    D3   v
    D4   u, integer part in the low byte

   The loop, in full:

       .nextspan
         movem.l (A1)+,{D0,D2,D3,D4}
         swap    D0
         swap    D2
         sub.w   D0w,D2w            ; width
         subq.w  #1,D2w             ; for dbf
         blt     .skip              ; empty span
         lea     (0,A2,D0w*4),A3    ; A3 = row + x_start*4
       .pixel
         move.w  D3w,D6w            ; D6 low word := v
         add.l   D5,D3              ; v += dv/dx
         move.b  D4b,D6b            ; D6 low byte := u
         addx.w  D1w,D4w            ; u += du/dx, with carry
         move.l  (0,A0,D6*4),(A3)+  ; texel -> framebuffer
         dbf     D2w,.pixel
       .skip
         adda.l  D7,A2              ; next scanline, +640
         cmpa.l  A6,A1
         blt     .nextspan
         rts

   NINE INSTRUCTIONS PER PIXEL AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS A MULTIPLY, A
   DIVIDE, A SHIFT OR A COMPARE. There is no perspective divide, no
   clipping test and no z-buffer read in the inner loop. Depth and
   clipping are therefore resolved before this routine runs - by
   ordering, by the edge walker, or not at all.

   Six texel-fetch sites exist in Rast_TexturedPoly, so there are
   several specialised variants. One of them stores with -(A5) instead
   of (A3)+, which is a right-to-left span.

3.  The texel index trick

   This is the part worth transcribing carefully, because it is where a
   reconstruction will silently diverge.

       move.w  D3w,D6w        D6 low WORD := low word of v
       move.b  D4b,D6b        D6 low BYTE := low byte of u

   After both, D6 = (v & 0xff00) | (u & 0x00ff). The byte that
   survives from v is its bits 8..15 and the byte from u is its bits
   0..7, so D6 is v_int * 256 + u_int with no arithmetic at all. The
   addressing mode then scales by the texel size:

       move.l (0,A0,D6*0x4),(A3)+

   So THE TEXTURE IS 256 x 256 AND A TEXEL IS 4 BYTES, read out of the
   renderer rather than guessed from chunk sizes. formats/textures.txt
   reached the same 4 bytes from the data; these are independent.

   THE CONSEQUENCE THAT MATTERS: u and v WRAP, and they wrap for free.
   u is taken modulo 256 by the byte move and v by the word move.
   There is no clamp and no bounds check anywhere, so a polygon whose
   coordinates run off the texture tiles rather than smearing or
   crashing. Any reconstruction must mask, not clamp.

   u AND v ARE NOT PLAIN 16.16. The fraction is carried in the bits
   the merge discards and the carry is folded back with addx.w, so
   stepping them is add.l followed by addx.w rather than a single add.
   Warp_FullscreenAffine prepares the same form with ror.l #8. Reading
   D4 as straight 16.16 gives a picture that looks almost right and
   drifts across the span.

   WHICH BITS CARRY WHICH FRACTION IS OPEN, and it cannot be settled
   from the loop alone. The instructions fix only this much:

       v_int = bits 8..15 of D3        move.w D3w,D6w
       u_int = bits 0..7  of D4        move.b D4b,D6b
       D3 steps by D5 with add.l, and its carry out of bit 31 is the X
       that addx.w folds into D4w

   A carry out of bit 31 is reachable only if D3's top half holds
   something that overflows, so either u's fraction lives in D3 bits
   16..31 - making (D4w : D3) one 48-bit accumulator - or v simply
   never overflows and the addx is an add. The two differ in how the
   span drifts, and choosing wrong is the divergence this section
   warns about. Settling it needs the edge walker that writes the span
   records, and that is reached through an unresolved indirect call.

4.  The full-screen affine warp

   Warp_FullscreenAffine (0x002d3f5c) is a second, simpler path: it
   maps ONE texture over the entire framebuffer with no polygon
   involved.

   It projects three corners at +/-0x5000, +/-0x3200 through
   maybe_Warp_ProjectCorner with g_WarpParamA and g_WarpParamB,
   differences them to get spans, and divides by 160 and 100 to get the
   per-pixel and per-row steps. It then biases the texture pointer by
   0x20200 - which is (128*256 + 128) * 4, the centre texel of a
   256x256 image - so its index is a signed offset from the middle.

   The inner loop is the same merge trick and writes longwords straight
   to g_pFrameBuffer, 160 across by 100 down.

   maybe_Ship_DrawFullscreenHull (0x002cdaac) calls it with ship
   texture 0. SO AT LEAST ONE SHOT OF THE SHIP IS A 2D WARP OF A
   PRE-RENDERED HULL TEXTURE, NOT 3D GEOMETRY. That is worth knowing
   before trying to reproduce the close-up pass with a polygon
   renderer.

4.1.  What the reconstruction does with all this

   src/rast.c fills a triangle from three projected corners and three
   (u, v) pairs, masking both to eight bits as section 3 requires.

   THE AFFINE MAPPING IS NOT AN APPROXIMATION HERE. The perspective
   divide happens once per VERTEX in Scene_TransformProject and the
   span filler then steps u and v with a plain add, so interpolating
   them linearly over the projected triangle IS the demo's model.
   What is not transcribed is the fixed-point PACKING of that step -
   section 3's open question - so a texel can be one off along a span.

   src/uv.c generates the coordinates. ONLY FOUR OF THE NINE MODE BITS
   ARE IMPLEMENTED: 0x0001, 0x0002, 0x4000 and 0x8000, which is every
   bit any object Space_BuildObjects parameterises actually sets -
   formats/geometry.txt section 6. Uv_Generate counts a pass asking
   for anything else rather than silently drawing nothing.

   WHAT THE PORT SKIPS IS THE WHOLE OF SECTION 8'S DISPLAY CHAIN, and
   that is visible rather than academic. Ham8_EncodeFrame turns each
   rendered pixel into FOUR HAM8 pixels, and HAM8 reaches a colour by
   successive single-channel modifies, so a pixel very different from
   its left neighbour takes the full four to converge. On a small
   bright object over dark space that is a horizontal smear on the
   leading edge. The port resolves its four-byte pixel straight to RGB
   and so shows the frame the demo had BEFORE its display chain:
   cleaner, and not what a viewer of the real thing sees. The spark
   shower of effects/portal.txt section 4 is where the difference
   shows most.

5.  What is still missing

   o WHAT DRIVES THE UV GENERATOR for parts other than the ship and
     Scene_SetupPart14. Those two callers are read; the rest are not.

   o THE SCENE IS A FLAT SLOT ARRAY AND A PREFIX COUNT.
     Scene_SortAndDrawAll (0x002d28ca) walks g_SceneObjectCount
     slots of 0x50 bytes from g_SceneObjects (0x0033be20), writes
     {pointer, depth} pairs into g_SceneSortList, bubble-sorts them
     by depth and dispatches each on its type word at +0x2c.

     RAISING THE COUNT IS HOW GEOMETRY APPEARS. Slots past it are
     built and simply not drawn, so a part can bring objects into
     the scene for the cost of a counter - formats/geometry.txt
     section 6 and parts.txt section 4.

     g_SceneSortSkip holds the first N objects out of the sort so
     they always draw first whatever their depth, which is how a
     backdrop stays behind everything. Space_RunPart sets it to 2.

   o HIDDEN SURFACES ARE HANDLED WITHOUT A DEPTH BUFFER AND WITHOUT A
     PER-FRAME SORT. Mesh_SortFacesByC2 (0x002d17fc) orders each
     object's faces once at setup by comp2 of their first vertex, and
     Poly_DrawFaceList backface-culls per g_CullMode. Nothing else
     resolves depth, so a part is only correct over the range of
     angles it is actually seen from - which is affordable when the
     camera path is authored.
   o THE OTHER FIVE TEXEL-FETCH VARIANTS in Rast_TexturedPoly. One
     stores right-to-left with -(A5); the rest are unread, and
     whether any of them shades rather than copies is open. So is
     maybe_Rast_FlatPoly (0x002cb3aa), the untextured path.
   o Space_BuildObjects (0x002d0990) is the largest function in the
     program at 2,300 bytes and reads the ship texture pointers at
     four sites.

6.  Open questions

   o Is there any depth resolution at all, or are objects drawn in a
     fixed order?
   o What are the other five texel-fetch variants in
     Rast_TexturedPoly for - direction, alignment, or shading?
   o Does anything read the pad byte at texel+2? If a shaded variant
     adds two texels with one add.l, that byte is the headroom that
     makes it safe - formats/textures.txt section 2.
   o How does the demo drop resolution as the camera pulls away
     (parts.txt 2.8)? Nothing here switches texture size, so either a
     different routine does it or the effect comes from the display
     conversion.
   o What sets g_WarpParamA and g_WarpParamB?
