PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                    THE DISPLAY, AND THE COPPER LISTS


Abstract

   THE LIGHT BYTE'S MEANING IS PER SCENE. Ham8_BuildTables blends
   each pixel toward a base colour by the light amount, and the
   base is whatever the scene passes: Space_RunPart passes
   (0x3f, 0x4f, 0x6f) so light brightens toward blue-white, and
   maybe_Space_RunSunScene passes (0, 0, 0) so the same byte
   DARKENS. Scene_DrawVignette depends on the second reading.
   subsystems/effects.txt section 8.

   RELIC does not assemble its copper lists from nothing. Each one is
   a STATIC SKELETON authored into CODE_05, the chip-RAM hunk, with
   every pointer data word left zero. Code fills those words in and
   points COP1LC at the list. A static read of the image therefore
   finds what look like inert or blanking lists, and the interesting
   half is all zeros until the demo runs.

   TWO DIFFERENT SCREENS, AND NOT THE SAME MODE. The space scene is
   HAM8. The part at 0x310000 is six bitplanes and 64 indexed
   colours. Both feed from their own chunky buffer through
   their own copy of a chunky-to-planar converter.

1.  Where the lists live, and who installs them

   COP1LC is 0x80 off the custom base. The demo almost always holds
   0xdff000 in A5, so the writes are base-relative and invisible to a
   search for the absolute address - which is why they are easy to
   miss.

   Six installs write a register, three write an immediate:

    site                      list          what it is
    ------------------------  ------------  --------------------------
    Space_DisplayOn 002d5410  0x343374      HAM8, or 0x34437c if
                                            g_OptHires is set
    FUN_003102c2    003102dc  0x345478      6 planes, 64 colours
    FUN_00313830    00313860  (register)    unread
    FUN_0031667a    00316698  (register)    unread
    FUN_0033bcc0    0033bcd0  (register)    unread
    FUN_0037210a    00372128  (register)    unread
    FUN_002ceca4    002cecc4  0x343298      1 plane, small window
    FUN_002cecd2    002cecf2  0x3432e0      1 plane, small window
    FUN_002c9918    002c99d8  0x345384      unread

   Installing is always the same two instructions followed by a
   strobe: move.l A0,(0x80,A5) then clr.w (0x88,A5), which is COPJMP1.

   The lists at 0x2c8228 and 0x34546c are genuine blanking lists -
   BPLCON0 = 0, COLOR00 = 0, end - used to turn the display off
   between parts.

2.  The HAM8 screen

   g_CopperHam8 at 0x343374, installed by Space_DisplayOn. The header
   is static and readable [verified]:

    WAIT   VP=2f HP=04
    DDFSTRT  0034      DDFSTOP  00b8
    DIWSTRT  3e83      DIWSTOP  00bf
    BPLCON0  8811      HIRES=1  BPU=8  HAM=1  ECSENA=1
    BPLCON1  0000      BPLCON2  0000   BPLCON3 0000  BPLCON4 0000
    BPL1MOD  ffb0      BPL2MOD  0000
    FMODE    4047
    BPL3PTH..BPL8PTL   zeroed, patched by Copper_PatchPlanesHam8
    then 200 slices    zeroed, built by Copper_BuildSlices

   BPLCON0 = 0x8811 IS THE WHOLE ANSWER: bit 15 HIRES, bit 11 HAM, and
   BPU = 8 once bit 4 is folded in as the AGA high bit. Eight planes
   with HAM set is HAM8.

   DIWSTRT/DIWSTOP give a window of H 131..447 and V 62..256, so
   roughly 640 hires pixels by 194 lines, and Copper_BuildSlices emits
   exactly 200 slices. Call it 640 x 200.

   2.1  HAM8 smears horizontally, and the star trails are NOT it

   Hold-And-Modify spends six of its eight bits on data and two on
   control. Control 00 takes the six bits as one of 64 palette
   indices; 01, 10 and 11 replace the R, the G or the B of the
   PREVIOUS PIXEL and hold the other two. So a run of pixels can only
   change ONE CHANNEL AT A TIME, and moving from one arbitrary colour
   to another takes up to three pixels.

   A BRIGHT STAR ON DARK SKY THEREFORE HAS A GRADED TAIL. The star's
   own pixel is right; the next pixels have to bring R, then G, then B
   back down, one per pixel, so each is dimmer than the last. A viewer
   sees the star plus two or three fading copies beside it and reads
   them as previous positions - but they are the pixels to one side,
   not earlier frames. The planet's edge against the sky smears the
   same way, for the same reason [observed].

   THE SMEAR CANNOT ACCOUNT FOR THE OBSERVED TRAILS. A viewer reports
   a star, then background for several pixels, then a dimmer copy of
   the star, and some of the trails running DIAGONALLY [observed].
   Hold-and-modify can only alter the pixel immediately after, on the
   same scanline - it cannot skip pixels and cannot leave the line -
   so whatever makes those copies is not this.

   NOR IS IT IN THE DRAWING CODE, and two measurements say so. A write
   watch over one framebuffer row shows Scene_DrawTexturedSky writing
   each pixel exactly ONCE per frame from a single PC, which rules out
   the scene being drawn several times at different times. And
   Ham8_EncodeFrame (0x002ca2a8) is a single-pass chunky-to-planar: it
   reads the 4-byte framebuffer once, looks each pixel up in two
   tables and XOR-transposes into bitplanes, with no second source and
   no blending. So there is no accumulation buffer, no fade, and no
   multi-pass star draw anywhere in the render.

   WHAT IS LEFT UNEXAMINED is the display path - the plane buffers the
   copper cycles, Copper_BuildSlices' 200 slices - and the emulator's
   own display filtering. The mechanism is OPEN.

   WITH THE HIRES SWITCH it installs g_CopperHam8Hires at 0x34437c
   instead:

    BPLCON0  0a51      SHRES=1  BPU=8  HAM=1  COLOR=1  ECSENA=1
    DIWSTRT  5cd1      DIWSTOP  be71
    FMODE    0047

   Super-hires HAM8, and the window is H 209..369 by V 92..190 -
   640 super-hires pixels by EXACTLY 98 LINES. That is the readme's
   "snapshots are 640 x 98" and its "hires mode in a small window
   instead of the usual 2x2 mode", both confirmed from the hardware
   registers [verified].

3.  The 64-colour screen

   g_Copper6Plane at 0x345478, installed by FUN_003102c2:

    WAIT   VP=28 HP=04
    DDFSTRT  0034      DDFSTOP  00a4
    DIWSTRT  6c81      DIWSTOP  ebc1
    BPLCON0  6001      HIRES=0  BPU=6  HAM=0  ECSENA=1
    BPLCON2  0200      KILLEHB
    BPL1MOD  0000      BPL2MOD  0000
    FMODE    0007
    BPL1PTH..BPL6PTL   zeroed, patched by Copper_PatchPlanes6

   Lores, six bitplanes, HAM off, and KILLEHB set so the upper 32
   registers are real colours rather than half-brights. Six planes is
   64 colours directly.

   The window is H 129..449 by V 108..235: 320 x 128. A plane is
   therefore 40 bytes per row times 128 rows = 5,120 bytes, which is
   the 0x1400 stride the converter and the patcher both use, and
   320 x 128 = 40,960 is exactly the pixel count the converter
   produces.

   THE SIXTY-FOUR COLOUR REGISTERS HERE ARE EXPLAINED BY THE PLANE
   COUNT, NOT BY HAM. Six planes need 64 entries. An earlier reading
   of this project took 64 registers plus a chunky-to-planar step as
   evidence of HAM8; the copper list says otherwise, and the plane
   count in both the converter and the patcher is six, not eight.

4.  The slice builder

   Copper_BuildSlices (0x002d5384) is the routine that actually
   BUILDS copper instructions at run time [verified]:

       move.w  #0xc7,D7w          ; 200 iterations
       move.w  #0x3dc1,D0w        ; first WAIT word, VP=0x3d
       move.l  #0x3431a8,D2       ; g_Ham8LowPlane1
       move.l  #0x3431f8,D3       ; g_Ham8LowPlane2
       lea     g_CopperHam8Slices,A0      ; 0x3443e0 if g_OptHires
     .loop
       move.w  D0w,(A0)+          ; WAIT first word
       move.w  #-2,(A0)+          ; WAIT second word, 0xfffe
       addi.w  #0x100,D0w         ; next scanline
       move.w  D2w,(0x6,A0)       ; BPL1PTL
       swap D2 ; move.w D2w,(0x2,A0)      ; BPL1PTH
       swap D2 ; addq.l #0x8,A0
       move.w  D3w,(0x6,A0)       ; BPL2PTL
       swap D3 ; move.w D3w,(0x2,A0)      ; BPL2PTH
       swap D3 ; addq.l #0x8,A0
       dbf     D7w,.loop

   200 slices of 20 bytes, one per scanline: a WAIT for the next line
   then a re-point of bitplanes 1 and 2.

   D2 AND D3 ARE NEVER ADVANCED. Every slice points BPL1 and BPL2 at
   the same two fixed addresses, 0x50 = 80 bytes apart, so the two
   lowest bitplanes repeat one 80-byte pattern on every scanline while
   BPL3..BPL8 scroll normally. 80 bytes is 640 bits, one per pixel of
   the display width.

   WHAT THAT FIXED LOW-PLANE PATTERN IS FOR HAS NOT BEEN READ, and it
   is the most interesting thing left in the display path. In HAM8 the
   low planes are part of the six-bit payload, so a constant pattern
   there is a per-pixel bias applied identically on every line - a
   dither, or a fixed ramp that the six upper planes modulate.

5.  The pointer patchers

   Copper_PatchPlanes6 (0x003106f4) is the simple one and worth
   reading as the model for the shape:

       lea     g_Copper6PlanePtrs,A0   ; the BPL1PTH MOVE itself
       move.l  g_ScreenShown,D0
       addi.l  #0x28,D0                ; +40 bytes
       moveq   #5,D7                   ; dbf 5 -> six planes
     .loop
       move.w  D0w,(0x6,A0)            ; low word  -> BPLxPTL data
       swap    D0
       move.w  D0w,(0x2,A0)            ; high word -> BPLxPTH data
       swap    D0
       addq.l  #0x8,A0                 ; next PTH/PTL pair
       addi.l  #0x1400,D0              ; next plane
       dbf     D7w,.loop

   The data word of a copper MOVE is at +2 of the instruction, so a
   PTH/PTL pair is 8 bytes and its two data words are at +2 and +6.
   That is the whole idiom, and it is how every pointer in every list
   here gets written.

   Copper_PatchPlanesHam8 (0x002d5312) does the same for BPL3..BPL8 of
   the HAM8 list at g_CopperHam8Ptrs (0x3433a8), and also patches
   DDFSTRT and the display window at 0x343380.

6.  The chunky-to-planar converter

   C2p_Chunky_To6Plane (0x00310792). Reads 8 longwords - 32 chunky
   bytes, one per pixel - and writes one longword into each of six
   bitplanes, 32 pixels per pass. All five merge levels:

   +-------+------------+---------------------------------------------+
   | Shift | Mask       | Level                                       |
   +=======+============+=============================================+
   |    16 | -          | swap and move.w pairs, not a mask           |
   +-------+------------+---------------------------------------------+
   |     8 | 0x00ff00ff | byte                                        |
   +-------+------------+---------------------------------------------+
   |     4 | 0x0f0f0f0f | nibble                                      |
   +-------+------------+---------------------------------------------+
   |     2 | 0x33333333 | two-bit                                     |
   +-------+------------+---------------------------------------------+
   |     1 | 0x55555555 | one-bit; the lsl is written add.l D7,D7     |
   +-------+------------+---------------------------------------------+

   A1, A2, A5 and A6 are pressed into service as data registers
   through exg, because six planes need more live values than D0-D7.

   1,280 passes, 40,960 pixels, plane stride 0x1400, frame 30,720
   bytes. Three screens rotate through g_ScreenBuffers; the chunky
   source is double buffered through g_pChunkyFront / g_pChunkyBack
   and built by maybe_Blend_BuildChunky (0x003104a4), which combines
   two 0xa000 buffers through a lookup table.

   AT LEAST EIGHT COPIES OF THE CONVERTER EXIST, one per part, inlined
   rather than shared, findable by their masks:

    0x00310792  C2p_Chunky_To6Plane         0x33333333 form
    0x00313a5c  C2p_Chunky_To6Plane_b       0x33333333 form
    0x002ca1a4  maybe_C2p_Variant_Space     0xcccc3333 form
    0x002ca2a8  maybe_C2p_Variant_Space2    0xcccc3333 form
    0x00315fbc  maybe_C2p_Variant_c         0xcccc3333 form
    0x003160c0  maybe_C2p_Variant_d         0xcccc3333 form
    0x0036db6c  maybe_C2p_Variant_Temple    0xcccc3333 form
    0x0036dc70  maybe_C2p_Variant_Temple2   0xcccc3333 form

   Only the first has been read. The 0xcccc3333 form folds two levels
   together, which suggests a different plane count, and the space
   scene's HAM8 screen has eight planes rather than six - so do not
   assume one converter serves the demo.

7.  The palette

   Pal_Set64 (0x00310a36) loads colour registers in a dbf from 0x3f,
   so exactly 64, index reversed with eori.w #0x3f. Entries are read
   one longword at a time from a table with a 16-byte stride, and
   maybe_Part310_MainLoop picks a table with index * 0x400 +
   g_PaletteTables - so 64 x 16 = 1024 bytes per palette and several
   palettes in a row.

   Pal_ScaleLongword (0x00310a58) is the whole fade: each of the four
   bytes is multiplied by g_PalFadeScale in 8.8, offset by
   g_PalFadeOffset, and clamped to 0xff on carry rather than wrapped.
   Four ror.l #8 steps walk the bytes and leave the longword in its
   original rotation.

8.  The HAM8 pipeline, end to end

   The chain from a textured triangle to a HAM8 scanline
   [verified]:

    1  Rast_TexturedPoly, Warp_FullscreenAffine and Draw_LineAA write
       g_pFrameBuffer: 160 x 100, FOUR bytes per pixel, six-bit
       channels - subsystems/renderer.txt
    2  Ham8_EncodeFrame (0x002ca2a8) reads all 16,000 pixels and
       writes SIX bitplanes of 8,000 bytes
    3  Copper_PatchPlanesHam8 points BPL3..BPL8 at those six planes,
       stride 0x1f40 = 8,000 - the same number
    4  Copper_BuildSlices points BPL1 and BPL2 at a fixed 80-byte
       pattern, re-pointed on every one of 200 scanlines
    5  g_CopperHam8 displays it: HIRES, 8 planes, HAM bit set

   THE COUNTS CLOSE EXACTLY. Ham8_EncodeFrame runs 2,000 iterations of
   8 pixels = 16,000 = 160 x 100, and 8,000 bytes per plane at 80
   bytes per row is 100 rows of 640 pixels. So 160 rendered pixels
   become 640 HAM8 pixels: FOUR HAM8 PIXELS PER RENDERED PIXEL, which
   is exactly what HAM8 needs to build one full-colour pixel out of
   successive red, green and blue modifies. The 1:4 ratio falls out
   of the arithmetic rather than being assumed.

   THE ENCODE IS THREE TABLE LOOKUPS OR'D TOGETHER:

       move.l (A0)+,D1                   ; pixel, 0xRRGG??BB
       move.l (0x0,A5,D1w*0x8),D0        ; index on 0x??BB
       lsr.l  #0x8,D1
       or.l   (0x4,A5,D1w*0x8),D0        ; index on 0xGG??
       lsl.w  #0x8,D1w ; lsr.l #0x8,D1
       or.l   (0x0,A6,D1w*0x4),D0        ; index on 0xRRGG

   Three overlapping 16-bit windows into the pixel, three tables
   owning disjoint bit positions, OR'd into one planar longword.

   ONLY THE 4-BIT AND 8-BIT MERGE LEVELS FOLLOW, not the full five of
   a normal c2p. The finer interleave is already baked in, because
   Ham8_BuildTables (0x002ca456) ran every table entry through
   Ham8_TransposeLongword before storing it. THAT IS THE TRICK OF THE
   WHOLE DISPLAY PATH: one indexed read does the colour conversion and
   most of the chunky-to-planar transpose at the same time.

   Ham8_BuildTables makes 0x80 x 0x100 entries, saturating each
   channel at 0x3f00, splitting each transposed result with the masks
   0x03330333, 0x04440444 and 0x08880888.

   BYTE +2 OF A FRAMEBUFFER PIXEL IS A REAL CHANNEL. Draw_LineAA
   writes 0x3f into it for the line and adds 0xf to the pixel either
   side, which is antialiasing coverage, and the encode's three
   windows all overlap it. It is zero in every TEXEL of every asset
   chunk, which is why formats/textures.txt measured it as a pad -
   that is true of the texture data and not of the framebuffer.

10. Open questions

   o What is the 80-byte fixed pattern that every slice points BPL1
     and BPL2 at? Section 4. With BPL3..BPL8 carrying the frame, bits
     0 and 1 of every HAM8 pixel come from this pattern, so it is a
     fixed two-bit dither applied identically to all 200 lines.
   o What exactly does byte +2 of a framebuffer pixel mean? It is
     written by Draw_LineAA as coverage and it is inside all three
     encode windows, but nothing separates its contribution from the
     colour channels.
   o Why does the 6-plane patcher add 0x28 to the frame base?
   o What are the other 12 bytes of each 16-byte palette entry?
     Pal_Set64 reads one longword and skips three.
   o BPL1MOD is -80 on the HAM8 list and 0 on the 64-colour list. A
     negative modulo on the odd planes only would make them re-read
     the same line; whether that is the vertical half of "2x2 mode"
     is untested.
   o Does anything use the blitter? DMACON enables BLTEN but no
     blitter register write has been found.
