1. Shape of the demo

   RELIC tells a story end to end rather than running a sequence of unrelated
   effects: a saviour returns in 10,191 AD to reveal a relic hidden on Earth,
   and ships the chosen away at the cost of his own immortality. The readme says
   the group designed it that way on purpose.

   The main visual subject is A CIRCULAR TOWER-SHAPED SPACESHIP, reminiscent of
   a jellyfish or an umbrella, in blue, grey, purple and magenta. It is the
   focus of the opening and the first target of this project.

   Two classes of part exist, which the demo calls 2x2 and 1x1. Only
   2x2 parts can be snapshotted - formats/hunk.txt and overview.txt section 5.
   Which parts below are which is not known.

2. The timeline, end to end

   Every scene below is [observed] - what a viewer sees, not what the code does.
   The numbering is this document's, not the demo's: no part list has been found
   and nothing confirms the demo runs these in a flat sequence rather than
   branching.

   2.1 THE TERMINAL. Black screen. Green text types itself out
        along the bottom, one character at a time with a blinking
        cursor, like an old computer terminal: "10,191 AD". The
        letters carry a glow.

   2.2 THE LOGO. Text fades in under a bright light: "nerve axis".
        Glow on the letters and across the screen.

        THE FADE IS IN THE PALETTE, NOT IN THE FIELD. From the field
        the logo's copper list goes up, 0x0030a2d0-odd writes
        COLOR00 and COLOR01 twice every field - once with the base
        and once with a ramped value - and the ramp falls 0x222 a
        field from 0xfff to 0, then restarts from a base one step
        lower [observed, watching 0xdff180]. The copper list itself
        carries no colour at all: it is DDF, DIW, BPLCON and the six
        bitplane pointers, then the end word. The reconstruction
        draws the field and does not ramp anything, so it has no
        fade - [open].

   2.3 THE PLANET. A planet fades in with a sun behind it. The
        camera pans, the sun edges out from behind the planet and
        lights the scene. A milky starfield lies behind. Heavy lens
        flare on the sun.

        SCENE ONE OF maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes [verified]. Base
        colour (0x3f,0x3f,0x3f), so the light byte brightens toward
        white. Two loops, both on Space_DrawSunFrame, cut by music
        cues 4:0 and 4:16 - the camera pan and the sun emerging.
        The frame is the sun, its sprites and the flare; it never
        calls Scene_SortAndDrawAll.

   2.4 THE ASTEROID. An asteroid flies past, TEXTURE MAPPED. The
        camera tracks it and it explodes. It comes through
        Scene_SortAndDrawAll's sorted object list rather than the
        octree renderer the same scene's light uses -
        effects/approach.txt section 3.

   2.5 THE SHIP ARRIVES. As the explosion fades the circular
        spaceship appears - the jellyfish or umbrella shape. It
        flies very close to the camera and then away toward the
        planet.

        SCENE TWO OF maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes COVERS BOTH, in
        two phases [verified]. Base colour (0x1f,0,0x2f), so the
        light byte tints purple rather than white.

            g_ApproachRange = 0xe60, counting down
            loop  Space_DrawApproachFrame   while it is above 0
            loop  Space_DrawFullFrame       until music cue 8:0

        THE OBJECTS ARE GATED ON RANGE: the approach frame calls
        Scene_SortAndDrawAll only once g_ApproachRange drops under
        0x961, and draws no sun and no flare. g_ApproachSpin turns
        by 4 a frame throughout. The full frame then draws the sun,
        the sprites, the objects and the flare together.

        WHICH PHASE IS THE ASTEROID AND WHICH THE SHIP IS NOT
        SETTLED - the range gate suits something arriving from far
        off, and the handover to a sun-lit frame suits the ship
        reaching the planet, but neither is confirmed.

   2.6 LOGO AGAIN, reading "schlott meson".

        IT RUNS 469 TICKS [observed, on hardware].
        g_LogoCounter dates it: Logo_SetupVariant seeds it 0x96 for a
        multi-line screen and Logo_Advance adds 0xe a frame for two
        lines, and its last write reads 0x1a2e - so
        (0x1a2e - 0x96) / 0xe is 468 and the screen is 469 ticks.
        src/part_logo.c carries it as PART_Logo2, a second entry at
        variant 1 rather than a second implementation: the variant
        index picks the line art AND the 64-colour palette, which is
        all that differs between 2.2, 2.6 and 2.9.

   2.7 THE CAPTAIN. A pixel-art image: a man standing at a window,
        a Star-Trek-like helm behind him.

        IT IS Fwadepic, AND IT IS AN IFF ILBM rather than a texel
        block like every other chunk the port reads [verified]. Its
        BMHD is 640 x 400, EIGHT planes and compression 1, with a
        768-byte CMAP - so 256 colours, hires, packed with ByteRun1.
        src/part_window.c decodes it: unpack, then deplane.

        THE PLANES ARE INTERLEAVED PER ROW, not per image - each row
        carries plane 0's bytes, then plane 1's, every plane
        (width + 15) / 16 * 2 bytes wide, and the HIGH bit of a byte
        is the LEFTMOST pixel. Reading them as whole images gives a
        picture that still looks like a picture in the wrong colours,
        the same trap formats/textures.txt records for the texel.
        CMAP is eight bits a channel and the frame wants six, so each
        is taken down by two.

        THE STILL IS A SCREEN, NOT THE PANE. Wadepic_ShowHiresAndFade
        displays those planes at 640 x 400 and the scene holds there
        on a music wait, so nothing the chunky pipeline draws is on
        the display until the pull-back - 2.8. src/part_window.c
        window_still samples the decoded image over whatever the
        frame is, by the box each pixel covers: one texel a pixel at
        scale 4, and the demo's own 4 x 4 mean at scale 1.

   2.8 THE PULL-BACK. The camera pans away from the man and keeps
        going, revealing how large the ship is.

        THE RESOLUTION DROP IS REAL, AND IT IS THE SCENE'S ONE CUT
        [verified, Wadepic_ShowHiresAndFade 0x002c9918]. The hold
        shows Fwadepic at its own 640 x 400: that routine patches two
        copper lists with eight bitplane pointers apiece, plane
        stride 0x50 and the second list one interleaved row on at
        +0x280 - 640 wide, eight planes, interlaced for 400 lines -
        installs them at cop1lc and fades the picture up over 127
        fields. Only then does it call Wadepic_DownsampleToTexture.

        WHAT CUTS IS Space_DisplayOn (0x002d5410), which replaces
        that copper list with g_CopperHam8, and
        maybe_Space_RunWindowScene calls it only AFTER its
        Music_WaitCue on (0x0c,0). So the picture is a hires SCREEN
        for the whole hold and a 160 x 100 TEXTURE from the first
        frame of the pull-back.

        The texture is the 4 x 4 box filter - sixteen palette colours
        summed a channel at a time and shifted down four for the
        mean. The demo's own allocation sizes it: g_WadepicTexSize
        declares 0x19000 bytes, 256 x 100 four-byte pixels, the same
        layout as the snapshot that replaces it. From the cut onward
        what looks like a further resolution drop is only the polygon
        getting smaller. maybe_Space_RunWindowScene (0x002cceb4) runs
        the scene in two phases split by one copy:

            mode 4   render the window scene to g_pFrameBuffer
            copy     g_pFrameBuffer -> g_pBillboardSnapshot
            mode 5   render with object 11 textured from it

        Nothing is re-rendered smaller. The mode 4 frame stops being
        the framebuffer and starts being a surface, so the same
        pixels come back through the texture mapper at whatever size
        the polygon is. Uv_WriteBillboard11 (0x002d1638) is the hand-
        over: per face it writes the snapshot pointer and three
        (u,v) pairs from g_Billboard11Uv, which is two triangles
        over 1,1 to 159,98 - the whole 160 x 100 frame inset by a
        pixel.

        THE PULL-BACK ACCELERATES, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF MODE
        4'S TICK [verified, Anim_SceneTick mode 4]:

            position += velocity
            velocity += 9

        seeded at Window_SeedPullBack as -0x5d00 and 0, with the
        first loop running until the position taken >> 2 reaches
        0x5000. So the camera leaves slowly and is still gaining
        speed when the loop ends, which is about 153 ticks - the
        reconstruction crosses the limit on tick 154.

        MODE 4 DRAWS A TEXTURED POLYGON PAIR, NOT A BITMAP.
        Window_DrawMode4Frame is one call to maybe_Scene_DrawPair,
        which takes two scene slots from g_pWindowPairSlot through
        maybe_Scene_TransformPair and Poly_DrawFaceList. So the
        window is already a surface before the snapshot is taken,
        and what the snapshot changes is only WHICH surface.

        THE PAIR IS OBJECTS 19 AND 20 - THE SURROUND AND THE PANE
        [verified]. Space_BuildObjects hands the pair slot
        g_pSpaceDataBlocks + 19 * 12 and the slot after it + 20 * 12.
        Object 20 is four vertices and two faces carrying the
        downsampled picture, with literal UVs off g_Billboard20Uv
        that cover the full 160 x 100. OBJECT 19 IS THE HULL THE
        OPENING SITS IN: 158 faces, a sixth of every face in the
        chunk, textured by Uv_ProjBoxish out of ship sub-block 2.

        IT IS CLOSE-RANGE DETAIL AND NOTHING ELSE DRAWS IT. Object 19
        is in no other scene's object list, so the panelling around
        the window exists only for the seconds the camera is close
        enough to see it; once mode 5 takes over, the same opening is
        object 11's two triangles on the plain hull. That is why the
        chunk holds one object far larger than any ship part, and why
        it never appeared in any other part's geometry.

        NEITHER OBJECT IS ROTATED. maybe_Scene_TransformPair brings
        vertices down SIX bits rather than four, adds
        g_PullBackScaled to comp1 as the depth, offsets x by
        -0x4b4 - 0x50 and z by +0x3a2, and rejects at 0x1f4. So the
        viewpoint is fixed and the two objects slide away from it.

        THE PANE IS WIDENED AT BUILD TIME AND THE STRETCH
        ACCUMULATES [verified, Pane_StretchX 0x002d128c]. The tail of
        Space_BuildObjects takes every pane vertex through

            c0 = (c0 * 0x10e) >> 8          270/256, about 1.0547

        and the vertex block it edits is Fspacedata's own sub-block,
        assigned without a copy - so each call to Space_BuildObjects
        made while that chunk stays loaded stretches the pane again.
        The surround is never passed through it. The window's build
        is the second the chunk has seen: field 1291 for the space
        scene before it against 3176 for its own [observed], which
        carries the pane's c0 from the 0x2ce7..0x21089 on disk to the
        0x31f1..0x24bec the transform reads.

        That is what centres the picture. At the opening depth two
        stretches put the pane's corners on (-3, 0) and (161, 100),
        filling the frame a shade over both edges; one puts them on
        -4 and 152, and the disk geometry on -4 and 143 - the
        picture a tenth of a frame left of centre with its 160 texels
        crushed into 147 pixels.

        MODE 5 IS Space_DrawFullFrame'S LIST MINUS THE PLANET
        [verified, Window_DrawMode5Frame]: the same nine latch
        copies, then Scene_DrawTexturedSky,
        Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun, Sprite_StampSaveUnder,
        Scene_SortAndDrawAll, Sprite_RestoreSaveUnder, Flare_Draw
        and maybe_Window_DrawMode5Tail, which is a bare rts - the
        planet scenes put their horizon band at that position and this
        scene draws nothing there, so the call list is fully
        accounted for. g_SceneFuseTicks counts down from 0x3e80, 16000 ticks,
        which is a fuse and not the length: the loop ends on
        Music_PollCue.

        MODE 5 IS 2002 TICKS, forty seconds [observed, on hardware,
        frames 139 to 2141], and it is one unbroken move. The camera
        comes off a Catmull-Rom spline whose keyframes share
        Fspacedata with the geometry - formats/geometry.txt 6.2 -
        walked two cursor steps a tick, so 128 ticks a keyframe and
        keyframe 15 of the 26 filled ones by the end.
        Window_DrawMode5Frame copies the spline's output straight
        into the camera position, so for this scene the spline IS the
        camera.

        THE AIM POINT IS NOT THE VESSEL. CamPath_Walk rewrites both
        of Camera_TrackTarget's offsets from the sine table every
        tick, at indices that differ by a factor of two:

            z offset = (sin[(cursor * 4) & 0x1fff] >> 3) - 0x2c3
            x offset =  sin[(cursor * 8) & 0x1fff] >> 4

        so what 0x002ccf40 seeds survives only as the z term's
        offset, and the point being tracked traces a Lissajous figure
        around the ship. The camera never quite settles on it, which
        over forty seconds is what keeps an uncut orbit from reading
        as a turntable.

        TWENTY-THREE OBJECTS ARE DRAWN FROM A COUNT OF TWENTY-TWO,
        and slot 8 - object 11, this scene's billboard - is the only
        geometry the planet scenes hide. Both facts are
        formats/geometry.txt 6.1.

        THE HANDOVER IS SEAMLESS IN THE DEMO AND IS NOT YET IN THE
        PORT. Mode 4's last frame scores 4.0 mean luminance error
        against the demo's own framebuffer and mode 5's sixth frame
        14.0 [observed, on hardware at frame 136 and 145]: the camera is
        right - its yaw is within 4 of the demo's at the same cursor
        and its pitch within 3, and the two converge by cursor 0x7fc -
        but the frame is nearly empty where the demo's is full of
        panelling.

        THE DECAL ITSELF IS RIGHT. Object 11's four vertices read the
        same out of guest memory as out of Fspacedata, and working the
        demo's own transform by hand from its own camera puts the
        first of them at (38, -28) - exactly what slot 8's projected
        scratch holds [verified]. g_snap holds the final mode-4 frame
        correctly too: 92 per cent lit, the surround with the small
        bright pane in it.

        THE DECAL IS DRAWN FROM MODE 5'S FIRST FRAME. A write watch
        on slot 8's projected scratch fires from frame 139 - the frame
        the mode becomes 5 - and every second frame after, always from
        Scene_TransformProject's store [verified]. Nothing gates it,
        and by cursor 0x22 it is a quad spanning x 22 to 144 and y 15
        to 91: the decal fills most of the frame while the camera is
        still at the window, which is what makes the handover read as
        continuous rather than as a cut.

        THE DEMO RENDERS EVERY SECOND VERTICAL BLANK. That watch fires
        on alternate frames, so the scene runs at 25 Hz while
        Anim_SceneTick advances at 50 - which is why the camera-path
        cursor runs ahead of the rendered frame count, 484 advances
        over 461 frames measured on hardware. A cursor
        must be READ from g_CamPathCursor; keying a comparison on a
        frame number lines up the wrong two frames and the error looks
        like camera drift.

        SCORED AGAINST THE DEMO'S OWN FRAMEBUFFER at four cursors read
        out of g_CamPathCursor [observed]:

            cursor    8      7.6      the handover
            cursor 0x22      6.9      the decal at full size
            cursor  968      6.5      mid sweep
            cursor 2044      8.5      well outside

        THE DEMO'S ANIMATOR RUNS AHEAD OF THE VIDEO FRAMES. It ticks
        on VERTB, and over a measured span it advances 484 times for
        461 frames, so a cursor must be READ from
        g_CamPathCursor and never inferred from a frame count - a
        comparison keyed on the frame number lines up the wrong two
        frames and the error looks like camera drift.

        The scene's light base is BLACK: maybe_Space_RunWindowScene
        opens with Ham8_BuildTables(0, 0, 0), not the white the first
        planet scene uses.

        THE SNAPSHOT'S STRIDE IS 256 AND ITS IMAGE IS 160 WIDE. The
        copy takes 256 longwords from each 160-longword source row,
        so 96 pixels of the next row trail every one and the last
        row reads 384 bytes past the framebuffer. Both are slop no
        UV reaches; the power-of-two stride makes the mapper's row
        address a shift instead of a multiply.

   2.9 LOGO AGAIN, green, reading something like "gan ia wade
        zone". 478 TICKS [observed, on hardware, frames 2141 to 2619
        - the gap between the window scene leaving mode 5 and the next
        ship scene entering mode 6], which is within ten of the 469
        measured for 2.6.

   2.10 THE ORB FORMS. Back on the ship. Lightning strikes from the
        disc perimeter to three wedges and then inward to the centre
        of the claw, and a sphere forms there. Many camera cuts
        through the sequence.

   2.11 THE CLAW CLAMPS. The six struts close on the sphere, which
        spins with heavy glow and ray effects.

   2.12 RELEASE. Several cuts of the sphere let go and falling
        toward the planet.

   2.12b THE ORB FALLS TO EARTH. The camera holds on the planet and
        the sphere drops away toward it, shrinking into the
        atmosphere. Space_RunEarth, anim mode 0xe -
        effects/earth.txt.

   2.13 THE SAND. Cut to a desert, opening from black bars. An Aztec
        stone relief lies buried under dunes with a trail of
        footprints across them; the sand blows away to reveal it, and
        the sky flickers with distant lightning. Sand_RunScene
        0x003132a4 - effects/sand.txt.

   2.14 THE RUINS. A flyby of green mountains and Aztec ruins, with
        heavy fog cutting the draw distance. Three camera cuts, and
        the scene fades in from black over about three frames.
        TempleModule_Run 0x003678d0 mode 0 - effects/temple.txt.

   2.15 THE TEMPLE RISES. The sphere races to a temple among the
        ruins and floats above it. It charges the temple, and the
        temple lifts out of the ground - it is a giant spaceship.

   2.16 THE ARK. Several cuts of that ship, which looks like Noah's
        ark with an Aztec temple amidships, climbing into space.

   2.17 THE PORTAL. A portal opens in space and the ark flies
        through it. Heavy glow.

   2.18 THE PORTAL CLOSES. Cut to the original ship with the planet
        and sun in frame, and an explosion where the portal was.

   2.19 THE DEBRIS. Debris rains down on the planet and explodes on

        being destroyed by the falling debris.

   2.20 THE CITY. A city landscape takes a nuclear blast - the
        shockwave crosses the screen and turns the buildings into
        debris, which is a great many cubes. THREE DISTINCT CAMERA
        CUTS of this. Some viewers believe it is pre-rendered;

   2.21 THE PLANET DIES. Back to space with the original ship and

   2.22 THE END. The ark flies off into a white light.

3. The part list in code

   THE DEMO IS SIX PARTS IN TWO ACTS [verified]. There is no flat part table
   anywhere; this call sequence is the order.

   Demo_RunAct1 (0x0021f7f4):
       load the music module Cmodule1
       D0 = 0 -> SpaceModule_Run

   Demo_RunAct2 (0x0021f82a):
       load the music module Cmodule2
       0x003132a4              the relic module, init
       D0 = 0 -> TempleModule_Run      CODE_06
       D0 = 1 -> SpaceModule_Run
       0x00313f6c              the relic module
       D0 = 2 -> SpaceModule_Run
       D0 = 1 -> TempleModule_Run

   Three modules, each entered with a part index in D0: the space module at
   CODE_03's start, the temple module at CODE_06's start, and the relic module
   at 0x00313xxx. Every step is guarded by g_AbortFlag and branches to a common
   exit, so one keypress unwinds a whole act.

   ACT 1 IS ONE CALL AND IT IS THE WHOLE OPENING - everything from the first
   frame to the orb forming. SpaceModule_Part0 runs:

   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+
   | Space_LoadAssets,             | loading and display setup       |
   | FUN_002ced9c, Display_Setup   |                                 |
   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+
   | Space_RunSunScene             | 2.3, the planet and the sun -   |
   |                               | all 2D, no geometry at all.     |
   |                               | effects/planet_sun.txt          |
   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+
   | D0 = 0, Logo_Run              | 2.2, a text screen              |
   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+
   | maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes   | 2.3 to 2.5, the planet and the  |
   |                               | approach - TWO scenes           |
   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+
   | D0 = 1, Logo_Run              | 2.6, a text screen              |
   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+
   | maybe_Space_RunWindowScene    | 2.7 and 2.8, the captain's      |
   |                               | window and the pull-back        |
   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+
   | D0 = 2, Logo_Run              | 2.9, a text screen              |
   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+
   | Space_RunPart                 | 2.10 to 2.12, the ship and orb  |
   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+
   | Space_RunRelease              | the orb release -               |
   |                               | effects/orb_release.txt         |
   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+
   | Space_RunEarth                | the orb falls to Earth -        |
   |                               | effects/earth.txt               |
   +-------------------------------+---------------------------------+

   THAT IS THE END OF SpaceModule_Part0, AND OF ACT 1. Demo_RunAct1
   is one call, SpaceModule_Run(0) [verified, 0x0021f80c], so the
   whole space sequence above is act 1.

   ACT 2 OPENS ON THE SAND. Demo_RunAct2's first call is
   Sand_RunScene at 0x003132a4 - effects/sand.txt - and only its
   FOURTH is maybe_DunesModule_Run at 0x00313f6c, after a further
   space section [verified, 0x0021f860 onward]. The two live in one
   hunk and share no code; effects/dunes.txt covers the later one.

   2.2 AND 2.3 RUN THE OTHER WAY ROUND FROM THE NUMBERING. Space_RunSunScene
   comes before the first Logo_Run [verified], and it is 2.3's planet and sun
   [observed] - so the running order is the terminal, the planet, then the logo.
   The numbers above are kept as transcribed; the table is the running order.

   Space_RunSunScene passes Ham8_BuildTables a base colour of (0,0,0), so its
   light byte DARKENS, and that is how the planet gets on screen at all - see
   effects/planet_sun.txt. Scene one of maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes draws a sun
   and a flare too but passes (0x3f,0x3f,0x3f) and brightens toward white.

   2.3 IS ONE SCENE SPLIT ACROSS THE LOGO [observed]. The planet and sun return
   immediately after it with the same backdrop and the same frame step for step,
   the shadow gone and the base colour white. So the running order is the
   terminal, the planet in shadow, the logo, then the planet lit - and 2.3's
   description above covers both halves.

   THE TERMINAL OF 2.1 IS A FLIPBOOK, AND IT GATES THE LOADER [verified].
   Palette_Init installs Terminal_VblHook through Vbl_SetHook before act 1
   begins, so Terminal_Tick (0x0033bc0c) runs once a frame from then on:

       i = g_TerminalStep >> 1
       f = g_TerminalScript[i]
       if f < 0:                    ; the 0xffff terminator
           g_TerminalStep -= 0x10   ; back EIGHT entries
           f = g_TerminalScript[g_TerminalStep >> 1]
           g_IntroDone = 1
       g_TerminalStep += 1
       Terminal_ShowFrame(f)

   NO FONT, NO GLYPH BLITTER, NO CHARACTERS. Every state of the line is a
   prerendered frame and the script only names which to show, which is why no
   screen's wording is in the string table. Terminal_ShowFrame writes eight
   bitplane pointers into the copper list and returns - a frame costs eight
   pointer patches and nothing else.

   THE SCRIPT INDEX IS THE STEP HALVED, so every entry lasts two frames. The
   script, read out of the image:

       1,1,1,1,0,0,0,0   x5        blink on the empty line
       1,1,1,1,0,0,2,2
       3,3,5,5,4,6,6,6
       7,7,9,9,8,10,10,10
       11,11,12,13,13,13,13
       12,12,12,12,13,13,15,15
       14,14,14,14
       17,17,17,16,16,16
       18,18,18,18,19,19,19,19   x4
       0xffff

   Frame 0 is the blank at g_TerminalBlank, which is all zeros [verified]. Odd
   and even frames are the same text with and without its trailing cursor, so
   alternating them is the blink and stepping to the next pair types a
   character.

   THE TERMINATOR DOES NOT STOP ANYTHING. It rewinds eight entries, and the last
   eight are 18,18,18,18,19,19,19,19, so the cursor blinks on the finished line
   for ever at eight frames a state.

   THE RATE IS VERTICAL BLANKS. Terminal_VblHook (0x0033bc4c) is two
   instructions - bsr Terminal_Tick, rts - and Vbl_SetHook installs it, so the
   step rises once per VBL [verified]. An entry is 2 VBLs = 40 ms, a character
   is one entry pair, and the four-entry tail states give a cursor 160 ms on and
   160 ms off: a 3.1 Hz blink. See reconstruction.txt section 3 for what that
   costs a host that ticks on its own refresh instead.

   AND THAT IS THE LOADER'S GATE: reaching the terminator sets g_IntroDone, the
   flag SpaceModule_Part0 blocks on after Space_LoadAssets. The text types while
   relic.dat is read and the demo begins when the slower of the two finishes.

   THE FRAMES ARE STORED 256 x 25, EIGHT PLANES, 19 of them, at
   g_TerminalFrames. A frame steps 0x320 within a plane and a plane steps
   0x3b40, so plane 0 holds every frame back to back before plane 1 begins -
   frame-major inside each plane.

   THE DISPLAY IS 192 x 24 AND THE REST IS PADDING [verified]. None of that is
   in the code - Terminal_ShowFrame writes pointers and nothing else - it is in
   g_CopperTerminal at 0x00367860:

       BPLCON0 8011   HIRES, BPU3 set with BPU 0 - eight planes
       DIWSTRT ec81   vstart 236, hstart 129
       DIWSTOP 04c1   vstop  260, hstop  449
       DDFSTRT 0070   lores 224 to 312: a narrow window inside
       DDFSTOP 009c    that 320-lores span, not filling it
       BPL1MOD 0000
       FMODE   0007   64-bit bitplane fetch, 32-bit sprite

   DIW gives the height outright: 260 - 236 is 24 of the stride's
   25 rows. The DDF span comes to 24 bytes a row in every AGA fetch mode, so 192
   of the stride's 256 pixels are fetched - and nothing in any frame inks past
   x=135, well inside that.

   THE STRIP IS EXACTLY CENTRED, which is the check on the whole derivation.
   Fetched data appears 17 lores pixels after DDFSTRT*2, the delay a stock
   screen's DDFSTRT 0x38 against hstart 0x81 also shows, so the 96 lores pixels
   land at 241..337 against a window of 129..449. Both have centre 289.
   Vertically the display opens at v=44, so line 236 is 192 rows down a
   256-line PAL screen: the strip sits low, with the rest of the screen
   background.

   TAKING 0x320/32 FOR THE HEIGHT CORRUPTS FRAME 19 AND NOTHING ELSE. 25 looks
   like the height and is not. Frame 19's row 24 begins at exactly +0x3b40, so
   it reads plane p+1's row 0 and lays a strip of garbage under the line. Frames
   1 to 18 have blank spare rows, and frame 19 is the one that loops for ever
   with the cursor on it, so the fault shows only on the finished line. The
   stride confirms 24: 18 frames of 0x320 plus 24 rows of 32 is 0x3b40 exactly,
   the data ending on the plane boundary.

   The 32-byte stride itself is not in the code either; it was recovered by
   scoring row-to-row correlation across candidate strides, where it wins by a
   wide margin, and DDF's 24 bytes agrees with it being the larger of the two.

   THE PALETTE IS g_TerminalPalette, 256 longwords of 0x00RRGGBB, and EVERY
   ENTRY IS A GREEN - the brightest about (21, 243, 21), index 0 black. There is
   no colour cycling; the halo around the glyphs is painted into the eight
   planes.

   CODE_05 LAYS THE OPENING OUT AS ONE RUN, which is how the palette was found:
   g_TerminalBlank is 0x320 bytes, g_TerminalPalette's 256 longwords are the
   next 0x400, and g_TerminalFrames begins immediately after -
   0x0034973c + 0x320 + 0x400 = 0x00349e60.

   Rendered that way the line reads "10,191 - AD", typed one character at a time
   behind a solid block cursor.

   sim/terminal.py decodes the script, the palette and the frames straight out
   of the executable, and sim/run.py plays it as part
   1.

   SCENES END ON THE MUSIC, NOT ON A FRAME COUNT. 0x0021ff3c arms a position in
   the module and 0x0021ff64 polls whether the replayer has reached it;
   maybe_Space_RunSunScene and several steps of Space_RunPart loop on exactly
   that.

   THE TEXT SCREENS ARE A RIPPLING SURFACE, NOT A BLIT. Logo_Run's frame loop is
   four steps:

   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Logo_DiffuseField       | one relaxation step over a height   |
   | 0x0031033e              | field about 320 x 128               |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Logo_DrawZoomedText     | the glyph image, scaled by 1/frame  |
   | 0x00310576              | into the field                      |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Logo_ShadeRefraction    | shade through the field's own       |
   | 0x003104a4              | gradient                            |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Logo_ChunkyToPlanar     | a plain c2p, triple buffered        |
   | 0x00310792              |                                     |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+

   THE SCENE OPENS WHITE AND ENDS BLACK, AND BOTH ARE THE PALETTE
   rather than the picture [verified]. Pal_ScaleLongword 0x00310a58
   builds every one of the 64 colours as

       c = (c * g_PalFadeScale) >> 8        0x00310be2, from 0xff
       c = min(0xff, c + g_PalFadeOffset)   0x00310be4, from 0xfe

   and Logo_Advance steps the two in opposite circumstances:

   +------------------+--------+------+---------------------------+
   | Counter          | From   | Step | When                      |
   +==================+========+======+===========================+
   | g_PalFadeOffset  | 0xfe   | -2   | every field until it      |
   |                  |        |      | reaches 0 - field 127     |
   +------------------+--------+------+---------------------------+
   | g_PalFadeScale   | 0xff   | -5   | only once g_LogoTicks is  |
   |                  |        |      | 0x40 or below, so fields  |
   |                  |        |      | 406 to 457                |
   +------------------+--------+------+---------------------------+

   So field 0's palette is the picture plus 0xfe, which saturates:
   THE SCREEN IS WHITE BEFORE ANYTHING IS DRAWN - Logo_MainLoop calls
   Pal_ScaleLongword once before its loop [0x00310304] - and the text
   fades up out of the white over the first 127 fields. At the other
   end the scale takes it to black on field 457, leaving 13 black
   fields before the countdown ends the scene.

   THE WHITE IS NOT AN OVERLAY and nothing is drawn twice. The same
   one-line change to each colour does both ends, and a reconstruction
   that fades the PICTURE instead has to fight the shader, which is
   writing the field's own brightness into the same pixels.

   ONE COUNTER DRIVES THE WHOLE EFFECT. g_LogoCounter starts at
   0x32 or 0x96 and Logo_Advance adds g_LogoCounterRate - 6, 0xe or
   0x18 by line count - once a frame. Logo_DrawZoomedText divides its numerator
   by it, so the text shrinks as it climbs; Logo_DiffuseField injects 0x28 into
   every cell while it has not passed g_LogoZoomLimit. The counter itself
   never stops growing - Logo_Advance has no ceiling - and it is
   Logo_DrawZoomedText that clamps it to the limit for its own divide
   [verified, 0x003105e0], which is why the zoom and the ripple settle
   together. The glow is the
   refraction, not a blur.

   g_LogoZoomLimit IS FIXED AND g_LogoCounter GROWS, and the two are easy to
   swap. Read them the other way round and the zoom divides by a constant: the
   text sits at its opening magnification for ever and the injection never
   stops. Both still run and both look plausible, which is what makes it worth
   stating.

   At the limit the geometry closes exactly - a one-line screen reaches a step
   that maps 521 of the art's 528 rows across the
   320-pixel screen.

   Logo_DiffuseField damps with `v -= v >> 4`, which is what makes it settle
   rather than ring forever.

   THE RELAXATION IS ONE FLAT LOOP OVER 40,959 CELLS AND NOT A LOOP OVER
   ROWS [verified, 0x00310374]. Three source pointers are set to
   cur - 1, cur - 321 and cur + 319, every read is an (An)+ and none of
   them is ever reset, so 0x3554 iterations of three cells walk the
   whole field in one pass. The stencil's +-1, +-320 and +-321 are
   therefore FLAT offsets:

       corners = c[-321] + c[-319] + c[319] + c[321]
       edges   = c[-320] + c[320] + c[-1] + c[1]
       v       = ((corners >> 6) + edges) >> 1
       v      -= out[n] ; v -= v >> 4 ; v += 0x28

   SO THE BORDERS RELAX LIKE EVERY OTHER CELL, and column 0's left
   neighbour is the row above's last cell. That coupling across the row
   ends is not visible in the picture, because the field either side of
   a row break is at much the same level, but confining the loop to the
   interior IS: the border cells then never damp while
   Logo_ShadeRefraction keeps feeding them through its `field -= d - a`,
   so they drift until the edge columns freeze into an imprint of
   whatever art last crossed them - a glowing fringe down both sides of
   the screen with the text's own shapes in it.

   IT READS 321 CELLS PAST BOTH ENDS OF THE BUFFER and writes 40,959 of
   the 40,960, so the last cell is never written either.

   [open] WHAT LIES EITHER SIDE OF THE FIELD. The stencil reads it every
   frame and it sets the first and last rows, but the buffers come from
   the pool and reading them needs the demo stopped inside Logo_Run. The
   reconstruction pads with zero, so its first and last rows relax
   toward nothing.

   Logo_Run indexes g_LogoVariants, a 3 x 16-byte table of three longword
   offsets plus the variant index. THE OFFSETS ARE LINE ADDRESSES into
   g_LogoLineArt (0x0031183c), and how many are non-zero decides the shape of
   the screen [verified]:

   +---------+---------------------------+----------------------+
   | Variant | Lines, top to bottom      | Stamped at columns   |
   +=========+===========================+======================+
   | 0       | nerve axis                | 0x10                 |
   +---------+---------------------------+----------------------+
   | 1       | schlott / meson           | 0x28, 0x10           |
   +---------+---------------------------+----------------------+
   | 2       | zone / wade / ganja       | 0x40, 0x28, 0x10     |
   +---------+---------------------------+----------------------+

   The last three are member names; the readme credits Wade with pixelled art
   and zONE with additional design.

   EACH LINE IS 528 x 16 OF 1-BIT ART, 66 bytes a row, at
   0x0031183c + the offset, with a 17th row of padding that brings the stride to
   1122. A SET BIT IS THE LETTER [observed]: Logo_StampLine turns a set bit into
   0x4f and a clear one into 0.

   THE INK COUNTS DO NOT SETTLE THE BIT SENSE, which is the trap here. The art
   stretches each screen's words across the same 528 pixels, so a ten-character
   line is 77.5% set and a four-character line 37% - and that fits BOTH senses,
   because fewer letters either means less ink or means bolder ones. Nor can the
   art be eyeballed: the lettering is stylised to the point of being unreadable
   at a glance in either sense. The sense was settled against the running demo
   and nothing else.

   THE LOGO SCREEN IS THE FULL 320 x 128 AND THERE IS NOTHING TO CLIP TO
   [verified]. Three independent sources agree: g_CopperLogo at 0x00345478 sets
   DIWSTRT 6c81 and DIWSTOP ebc1 - hstart 129 to hstop 449 is 320 lores pixels,
   vstart 108 to vstop 235 is 128 lines; Copper_PatchPlanes6 steps the six
   bitplane pointers by 0x1400, which is 40 bytes a row times 128 rows; and
   Logo_ChunkyToPlanar's loop ends when its output pointer has advanced exactly
   0x1400. BPLCON0 0x6001 is LORES with BPU 6, so 64 colours. Unlike the
   terminal, no part of the buffer goes undisplayed.

   THE ART IS STORED SQUASHED, sixteen rows to a line, and the display stretches
   it about eight times vertically. Read at 1:1 it is unrecognisable, which is
   how it hid: the stride and the boundaries were right long before anything was
   readable.

   Logo_StampLine TRANSPOSES as it writes - 16 columns of 528 into a buffer of
   0x100-byte rows - and averages each cell with the one above, so an edge
   reaches the field already soft. That running average is the seed glow.

   The variant index separately selects a 0x400-byte block from g_LogoPalettes,
   which Logo_SetPalette reads as 64 records of 0x10 bytes and uses the first
   longword of each. So EVERY TEXT SCREEN HAS ITS OWN 64 COLOURS.

   Logo_SetupVariant also gives each shape its own pace:

       lines  0x310b90  0x310b94  0x310b98  0x310ba0  inject
       1      0x348     0x3e800   0x28      0x14400   0x32
       2      0x7d0     0xbb800   0x10      0x16800   0x96
       3      0xaf0     0x177000  0x10      0x21c00   0x96

   THE SHADING PASS FEEDS BACK INTO THE FIELD. It does not only read it:

       d   = (the other chunky buffer + art) >> 3
       i   = (field >> 5) + d            ; a BYTE add
       out = ramp[i]
       field -= (d - art)

   so the surface keeps moving after the injection stops. Logo_BuildShadeRamp
   (0x0031029e) makes that ramp - 1024 bytes of min(i, 0x3f), linear and
   clamping at the screen's six planes. It is 1024 and not 256 because the index
   is taken as a WORD and the add only carries inside the low byte.

   SUBSTITUTING ANOTHER 256-BYTE TABLE OF THE RIGHT SHAPE DOES NOT WORK.
   g_BlobFalloff's first row is 0 then 255 repeated - a step, not a ramp - and
   shading through it drives every pixel to one colour.

   src/part_logo.c carries all four steps. The c2p has no counterpart there:
   with no chipset the chunky buffer IS the picture and the 64-colour palette is
   looked up directly.

   THE FIELD IS COMPUTED AND DELIBERATELY NOT DISPLAYED. Its injection is
   uniform, so on its own it relaxes to a featureless dome - there is nothing to
   look at until the shading pass refracts the art through its gradient, and a
   false-colour view of it would show our choices rather than the demo's. The
   part reports the field's range instead, which is enough to check the
   relaxation: damping of v >> 4 against an injection of 0x28 puts equilibrium
   at 640, and it climbs to 317 by tick 200.

3.1. How long each part lasts, and what decides it

   TWO MECHANISMS, NOT ONE. A part either counts its own ticks down or waits on
   a music cue, and which it does is not obvious from watching it.

   +--------------+--------+-----------+------------------------------+
   | Part         |  ticks | decided by| evidence                     |
   +==============+========+===========+==============================+
   | 2.1 terminal |    242 | a script  | [verified] 0x0033bd18        |
   | 2.2 logo     |    470 | a counter | [verified] 0x003100be        |
   | 2.3 sun      |    430 | music cue | [observed] frames 93-523     |
   | 2.3b planet  |    242 | music cue | [observed] frames 994-1236   |
   | 2.4-2.5      |   1028 | music cue | [observed] frames 1242-2270  |
   | 2.6 logo     |    470 | a counter | [verified] 0x003100be        |
   | 2.7-2.8      |   2155 | music cue | [observed]                   |
   | 2.9 logo     |    470 | a counter | [verified] 0x003100be        |
   +--------------+--------+-----------+------------------------------+

   The frame numbers are measured on hardware, from a write watch on
   g_SceneMode: it goes to 3 for the sun scene, 0, then 2 for the lit planet, 0,
   then 1 for the approach, and 0 again. The logo between the first two measured
   471 against its own constant of 470, which is the boundary and not a
   discrepancy.

   2.4 AND 2.5 SHARE ONE FIGURE because the mode stays at 1 across both of
   maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes' second-half loops - the approach, which runs
   while g_ApproachRange is positive, and the Space_DrawFullFrame loop after it,
   which runs to a cue. The mode is only cleared once both are done.

   THE GAP BEFORE 2.7 IS NOT ALL LOGO. Mode 1 clears on frame 2270 and mode 4
   appears on 3229, which is 959 ticks for a 470-tick logo: the window scene's
   setup then blocks on cue (12,0) through 0x0021ff50 before it draws anything,
   and that wait is the rest.

   THE THREE LOGO SCREENS ARE THE SAME 470 TICKS. Logo_SetupVariant seeds 0x1d6
   into g_LogoTicks and the logo's own VBL hook takes one off per vertical
   blank, with Logo_MainLoop running while it stays positive - so the count is
   in the same unit as everything else here and does not depend on the variant.
   Measuring 2.6 and 2.9 off the emulator gave 469 and 478; the 478 came of
   timing the gap between two scene modes, which includes a scene's teardown and
   the next one's setup, so the constant is the better number.

   2.1 IS A FLIPBOOK SCRIPT AND A LOADING GATE. g_TerminalScript holds
   121 entries before its 0xffff terminator and Terminal_Tick indexes it with
   the step HALVED, so the typing finishes on tick 242 and sets g_IntroDone.
   SpaceModule_Part0 blocks on that flag after Space_LoadAssets, so the part
   lasts the LONGER of the typing and the relic.dat read - 242 is a floor, not a
   length, and on a slow drive the cursor blinks on the finished line until the
   load catches up. Past the terminator the script rewinds eight entries and
   repeats 18,18,18,18,19,19,19,19 for ever.

   THE SCENE PARTS ARM A CUE AND POLL IT. Each calls Music_ArmCue (0x0021ff3c)
   with a position in D0 and a row in D1, then loops on Music_PollCue
   (0x0021ff64) [verified]:

       2.3        waits (1,0x10), ends (2,0x18)
       2.3b-2.5   ends (4,0), then the approach ends (4,0x10)
       2.7-2.8    waits (12,0), ends (21,0)

   So their lengths are the module's, not the code's, and only a run settles
   them. ONE CALIBRATION EXISTS: 2.7-2.8 spans D0 12 to 21 in
   2155 ticks, which is about 239 ticks per unit of D0 [inferred] - enough to
   sanity-check a measurement, not to replace one.

3.2. Space_RunPart, the orb sequence, phase by phase

   2.10 TO 2.12 ARE ONE ROUTINE. Space_RunPart (0x002cc590) is a linear script
   of ten phases that walks g_SceneMode through 6, 0x13, 0x12, 0xa, 0xb and
   0xc, and the only frame routines it uses are Scene_RenderFrame (0x002cd892),
   0x002cd8fe and 0x002cd96e [verified]. IT RETURNS AFTER PHASE 10;
   SpaceModule_Part0 then calls Space_RunRelease for the release scene
   [verified, 0x002c83fa] - see effects/orb_release.txt.

   THE GEOMETRY ARRIVES BY COUNTER, NOT BY LOADING. g_SceneObjectCount starts at
   0x16 and the script raises it between phases, so the beams, the sprites and
   the orb appear as the slots Space_BuildObjects already filled come into range
   - formats/geometry.txt 6.1:

    +----+------+-------+---------------------------------------------+
    | ph | mode | count | ends on                                     |
    +====+======+=======+=============================================+
    |  1 |    6 |    22 | the fuse, 0xc8 - 200 ticks                  |
    |  2 | 0x13 |    25 | 0x002e0ac4 climbing -0x32 to 0x20           |
    |  3 | 0x13 |    34 | the fuse, 0x14 - 20 ticks                   |
    |  4 | 0x13 |    37 | 0x002e0ac8 climbing 0 to 0x20               |
    |  5 | 0x13 |    37 | the fuse, 5 ticks                           |
    |  6 | 0x13 |    38 | music cue (0x19, 0)                         |
    |  7 | 0x12 |    38 | music cue (0x1e, 0x34)                      |
    |  8 |  0xa |  0x26 | music cue (0x20, 0)                         |
    |  9 |  0xb |  0x26 | the fuse                                    |
    | 10 |  0xc |     - | the fuse, 0x104 - 260 ticks                 |
    +----+------+-------+---------------------------------------------+

   PHASE 7 MEASURES 1382 TICKS, bracketed by stepping the mode word on
   hardware: still 0x12 at 1375 frames and 0xa at 1388 [observed].
   src/part_underside.c is part 2.11.

   FOUR OF THE TEN END ON A CUE AND THEY ARE THE LONG ONES. A phase
   with no fuse seeded keeps counting whatever the previous phase left
   it, so its measured length says nothing about its rule - phase 6's
   136 ticks are a stand-in for cue (0x19, 0) and nothing more. Phase
   7, mode 0x12, is the from-underneath shot: effects/orb_underside.txt.

   PHASE LENGTHS, MEASURED off a write watch on g_SceneObjectCount
   [observed, on hardware]. THEY ARE NOT THE FUSES: each transition costs a
   frame or two outside the loops, so phase 3's fuse of 20 measures 22.

       frame    count   phase
           7       22   1 begins
         208       25   2 begins, so phase 1 is 201
         290       34   3 begins, so phase 2 is 82
         312       37   4 begins, so phase 3 is 22
         352       38   6 begins, so phases 4 and 5 are 40
         488        -   mode 0x13 ends, so phase 6 MEASURES 136,
                           against a cue rather than a fuse

   PHASES 2 AND 4 ARE THE TWO BEAM GROUPS GROWING, so their exit counters are
   the beams' own strengths rather than a fuse: 0x002e0ac4 is g_BeamPhaseA and
   0x002e0ac8 g_BeamPhaseB. How a beam is aimed, grown and drawn is
   effects/orb.txt.

   The fuse is g_SceneFuseTicks: Anim_SceneTick counts it down and raises
   g_QuitRequested at zero, which is the flag each phase loop polls - so a phase
   seeded with a fuse has a fixed length in ticks and one waiting on a cue does
   not. PHASE 1 MEASURES 201 TICKS on hardware, frames 2619 to 2820, against
   its fuse of 200 [observed]; mode 0x13 measures 280 across phases 2 to 6 and
   mode
   0x12 begins at 3100.

   THE SCENE HAS ITS OWN CAMERA PATH AND ITS OWN LIGHT BASE. Ham8_BuildTables
   takes (0x3f, 0x4f, 0x6f) - the green and blue arrive past the 0x3f00 clamp,
   so the base is white and those two channels saturate first - and
   CamPath_Setup is handed g_pSpaceDataBlocks' SECOND tail block rather than the
   window scene's first. Phase 10 rebuilds the tables again at (0x5f, 0x5f,
   0x7f) and applies a THIRD skip table at 0x002ccc24, which is where the
   sequence leaves the ship.

   MODE 6'S TICK IS THE SIMPLEST OF THEM. The cursor advances ONE a tick where
   mode 5 advanced two, g_CamTrackOffsetZ climbs 0x14 a tick from -0xa00 so the
   aim point rises, and 0x002e0bfc counts down from 0x32 to call
   Strut_SetClawPose - so THE CLAWS BEGIN TO OPEN 50 TICKS IN. The horizon band
   at the end of Scene_RenderFrame is switched off for the whole early sequence
   by 0x002e0bf8 = 1 and only appears when phase 6 clears it.

   PHASE 1'S CAMERA PATH IS IN THE EXECUTABLE, NOT IN relic.dat. Mode 6's arm
   does lea (0x002eea14), A0 and indexes it as base + (cursor >> 8) * 12, with
   t the cursor's low byte and the cursor advancing ONE a tick where mode 5
   advanced two [verified]. The layout is the window path's - three longwords a
   key, and a pre-entry at base - 12 duplicating key 0 because
   CamPath_EvalCatmullRom wants a P[-1]:

       P[-1]   0, -6998, 4000        the duplicate
       P0      0, -6998, 4000
       P1      12000, -12000, 9000
       P2      18000, -11000, 10000
       P3      22000, -10000, 10000

   SO THE KEY NEVER CHANGES IN THIS PHASE. 200 ticks of a cursor stepping one
   is t = 0 to 200 of 256, which is keyframe 0's segment and nothing more - the
   camera leaves (0, -6998, 4000) and is at about (9449, -11176, 8038) when the
   fuse ends. Nothing clamps the key, so a longer phase would walk on into the
   table.

   THE SETUP'S g_CamSrc IS NOT THE OPENING POSITION. Space_RunPart writes
   (0x1c54, -0x3458, 0x25ab) but mode 6's first tick overwrites all three from
   the spline, so that triple only feeds the two frames drawn before
   Space_DisplayOn. Holding the camera there instead leaves it somewhere the
   demo is never at and the aim swings in over the following seconds - which
   reads as the camera snapping into position.

   Verified against the demo at cursor 98 [observed, on hardware at frame 107]:
   the position comes back (3937, -8797, 5707) on both sides to the unit, with
   the latched yaw and pitch within a couple of ticks of easing.

   src/part_orb.c covers phases 1 to 6 - 481 ticks - with the camera path, the
   claw pose and the beams. Verified against the demo at tick 339 [observed,
   frame 348]: the camera reads (14435, -12124, 9651) on both sides to the unit
   and both beam strengths come back 88 and 34.

   PHASE 3'S RAISE OF NINE IS THE THREE ENVELOPE SHELLS, three faces each,
   and they arrive on the same tick as the six attach flares. The port draws
   them from phase 3: its face count steps 228 to 237 at tick 284 [observed].
   effects/orb.txt 9.

   WHAT IS STILL MISSING IS THE ORB. It is a type 3 object drawn by the
   branch at 0x002d2b3c, is not transcribed, and the demo's bright cluster in
   the claw is the scene's largest remaining difference.

   SCORED ON THE RGB CHANNELS ALONE: 11.2 at tick 276 and 18.7 at tick 340

       0x002cc8ce   = 0x26     and Strut_ResetClaw straightens the
                               struts three instructions later

   So tentacles and spikes appearing partway through the part are not geometry
   being created. They were always there and the renderer had not reached them
   yet.

   It is the first sequencer found. Whether the other parts have one each or
   share a driver is not known, and nothing yet shows what calls Space_RunPart
   itself.
