PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                        THE PORTAL FLASH AND ITS SCENE


Abstract

   maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes runs two scenes. The first is the lit
   planet with the voxel object, effects/approach.txt. This note is
   the second: the white cut between them, the flash that blooms out
   of it, the spark shower, and the two passes that are not yet read.

   The scene is mode 1 in Anim_SceneTick's dispatch and
   Space_DrawApproachFrame is its frame routine.

   THIS IS THE ASTEROID EXPLODING, not a portal opening. parts.txt
   numbers it 2.4, an observer describes the asteroid bursting here,
   and the routine is maybe_Explosion_DrawFlash. The "portal" reading
   of section 3's flash is [inferred] from an observer reporting a
   light the ship emerges from, and the ship does arrive in the same
   scene's second phase - but the flash itself sits where the asteroid
   was, so the explosion is the better name. This file keeps its
   name because the cross-references do.

1.  The Handover

   SCENE TWO DOES NOT FADE. The sequencer fills both framebuffers
   with a single colour between the two scenes and mode 1's first
   pass draws over the result [verified, 0x002cd422]:

       A0 = g_pFrameBuffer          0x002f101c
       A1 = the other buffer        0x002f1020
       D0 = 0x3f3f003f
       0xf9f + 1 passes of four longword stores into each

   4000 passes of four is 16000 longwords, which is 160 x 100
   exactly. 0x3f3f003f is R, G, light, B with the light byte zero, so
   it is the same white the ramp builders produce - see
   subsystems/display.txt for the pixel.

   IT RUNS ONCE AND OUTSIDE EITHER FRAME LOOP, which is what makes it
   a cut rather than a wash. Section 5.1's ripple overwrites the
   framebuffer from its own source on every pass, so the white
   survives for as long as it takes mode 1 to draw its first frame
   and no longer.

   Reproducing it per frame instead pins every channel at CHAN_MAX,
   and because Draw_Flare ADDS into the framebuffer that swallows the
   flash of section 3 whole: a white screen with nothing on it.

   The same longword goes through Ham8_BuildTables at the handover -
   D0 = 0x1f, D1 = 0, D2 = 0x2f at 0x002cd2ba - and the camera is
   snapped to the limit scene two's yaw gate stops at:

       g_CameraYaw     -0xaca       0x002cd2dc
       g_CameraPitch   -0x4f        0x002cd2e6

2.  The Scene's Clock

   g_ApproachRange IS THE WHOLE SCENE'S CLOCK and there is no other
   [verified]. The sequencer seeds it and mode 1 counts it down:

       seed       0xe60            0x002cd2f0
       per tick   -= 0xc, floored at zero, and skipped at zero
                                   0x002d5d44

   So it reaches zero 0xe60 / 0xc = 312 ticks in. Three things read
   it: the flash's radius, the ripple's amplitude, and the gate on
   the object pass - which runs only at 0x960 or below
   [verified, 0x002cd53a].

   THAT IS NOT THE SCENE'S LENGTH. Mode 1 runs to a music cue, like
   every other scene here, so 312 ticks is how long the countdown
   lasts and not how long the scene does.

   THE SCENE'S LAST TICK IS 1028, AND g_RipplePhase DATES IT
   [observed, on hardware]. Mode 1 subtracts 0x1f4 from it once a tick
   for the whole scene, both loops, and nothing writes it afterwards -
   so the last write is the last tick. On hardware it reads
   -503500, which is tick 1007, and the final write is -514000, which
   is tick 1028. So the scene is 1029 ticks and src/part_portal.c
   declares that.

   WHAT FOLLOWS IS 2.6, THE SECOND TEXT SCREEN. SpaceModule's table
   calls Logo_Run with D0 = 1 - parts.txt section 3 - and the variant
   index is the only thing that differs from 2.2: it picks up to three
   line-art offsets out of g_LogoVariants and its own 64-colour
   palette. Variant 1 has two lines. So it is the same effect with
   different letters and different colour, and src/part_logo.c carries
   it as a second Relic_Part rather than a second implementation.

3.  The Flash

   maybe_Explosion_DrawFlash (0x002cd71c) is one Draw_Flare at the
   screen centre, and its whole content is the radius [verified]:

       t = (g_ApproachRange >> 4) - 0xc8
       t = t < 0 ? -t : t * 2
       r = 0x80 - t - 0xa
       nothing is drawn at r <= 0

       D2 = 0x56, D3 = 0x34        the screen centre
       A1 = g_pRayStreak           0x002f1030
       A4 = g_ColRampAmber         0x002ef430

   A V IN THE RANGE, WITH TWO DIFFERENT SLOPES. The peak is where
   range >> 4 reaches 0xc8, which is range 0xc80, and above that the
   term is doubled. From the 0xe60 seed:

       +-------+------------+--------+------------------------+
       | tick  | range      | radius | what it is doing       |
       +=======+============+========+========================+
       | 0     | 0xe60      | 58     | opens, already large   |
       | 39    | 0xc8c      | 118    | the peak               |
       | 119   | 0x8cc      | 58     | ebbing                 |
       | 197   | 0x520      | 0      | gone                   |
       +-------+------------+--------+------------------------+

   So it rushes in over forty ticks and takes four times as long to
   leave. It is gone less than two thirds of the way through the
   countdown.

   ITS COLOUR IS NOT ITS RAMP. g_ColRampAmber is built (0x3f, 0x2f,
   0x20) from 0x780000 [verified, 0x002c9cec], which on its own is
   amber. The scene reads pastel magenta because of two other things:
   Ham8_BuildTables is called (0x1f, 0, 0x2f) at the handover, so the
   light channel resolves toward PURPLE here and not toward scene
   two's white, and Draw_Flare ADDS over the sky of section 5.1
   rather than over black. Amber added to a dark purple field is the
   pastel; amber on black is yellow.

   ITS RAY TABLE IS A STREAK, NOT A RING. g_pRayStreak is the third
   of Ray_BuildTables' three tables and the only one built without a
   loop - ten explicit word stores and nothing else
   [verified, 0x002ca104]:

       index    0    1    2    3    4
       value    2    4    6    4    2

       index  126  127  128  129  130
       value    2    4    6    4    2

   Two five-entry bumps 128 angles apart, which is half of the
   table's 256, so they face opposite ways: one streak across the
   flare rather than the ring of spikes the other two tables give.
   Every other entry is left as the buffer was. The d7 of 0xff set
   before the stores is never used by anything.

4.  The Sparks

   g_SparkList (0x002f0f50) is ten entries of five longs,
   [x, y, vx, vy, life], with x and y in 12.4 fixed [verified].

   maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes seeds it at the handover from the
   object's last projected position [verified, 0x002cd44a]:

       x     = g_LightProjX + (rand(0x190) - 0xc8)
       y     = g_LightProjY + (rand(0x190) - 0xc8)
       vx    = rand(0x18) + 4
       vy    = -(rand(0x10) + 4)
       life  = (rand(0x10) + 8) << 8

   Every vx is positive and every vy negative, so the shower always
   goes up and to the right. Math_Rand is the demo's own LCG and the
   only randomness in it - see subsystems/effects.txt.

   maybe_Flare_StepSparks (0x002cd5da) steps and draws them:

       life -= 0x80, and the entry is skipped once it is <= 0
       x += vx,  y += vy
       r = (life >> 8) + rand(4)
       Draw_Flare(r, (x >> 4) + 0x50, (y >> 4) + 0x32)
       A4 = g_ColRampAmber

   THE FLARE IS DRAWN AT THE PRE-STEP POSITION. The value written
   back is x + vx but the one passed to Draw_Flare is the x the entry
   was loaded with, so the drawn position trails the stored one by a
   tick.

   ONLY SEVEN OF THE TEN ARE EVER STEPPED. The dbf runs 0x6 + 1
   times over a list the seeder fills ten entries of, so the last
   three are written once and never read. Nothing else in the demo
   was found reading them.

   Life starts between 8 << 8 and 0x17 << 8 and falls 0x80 a tick, so
   a spark lasts between 16 and 46 ticks and its radius halves every
   two of them. The shower is over well before the flash is.

5.  The Background

5.1.  The Warp Buffer, And Why The Scene Has Stars

   Warp_FullscreenRipple (0x002cd760) is mode 1's first drawing step
   and it is what carries the scene's picture. Its source is
   g_pWarpBuffer + 0x2e0 [verified, 0x002cd52a].

   THE BUFFER IS 176 x 102 FOUR-BYTE PIXELS, and the allocation says
   so outright. Mem_AllocSubBlocks (0x0021fdca) takes a descriptor
   [count, on hardware...] in A0 and a size list in A1, sums the sizes,
   allocates once, zeroes the lot and fills each slot with a
   successive pointer. The handover passes A0 = g_WarpAllocCount,
   A1 = g_WarpAllocSize and D1 = 4 for MEMF_FAST, and those two read
   out of the image as [verified]:

       g_WarpAllocCount    1
       g_WarpAllocSize     0x11880

   0x11880 is 71808 bytes, 17952 longwords, and 17952 / 176 is 102
   with no remainder. So one buffer, 176 x 102, and g_pWarpBuffer is
   its only slot.

   0x2e0 IS ONE ROW PLUS EIGHT LONGWORDS - 736 bytes, 184 pixels,
   176 + 8 - so the live region starts at the buffer's row 1, column
   8. The padding is a row top and bottom and eight columns each side.

   SKY_RENDERTOWARPBUFFER FILLS IT, AND THE STARS ARE THE SKY
   [verified, 0x002d32dc]. It is Scene_DrawTexturedSky with two values
   changed - 176 columns instead of 160, and g_pWarpBuffer instead of
   the framebuffer - and the inner loop is instruction for instruction
   identical to it. So this scene's background is the same generated
   sky the planet scenes use: Sky_GeneratePlasma's field, Sky_AddStars'
   stars and Sky_AddNebulae's blobs.

   THE EIGHT COLUMNS ARE NOT SLACK, THEY ARE SKY. The corners here are
   +-0x5800 over 0xb0 columns and Scene_DrawTexturedSky's are +-0x5000
   over 0xa0; 0xb000 / 0xb0 and 0xa000 / 0xa0 are both 0x100, so the
   two passes sample at exactly the same rate and the extra sixteen
   columns are eight more of real sky each side. That is what lets the
   ripple displace by up to eight pixels without ever reading off the
   end.

   IT RUNS ONCE, at 0x002cd376, between the two scenes. The sky is a
   still, baked with the camera angle scene two ended on, and every
   frame after that is the ripple resampling it. Only rows 0 to 99 of
   the 102 are written, from offset 0, so the live region's last row
   is one the sky pass never reached and the allocator left at zero.

5.2.  The Ripple Itself

   Warp_FullscreenRipple's per-pixel work is [verified]:

       dx   = (s8)(col - 0x56),  dy = row - 0x34
       dist = g_PolarTable[(dy << 8) | dx] & 0xff      low byte only
       d4   = dist - (g_ApproachRange >> 3)
       t    = (dist << 7) + g_RipplePhase
       if t >= 0:   dst = src[-8]                      plain copy
       else:
           v  = (g_CosTable[(t & 0x1fff) - 8] * range) >> 10
           v  = (v + 0x10000) >> 10
           d5 = v >> 3                                 0..16, 8 centred
           w  = min(v + 0x1f4 + d4, 0xff)
           dst = src[-8 + d5], byte 2 := (w ^ 0xff) >> 2

   WHAT RIPPLES IS THE LIGHT CHANNEL, NOT THE COLOUR. ror.l #8 brings
   byte 2 - the HAM light index - into the low byte, the computed w
   replaces it outright, and rol.l #8 puts it back. R, G and B are
   taken unchanged from the displaced source. So this routine writes a
   light index that resolves toward section 3's purple base, and THAT
   IS THE SCENE'S EXPANDING MAGENTA. It is one effect, not a second
   glow layered over the flash.

   THE DISPLACEMENT IS HORIZONTAL ONLY. There is no vertical term; d5
   indexes along the row, spanning -8 to +8 pixels about the pixel's
   own column - exactly the slack section 5.1's buffer carries. The
   vertical padding is never used by this caller.

   THE WAVEFRONT EXPANDS FROM THE CENTRE. A pixel takes the plain copy
   unless (dist << 7) + g_RipplePhase is negative, and mode 1 winds
   g_RipplePhase backwards 0x1f4 a tick from zero - 0x002d5d32, before
   anything else in the tick - so the rippling disc is
   dist < -phase / 128 and reaches the corners about thirty ticks in.

   THE COPY BRANCH IS NOT THE ZERO-DISPLACEMENT CASE. It takes src[-8]
   flat, while a displaced pixel at range 0 works out at src[-8 + 8],
   the pixel itself. The untouched outside of the wavefront therefore
   sits eight pixels left of the rippled inside, which is a visible
   seam at the wavefront and not a transcription slip.

   Both the cosine index and the source carry a -0x20 byte bias, which
   is -8 entries and -8 pixels; both are the demo's own.

5.3.  Where The Cut Lives

   THE WHITE BELONGS TO NEITHER SCENE. Space_FillWhite is called from
   maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes between the two frame loops, so it is
   not a step of either effect - the demo structures a transition as
   sequencer code, and the incoming scene is simply what is underneath
   it. Both framebuffers are filled, so nothing on screen changes
   until mode 1 finishes a frame.

   IT LASTS FOUR PAL FRAMES [observed, on hardware]. The seed at
   Portal_SeedRange writes g_ApproachRange on frame 28 and
   Anim_SceneTick's first decrement of it lands on frame 32. After
   that mode 1 decrements once per frame from 32 to 70 with no gaps,
   so the scene runs at a full 50 Hz and only its first frame is slow.

5.4.  The Ship

   Scene_SortAndDrawAll (0x002d28ca) is called by both mode 1's frame
   and Space_DrawFullFrame, gated on g_ApproachRange <= 0x960, and it
   is the ship. Space_DrawFullFrame (0x002cd55c) is eight such calls
   behind the same nine latch copies, and is the scene after this one.

   THE SHIP IS NOT A SCENE OF ITS OWN, and treating it as one cannot
   work. parts.txt numbers the asteroid exploding 2.4 and the ship
   arriving 2.5, but both are mode 1 - one scene, one
   g_ApproachRange - so the vessel appears while the ripple and the
   flash are still running and shares their clock. The pass sits
   between the ripple and the flash in the frame's order, so the ship
   is drawn over the warped sky and under the glow.

   TWO GATES, NOT ONE, AND THEY DIFFER. g_ObjectAngle starts turning
   at range 0xa00 (0x002d5d56) but Scene_SortAndDrawAll only runs at
   0x960 (0x002cd53a), so the vessel is already spinning before it is
   drawn - about 13 ticks of spin by the time it appears.

   THE OBJECTS ARE PLACED BY THEIR OWN VERTICES, not by the scene
   slot. Obj_SetCentroid writes the vertex MEAN into +0x18, +0x20 and
   +0x1c, and Scene_SortAndDrawAll reads it only to compute +0x24, the
   sort key - Scene_TransformProject never touches it. Fspacedata
   already holds the hull discs at comp2 -4024 and -2438 and the
   struts at 2415, so the vessel is assembled in the chunk. That also
   settles what Space_BuildObjects' per-slot subi instructions are:
   0x186a0 off slot 0's +0x1c and 0xc350 off slot 1's are SORT-KEY
   BIASES reaching the key through the latched pitch, not placements.

   THE LATCHED CAMERA YAW IS WHAT PUTS THE SHIP IN FRONT OF THE EYE,
   and nothing else does. Scene_TransformProject subtracts the camera
   POSITION before rotating, so maybe_Camera_Rotate2AxisLatched
   receives a huge offset vector rather than a small object-space one.
   The yaw is -0xaca here, about -121 degrees, and it turns that
   offset's comp0 into the depth. Measured live for vertex 0 of scene
   object 0 [observed, on hardware]:

       after the camera subtraction   comp0 +78448, depth -51320
       after the yaw                  depth  +93650
       projected                      x = 88

   and 88 is the value the demo writes at 0x002d2d2e, so the chain is
   confirmed end to end. The camera position alone would leave every
   depth around -51000; it is the rotation that rescues it.

   TAKING THE LATCHED ANGLES AS ZERO IS THEREFORE FATAL AND SILENT.
   Every vertex of every object falls behind the near plane, nothing
   is drawn, and the result is indistinguishable from geometry that
   failed to load. g_CameraYawLatched is zero in the planet scenes,
   which is what makes the assumption tempting.

   SO THE SHIP ARRIVES WITH THE GATE, NOT WITH THE CAMERA. It is
   drawn from the first frame g_ApproachRange is at or under 0x960 -
   tick 107 of the scene, while the flash is still around radius 67
   and shrinking - and the camera's travel afterwards only brings it
   closer. The demo's own first projected write lands on that same
   tick [observed, on hardware].

   src/ship.c IS A DRAWING MODULE AND NOT A PART for that reason: the
   owning part passes the angle in. It carries Fspacedata's objects,
   Scene_TransformProject's chain and Draw_LineAA, which is the
   demo's; the textured fill is section 5.2's open question.

   GFX_LINEAA SCALES BY THE FRAME ITSELF. Scaling the projected
   coordinates before handing them to it multiplies the scale twice,
   which is the identity at 1.0 and throws every line clean off a
   larger frame - so a reference render looks right and a window shows
   nothing at all.

5.6.  The Sky's Two Axes

   Scene_DrawTexturedSky builds its pan offset from BOTH camera
   accumulators, and only the low byte of it comes from the one that
   is obvious [verified, 0x002d2e64]:

       D4 = g_CameraAngleLatched  ; D4 += D4 ; swap D4
       D7 = (0x002e0ad4)          ; D7 += D7 ; D7 >>= 8
       move.b D4b,D7b
       A6 = D7 * 4                  a BYTE offset into the texture

   THE SWAP IS WHAT MAKES THE HORIZONTAL TERM (angle * 2) >> 16, and
   the byte move is what confines angle2 to WHOLE ROWS: 256 texels of
   four bytes is 1024, which is exactly what D7's upper bits are
   scaled by. So angle slides the field sideways inside a row and
   angle2 moves it vertically, a row at a time.

   TAKING ONLY THE LOW BYTE LEAVES A SKY THAT CAN TRAVEL SIDEWAYS AND
   NOTHING ELSE, which is the whole of the vertical drift missing.

   THE ACCUMULATORS CARRY ACROSS THE HANDOVER. The sequencer clears
   neither at 0x002cd2a8 onward, so scene two's values are what
   Sky_RenderToWarpBuffer bakes with AND what mode 1's live sky starts
   from. Starting the live sky at zero instead moves the entire field
   by the accumulated pan in a single frame, on the exact frame the
   ripple stops - which reads as the starfield jumping as the waving
   ends.

   Space_CamMove is what feeds them: the yaw delta resolved through
   the heading accumulates into angle and the pitch component into
   angle2, so the tracker of section 5.5 drives both axes at once.

6.  The Reconstruction

   src/part_portal.c is part 2.4. It carries the handover fill, the
   countdown, the flash and the sparks; sections 5.1 and 5.2 are
   marked [open] in it with what each needs.

   Space_BakeSky fills the demo's own 176 x 102 buffer and
   Space_Ripple reads its live region, so the background and its
   wavefront are both the demo's.

   THE SHIP IS TEXTURED. src/ship.c carries the nine bit-selected
   objects, Mesh_SortFacesByC2's setup-time face order,
   Poly_DrawFaceList's backface cross product and the texel fill;
   src/uv.c generates the coordinates and src/rast.c fills the spans.
   Of 267 faces about 90 survive the cull at any angle.

   THE FILL IS WHAT PUTS THE SHIP UNDER THE EXPLOSION. A texel is
   0xRRGG00BB and the light byte is zero in every one, so filling a
   face writes zero there and the flash's additive pass lies OVER the
   vessel. Drawing it with Draw_LineAA instead writes 0x3f into the
   light channel, which nothing later reduces, and the wireframe then
   sits on top of the glow however the order is arranged.

   THE REPLAY MUST RESET AND RESTORE. Approach_FinalLightProj and
   Approach_FinalSkyPan both replay scene two's step, and
   approach_step reseeds only the light's position - not the camera.
   Replaying on top of a scene that has already run therefore starts
   with g_CameraYaw already past its -0xaca gate, so the pan never
   runs and the projection lands somewhere else entirely. The visible
   symptom was the spark shower flying up and to the LEFT after
   watching the scene through, and up and to the right after jumping
   straight to it.

   THE SPARKS NEED SCENE TWO'S LAST PROJECTION, which is the product
   of that scene's whole accumulated pan and has no closed form.
   Approach_FinalLightProj replays the step - arithmetic only,
   nothing drawn - and reports where it ends, so part 2.4 seeds
   correctly whether or not part 2.3b was watched first.

   THE HANDOVER IS IN THE CORE, NOT A HOST. Relic_Part carries a
   ticks field and demo.c hands over to the next part when a part
   reaches it; a part declaring zero never hands over, which is every
   part whose length is still a music cue. Both scenes end on a cue
   and carry a measured fallback for a host that cannot answer one -
   242 for scene two, 1028 here.

   THE MOUSE LOOK REACHES BOTH FRAME ROUTINES, so the vessel swings
   with the pointer from the moment it arrives. It is a control of
   ours and effects/approach.txt section 8 is its home; what matters
   here is that the ship carries its own camera pair rather than
   reading Space_SetCamera, so the offset goes into a COPY of
   g_CamSrc and the tracker's camera is left untouched. The first
   loop's background cannot follow - it is the sky baked once at the
   handover - so for the countdown's ticks the ship moves over a
   still picture.

7.  Open Questions

   o  The object pass, section 5.4, and with it the ship.

   o  Why the seeder fills ten sparks and the stepper walks seven.
      Both counts are immediate constants and neither is derived, so
      it is not a shared symbol that drifted. About four are visible
      at once on the running demo [observed], which is consistent with
      seven stepped on staggered lives rather than with ten.

   o  Which music cue ends the scene, and so how much of the 312-tick
      countdown is ever seen. The flash is gone by 197 either way.
