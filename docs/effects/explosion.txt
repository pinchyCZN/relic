PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                   THE SHIP BLOWS UP, SEEN SIDE ON


Abstract

   Part 2.12, anim mode 0xc, Space_RunPart's phase 10. The scene opens
   almost entirely white - the previous part's flash still filling the
   frame - and a glow CONTRACTS to reveal the blast behind it. The
   blast is a train of four rings seen EDGE ON, which is why it reads
   like Star Wars or Star Trek rather than a fireball: the explosion is
   a disc and the camera sits in its plane.

   Everything in the scene is driven by one counter. The rings, the
   glow and the wash write only byte +2, so their colour is entirely
   the light base; the ray burst is the exception and adds all four
   channel bytes at once.

1.  One Counter Drives Everything

   Phase 10 zeroes g_WhiteoutLevel at Space_RunPart +0x45a and mode 0xc
   ramps it ONE a tick [verified, 0x002d6332]. The glow's radius, every
   ring's size and the closing wash are all functions of that single
   value - there is no per-effect clock anywhere in the scene.

   THE PHASE RUNS ABOUT 420 TICKS AND THE FUSE DOES NOT SAY SO.
   Space_RunPart +0x452 seeds g_SceneFuseTicks 0x104 and mode 0xc
   counts it down one a tick against a level that rises one a tick, so
   the two ought to sum to 260 - and they do not. Stepping the demo,
   the phase still reads mode 0xc at level 413 with the fuse at 7, and
   has cleared twelve frames later [observed]. So it ends near level
   420, and something between the seed and the loop moves one of the
   two. The length is what makes the scene end white - section 4.

   G_WHITEOUTLEVEL IS A LONGWORD AND READING ITS WORD READS THE WRONG
   HALF. Mode 0xc and every consumer use sub.l against 0x002e0bb2
   [verified], so a two-byte read answers 0 for every level below
   65536 - which looks exactly like a counter that has not started
   ramping yet, and sends you looking for a gate that is not there.

   AND THREE OF THE FOUR EFFECTS WRITE ONLY BYTE +2. The rings, the
   glow and the wash all add into LIGHT and never touch a colour
   channel, so their colour is entirely the light base - and phase 10
   is handed (0x5f, 0x5f, 0x7f) at Space_RunPart +0x3c8 [verified], a
   pale blue-white. That is why the blast is light blue.

   THE RAY BURST IS THE EXCEPTION. Draw_Flare indexes a colour ramp and
   does add.l into the pixel, so it writes R, G, B and LIGHT together -
   it is the only thing in the scene with a colour of its own.

   The render path is 0x002cd96e: the sky, Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo,
   Scene_SortAndDrawAll, Explosion_DrawFrame and Flash_WashFullScreen.

2.  The Contracting Glow

   The last thing Explosion_DrawFrame does [verified, 0x002d2350
   +0xbc]:

       r = 0xbb - g_WhiteoutLevel
       if r < 0: draw nothing
       if r > 0x7f: r = 0x7f
       Draw_GlowBlob(r, x = 0x4e, y = 0x32, ramp = g_RampBolt)

   BECAUSE THE LEVEL RISES, THIS SHRINKS: 127 at the phase's open, gone
   by level 187. It is the same primitive the flash before it used,
   run backwards, and it is what reveals the scene.

   THE SCENE'S WHITE PEAK IS AT THE PHASE BOUNDARY, NOT INSIDE EITHER
   PHASE. The flash that closes part 2.11 grows a radius-100 blob at
   (0x46, 0x37), which leaves the frame's corners dark - they sit 89 to
   99 away, where the falloff is nearly spent, and the port measures
   p10 at 161 of 255 there [observed]. This phase then opens with a
   radius-127 blob at (0x4e, 0x32), which reaches them but does not
   whiten them. So the screen is whitest across the cut, and a
   reconstruction that stops at the end of phase 9 never gets there.

   THE RADIUS HOLDS AT THE CAP FOR THE FIRST SIXTY TICKS, because
   0xbb - level stays above 0x7f until level 60. The blob sits at its
   full size for over a second before it begins to shrink, and that
   hold is the pause before the scene is revealed, not a stalled
   timer. Read live at the call, level 100 gives D1 = 87 [observed,
   on hardware], which is the formula to the unit.

   AND THE FOUR CORNERS ARE NEVER WHITE, IN THE DEMO EITHER. A corner
   sits 92 from (0x4e, 0x32) by g_PolarTable, and the demo's own
   tables carry that to g_BlobFalloff[127][92] = 185 and
   g_RampBolt[185] = 35 of 0x3f; at radius 106 they give 222 and 16
   [verified]. So the background shows through the corners of the
   opening white-out by construction. Widening the blob to cover them
   would be inventing a radius no caller asks for.

3.  The Rings

   Explosion_DrawFrame calls Explosion_DrawRing four times [verified,
   0x002d2350]:

       D1 = (K - g_WhiteoutLevel) * 2,  K = 0x12c, 0x118, 0x104, 0xfa

   D1 IS AN INVERSE RADIUS, which is the thing to get right.
   Explosion_DrawRing computes D4 = 0xa0000 / D1 and returns at once
   for D1 <= 0, so as the level rises each ring's D1 falls, its D4
   grows, and the band EXPANDS. The four Ks are 300, 280, 260 and 250,
   so the rings are a STAGGERED TRAIN at four different sizes rather
   than one shell - at any level the K=250 band is the largest.

   THE SHAPE IS A BAND, NOT A CIRCLE [verified, 0x002d2444]:

       A0      = g_pFrameBuffer + 0x7a80, which is pixel 7840 = row 49
       half    = D4 >> 8, capped at 0x31
       two loops, one walking UP from that row and one DOWN
       each row 0xa0 = 160 pixels, the full width

   So it is a full-width horizontal band centred on the middle
   scanline, growing symmetrically. Seen from the disc's plane that is
   exactly what a ring looks like.

   THE TEXTURE IS Fplasma ITSELF. g_pRingTexture is slot 0 of the
   second Dat_LoadSplit, whose chunk name at 0x002e0cf3 reads "Fplasma"
   [verified], and the live pointer is the chunk base [observed]. It is
   read as big-endian LONGWORDS, and the +0x20200 puts the window on
   the chunk's midpoint because the index is a SIGNED word times four
   and so reaches 128K either side of it - Fplasma is 0x40000, so that
   is the whole asset centred.

   AND THE SCENE FADES THAT TEXTURE'S ROWS BEFORE IT DRAWS ANYTHING.
   Space_RunPart calls Explosion_FadePlasmaRows 0x002ccaa4 on its way
   into the phase, right after Ham8_BuildTables sets the light base
   [verified, 0x002cc96e]. Per row of the 256 x 256 longword grid:

       d = max(0, |row - 0x80| - 0x28)
       R -= d ; G -= d ; B -= d          saturating at 0

   THE LIGHT BYTE IS NOT TOUCHED - the three sub.b's walk bytes 0, 2
   and 3 of the longword, which is R, G and B with LIGHT skipped.

   THIS IS WHAT MAKES A BAND END IN WISPS RATHER THAN AT AN EDGE, and
   it is the one thing the geometry cannot do. The band's row sits in
   the index's HIGH byte, so the texture row IS the distance from the
   band's middle, and the pass turns that distance into a fade. What
   reaches the screen is one sample's B channel alone (below), B peaks
   at 0x24, and the subtraction passes 0x24 at row 0x4c - so every
   texture row past 76 is BLACK. A band's own last row is 89 to 95 out
   [observed, the four rings at level 187], so the dbf's hard cut at
   +-half falls where the texture already adds nothing.

   WITHOUT THE PASS THE BAND HAS A DEFINED EDGE, because Fplasma's B
   never reaches zero - its minimum is 1 over all 65536 longwords
   [observed]. A reconstruction that reads the raw chunk gets a strip
   with a horizontal cut top and bottom instead of smoke.

   Destruct_DarkenPlasma 0x002ccb08 is the same pass with 0x1e in
   place of 0x28, for the destruction.

   THE FADE NEVER REACHES ANOTHER PART, even though both passes edit
   the chunk in place. Every scene that wants Fplasma LOADS ITS OWN
   COPY first - 0x0021fab0 allocates and reads the archive afresh on
   each call - and frees it after:

   +-----------------------+-----------+----------+-----------+
   | Caller                | Loads at  | Fades    | Frees at  |
   +=======================+===========+==========+===========+
   | Space_LoadAssets      | 0x2ced48  | -        | 0x2ced50  |
   +-----------------------+-----------+----------+-----------+
   | Space_RunPart         | 0x2cc5a6  | 0x28     | 0x2cca4a  |
   +-----------------------+-----------+----------+-----------+
   | Space_RunEarth        | 0x2cc0ce  | -        | 0x2cc1c8  |
   +-----------------------+-----------+----------+-----------+
   | Space_RunReturn       | 0x2c8ddc, | -        | 0x2c8e0e, |
   |                       | 0x2c8e12  |          | 0x2c8e48  |
   +-----------------------+-----------+----------+-----------+
   | Space_RunDestruction  | 0x2c8438  | 0x1e     | 0x2c85dc  |
   +-----------------------+-----------+----------+-----------+

   So exactly two scenes see a faded plasma and they see different
   fades; every other reader gets the chunk as it sits on disk.

   [trap] g_PlasmaFadedFlag 0x002ccaa2 LOOKS LIKE A RUN-ONCE GUARD AND
   IS NOT ONE. Explosion_FadePlasmaRows opens with a tst.w on it and
   nothing branches on the result; Destruct_DarkenPlasma only writes
   it. Nothing is protected by it - what stops the fades compounding
   is the reload, not the flag.

   EACH PIXEL SAMPLES IT TWICE, once at +g_RingPhaseB with the result
   shifted down 8, once a further +g_RingPhaseA, and the sum is added
   to byte +2 as a RAW byte add with no saturate. ONLY ONE CHANNEL OF
   ONE SAMPLE EVER REACHES THE SCREEN: Fplasma's LIGHT byte is zero
   throughout [observed], so the shifted sample contributes nothing to
   the low byte and what is added is the second sample's B. Explosion_DrawFrame
   turns those two counters +5 and -3 a frame, in OPPOSITE directions
   [verified, 0x002d2398], and that counter-rotation is what makes the
   surface churn instead of sliding.

   The per-row and per-pixel index steps all come from D4:

       row start = -0x190000 / D4
       row step  =  0x640000 / D4
       col step  = (0x500000 / D4) rotated right 8

   so a big ring is sampled coarsely and a small one finely.

   MEASURED IN THE PORT: the band is about 5 rows at the phase's open
   and grows to the 48-row cap by its end; the centre column lights 52
   rows at tick 200 and all 100 by tick 259 [observed].

   THE TEXTURE'S LOW TWO BYTES ARE CONSTANT. Every longword in Fplasma
   has the form XX YY 00 01 - byte 2 is always 0x00 and byte 3 always
   0x01 [verified, the chunk's own bytes]. Explosion_DrawRing forms
   (tex1 >> 8) + tex2 and adds the LOW BYTE of that, which is byte 2 of
   the first sample plus byte 3 of the second, so the sum's low byte is
   0x00 + 0x01 = 1 for every pixel in every band.

   SO A RING IS A UNIFORM +1 AND NOT A TEXTURE AT ALL. The churn the
   two counter-rotating phases produce is in bytes the add throws away.
   Four rings give four, which against the demo's mean LIGHT of 7.1 in
   this frame is most of it.

   AND Fplasma IS FREED BEFORE THIS SCENE RUNS. Space_LoadAssets loads
   it, hands it to Sky_BuildTexture, then calls Dat_Free on it
   [verified, 0x002ced50 and 0x0021f93e, which matches the 16-byte name
   and calls exec's FreeMem]. The slot pointer is left dangling and the
   block is not overwritten - the guest's bytes at the sampled offset
   still match the file exactly [observed] - so the demo reads freed
   memory that happens to still hold its texture. The port reads the
   file, which is the same bytes by luck rather than by design.


4.  The Closing Wash

   Flash_WashFullScreen [verified, 0x002cdb9a] is uniform over all
   16000 pixels with no geometry at all:

       amt = g_WhiteoutLevel - 0xe1
       if amt < 0: do nothing
       amt >>= 1, capped at 0x3f
       every pixel: LIGHT = g_LightSatLut[LIGHT + amt]

   SO IT IS THE SCENE'S CLOSING WHITE-OUT. It does nothing below 225
   and saturates at 351, and the phase runs to about 420 - so the last
   seventy ticks are a flat full-frame white with no geometry left in
   them. The port measures mean LIGHT 58.0 at tick 340 and a uniform
   63 from tick 352 to the end [observed].

   A PART CUT OFF AT 260 NEVER REACHES IT. The fuse appears to promise
   that length and the wash is only a quarter up by then, so the scene
   ends on a dim haze instead of a white-out and the join into the next
   part is lost. Section 1 has the real length.

5.  The Ring Must Scale, And Scale 1.0 Cannot Catch It

   THE DEMO WRITES RAW ROWS either side of pixel 7840, which is row 49
   of its 160 x 100 frame [verified, 0x002d2444 +0x34]. A port that
   copies that literally puts the band at row 49 of whatever height the
   HOST asked for - the upper seventh of a 360-row window - while every
   other effect in the scene scales its centre properly and stays in
   the middle. The band also becomes a thin strip with a hard top and
   bottom instead of four overlapping bands blending into a gradient.

   AND SCALE 1.0 CANNOT DETECT IT. At 1.0 the raw row and the scaled
   row are the same number, so the row profile measures correct to a
   LIGHT mean difference of 1.0 against the demo while the scaled
   window is visibly wrong. The port measured right and looked wrong
   for exactly this reason.

   THE GEOMETRY SCALES AND THE TABLE DOMAIN DOES NOT, which is rule
   20's rule. The centre row and the half extent go through
   FX_MUL(.., f->scale) like Draw_GlowBlob's centre does, while the
   column accumulator is walked over the demo's OWN 160 columns -
   carry fixup and all - and a frame pixel reads the entry for its demo
   column. Scaling the accumulator instead would step the texture
   index at the frame's resolution and sample a different part of
   Fplasma at every scale.

   VERIFIED AT THREE SCALES, band centre as a fraction of height:

       +-------+------------+--------------+
       | scale | frame      | band centre  |
       +=======+============+==============+
       |   1.0 | 160 x 100  | 50%          |
       |   2.0 | 320 x 200  | 50%          |
       |   4.0 | 640 x 400  | 50%          |
       +-------+------------+--------------+

   [observed], with scale 1.0's LIGHT still matching the demo at a mean
   difference of 1.0.

   AND THE DEMO ROW IS CARRIED IN 8.8, NOT ROUNDED. Rounding it makes
   each demo row a block of `scale` identical frame rows and the band
   comes out a staircase; resampling between them gives steps of 0.8 to
   2.3 per six rows at scale 4 where rounding gives runs of zero and
   then a jump [observed]. That is relic.h's rule about resampling a
   table domain rather than stepping it, and it is exact at 1.0 where
   the fraction is zero.

   AND THE MIRROR HAS A SEAM THAT ONLY SHOWS WHEN SCALED. The demo
   draws the band as two loops from the middle outward - one walking
   up from row 49, one down from row 50, each starting its row
   accumulator at zero [verified, 0x002d24ac and 0x002d24f2]. So rows
   49 and 50 BOTH carry texture row 0, and the port's two branches
   reproduce that at scale 1.0 exactly.

   A SCALED ROW CAN LAND BETWEEN THEM, and it indexes negative: at
   scale 4 the frame rows for demo rows 49.25, 49.5 and 49.75 all fall
   in the upward branch with a negative index. Rejecting those leaves
   `scale` - 1 blank rows straight across the frame, which reads as a
   dark ribbon through the middle of the ring. They clamp onto the
   innermost row instead, which is the row the demo's own duplicate
   already puts there.

   THE BAND'S OUTER BOUNDARY IS STILL A CUTOFF, and that is the demo's
   own: at i = half the row loop simply ends, with the last row holding
   whatever the texture gave it rather than tapering to nothing. Scaling
   makes the step `scale` pixels wide instead of one, so it reads harder
   than it does on the Amiga, but softening it would be inventing a
   falloff the binary does not have.

   THE RENDER RATE HAS NOTHING TO DO WITH POSITION. g_WhiteoutLevel
   steps once a VBL while the ring phases step once per
   Explosion_DrawFrame - measured 7 steps per 20 VBLs on a slow
   configuration and 14 per 20 with the host CPU set to fastest
   [observed] - so a slow host samples a different part of Fplasma.
   That changes which texture values a band gets. It cannot move a row.

6.  The Camera And The Ship

   THE CAMERA IS 70000 UNITS OUT AND NEVER MOVES. Phase 10's setup
   writes the target (0, -0x11170, 0xdac) and zeroes both angles, the
   render's latch copies target to camera, and mode 0xc touches none of
   them [verified, Space_RunPart +0x416; read back live as 0, -70000,
   3500 with yaw and pitch zero]. That distance is what makes this a
   side view from far away and the wreck a speck.

   AND THE SHIP IS STILL IN THE SCENE, BUT ALONE. Phase 10 applies its
   own skip table before the loop: g_ExplosionSceneSkipTable, 41
   longwords at 0x002ccc24, one a g_SceneObjects slot, written into
   each slot's +0x40 by Scene_ApplySkipTable [verified, Space_RunPart
   +0x44e]. Non-zero means skip, and it draws 0-7, 9-14 and 38 only.

   WHICH IS HOW THE ORB RIG LEAVES THE SCREEN. Against
   g_PlanetSceneSkipTable, which draws every slot but 8, this one turns
   off 15-37, 39 and 40 - the 25 slots the orb sequence filled with its
   arcs, its attach flares and the orb. A part that inherits the
   previous table draws that whole rig, arcs still animating, behind
   the explosion.

   THE OBJECT ANGLE SPINS, gaining 0x14 a tick
   [verified, Anim_SceneTick +0x6c2; measured 396 to 596 over ten
   ticks]. Phase 10 does not reseed it, so it carries the 0x100 the pan
   held and starts turning from there. The port draws 381 pixels of ship
   at level 46, x 60 to 98 and y 20 to 51 [observed] - mostly swamped by
   the contracting glow that early, which is why its arrival barely
   moves the frame score.

7.  Open questions

   -  THE PORT'S FRAME IS FIFTEEN TIMES TOO BRIGHT AND THAT BLOCKS THE
      SCENE. At level 221 the demo's back buffer means are R 8.2
      G 6.9 B 13.3 with LIGHT 7.1, peaking near 110; the port renders
      R 126.8 G 123.6 B 159.8 [observed]. The demo's phase 10 frame is
      very nearly black with a faint glow in it, and the port's is lit
      throughout.

      IT IS THE SAME DEFERRED CHANNEL-SCALE PROBLEM as overview.txt 7,
      reaching a second scene. Nothing here is mis-transcribed: the
      light base (0x5f, 0x5f, 0x7f) is the demo's own, and the port's
      resolve clamps it correctly rather than extrapolating. What is
      wrong is that the port's accumulated LIGHT is far above the
      demo's 7.1, so every pixel resolves most of the way to the base.
      Scaling the rings or the burst down to compensate would be
      tuning a constant against a screenshot, which rule 12 forbids
      and which was already tried and rejected once on the envelope.

      So this scene cannot be judged until the channel scale is
      settled. It is the second effect gated on that question, the
      envelope's shading being the first.

   -  WHAT THE RAY BURST'S THREE SETUP GLOBALS DO. Explosion_DrawFrame
      writes 0x002e0b6e from the UNCAPPED pulse value as
      (n - 0x38) * 2 << 8, 0x002e0b62 = 0x7f00 and 0x002e0b42 = 0x35
      before calling Draw_Flare. None is read by anything transcribed,
      and because the flare's own radius is capped the pulse table's
      only visible route into the frame is through 0x002e0b6e.

   -  WHAT FOLLOWS PHASE 10. Space_RunPart clears g_SceneMode at
      0x002cca24 after the loop, so the orb sequence ends there, but
      where control goes next is not traced.

   -  THE RING'S INDEX CAN RUN OFF Fplasma. A large positive index
      reaches 0x81e3a0 against the chunk's end at 0x8161a0 [observed],
      so the demo samples whatever follows it in fast RAM. The port
      clamps instead, which is a deliberate divergence - matching it
      would mean knowing what the allocator put there.
