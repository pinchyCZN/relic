PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                           THE RETURN FLIGHT


Abstract

   The flight back off the planet: the ship departing with the planet
   edge behind it, then the run at the portal. It is the space
   engine's work, not the temple's, and it runs BETWEEN the temple
   module's two calls.

1.  Where It Sits

   SpaceModule_Run 0x002c8234 takes a selector in D0 and keeps it at
   0x002e0af8, the same shape Temple_RunShot3 uses: 0 is the opening
   space scenes and 1 is the return flight. Demo_RunAct2 runs them

       0x0021f872   TempleModule_Run   D0 = 0
       0x0021f884   SpaceModule_Run    D0 = 1    <- this
       0x0021f8b8   TempleModule_Run

   so THE TEMPLE MODULE RUNS TWICE and this sits between them
   [verified]. The reconstruction's parts end at the first temple
   call; both this and the second temple run are untranscribed.

2.  The Sequence

   IT OPENS ON THE TEMPLE'S BLANK SCREEN. SpaceModule_Run's mode 1
   arms (0x1f,0x30) at 0x002c8264 and WAITS on it at 0x002c826a
   before it stages anything [verified], and the temple module left
   a copper list up that fetches no bitplane - effects/temple.txt
   11.9. The wait is about 21 fields and the build after it about
   21 more, with Space_DisplayOn 0x002d542e ending the blank
   [observed]. So the scene's counters start 42 fields after the cue
   that ended the temple.

   Space_RunReturn 0x002c8ddc runs three things in order:

       Space_RunDeparture  0x002c8e58   the ship leaving
       Space_HalveBuffer   0x002c918c   the fade between
       Space_RunPortalRun  0x002c8f76   the run at the portal

   Space_HalveBuffer walks g_pRingTexture, masks 0xfcfcfcfc and
   shifts down two, so every channel is quartered in place - the
   same shape as Temple_HalveWarpTex but two bits rather than one,
   over longwords rather than bytes, and ON THE PORTAL RING'S OWN
   TEXTURE rather than on a frame.

   Space_RunDeparture builds the flare ghosts and the saturation
   ramp, calls Scene_SetupPart14, then Ham8_BuildTables with
   0x3f, 0x3f, 0x5f - the same base the temple's mode 0/1 uses - and
   runs Space_StepDeparture 0x002c9272, which latches a camera the
   way every other shot in this corpus does.

2.1.  What The Departure Shot Draws

   THE PLANET EDGE IS A SCROLLING PANORAMA, NOT GEOMETRY.
   Space_BlitPanorama 0x002c989e copies a 160 by 100 window out of
   g_pPanorama into g_pFrameBuffer, 0xa0 longwords a row for 0x64
   rows, stepping the SOURCE on by 0x780 bytes past each row - so
   the source is 640 pixels wide and the window slides along it.

   The offset is g_PanoramaScrollX, which Space_StepDeparture sets
   from the camera as

       g_PanoramaScrollX = (-g_CameraYaw) >> 2 + 0xc8

   NEGATE THEN SHIFT, not the other way round [verified, 0x002c92be]:
   asr floors, so the two spellings differ by one for every odd yaw.
   Turning the camera pans the backdrop and nothing is projected for
   it at all.

   THE WINDOW RUNS OFF EACH ROW AND INTO THE NEXT, and that is the
   backdrop for the last third of the shot. Camera_AimAhead keeps
   turning the yaw, so the scroll passes 0x1e0 - the last window a
   640-wide panorama holds whole - about two thirds of the way
   through. The source index is LINEAR: a row's 160 pixels simply
   continue into row y+1, which is still inside the buffer.

   CLAMPING IT PER ROW FREEZES THE BACKDROP: the stars stop dead at
   the tick the scroll reaches 0x1e0 and never move again, which
   looks like a camera fault at a cardinal heading and is arithmetic
   [observed]. Only the last rows truly overrun the 0x3e800
   allocation, and there the demo reads its pool - row 99 at tick 492
   holds blues of 82, 90 and 107 against the frame's usual 4 to 14,
   which RESOLVES TO A BRIGHT BLUE DASHED LINE along the bottom of
   the demo's own picture [observed]. That is not reproducible;
   part_return.c wraps the index back into the buffer, so the row
   carries ordinary sky and reads continuous with the one above it.

   IT MUST WRITE SOMETHING, AND NOT BLACK. Nothing clears the frame,
   so leaving those pixels keeps the PREVIOUS tick's ship in them and
   the hull drags across the bottom row. Blacking them instead trades
   that for a dark line the demo does not have - its row is brighter
   than the sky, not darker.

   THE PLANET IS WIPED OUT OF THE PANORAMA BEFORE THE WRAP BEGINS,
   and without that it scrolls into view a second time.
   Space_ResetPanoramaToSky 0x002d2ed2 is Space_BuildPanorama's first
   pass run again over the same buffer - 100 rows of 640 from the
   tiled sky, NO Fearth stamp and NO limb glow. Space_RunDeparture
   calls it once, on the way out of its first loop, at the moment the
   scroll reaches 0x12c [verified, 0x002c8eac].

   0x12c IS WELL BEFORE 0x1e0, so by the time a row's window starts
   running into the next row the buffer is already plain sky. Read
   out of the running demo at tick 347 the panorama is dark starfield
   across every column of rows 1 and 40, with no trace of the Earth
   that was there at tick 52 [observed].

   THE SUN IS A TEXTURED SPHERE, NOT A BLOB. Space_DrawSunBlob
   0x002d20fa is Orb_DrawTexturedSphere's skeleton - the same
   g_SphereChordAngle and g_SphereNormalTable at the same 0x10100
   bias - with a different fetch: it samples g_pRingTexture at
   +0x20200 twice, one ang_a step apart, and writes the SUM into byte
   +2 alone with an add.b that wraps rather than saturating.

   Its two angles are g_SunAngleB, a byte the step sets to 0x5a, and
   g_SceneAngleALatched >> 2, which mode 1 turns one a tick. So the
   sun turns in the LIGHT plane, and Space_HalveBuffer's quartering
   of that same texture - g_pRingTexture is Fplasma, effects/
   explosion.txt 3 - is what dims it between the two shots.

   The step calls it at radius 0x7f, x of 0x70 less the scroll and y
   of -0x24, so most of the sphere is above the top of the frame.

   SPACE_DRAWSUNFLARE DRAWS ONE FRAME IN EIGHT. Its first Math_Rand
   is a gate rather than a jitter [verified, 0x002cdff0], and all
   three of x, y and radius are then jittered, so it reads as a
   flicker beside the sun rather than a halo on it.

   THE WHOLE STEP, IN ORDER, IS SHORT:

       latch the camera, and g_PanoramaScrollX from the yaw
       Space_BlitPanorama      the planet edge
       Space_DrawSunBlob       radius 0x7f
       Space_DrawSunFlare      the ghosts
       Draw_Flare              0x002d4452, at g_FlareScreenX
       Portal_DrawGlow         0x002cd642, at g_PortalGlowX
       Scene_SortAndDrawAll    0x002d28ca - THE SHIP
       Flare_Draw              0x002cf2aa

   g_FlareScreenX is 0x12c less the scroll and g_PortalGlowX is the
   scroll less 0x3b6, so BOTH TRACK THE PANORAMA - everything in the
   shot except the ship is pinned to the backdrop and slides with
   it.

2.2.  Where The Panorama Comes From

   Space_BuildPanorama 0x002d2f02 fills it, once, from
   Space_RunDeparture.

   IT IS ALLOCATED, NOT BAKED. The allocator's tables are (flag,
   pointer, size) THREES, and the panorama's is at 0x002f11ac -
   g_pPanorama is the pointer slot and the size beside it reads
   0x3e800, which is 640 x 100 x 4 exactly [observed, on hardware].
   There is no lea of g_pPanorama anywhere in the image; the only
   way to find the entry is the triple.

   THE SOURCE IS 256 WIDE AND IS TILED. The fill walks 640 columns
   but indexes g_pSkyTexture at (row << 8) | col with col taken as a
   BYTE, so the 256-wide sky repeats two and a half times across the
   output and the scroll can never run off the end of it.

   g_pEarthOverlay is then composited over it, 240 columns by 92
   rows, skipping zero longwords. It is the Fearth chunk, which
   Earth_BuildTexture stamps on the planet the same way - effects/
   earth.txt 2. THAT IS THE PLANET EDGE: a flat overlay on a tiled
   sky, with nothing projected and no geometry involved.

   A THIRD PASS ADDS THE LIMB GLOW, and it is the same one
   Earth_BuildTexture bakes into the planet: 100 rows of 250 from
   column 3, |Isqrt(x*x + y*y) - 0x41a| doubled and capped, read out
   of g_HaloRamp with every channel halved. Being an absolute
   difference it peaks ON a circle, so it is the blue rim at the edge
   rather than a wash over the disc.

2.3.  The Portal Run

   THE RUN IS THREE PHASES, not one [verified, Space_RunPortalRun]:

       mode 3   the swirl        fuse 0x258, tables at 0, 0, 0
                Space_FillWhite 0x3f3f003f, ONE frame
                tables rebuilt at 0x5f, 0x4f, 0x3f
       mode 4   the burst        fuse 0x1f4

   the same white-frame hinge the departure's ending uses. BOTH
   LOOPS END ON A MUSIC CUE and the fuse only sets the quit flag as
   a backstop, so neither count is the length.

   THE SWIRL IS BLACK BECAUSE OF THE TABLES. Ham8_BuildTables runs
   with 0, 0, 0 where the departure uses 0x3f, 0x3f, 0x5f, so a
   LIGHT-only pixel goes DARK here rather than pale - the opposite
   end of the same dial the temple's fog base sits on. Nothing draws
   a swirl; the portal's own glow resolves black.

   It sets g_SceneMode to 3, parks the camera square - g_CameraYaw
   and g_CameraPitch both zero - puts the planet at (-0x1f4, 0x96),
   the horizon at 0x9300 and a fuse of 0x258 ticks.

   THIS IS NOT PART 2.4's PORTAL. part_portal.c transcribes
   Space_DrawApproachFrame, the white flash the OPENING's portal
   blooms out of, reached from maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes. The
   return's run is its own path and shares no code with it, even
   though it is the same portal on screen.

   Space_StepPortalRun 0x002c9378 is a frame:

       Scene_DrawTexturedSky    0x002d2db2   part_sun.c
       Draw_Flare               0x002d4452   gfx.c
       Space_DrawLightBlob      0x002d4780
       Space_DrawPortalSparks   0x002c970c
       Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo  0x002ceaf8   space.c
       Space_BlitPortalSprite   0x002c8944
       Space_DrawPlanetMasked   0x002c97b8

   THE FLARE OPENS ON A COMPLEMENT. Its radius is g_PortalOpen EOR
   0x7f capped at 0x46, plus Math_Rand(0xa) - so as the counter
   climbs the flare SHRINKS, and the jitter keeps it alive. The same
   counter goes straight through as the next draw's radius,
   uncomplemented, so the two move opposite ways as the portal
   opens.

   Space_DrawLightBlob is the blob again - the same bounds as
   Space_DrawSunBlob and Temple_DrawGlowBlob - but it reads
   g_LightSatLut rather than a colour ramp, so it SATURATES LIGHT
   where those add colour. It takes the uncomplemented g_PortalOpen
   as its radius.

   Space_DrawPortalSparks places amber sparks around the mouth, all
   three of position and size from Math_Rand:

       x = Rand(0x1e) + 0x55
       y = Rand(0xa) + 0x23
       r = (Rand(0x46) + 0x1e) * g_PortalOpen >> 7

   so THE SPARKS GROW WITH THE PORTAL rather than merely appearing
   with it.

   Space_BlitPortalSprite is a 35 by 29 baked sprite from
   g_PortalSprite at a byte offset in D0 - the run passes 0x7d50,
   column 20 of row 50 - a zero longword meaning leave what is
   there, the same shape as the temple's foreground overlay.

   Space_DrawPlanetMasked projects through Scene_ProjectPlanet,
   which part_explosion.c already carries, and clips a 0x3f-wide run
   against g_pPlanetMask: the planet's silhouette put in front of
   what is behind it.

   SO THE SWIRL NEEDS FOUR NEW ROUTINES and reuses three. All four
   are written: Gfx_GlowEllipse and Gfx_GlowBlobSub in gfx.c,
   Space_BlitPortalSprite and Space_DrawPlanetMasked in space.c.

   THE SWIRL ITSELF IS Space_DrawLightBlob 0x002d4780, and it is a
   glow blob with two things done to it. It is an ELLIPSE - the
   vertical half-extent is the radius shifted down one and the row
   index steps 0x200 rather than 0x100 - and ITS FALLOFF IS WARPED
   BY TWO SAMPLES of a 1024-word table, one taken at the distance
   and one at the distance plus a QUARTER OF THE ANGLE. Folding the
   angle in is what curves it into a swirl rather than a ring.

   The two table offsets scroll at different rates, one backwards
   every four ticks and one forwards every eight [verified,
   0x002d5ae4 and 0x002d5b10], so it turns. The table is
   Gfx_BuildTurbulence's: two sines at different rates plus a random
   term, seeded from g_SinTable.

   WITHOUT THAT TABLE THE SWIRL IS A PLAIN ELLIPSE. It is shared
   with part 2.9, which is where Gfx_BuildTurbulence was already
   called from; a part that does not build it gets a g_turb full of
   zeros, both samples modulate nothing, and the result is a smooth
   featureless blob with no arms and no sign of why. Building it
   took the LIGHT channel from 3.71 to 2.38 against hardware
   [observed].

   THE ARMS CANNOT BE MADE TO LINE UP. Part of the table is a random
   term off the demo's own Math_Rand stream, so the phase of the
   banding is whatever that stream had reached - the same reason
   g_SceneAngleA cannot be matched, section 3.1.1. The character of
   the spiral matches; the position of a given arm does not.

   AND THE PLANET IS SWALLOWED BY LIGHT, not by anything drawn over
   it. Space_DrawPlanetMasked writes LIGHT outright - it does not
   add - to g_PortalOpen >> 1 wherever g_pPlanetMask is set, nine
   rows below the projected planet. With the tables at 0, 0, 0 a
   high LIGHT resolves black, so the disc darkens as the portal
   opens.

   SPACE_DRAWPLANETANDHALO TAKES SCREEN COORDINATES, not the world
   point - the shot stages g_PlanetWorldX/Z at -0x1f4 and 0x96 and
   those must go through Space_Project first. Handing it the world
   pair draws the planet off screen and leaves only the mask's flat
   disc, which looks like a planet with no texture.

2.3.1.  The Burst

   Space_StepPortalBurst 0x002c943c is mode 4's frame, and only
   three of its eight calls are new:

       Scene_DrawTexturedSky    part_sun.c
       Space_DrawSunBlob        at (0x64,0x28), radius
                                (g_BurstGrow >> 5) + 0xf
       Burst_DrawParticles      0x002c9526   NEW
       Draw_Flare               radius (g_BurstGrow >> 4) + 0x28
                                + Rand(0x14)
       Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo  space.c
       Space_BlitPortalSprite   the same 0x7d50 sprite
       Burst_DrawWavefront      0x002c9650   NEW
       Draw_FlareBand           0x002d25f8   NEW

   EVERYTHING GROWS FROM ONE COUNTER. g_BurstGrow 0x002e0a96 is
   staged to 0xa0 and turned one a tick by mode 4; the sun blob's
   radius and the flare's are both shifts of it. The blast is one
   number driving routines we already have.

   THE SUN BLOB IS REUSED WITH g_SunAngleB FORCED TO ZERO where the
   departure passes 0x5a, so the sphere does not counter-rotate.

2.3.2.  The Particles

   3000 of them, 20 bytes each at g_BurstParticles 0x002fcef4:
   position as two 16.16 longs, velocity as two more, and a
   brightness counter at +0x10.

   Burst_SeedParticles 0x002c91aa lays them on a disc about
   (0x64, 0x28) - the same centre as the flare and the blob - taking
   an angle from g_SinTable at Rand(0x2000) and a radius of
   Rand(0x100) + 8. The velocity is the offset from the centre
   shifted down seven, jittered by Rand(0xc350) - 0x61a8 on each
   axis and scaled by 0x4a/256, so A PARTICLE'S SPEED IS ITS
   DISTANCE: the disc expands rather than scattering.

   Burst_StepParticles 0x002c9500 is the whole of the motion - two
   adds a particle, called from mode 4's VBL block.

   Burst_DrawParticles 0x002c9526 plots them, adding the LOW NIBBLE
   of the brightness counter to LIGHT with a byte add that wraps,
   then advancing that counter by 0xd. The nibble cycles, so each
   particle twinkles rather than fading.

2.3.3.  The Wavefront, And What Is Not Yet Read

   Burst_DrawWavefront 0x002c9650 walks 160 x 98 pixels and does two
   things per pixel: it adds a per-pixel byte from g_BurstLightMap
   into LIGHT through g_LightSatLut, and where a second per-pixel
   byte from g_BurstFrontMap exceeds g_BurstFront >> 2 it adds a
   longword from g_BurstFrontRamp to the whole pixel. g_BurstFront
   0x002c9708 is staged to 0x35c and falls one a tick, so THE FRONT
   IS A THRESHOLD SWEEPING DOWN A BAKED HEIGHT MAP.

   While g_BurstDecay 0x002c970a is still running - 0x20 of them -
   the light map is also decayed in place through the same LUT.

   TWO CLOCKS, AND THEY ARE NOT THE SAME ONE. g_BurstFront and
   g_BurstGrow are stepped by the VBL hook's mode 4 block at
   0x002d5b44, once per field. g_BurstDecay is stepped inside
   Burst_DrawWavefront, once per DRAW, and so is the decay it
   gates. The burst's frame is the most expensive in the scene and
   the demo does not hold a field rate through it: about 0.67 draws
   a field once the one-off setup - the map rebuild, the
   3000-particle seed and Space_FillWhite - is behind it
   [observed], so its 0x20 decays take about 48 fields.

   THE PORT RUNS BOTH IN step(), WHICH IS A DELIBERATE DIVERGENCE.
   Splitting them - the counter per tick, the decay per draw - is
   what a host that steps more often than it draws will do with the
   demo's arrangement, and it leaves the decay unfinished while the
   counter expires: the light map stays at its full 0x30 to 0x3f
   and the blast comes out at TWICE its light. Measured against
   hardware 125 fields in, the LIGHT channel was 56.8 of 63
   against the demo's 27.0, an error of 29.9 [observed]; with both
   clocks in step() it is 1.70.

   What the divergence costs is the first 48 fields, where the
   demo's map is still coming down and ours has finished at 32. The
   end state is identical, and it is the end state that the rest of
   the scene is drawn against.

   ITS LOW BYTE IS THE SOURCE'S LOWEST, NOT ITS SECOND. lsr.w #8
   acts on the low word, where B0 sits in the high half, so the
   result's last byte is v & 0xff. Reading it as (v >> 8) & 0xff
   picks up B1 and puts the wrong channel in B - worth 3.30 against
   2.89 here [observed], and more in the stills module where the
   same three instructions decode a palette.

   The ramp cannot brighten LIGHT. Burst_MakeFrontRamp's lsl.l #8
   followed by lsr.w #8 rewrites each source longword B3.B2.B1.B0
   as B2.B1.00.B0, so BYTE +2 OF EVERY RAMP ENTRY IS ZERO and the
   add.l leaves the LIGHT byte alone; the andi.l #0xfcfcfcfc before
   the lsr.l #2 is what stops a byte's low bits carrying into its
   neighbour. A reconstruction that adds the ramp across all four
   channels puts an over-range ring round the blast.

   EVERY TABLE IT READS IS BAKED IN THE EXECUTABLE. Nothing
   generates them and nothing in the image so much as names
   g_BurstFrontMap - a byte search finds its address only in the two
   reads above - because they are simply data.

       g_BurstMaskBits    0x002f9074, a 1BPP 160 x 100 silhouette:
                          100 rows of 20 bytes, 687 of its 2000
                          non-zero, the first at row 19
       g_BurstFrontMap    0x0030b954, 16000 bytes
       g_BurstFrontRampSrc 0x0030f7d4, 256 longwords

   SAMPLING A MAP AT ITS BASE SAYS NOTHING. The first fifty rows of
   g_BurstFrontMap are zero and so are the first nineteen of the
   mask, so four longwords off the front of either reads empty and
   looks generated. Both match the running demo byte for byte as
   early as the temple scene [observed].

   THE MAP IS BUILT 160 x 100 AND SWEPT 160 x 98. Burst_BuildMaps
   fills 16000 cells - 100 rows of 20 bytes of 8 bits - Burst_BlurMap
   runs 15680 from one row in, so it writes cells 160 to 15839, and
   Burst_DrawWavefront sweeps 0 to 15679. Building only what the
   sweep reads leaves the last two rows zero and the two above them
   blurred against zero.

   The blur's first cell reads its own index less 0xa1, which is -1.
   The demo reads the byte in front of a static buffer; a
   reconstruction reading in front of a heap block wants a LEADING
   GUARD ROW instead.

   So the only work is reading them:

       Burst_BuildMaps 0x002c9588     mask -> g_BurstLightMap, a set
                                      bit 0x30 and a clear one 0x3f,
                                      then Burst_BlurMap twice
                                      through g_BurstBlurScratch,
                                      then g_pPlanetMask punched in
                                      at +0x1190 so the front does
                                      not cross the planet
       Burst_BlurMap 0x002c960c       nine taps, the centre a half,
                                      each edge an eighth and each
                                      corner a thirty-second
       Burst_MakeFrontRamp 0x002c9166 the ramp source shifted and
                                      masked into g_BurstFrontRamp

   Draw_FlareBand 0x002d25f8 lays ten rows about g_BandRow. Each
   pixel is keyed on a polar distance taken on a SQUASHED grid -
   the column halved and the row stepping 0x14 of the table per
   screen row - so the shape is a wide flat ellipse and not a disc.
   The blend at 0x002d3d72 adds one scalar to R, G and B, each
   saturating at 0x3f, and leaves LIGHT alone.

2.4.  The Ship Is The Temple's

   SCENE_SETUPPART14 0x002d0146 REBUILDS g_SceneObjects FROM SCRATCH
   and nothing of Space_BuildObjects' vessel survives it, so the
   departure does NOT draw the ship of the opening scenes. That is
   why the staging sets g_SceneObjectCount to 0xe: fifteen slots,
   thirteen meshes and two glows.

   THE GEOMETRY IS THE SAME BYTES AS CODE_06'S. Objects 0 to 12 are
   byte-identical to Temple_BuildObjects' first thirteen - every
   block size matches and the vertex data compares equal [verified].
   The two hunks hold one ship twice:

       temple 0x003d5e00 ... 0x003d7cce   src/part_temple.c TE_OBJ
       here   0x002e26e2 ... 0x002e45b0

   BOTH COPIES ARE HALVED IN PLACE. Mesh_HalveVerts 0x002cfefe is
   CODE_03's copy of Temple_HalveVerts, the same loop without the
   one-shot guard, and Scene_SetupPart14 calls it once per object. At
   run time the live vertex block reads exactly half the file's
   values [observed, on hardware].

   IT SITS WHERE AN ENVELOPE BUILDER WOULD. Between
   Uv_GenerateFaceParams and Obj_SetCentroid in every build, which is
   Mesh_MakeAdditiveEnvelope's place in the other builders; taking it
   for one puts the ship on screen at twice size.

   The UV passes match too, with three exceptions:

       object 10   texture 0x24fae2, D2 0xb2, against the temple's
                   0x23a1ca and 0x4e
       object 11   D2 0x100 against 0xf0
       object 12   the same mirrored

   and the temple's FOURTEENTH object, the foreground temple on the
   hull, has no counterpart here.

2.4.1.  The Two Sort Biases

   OBJECTS 11 AND 12 CARRY +/-0xb71b0 ON COMPONENT 0, written to the
   slot's +0x18 after Obj_SetCentroid [verified, 0x002d0772 and
   0x002d0808]. THAT IS A SORT BIAS AND NOT A MOVE: a mesh's vertices
   carry their own placement, so +0x18 is read only to compute the
   key at +0x24.

   0xb71b0 IS 750,000, which dwarfs every real centroid, so the two
   are forced to the far and near ends of the painter's order. Read
   out of the running demo at tick 280 the sorted list runs

       slot 11   key  38459     drawn first
       slot 14   key  17125     the far glow
       ... the eleven other meshes, +3320 down to -3712 ...
       slot 13   key -16664     the near glow
       slot 12   key -38899     drawn LAST

   so OBJECT 12 IS PAINTED OVER THE NEAR GLOW and the hull occludes
   the thruster. Without the bias that glow sorts last, the plume
   paints over the fenders, and the frame is brighter everywhere the
   hull should have cut it - LIGHT scored 1.94 against the demo with
   the bias missing and 0.48 with it [observed].

   THE TWO RENDERERS ARE ONE ROUTINE IN TWO HUNKS. The temple's
   object projection and CODE_03's Scene_TransformProject agree in
   every constant - the >> 4, the 0x1f4 added before the divide, the
   0xc8 near clip and the 0x50, 0x32 centres [verified] - so the
   reconstruction needs one body, not two.

2.5.  The Thrusters

   THE TWO TYPE-4 SLOTS ARE THE THRUSTERS, at x of -0xb400 and
   +0xb400 with y zero and z 0x3200. The 0x493e0 Scene_SetupPart14
   adds and subtracts goes on the SORT position only, the same way
   the temple's two carry it.

   THEY ARE NOT THE TEMPLE'S BLOB. Draw_GlowOval 0x002cf19e writes
   LIGHT alone, through g_RampSpark and then g_LightSatLut, where the
   temple's adds a packed colour longword.

       dist  = g_PolarTable[0x10000 + (ty << 8 | dx)], low byte
       level = g_BlobFalloff[radius << 8 | dist]
       LIGHT = g_LightSatLut[(LIGHT + g_RampSpark[level]) & 0xff]

   AND THE ROW INDEX DOES NOT ADVANCE EVENLY, which is the whole
   shape. Above the centre it steps a full table row, 0x100, per
   screen row; at and below it steps 0x50 [verified, 0x002cf29a], so
   the lower half is stretched to 3.2 times its height. The result is
   a teardrop - an oval with a plume below it - not a disc.

   Its row bound is 0x63 where every other blob in the file uses
   0x64, AND BOTH ITS LOOP BOUNDS ARE EXCLUSIVE: each takes
   last - first and subtracts one more before the dbf [verified,
   0x002cf20e and 0x002cf258], so the last row and column go
   undrawn. Together those make row 97 the last one lit, and the
   demo leaves 98 and 99 dark.

   READING THE BOUND AS INCLUSIVE LIGHTS ROW 98, which shows up as a
   bright line along the bottom of every frame of the shot and reads
   as a glow that stops one row short.

   THE RADIUS COMES FROM A TABLE, NOT A CURVE. Scene_SortAndDrawAll's
   type-4 branch reads the slot's +0x30 - g_Part14GlowRadii, eight
   longwords running 2400, 2240, 2000, 2240, 2400, 2560, 2800, 2560 -
   at (g_FlareSizeStep >> 1) & 7, shifts it left ten and divides by
   the depth, capping at 0x7f [verified, 0x002d2af8]. mode 1 turns
   g_FlareSizeStep one a tick, so the thrusters pulse on an
   eight-step loop.

   THE RADIUS IS LARGE AND THAT IS CORRECT. On hardware the demo
   draws one at screen (117, 69) with radius 111 over a depth of
   0x5be6 [observed, breakpoint on 0x002cf19e]. The reconstruction
   puts the same glow at (116, 69) over a depth of 23860.

3.  What It Stages

   SpaceModule_Run's D0 == 1 entry sets, in order [verified,
   0x002c8258], and almost every one was already named:

       g_SceneObjectCount  0xe        FOURTEEN OBJECTS, not a mode
       g_SceneSortSkip     0
       g_ObjectAngle       -0x350
       g_PanoramaScrollX   0x100
       g_CamSrcX           0x7724
       g_CamSrcY           -0x11170   the depth subtrahend
       g_CamSrcZ           -0x1770
       g_CameraYaw         0x230      written three times, 0x5a00
                                      and -0x100 first
       g_CameraPitch       0x50
       g_PortalPhase       0
       g_SunRange          0x6ff9
       (0x002e0b66)        0x6ff9

   then installs the VBL hook at 0x002d568e and calls
   SpaceModule_InitTables.

   THE CAMERA IS A POSITION AND A PAIR OF ANGLES, ALL STAGED
   DIRECTLY. g_CamSrcX/Y/Z are latched into g_CamX/Y/Z once a frame
   by every renderer in the engine, and the yaw and pitch are their
   own globals. src/part_portal.c already drives exactly these
   through Space_SetCamera.

3.1.  What Moves It

   ALL OF THE SHOT'S MOTION IS ONE BLOCK OF THE VBL HOOK.
   Anim_ModeStep 0x002d58a2 dispatches on g_SceneMode, which
   SpaceModule_Run sets to 1 immediately before the call [verified,
   0x002c8310], and mode 1 is 0x002d5b86:

       g_SceneAngleA  += 1        the sun's spin
       g_CamSrcY      += 0xc8     the depth, which CLOSES
       g_CamSrcX      -= 0x82
       Camera_AimAhead            with g_CamAim12A at 0x3e8
       g_PortalPhase  += 2        once g_CamSrcY passes 0x2af8,
                                  to a ceiling of 0xfe
       quit                       once g_CamSrcY passes 0x88b8

   so THE SHOT IS 525 TICKS OF ARITHMETIC, not a music cue:
   (0x88b8 + 0x11170) / 0xc8 exactly.

   THE YAW IS NOT IN THAT LIST AND TURNS ANYWAY. Reading only the
   addi.l instructions says the camera never turns; it swings most of
   a right angle, through Camera_AimAhead 0x002d5772. That is
   Camera_TrackTarget with the target pushed 0x3e8 ahead on Z and ONE
   ASYMMETRY: a positive yaw step is replaced by exactly 3 while a
   negative one passes through whole [verified, 0x002d57a8], so the
   camera whips one way and creeps back the other.

   Checked against the running demo: g_CamSrcX,
   g_CamSrcY and g_CameraYaw all read what 247 ticks of the above
   produce [observed].

   ONE TICK IS ONE VBL HERE. 100 frames of amiga_frames move
   g_CamSrcY by 20000, which is 100 steps of 0xc8 exactly
   [observed]. The loop waits one vertical blank per
   Space_StepDeparture and draws every time, so nothing in this scene
   runs at half rate.

3.1.1.  Which Counters Anchor A Frame

   ONLY THE TWO THAT ARE STAGED. g_CamSrcX and g_CamSrcY are set to
   known values by SpaceModule_Run, so a reading's tick is

       tick = (live - start) / step

   and the two cross-check each other. tools/anchor.py holds the
   table and refuses rather than guess when they disagree or when a
   value does not land on its own step.

   G_SCENEANGLEA AND G_FLARESIZESTEP LOOK LIKE ANCHORS AND ARE NOT.
   Both advance one a tick in the same block, but neither appears in
   SpaceModule_Run's staging, so each carries whatever the earlier
   scenes left: at a tick of 247 they read 0x3709 and 0x21a7, not
   247 [observed, on hardware].

   THAT IS ALSO A DIVERGENCE IN THE RECONSTRUCTION. part_return.c
   starts both at zero because it cannot replay the demo's whole
   history, so the sun's rotation - g_SceneAngleA >> 2 feeds
   Space_DrawSunBlob's ang_a, section 2.1 - sits at a different phase
   from the demo's. The sphere turns correctly; it is not at the same
   angle. Priming is the only way to make a scored frame agree there.

   Anchored so far: ticks 52 and 280 of the flight; in the swirl,
   ticks 18 and 261 of its 600-tick backstop, tick 430, and tick 24
   of the BURST [observed]. The swirl anchors on
   g_SceneFuseTicks, which is staged and falls one a tick; g_PortalOpen
   climbs one per FOUR ticks so it cross-checks but cannot anchor.
   The burst anchors on g_BurstGrow, staged to 0xa0 and turned one a
   tick.

   SLOT 8 IS THE WHITE FRAME BETWEEN THEM and reads g_SceneMode 0,
   which no phase sets while running - Space_RunPortalRun clears it
   at 0x002c90a0 for its Space_FillWhite frames - that one presents
   twice, see 4.2.

3.2.  The Rays Turn

   SPACE_STEPDEPARTURE WRITES THE FLARE'S RAY PHASE and a part that
   leaves it alone draws a still sun. g_ApproachSpin 0x002e0c1c takes
   0x12c less the panorama scroll [verified, 0x002c931a], and its LOW
   BYTE is the offset Draw_Flare adds to the polar angle before each
   of its two ray samples. The scroll climbs as Camera_AimAhead turns
   the yaw, so the rays turn with the view - the same sun as the
   opening's, moving the same way.

3.3.  The Light Base

   Space_RunDeparture calls Ham8_BuildTables with 0x3f, 0x3f, 0x5f
   [verified, 0x002c8e6a] - the same base the temple's mode 0 and 1
   use. The portal run rebuilds them at 0, 0, 0, which is what turns
   a LIGHT-only pixel black there rather than pale.

4.  What Is Already Ours

   The backdrop this scene needs is in src/space.c and was written
   for the opening: Space_DrawSky, Space_DrawPlanetAndHalo,
   Space_DrawFlareGhosts, Space_DrawHorizonBand, Space_SetCamera and
   Space_Project. part_approach.c is the closest worked example of
   driving them.

   Of Space_RunDeparture's own calls, Ramp_BuildSaturate 0x002cff1e
   and Ham8_BuildTables 0x002ca456 are transcribed in gfx.c, and
   Flare_BuildGhostSprites 0x002cf4cc and Scene_SetupPart14
   0x002d0146 are named but not transcribed.

   Of the eight things the step does, four were already transcribed:

       Draw_Flare            gfx.c, as Gfx_Flare
       Portal_DrawGlow       space.c, as Space_PortalGlow
       Scene_SortAndDrawAll  ship.c and orbscene.c, as
                             OrbScene_Register then Ship_Draw
       Flare_Draw            space.c, as Space_DrawFlareGhosts

   THE SHIP COMES THROUGH ONE SHARED RENDERER. src/objset.h declares
   an object set - geometry triples, UV passes, cull modes and the
   halve - and part_temple.c holds the body both parts draw with,
   because the temple's object projection and Scene_TransformProject
   agree in every constant. The departure supplies its own set and
   its camera; the temple's remains the default.

   TWO PARTS DRAW PORTAL_DRAWGLOW. It is CODE_03's routine, so
   space.c holds the body and both call it; part 2.3 walks the phase
   and the offset itself, the departure takes the phase from its
   camera and the offset from the scroll.

4.1.  Coming Into It

   THE SCREEN IS BLACK WHEN SPACE_RUNDEPARTURE IS ENTERED and stays
   black for under ten frames [observed, on hardware, run forward to
   a breakpoint on 0x002c8e58]. IT IS NOT A DELIBERATE PAUSE: nothing
   on the path waits. Space_RunReturn's prologue builds the sky's
   direction table at 0x002d3a04 - 65536 longwords - and
   Space_RunDeparture then builds the panorama, the ghost sprites,
   the saturation ramp and the whole object set before its first
   frame. The gap is that work, with the display already cleared.

   What precedes it is long: the underside shot fades for some 430
   frames before the cut. A reconstruction that
   ends the previous part cleanly and holds black briefly matches
   what is on screen, but the hold is the port's own and the demo has
   no counter for it.

4.2.  The Ship Goes, And The Screen Ripples

   ITS COUNTERS DO NOT START WHEN THE PHASE DOES. The flight ends at
   525 - that is arithmetic on the demo's own camera limits, 0x88b8
   to -0x11170 at 0xc8 a field - but the mode 2 block does not step
   g_RipplePhase or g_ApproachRange until SEVEN FIELDS LATER. The
   white frame, the panorama bake and Ham8_BuildTables are what sits
   in the gap, and it is the same effect the burst's setup frame has
   on its own decay [observed, section 2.3.3].

   Measured against hardware, where the ripple is at an age of 7
   rather than the 14 its absolute tick suggests: holding the
   counters for seven fields improves every channel, LIGHT 5.66 to
   2.11 and R 5.69 to 3.94. The step amounts themselves were never
   wrong - 0x002d5c38 subtracts 0x1f4 from the phase and 0xc from
   the range, which is what src/ already had.

   RT_BURST FOLLOWS IT, so the ripple still gets its full 227 steps
   before the swirl begins. The burst's own anchors move with it and
   improve slightly: LIGHT 0.21 to 0.06 at the decay-matched tick.

   THE DEPARTURE HAS A SECOND HALF and the reconstruction stops
   before it. Once the main loop ends, Space_RunDeparture runs
   [verified, 0x002c8ec4]:

       g_SceneMode = 0              mode 1's motion stops
       Space_FillWhite 0x3f3f003f   BOTH buffers, then presented
       Ham8_BuildTables 0x1f, 0, 0x2f
       0x0021fdca over 0x2f1180     a four-long block, paired with
                                    0x0021fdb4 at the end
       0x002c9868                   bake the panorama for the ripple
       g_ApproachRange = 0xdc0
       g_RipplePhase = 0
       g_SceneMode = 2
       loop 0x002c8f2a              until the range reaches 0x320

   THE WHITE HOLDS FOR THREE FIELDS AND ITS LENGTH IS THE BAKE'S.
   Space_FillWhite fills TWO buffers in one pass - A0 from
   (0x002f101c) and A1 from (0x002f1020), 0xfa0 turns of four
   longwords into each [verified, 0x002cd422] - and only one
   Space_PresentFrame follows it. Nothing is presented again until
   the panorama bake and the table rebuild have finished, so the
   white stays up for as long as that work takes: g_SceneMode is
   cleared on field 489 and set to 2 on field 491, the fill lands on
   490, the render path carries white again on 491, field 492 writes
   nothing at all, and content returns on 493 [observed, from the
   first fields of part 15].

   A RECONSTRUCTION CANNOT DERIVE THAT LENGTH, since the bake costs
   it nothing. Ours holds the white for the measured three fields -
   RT_FLASH_FIELDS - inside the seven-field setup window the
   counters already sit out. Painting one field instead runs every
   later tick of the part two fields early against the music, which
   reads as the audio lagging.

   THE SWIRL'S FLASH STATES ITS OWN LENGTH instead, and is the one
   place the demo does: Space_RunPortalRun calls Space_PresentFrame
   TWICE in a row [verified, 0x002c90b0 and 0x002c90b6] before its
   rebuild. Its total is not yet measured.

   so THE SHIP DOES NOT EXPLODE - IT STOPS BEING DRAWN. The white
   frame is the flash, and from there the frame routine draws only
   the ripple and one flare. Nothing projects the vessel again.

   THE FRAME IS ALREADY OURS IN FULL. 0x002c935a is three things:

       Space_Ripple           0x002cd760   space.c
       Explosion_DrawFlash    0x002cd71c   part_portal.c flash_radius
       g_ApproachSpin += 4                 the flare's ray phase

   and mode 2 of the VBL hook is the whole of its motion [verified,
   0x002d5c38]: g_RipplePhase -= 0x1f4 and g_ApproachRange -= 0xc,
   the latter clamped at zero. 227 ticks, which is (0xdc0 - 0x320)
   over 0xc rounded up.

   THE LIGHT BASE CHANGES FOR IT. 0x1f, 0, 0x2f against the shot's
   own 0x3f, 0x3f, 0x5f, so the ripple resolves toward a dark purple
   rather than the departure's pale blue.

4.2.1.  The One Routine That Is New

   0x002c9868 is Space_BlitPanorama written into g_pWarpBuffer rather
   than the frame: 0x64 rows of 0xb0 longwords from g_pPanorama at
   (g_PanoramaScrollX - 8) * 4, the source stepping 0xa00 bytes a
   row. That is 176 wide against the screen's 160 - eight columns of
   slack each side - which is the shape src/space.c already holds for
   Space_BakeSky.

   THE TWO BAKES DIFFER BY A ROW. Space_BakeSky fills from
   RIPPLE_LIVE, one row plus eight longs in; this one writes from
   offset zero and the frame hands the ripple g_pWarpBuffer + 0x20,
   which is eight longs with no row. src/space.h.

4.2.2.  Where The Readings Sit

   Tick 492 of the departure's 525, and tick 14 of the ripple's 227
   [observed]. Both counters agree in each case.

5.  The Reconstruction

   src/part_return.c is the departure shot, registered as part 2.15.
   It carries pan_build, pan_blit, sun_blob and sun_flare - the four
   that were new - and drives the rest.

   IT NEEDS RELIC.DAT. The panorama's overlay is Fearth and the sun's
   texture is Fplasma, so without -dat the part reports NO PANORAMA
   and draws the ship on an empty frame.

   THE ENDING IS IN IT TOO. Past tick 525 the part switches to the
   ripple: one Space_FillWhite frame, the light base at 0x1f, 0,
   0x2f, the panorama baked into the warp buffer, then 227 ticks of
   Space_Ripple and the flash. Only Space_BakePanorama was new -
   everything else was already in space.c and part_portal.c, and the
   flash moved to space.c so both parts draw one body.

   THE BAKE AND THE WHITE FRAME ARE SPLIT ACROSS step AND frame. The
   bake is state and lives in step(), because done in frame() it is
   skipped the moment a host seeks past the phase boundary and the
   ripple then has no source at all. The white frame is drawing and
   stays in frame().

   THE PORTAL RUN IS IN IT TOO, as two further phases. Both end on a
   music cue and the fuse each arms is a backstop the cue beats: the
   swirl runs 362 ticks from 759 against its 0x258, and the burst 442
   from 1121 against its 0x1f4 [observed, a clean run from the demo's
   first frame]. 0x002e0a92 reads 0xee on the field the swirl's cue
   is consumed and the burst's own re-arm still reads 0x77 on its.

   LANDMINE: 0x002e0a92 IS A SHARED SCENE FUSE. Eight routines write
   it, 0x002c90f8 among them, which re-arms it for the burst the
   moment the swirl ends. A reading taken anywhere after the swirl
   measures the burst, and that is what put the swirl at 556.

   The burst adds burst_maps, burst_seed, burst_particles and
   burst_front to the file, and Gfx_FlareBand to gfx.c. Everything
   else in its frame is the departure's - the sun blob, the flare,
   the planet and the portal sprite.

   IT DRAWS EVERY TICK WHERE THE DEMO DOES NOT, which is the one
   deliberate divergence in the phase. Section 2.3.3's two clocks
   are one clock here, so the light map decays about six times
   faster against its growth than the demo's does. Scored against
   hardware the two halves land at different ticks and each is
   right at its own [observed]:

           our tick 1320, matching its 4 decays
                          R 2.05  G 1.53  LIGHT 0.07  B 2.89
           our tick 1339, matching its 24 of growth
                          R 2.01  G 1.45  LIGHT 17.33  B 2.89

   At 1320 the LIGHT histogram is the demo's level for level - 60,
   45, 48, 59 and 0 on both sides - so the wavefront itself is
   right and only the rate it is driven at is ours.

6.  Open questions



   -  WHAT THE GLOW SLOTS' +0x44 IS FOR.
      maybe_g_Part14GlowAuxA 0x002ee7ec and its pair 0x002ee7f8 are
      written there by Scene_SetupPart14 and nothing in the type-4
      draw reads them. The radius is the +0x30 table - section 2.5.

   -  OBJECT 10'S FLAT PASS. 0x002d06be hands Uv_GenerateFaceParams
      a texture of 0x0024fae2 and a D2 of 0xb2 where the temple's
      call uses 0x0023a1ca and writes no D2 at all. What the 0xb2
      selects is not read, and the stride is taken as its
      neighbours' 128 [inferred].

   -  WHAT THE FLIGHT SCORES, raw to raw, per channel [observed]:

           tick  52   R 1.08  G 0.92  LIGHT 3.92  B 1.03
           tick 247   R 3.60  G 3.16  LIGHT 1.44  B 4.00
           tick 280   R 4.30  G 3.93  LIGHT 0.48  B 3.91
           tick 347   R 2.27  G 1.99  LIGHT 0.63  B 2.60
           tick 493   R 1.91  G 1.83  LIGHT 1.26  B 2.75
           tick 539   R 3.94  G 3.27  LIGHT 2.11  B 3.77

      The last is the ripple.

   -  WHAT RATE THE BURST SHOULD DRAW AT. The demo ties the light
      map's decay to the draw and the blast's growth to the field,
      and does not hold a field rate through the phase. A port that
      draws every field cannot have both. Section 5 keeps the
      code's own coupling and takes the faster fade; whether the
      scene reads better with the decay moved onto the field is not
      settled, and nothing may be tuned to a capture to decide it.

   -  SCORE IT RAW TO RAW, NEVER THROUGH A PPM. Against hardware
      the shot reads R 3.60 G 3.16 LIGHT 2.59 B 4.00, with 6.5% of
      pixels byte-identical [observed]. The same pair compared as
      PPMs reads 40.5: the emulator's PPM is the front buffer, taken
      AFTER the HAM8 resolve has consumed LIGHT in place, so every
      glow scores as undrawn and the colours are compared across the
      resolve. Adding the thrusters moved the PPM score the wrong way
      and the LIGHT channel from 8.24 to 2.59.

   -  THE SECOND TEMPLE CALL is the only part of this stretch of the
      demo still unread. Both of the return flight's shots are
      accounted for.

   -  THE SECOND TEMPLE RUN. TempleModule_Run is called again after
      this scene and nothing is known about what that call draws.
