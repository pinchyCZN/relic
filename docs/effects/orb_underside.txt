PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                   THE UNDERSIDE SHOT OF THE ORB


Abstract

   Part 2.11, the cut that follows the orb's formation: the camera
   comes out from under the ship and circles, looking up through the
   claws at the sphere they hold. It is ANIM MODE 0x12. Almost nothing
   about the effects changes from the previous shot - this note is
   mostly about the camera and about why the shot is as long as it is.
   The effects themselves are effects/orb.txt.

   2. Nothing About The Effects Changes
   3. The Camera
   4. The Light Base Decides The Orb's Core
   5. Open questions

1.  The Cut

   Space_RunPart's phase 7. The setup is nine stores and a music cue
   [verified, 0x002cc840]:

       mode                     0x12
       camera                   (7252, -13400, 9643)
       yaw   0x002e0b32         0
       pitch 0x002e0b36         -0x800
       0x002e0b26               0x100
       g_CamCursor12            0x100
       music cue                (0x1e, 0x34)

   THE PITCH IS THE SHOT. -0x800 of 8192 is -90 degrees, so the camera
   opens looking straight up; the camera the setup writes is replaced
   by the path on the first tick, but the angles are not.

   g_CamCursor12 STARTS AT 0x100, not zero, so the path begins at key
   1 and the duplicated key 0 is only ever P[-1].

   PHASE 6 ENDED ON A MUSIC CUE AND SO DOES THIS ONE [verified,
   0x002cc82e, 0x002cc8bc]. Each loop renders and polls 0x0021ff64,
   leaving only when it answers; no fuse is seeded for phase 7 at all,
   so what remains of phase 6's 0x2b8e keeps counting down while the
   cue decides. Mode 0x12 does decrement that fuse and would raise
   g_QuitRequested [verified, 0x002d6044], but the loop never looks at
   it.

   SO EVERY LENGTH FROM HERE IS MEASURED, NOT READ. A reconstruction
   playing nothing has no cue to wait for. That also explains the
   previous shot: its 136 ticks of phase 6 are a measured stand-in for
   a cue, which is why it can look cut short.

   MEASURED LENGTH: stepping the mode word on hardware, it still
   reads 0x12 at 1375 frames and 0xa at 1388, so the cut lands between
   [observed]. src/part_underside.c carries 1382. The cursor also puts
   mode 0x12's start about eleven frames before that state: at 900
   frames in it read 2078, which is 0x100 plus twice 911.

2.  Nothing About The Effects Changes

   Mode 0x12 advances the four effect clocks with the SAME increments
   as mode 0x13 [verified, 0x002d5fec]:

       g_BoltSeed     += 2         g_BoltPhase  += 0xc8
       g_OrbSpinA     += 0x64      g_OrbSpinB   += 0xa

   So the arcs wriggle and the orb's satellites orbit at exactly the
   rate they did in the previous shot. It is the same effects from a
   new camera, with nothing retuned - the glowing points circling the
   sphere are the same ten Draw_GlowBlob satellites, on the same
   g_RampBolt the bolts' own nodes use. effects/orb.txt 6.

   WHAT IT DOES NOT TOUCH IS AS INFORMATIVE. It never writes
   g_BeamPhaseA or g_BeamPhaseB, so both strengths stay where the last
   shot left them and all six beams are permanently struck - there is
   no growing head anywhere in this shot. And it never walks
   g_pTurbulenceA or g_pTurbulenceB, which only a head's flare reads,
   so the churn stops being computed because nothing would sample it.

   g_OrbNearMode is still ZERO here [observed, on hardware], so the
   orb is drawn by the ten-satellite branch throughout and not by
   whatever the formed sphere becomes.

   Two counters are stepped whose use is unread: 0x002e0afc by -0x100
   and 0x002e0c1c by +0x14.

3.  The Camera

   THE PATH IS READ BEFORE THE CURSOR MOVES [verified, 0x002d5ff2 then
   0x002d5ffe], so the camera a frame shows belongs to the cursor's
   previous value. Getting that backwards puts every position one step
   ahead of the demo's.

   AND THE TWO SINES ARE NOT THE ANGLES. Camera_TrackTarget adds them
   to the camera's own x and z to make a point to look at, and turns
   that direction into the yaw and pitch [verified, 0x002d57bc] - so
   the look DRIFTS around the orb rather than being aimed at it. Read
   as angles instead, the position still matches the demo exactly and
   the orientation is wrong, which looks like a path problem and is
   not.

   VERIFIED AT A MATCHED CURSOR: at cursor 2078 the camera comes back
   (833, 2917, 3957) against the demo's (833, 2917, 3957) [observed,
   on hardware]. The opening pitch reads -2051 against the setup's
   -0x800.

   THE ANGLES ACCUMULATE, SO THEY DRIFT RATHER THAN DIFFER.
   Camera_TrackTarget negates before halving - neg.l D0 then asr.l #1
   [verified, 0x002d57f2] - and asr floors, so (-d0) >> 1 and
   -(d0 >> 1) part company by one for every odd d0. Space_CamMove then
   ADDS the result into yaw and pitch rather than storing it, so a
   single unit never cancels. Only the low word counts: the ext.l pair
   at 0x002d671c sign-extends a word before each add.

   VERIFIED AT THE INSTRUCTION. Halted at 0x002d57fa the deltas read
   D0 -6 and D1 1, and the port's are -6 and 1 at every tick from 506
   to 514 [observed, on hardware]. The pre-negation value is
   11 there, and 11 is odd: -(11 >> 1) is -5 where (-11) >> 1 is -6,
   so the old form lost a unit on every odd tick.

   COUNTED IN ONE FRAME AT A MATCHED CAMERA, the envelope agrees
   exactly. Stepping the per-face entry at 0x002cb3aa and draining a
   watch on the gradient store in the same frame [observed, on
   hardware, camera -4129 -2346 8878]:

       +--------------------+-----------+-----------+
       | one frame          | demo      | port      |
       +====================+===========+===========+
       | faces entering     | 9         | 9         |
       | rejected           | 6         | 6         |
       | rasterised         | 3         | 3         |
       | dudx of the three  | -7 -7 +21 | -7 -7 +21 |
       +--------------------+-----------+-----------+

   The six rejected are two whole objects: one projects past the right
   edge, x 173 to 187 of 160, and one sits entirely above the frame,
   y -78 to -3. Three objects of three faces each is the whole of it;
   D7's low word counts the faces down from 2 at each object's first
   face.

   AND subpix's FRACTION STAYS SIGNED. The demo works in whole
   pixels: divsl.l truncates toward zero and so does C, so the
   quotient already matches. The port keeps eight fraction bits beside
   it, and every consumer recovers the pixel with >> RAST_FRAC, which
   FLOORS - so a negative fraction hands back one less and a vertex
   left of or above centre lands a pixel short. Only x shows it in
   this shot, the envelope sitting below centre where the y quotient
   is positive.

   FORCING THE FRACTION NON-NEGATIVE BUYS THAT PIXEL AND DESTROYS
   SUB-PIXEL MOTION. q truncates toward zero, so the stored value
   stops being monotonic: a coordinate crossing -3 toward -4 comes out
   -3.000, then -3+0.996, then -4.000, sliding backward before it
   snaps. Every vertex left of or above centre then shears a pixel
   back and forth as the object turns, which looks like integer-only
   projection and is worse than the one-pixel offset it cures.

   The two cannot both hold. trunc quantises the two sides of centre
   differently, so the demo's own projection has a one-pixel
   discontinuity there, and no monotonic reconstruction carries a
   discontinuity. The fraction stays signed and the offset stays.

   A SINGLE-FRAME SCORE CANNOT SEE THIS. fbcmp compares one frame, so
   it rated the non-monotonic form 1.6 better - it fixes static
   placement and breaks only motion, which the measure does not look
   at. Judge a projection change by projected corners across several
   ticks, not by a frame score.

   WITH BOTH IN PLACE the whole camera matches. At
   the tick whose camera equals the demo's render camera, position,
   both angles and all three projected corners agree exactly:

       +------------------+---------------------------+
       | quantity         | demo and port             |
       +==================+===========================+
       | camera           | -4143, -2305, 8840        |
       | yaw, pitch       | -1106, -1378              |
       | face 0 corners   | (41,66) (59,67) (64,115)  |
       | the two widths   | -18, -17                  |
       +------------------+---------------------------+

   AND THE FRAME PAIRING IS OFF BY ONE from the obvious reading. A
   capture taken N frames on shows the render of frame N-1, because
   the demo renders every second VBL; 500 frames on scores against
   the port's tick 509, not 510, and the corner reads taken from a
   breakpoint armed after that capture belong to tick 510. Scoring
   the two against each other compares
   consecutive renders and moves the result by about a whole unit of
   mean luminance.

   CamPath_AdvanceMode12 [verified, 0x002d661e]:

       key = g_CamCursor12 >> 8
       if key >= g_CamKeyCount12:          40
           yaw = -0x120, pitch = 0, cursor = 0, key = 0
       entry = g_pCamPath12 + 0xc + key * 12
       CamPath_EvalTriple(entry, cursor & 0xff) -> x, y, z
       camera = (x, y, z + 0x1388)

   Twelve bytes a key, three longwords, forty keys, and the cursor
   advances two a tick - so a full pass would take 5120 ticks, far
   longer than the cue allows. The wrap exists but the shot does not
   reach it.

   THE +0xc SKIPS A DUPLICATED FIRST ENTRY, so key 0 can be
   interpolated like any other.

   THE KEYS COME OUT OF relic.dat. Space_LoadAssets calls Dat_LoadSplit
   on Fspacedata with the slot table at g_SpaceDataSlots (0x002e472c)
   and the sizes at g_SpaceDataSizes, carving the chunk into 67
   sub-blocks; g_pCamPath12 is SLOT 64, at chunk offset 0x37da and 516
   bytes long - 43 entries of twelve, which is the duplicated P[-1],
   the forty keys, and two spare for the interpolator's lookahead
   [verified]. In the file that is relic.dat offset 0x0020a9a4, and the
   keys read there are identical to live memory - see below.
   formats/geometry.txt 1.

   AND CamPath_Setup'S SEED IS REDUNDANT. It writes (0, 0, 0x5a00)
   over the first two entries, and the asset already holds exactly
   that in both - so the duplication is baked into the data and the
   code only rewrites what was there. Nothing depends on the call,
   which is why the path looked unfilled: no analysed write reaches
   g_pCamPath12 because Dat_LoadSplit stores through the slot table,
   not through that address.

   THEN TWO SINE-DRIVEN AIM ANGLES, taken off the cursor at different
   rates so the look drifts rather than tracking anything:

       g_CamAim12A = (sin[(cursor * 4) & 0x1fff] >> 3) - 0x2c3
       g_CamAim12B =  sin[(cursor * 8) & 0x1fff] >> 4

   VERIFIED LIVE at cursor 2078, which is key 8 at t 0x1e. Keys 8 and
   9 read (432, 2873, -1069) and (4173, 2500, -1069) - in the file and
   in guest memory alike - and the camera read (833, 2917, 3957), the z
   being -1069 plus 5000 against 3957 [observed, on hardware 900
   frames on].

   THE BRANCH ON g_CamDebugDrive IS NOT PART OF THE SHOT. It is zero
   in the demo; taken, it reads POTGOR at 0xdff016 and adds straight
   to the camera, which is a mouse drive left in the build.

4.  The Light Base Decides The Orb's Core

   Ham8_BuildTables IS CALLED TWICE IN Space_RunPart, with different
   colours, and the second is this shot's [verified]:

       0x002cc590   0x3f, 0x4f, 0x6f   mode 6, the orb forming - 2.10
       0x002cc958   0x5f, 0x5f, 0x7f   this phase - 2.11

   IT DECIDES WHAT THE BRIGHTEST PIXELS BECOME, not just a tint. The
   resolve fades a pixel toward the base by its light byte, and the
   fade is total once the light passes 0x3f: d5 is 0xff - 4 * light
   clamped at zero, so a light of 64 or more leaves the pixel EXACTLY
   the base colour whatever its own channels held. Seven or eight
   pixels a frame reach that in this shot, in one small cluster at
   the orb's core, and they read as high as 108 [observed, ticks 408
   to 431].

   SO A MISSING BASE IS NOT A MISSING TINT, IT IS A BLACK HOLE. Every
   other ship and space part sets its own; this one did not, and took
   whatever the part before it left. Reached from a part whose base is
   dark - src/part_credits.c uses 0, 0, 0 - that cluster resolves to
   black inside the brightest part of the orb. It is only visible
   once something pushes the light past 0x3f there, which is why it
   surfaced with the centre flare and the band rather than before.

5.  Open questions

   -  WE DRAW LESS LIGHT THAN THE DEMO AS THE SHOT GOES ON. Byte +2
      measured against the hardware, the demo's render target against
      ours at the same tick of the camera path [observed]:

          tick    demo lit   max   total      ours lit   max   total
           500         879    66   29181           873   108   29475
           700        2530    84   97301          1673    84   58057

      At 500 the two agree in count and total and 478 of the lit
      pixels are the same pixels; by 700 we carry about three fifths
      of the demo's light. FOUR CANDIDATES ARE RULED OUT [observed]:

        o  the horizon band, which subtracts light - it costs 1625
           of the 39244, four per cent
        o  the attach flares, which add NO light in either, because
           byte +2 of the amber ramp reads 00 on the hardware
        o  the ten-satellite loop's geometry - 0x002d2b3c is the
           0x2800 ring, size 0x1e, steps 0x333 and 0x514, ten times,
           which is what src/orbscene.c already does
        o  its ramp and its blitter - 0x002cefa4 IS Draw_GlowBlob,
           and the table at 0x002f06b0 reads back byte for byte as
           our g_RampBolt, 0x3f flat to entry 129 then down by one
           every second entry

      All ten satellites project and draw at tick 700, depths 4412 to
      5642 and radii 10 to 13, so nothing is being rejected either.

      WHAT IS LEFT is where the light is missing, which is two places
      and not one: a solid core at the orb that we draw as a ring of
      ten separate blobs, and diagonal streaks on the left that are
      beam-shaped. So the shortfall is not one cause, and the next
      measurement wants the demo's own light decomposed - a hit
      counter on Draw_GlowBlob would say how many blobs it draws a
      frame, which ours cannot be short of at ten.

      AN EARLIER READING OF THIS HELD THAT THE DEMO'S LIGHT PLANE WAS
      EMPTY - not one non-zero byte +2 - and it does not reproduce.
      Driven to the matched tick by g_CamSrcX/Y/Z and read out of the
      render target, the demo has 879 lit pixels there. Treat a light
      measurement as suspect unless the frame was matched on the
      camera and the buffer named: amiga_fb's PPM drops byte +2
      entirely, and a work buffer carries no effects at all.

   -  Phases 8 and 9 do not set a light base of their own, so they
      inherit 2.11's. Whether the demo rebuilds the tables again for
      them is unread.

   -  The shot's length in ticks, which needs a watch on the mode word
      across the whole phase.

   -  What 0x002e0afc and 0x002e0c1c drive.

   -  Phases 8 and 9, which follow the same shape: mode 0xa ending on
      cue (0x20, 0), then mode 0xb, which DOES use the fuse and renders
      through 0x002cd8fe rather than Scene_RenderFrame.
