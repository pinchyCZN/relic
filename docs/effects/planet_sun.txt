PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                      THE PLANET AND THE SUN


Abstract

   Scene 2.3, the screen between the terminal and the first logo: a
   planet silhouetted against a sun, with a lens flare that fades as
   the sun crosses the frame. Not one polygon is drawn - the whole
   scene is 2D compositing into a 160 x 100 four-byte framebuffer,
   and the planet itself is written into the light accumulator rather
   than into any colour.

1.  Where It Runs

   Space_RunSunScene (0x002ce81e) is the first thing
   SpaceModule_Part0 runs after the loader's gate releases, before
   the opening logo [verified]. The terminal of 2.1 is still typing
   while relic.dat loads; this is what replaces it.

   +-----------------------------+----------------------------------+
   | Ham8_BuildTables(0, 0, 0)   | base colour BLACK                |
   | Mem_AllocSubBlocks          | g_ScenePool, MEMF_FAST           |
   | maybe_Sun_BuildBodySprite   | the 64 x 64 body, procedural     |
   | maybe_Sun_BuildBodyMask     | its 1-byte-per-pixel mask        |
   | g_SceneMode = 3             |                                  |
   | loop Space_SunSceneFrame    | until a music cue                |
   +-----------------------------+----------------------------------+

   THE BASE COLOUR IS THE WHOLE TRICK. Ham8_BuildTables blends every
   pixel toward the base by its light byte, so a base of (0,0,0)
   makes the light byte DARKEN. Space_RunPart passes (0x3f,0x4f,0x6f)
   and gets the opposite sense out of the same byte, and
   maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes passes (0x3f,0x3f,0x3f). The light
   byte's meaning is per scene, never global - see
   subsystems/display.txt.

   THIS SCENE IS THE FIRST HALF OF A SCENE SPLIT ACROSS THE LOGO.
   Scene one of maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes is the same backdrop
   again after the logo - the same sky, planet, halo and flare, the
   same frame routine step for step - with the shadow sweep gone and
   the base colour white, so the sun has emerged and lights the
   scene [observed]. See effects/approach.txt.

2.  The Frame, In Eight Steps

   Space_SunSceneFrame (0x002ce938) latches the camera and position
   state and then calls, in order [verified]:

       Scene_DrawTexturedSky          0x002d2db2
       Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun        0x002d2742
       Sprite_StampSaveUnder          0x002d1a2e
       Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo        0x002ceaf8
       Sprite_RestoreSaveUnder(0)     0x002d1e08
       Flare_Draw                     0x002cf2aa
       Scene_DrawShadowSweep          0x002cea48
       maybe_Scene_DrawHorizonBand    0x002d2530

   THERE IS NO Scene_SortAndDrawAll, which is the evidence that the
   scene holds no geometry at all [verified]. Every other scene in
   act 1 calls it.

   THE SAVE-UNDER PAIR IS AN OCCLUSION COUNTER, which is not what
   its name suggests. Sprite_StampSaveUnder (0x002d1a2e) walks a
   12 x 12 mask at 0x002d1d78 centred on the flare, saves each
   framebuffer pixel it covers into 0x002d1b38, and writes 0x00008000
   over it - a LIGHT byte of 0x80, chosen because nothing else in the
   scene sets bit 7 of that byte.

   Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo then draws the planet opaquely over part of
   it. Sprite_RestoreSaveUnder (0x002d1e08) tests the LIGHT byte of
   each cell and restores only the ones where bit 7 SURVIVED - that
   is, the ones the planet did not cover - and counts them. So the
   count is how much of the sun is still visible past the planet, and
   the mean of the surviving positions is where the visible part sits.

   That count drives g_SunRange, which is why one parameter runs the
   whole scene:

       n       = surviving cells, doubled, clamped to 0xb3
       target  = n < 0x82 ? n : 0x82 - (n - 0x82)      folded
       g_SunRange += (target << 8 - g_SunRange) / 4

   and when nothing survives at all, g_SunRange eases toward zero by
   HALVES instead of quarters and the second flare below is skipped.

   SO DRAW_FLARE RUNS TWICE A FRAME, not once, and the two calls differ
   in every argument:

   +----------+------------------+-------------------+----------------+
   | Call     | Ray table        | Ramp              | Radius         |
   +==========+==================+===================+================+
   | Project- | g_RayArcs        | g_ColRampWarm     | 0x50 - range   |
   | AndDraw- | 0x002f102c       | 0x002eef90        | >> 8, SKIPPED  |
   | Sun      |                  |                   | when <= 0      |
   +----------+------------------+-------------------+----------------+
   | Restore- | g_RaySpikes      | 0x002eeaf0        | range >> 8     |
   | SaveUnder| 0x002f1028       | warm + one hump   |                |
   +----------+------------------+-------------------+----------------+

   The two are complements: as the sun emerges the first shrinks to
   nothing and the second grows. Both tables were confirmed against the
   running demo - g_RayArcs matched all 256 entries and g_RaySpikes its
   nonzero run at 52..58.

   THE RAMP AT 0x002eeaf0 IS g_ColRampWarm PLUS A BLUE HUMP. Ramp_BuildAll
   builds both from the same (0x3f, 0x34, 0x2d, 0x500000), then passes
   0x002eeaf0 through 0x002c9e80 with 0xaa. That pass adds
   max(0, 8 - ((i' ^ 4) >> 6)) with i' = (255 - i) - 0xaa to the LOW BYTE
   of each entry - and the low byte of a pixel is B - so entries 81..89
   gain 4 to 8 of blue and nothing else changes.

   AND THE SECOND FLARE IS NOT CENTRED ON THE SUN. RestoreSaveUnder sums
   the positions of the surviving cells, divides by the count, and passes
   that MEAN to Draw_Flare [verified, 0x002d1e7e..0x002d1e88]. So the
   glow sits on the visible part of the sun and leads it out from behind
   the planet rather than staying with its centre.

3.  The Screen Is 160 x 100

   Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun's two offsets are the only place the
   scene's resolution is written down [verified]:

       g_FlareScreenX = (x << 8) / z + 0x50
       g_FlareScreenY = (y << 8) / z + 0x32

   0x50 and 0x32 are 80 and 50 - half of 160 and half of 100.
   Scene_DrawShadowSweep agrees from the other direction: it runs 100
   rows and rejects any x at or past 0xa0, and its row stride is
   0x280, which is 160 pixels at four bytes each.

4.  The Shadow Is Darkness, And It Opens The Scene

   Scene_DrawShadowSweep draws no colour. Per row, for y from -0x32
   to 0x31:

       x = isqrt(10000 - y*y)                  a RADIUS-100 ARC
       d = g_PlanetScreenX - g_FlareScreenX
       x += g_PlanetScreenX + 3*d
       x -= 1 ; skip the row if negative
       light = 0xa
       loop, dbf on x so it COUNTS DOWN to zero:
           if (x < 0xa0) frame[x].LIGHT = light
           light = min(light + 2, 0x40)

   It writes byte +2 of the four-byte pixel and never touches R, G
   or B. The scene's base colour is black, so that light darkens.

   THE LOOP WALKS LEFT, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE BEHAVIOUR. dbf
   decrements, so the shadow lies to the LEFT of the arc and deepens
   away from it, and the light steps whether or not the pixel was on
   screen - so an arc off the right edge arrives with the ramp
   already clamped at 0x40, which against a black base is total
   black.

   SO THE SCENE OPENS BLACK AND IS UNCOVERED FROM THE RIGHT. At the
   first frame the offset puts the arc past 0xa0 and the whole
   screen is dark; as Anim_SceneTick drifts the sun right the offset
   shrinks, the arc crosses leftward, and the frame is revealed
   behind it. The arc bulges right at mid-height, so its edge is a
   backwards C matching the planet's curve [observed].

   RUNNING THE LOOP RIGHTWARD INVERTS THE SCENE EXACTLY, and both
   directions look like a shadow: it opens clear and ends swallowed
   instead. The direction is in the dbf, not in the arithmetic.

   THE OFFSET CARRIES THE SUN'S POSITION. x is displaced by
   g_PlanetScreenX plus three times the gap between planet and
   flare, which is what ties the reveal to the sun's drift - the
   only thing in the scene that moves.

   RADIUS 100 IS THE SCREEN'S FULL HEIGHT, so this is not the
   planet. The planet is the 64 x 64 sprite of section 5. The name
   Scene_DrawVignette fits a frame darkening but not an isqrt, a
   radius, or an offset that follows the sun.

5.  The Sun

   Two things draw it, and they are separate.

   Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun rotates (g_SunWorldX, g_SunWorldY)
   against a z of 0xfa0 through maybe_Math_Rotate2D_b, rejects a
   non-positive z as behind the camera, divides for perspective, and
   calls Draw_Flare with g_pRayArcs and g_ColRampWarm. The radius is
   0x50 - (g_SunRange >> 8), so the sun shrinks with range and is
   skipped entirely once that reaches 0x5000.

   Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo draws the planet and its atmosphere in
   two passes that blend differently [verified]:

   +--------+--------------+-------+-----------------------------+
   | Pass   | Source       | Size  | Blend                       |
   +========+==============+=======+=============================+
   | one    | g_pPlanetRgb | 64x64 | masked OPAQUE copy          |
   +--------+--------------+-------+-----------------------------+
   | two    | g_pPlanetHalo| 80x80 | ADDITIVE, light byte too    |
   +--------+--------------+-------+-----------------------------+

   THE PLANET IS A PLAIN 2D SPRITE AND IT IS IN THE EXECUTABLE, not
   in relic.dat: g_PlanetSprite at 0x002e5290, 64 x 64 at four bytes
   a texel, 6 bits a channel with the light byte zero throughout. It
   is a crescent-lit sphere with its terminator painted in.

   ITS MASK IS COMPUTED, NOT STORED. Planet_BuildSpriteAndMask sums
   the three colour channels and calls anything at or below 1
   background, so the art needs no alpha channel and its near-black
   border becomes the cutout. The same pass takes 4 off every
   channel, clamped at zero.

   The halo is procedural. Planet_BuildHaloRing walks 80 x 80 cells
   over coordinates stepping 8 from -0x140 and takes

       abs(isqrt(x*x + y*y) - 0xf4) * 4, capped at 0xff

   as an index into g_HaloRamp. ABS OF A DISTANCE FROM A RADIUS IS A
   RING rather than a disc: the brightest entry falls where the
   radius is exactly 0xf4, 30.5 of the sprite's 40 cells out.

   THE RAMP ENTRY IS NOT THE PIXEL, and reading it as one makes the
   halo glare. Each entry is four bytes of which the FIRST IS UNUSED:
   bytes 1, 2 and 3 become R, G and B, the light byte is forced to
   zero, and each channel is then

       c = ((entry >> 2) * (x + 0x140) >> 8) >> 2

   so the brightest entry (0x0e, 0x27, 0x53) resolves to about
   (1, 5, 12) of 0x3f rather than to itself - a factor of seven down,
   with a horizontal gradient on top that fades the ring out to the
   left. The halo is a DIM blue rim, and it carries no light at all.

   [observed] The demo's halo is barely visible, which is the check
   on all of the above: taking the entry as the pixel gives a bright
   ring with a large light value, and both are wrong.

6.  The Lens Flare

   Flare_Draw (0x002cf2aa) computes one number and that number does
   two jobs:

       D4 = isqrt((fx - 0x50)^2 + (fy - 0x40)^2) >> 8
          + (((g_SunRange >> 8) ^ 0x7f) >> 5)
       if (D4 >= 3) return

   The widths are 32-bit throughout [verified, 0x002cf2c2]: each
   square is a muls.l that keeps the low longword, the sum can wrap,
   and Math_Isqrt (0x002ca766) reads it as UNSIGNED - an exact
   floor root of all 32 bits [verified, emulated against math.isqrt
   over 662,143 inputs]. The range term's final shift is lsr.l, not
   asr.l. A wrapped sum therefore gives a large root and shuts the
   gate; reading it as signed instead opens it.

   It is the gate, and it is also the shift the sprite blitters
   apply - so 0 draws at full brightness, 1 at half, 2 at a quarter
   and 3 or more draws nothing. The flare fades IN rather than being
   switched on.

   THE DISTANCE TERM IS ALWAYS ZERO ON A 160 x 100 SCREEN. The
   furthest two points are about 190 apart, Math_Isqrt returns a
   plain root, and the >>8 crushes anything under 256 to nothing. So
   despite how it reads, the gate is driven by g_SunRange alone:

   +-----------+---------+---------+---------------------+
   | g_SunRange| radius  | shift   | flare               |
   +===========+=========+=========+=====================+
   | 0x0000    | 80      | 3       | off                 |
   +-----------+---------+---------+---------------------+
   | 0x2000    | 48      | 2       | quarter             |
   +-----------+---------+---------+---------------------+
   | 0x4000    | 16      | 1       | half                |
   +-----------+---------+---------+---------------------+
   | 0x6000    | gone    | 0       | full                |
   +-----------+---------+---------+---------------------+

   ONE PARAMETER RUNS THE WHOLE SCENE. g_SunRange shrinks the sun
   and brightens the flare together, so the sun recedes to nothing
   while its ghosts bloom in - which is the effect the timeline
   describes as the sun edging out and lighting the scene.

   The ghost sprites themselves are one byte a pixel in the
   executable - 0x002e4990 and 0x002e4d90 at 32 x 32, 0x002e5190 at
   16 x 16 - and peak at 14 of a possible 0x3f, so the ghosts are
   faint even at full brightness. Flare_BuildGhostSprites
   (0x002cf4cc) expands them through 0x002cf52e with per-channel
   shifts of (1, 1, 0):

       R = s >> 1 ; G = s >> 1 ; LIGHT = 0 ; B = s

   so they are BLUE-WHITE, against the sun's amber g_ColRampWarm.

   The four ghosts are the sun's position reflected through
   (0x3c, 0x3c) at four scales [verified]:

   THE PLANET IS DRAWN NINE ROWS BELOW ITS CELL. Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo
   subtracts 0x20 from x and 0x28 from y, then adds 0x1680 to the
   framebuffer pointer - 5760 bytes, which at 0x280 a row is exactly
   nine rows and no columns [verified, 0x002ceb70]. The halo's loop
   pops the same x and y, takes a further 8 off x for its wider
   sprite, and does NOT add it, so the offset moves the planet alone.

   +---------------------+-----------------------+------------------+
   | Routine             | Position              | Sprite           |
   +=====================+=======================+==================+
   | Flare_GhostFar      | 0x3c - 2*(p - 0x3c)   | 32x32, ghost32a  |
   +---------------------+-----------------------+------------------+
   | Flare_GhostNear     | 0x3c - (p - 0x3c)>>1  | 32x32, ghost32b  |
   +---------------------+-----------------------+------------------+
   | Flare_GhostMirror   | 0x3c - (p - 0x3c)     | 16x16, ghost16   |
   +---------------------+-----------------------+------------------+
   | Flare_GhostOut      | 0x3c + 2*(p - 0x3c)   | 16x16, ghost16   |
   +---------------------+-----------------------+------------------+

   So the chain lies on the line through the pivot at -2, -1/2, -1
   and +2, which is how a real lens flare's ghosts behave. The half
   is asr.l #1 [verified, 0x002cf3b6], which floors, so a negative
   odd offset lands one pixel further out than a truncating divide.

   Sprite_Add32x32 and Sprite_Add16x16 take the centre as a WORD
   (subi.w at 0x002cf3d0) and clip it against all four edges of the
   160 x 100 frame. A centre beyond 16 bits therefore wraps in the
   original; that is reachable only with a camera the demo never
   turns that far.

   THE EIGHTH PASS IS A GLARE BAND ACROSS THE SUN, not a horizon.
   0x002d2530 lays ten rows starting seven above the MEAN ROW of the
   visible cells - so it tracks the uncovered part of the sun, not any
   fixed horizon - and runs the full 160 columns. Per pixel it reads
   g_PolarTable by ((x - g_FlareScreenX) as a signed byte) >> 1, takes
   the distance byte, halves it and inverts it in six bits, so the
   value peaks at the sun and falls away along the row.

   Its blend is its own [0x002d3d34]: R, G and B each take that value
   CLAMPED AT 0x3f, and the LIGHT byte loses TWICE as much, floored at
   zero. So the band tints and un-darkens together, which is what makes
   it read as light grazing the planet's limb rather than as fog.

   IT IS A TRANSIENT, AND THE GATE IS WHY. g_HorizonNow eases by halves
   toward the unfolded visible count, and the band's strength is
   -|((now >> 8) - 100)| / 4 - 30, abandoned at -50 or below. That is
   only above the floor while the count sits near 100 - the sun about
   half out from behind the planet - so the band blooms during the
   emergence and is gone at either end [observed: at tick 306
   g_HorizonNow read 0x9910, giving -44 and a live band; by tick 430
   the count had saturated and it was suppressed].

   The polar lookup's row runs -100 to +80 in twenties across the ten
   screen rows, so a tall slice of the table is squeezed into a short
   one of the screen.

   DRAW_FLARE HAS NO ZERO TEST, AND THAT MATTERS AT THE CENTRE. It
   indexes the ramp with a signed word and adds whatever comes back
   [0x002d4532]. Entry 0 is 0x3f3f003f - white - and the index reaches
   zero exactly at the flare's middle, where the falloff is zero and
   the ray value for that one angle happens to be zero too. Treating
   v == 0 as nothing to draw therefore removes the single brightest
   pixel the flare has and leaves a BLACK DOT in the centre of the sun
   for those frames [observed: at ticks 301, 338 and 416 the middle
   pixel read (3,3,0,6) against neighbours summing 200].

   AND THE INDEX LEAVES 0..255 AT BOTH ENDS. g_pRaySpikes holds up
   to 7 and is subtracted twice, so a spiked flare's index reaches
   -14 over its core; g_pRayArcs holds down to -12, so an arced
   flare's reaches 279 at its rim. Neither is a defect: THE RAMP
   BLOCK IS PADDED FOR EXACTLY THAT RANGE.

   Ramp_BuildAll lays six ramps 0x4a0 apart from 0x002eeaf0 and
   fills 0x400 of each, so forty longwords of zero follow every one,
   and forty longwords of 0x3f3f003f - saturated white carrying no
   light - sit in front of the first at g_ColRampPadWhite 0x002eea50
   [verified; no xrefs, and the same bytes read live]. Forty
   longwords at each end is what -14 and 279 need.

   SO WHAT A CORE LOOKS LIKE DEPENDS ON WHICH RAMP IT IS, and the
   two the scene uses differ:

   +------------------+-------------+----------------+--------------+
   | Ramp             | Address     | In front of it | A spiked     |
   |                  |             |                | core reads   |
   +==================+=============+================+==============+
   | g_ColRampWarm2   | 0x002eeaf0  | the white pad  | white, and   |
   | the second flare |             |                | saturates    |
   +------------------+-------------+----------------+--------------+
   | g_ColRampAmber   | 0x002ef430  | the zero gap   | nothing, so  |
   | the orb and the  |             | behind         | what was     |
   | return's burst   |             | g_ColRampWarm  | under stands |
   +------------------+-------------+----------------+--------------+

   THE PAD IS WHAT THE FIRST 46 ENTRIES HOLD ANYWAY, since the
   inverse-square ramp saturates until i = 46, so on the block's
   first ramp the pad behaves as a clamp to entry 0.

   NEITHER CLAMPING NOR DROPPING IS A SUBSTITUTE. Clamping to entry
   0 paints every ARCED flare's rim white, which is what made the
   portal burst a solid white-yellow disc with the particles behind
   it invisible; dropping the pixel punches a hole in every SPIKED
   flare's core, which on the sun shows as one to a dozen dark
   pixels against saturated neighbours [observed: at ticks 400, 404,
   411 and 415 of the port, 1, 2, 11 and 3 of them]. The
   reconstruction lays the block out as the demo does and indexes it
   raw - gfx.c.

   DRAW_FLARE'S RIGHT EDGE IS EXCLUSIVE. It clamps cx + chord to 0x9f,
   subtracts the left edge, then takes one more off before the dbf, so
   the last column it touches is one short of the span [0x002d4502].
   Treating it as inclusive leaves a bright fringe down the right of the
   screen whenever the disc runs off it.

   The half-chord comes from a table at 0x002f1024, indexed
   [dy << 8 | radius] with dy signed and the centre 0x8000 bytes in. It
   holds exactly isqrt(radius^2 - dy^2) - checked against every radius
   at dy -24 - so computing the root instead is equivalent. Entries
   where radius < |dy| read 0xff and are never reached, because the row
   loop never runs outside the disc.

   THREE DIFFERENT REFERENCE POINTS ARE IN PLAY AND ALL OF THEM ARE
   DELIBERATE. The screen centre is (0x50, 0x32), the flare pivots
   about (0x3c, 0x3c), and the gate measures distance from
   (0x50, 0x40). None of them is a typo for another.

7.  What Moves, And The Rotating Rays

   NOTHING IN Space_SunSceneFrame ANIMATES ANYTHING. The motion is in
   Anim_SceneTick (0x002d5c66), which the VERTB hook at 0x002d5676
   calls once per vertical blank - so it runs on the interrupt, not
   in the render loop, which is why no frame routine appears to touch
   the camera [verified].

   Anim_SceneTick dispatches on g_SceneMode, and MODE 3 - this scene -
   IS TWO INSTRUCTIONS:

       g_SunWorldX    += 2
       g_CameraAngle  += 0xfa0        (0x002e0ad8)

   That is the whole animation THE TIMELINE DRIVES. g_SunRange is
   not in it, but it is not constant either: Sprite_RestoreSaveUnder
   rewrites it every frame from how much of the sun the planet leaves
   visible - see section 2. While the planet covers the sun entirely
   it eases to zero and stays there, which is why the radius reads as
   a constant 0x50 and Flare_Draw's ghost chain stays gated off for as
   long as that holds [observed: 0x002e0b62 read 0 under WinUAE with
   the sun fully behind the planet]. The flare visible then is
   Draw_Flare's rays.

   THE SAVE-UNDER COUNT IS MEASURED THROUGH THE FRAMEBUFFER, so the
   reconstruction runs it inside the frame rather than the tick -
   there is nothing to measure until the frame is drawn. That ties the
   ease to the drawn frame where the demo's is tied to its fixed 25 Hz
   one; see effects/approach.txt section 4.4.9, which also covers why
   the sentinel has to be tested by its high bit alone.

   THE ANIMATOR RUNS AT 50 HZ AND THE SCENE RENDERS AT 25. The two
   rates are separate, and the gap is easy to miss because both are
   driven off VERTB. Anim_SceneTick advances the camera and the sun
   every blank; Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun writes g_PlanetScreenX,
   g_FlareScreenX and g_FlareScreenY on every SECOND blank only
   [observed - a write watch on 0x002e0b3e under WinUAE reported hits
   on frames 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15 and no others].

   The interval is exactly two blanks every time, which is what says
   this is a deliberate 25 Hz lock rather than an A4000 failing to
   keep up - dropped frames would jitter between two and three.

   So the demo moves the sun by 2 and the angle by 0xfa0 twice per
   drawn frame, not once. WALL-CLOCK RATE IS UNAFFECTED, and a
   reconstruction that renders every blank covers the same ground in
   the same time with twice the samples; what changes is how stepped
   the motion looks.

   AND THE PROJECTION QUANTISES IT FURTHER. Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun
   shifts the world coordinate up by 8 and divides by the focal
   length 0xfa0 - 4000 - so the 2 the animator adds arrives on screen
   as 2 * 256 / 4000, which is 0.128 of a pixel. The integer divide
   therefore leaves the sun's screen x UNCHANGED FOR SEVEN TICKS OUT
   OF EIGHT and moves it a whole pixel on the eighth [verified]. The
   shadow's terminator is offset by 3 * (planet - flare) - section 4
   - so it inherits three times that: it stalls for the same seven
   ticks and then jumps three pixels.

   At 160 x 100 none of that is visible, because a pixel is a pixel.
   It is what decides how the motion reads once the frame is bigger
   than the demo's own - see section 10.

   THE CAMERA ANGLE IS WHAT TURNS THE RAYS, and it closes a loop left
   open in gfx.c. Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun writes the angle to
   0x002e0c1c as angle >> 12; Draw_Flare adds the LOW BYTE of that
   word to the polar angle twice (0x002d451e and 0x002d4528). So

       phase = ((0x400000 + n * 0xfa0) >> 12) & 0xff

   advances by about one of 256 steps a frame - a full revolution in
   roughly five seconds - and because the phase is applied twice,
   each ray is split in two and both halves turn. That byte is the
   g_FlareRayPhase gfx.c recorded as "nothing found writes it"; it is
   the low half of a word written as a word.

8.  The Additive Sprite Blend

   Sprite_Add32x32 (0x002cf3d0) and Sprite_Add16x16 (0x002cf44e) are
   the same routine at two sizes. The inner loop is four
   instructions:

       D0 = (A3)+              one four-byte sprite pixel
       D0 >>= D4               the fade shift from Flare_Draw
       D0 &= 0x3f3f003f
       (A2)+ += D0             all four channels, one add.l

   ONE add.l BLENDS THE WHOLE PIXEL, and that is why the mask zeroes
   byte 2: the light accumulator must contribute nothing to a flare,
   and leaving it in would also let its carry run into the blue byte.
   R, G and B come back masked to the chipset's six bits.

   A channel can still carry into its neighbour when both the sprite
   and the destination are near 0x3f. The demo accepts that.

   Both blitters address rows through g_RowOffsetTable rather than
   multiplying, and clip all four edges by shortening the counters
   and walking the source pointer to match.

9.  The Sky Is An Environment Map, And It Is Generated

   Scene_DrawTexturedSky turns each screen pixel into a DIRECTION
   and lets the direction index a 256 x 256 texture [verified].
   There is no skybox.

   THE TEXTURE IS BUILT, NOT LOADED, AND THAT IS THE TRAP HERE.
   g_pSkyTexture is a 0x40000 slot of g_SpacePool, and 0x40000 is
   Fplasma's size and four-byte format exactly - so the chunk looks
   like the obvious source and is not one. Sky_BuildTexture
   (0x002d3a04) makes it in three passes:

   +------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | Sky_GeneratePlasma     | the field, from six sine tables    |
   +------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | Sky_AddNebulae         | ten soft grey blobs                |
   +------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | Sky_AddStars           | 500 five-pixel stars               |
   +------------------------+------------------------------------+

   SKY_ADDNEBULAE IS TEN GREY BLOBS AND IT MOVES THE STARS.
   0x002d3c1c picks a random texel and a size of 4..0x143 ten times over
   and calls 0x002d3c6e, which fills a size x size square with

       v = ((size * 32 - r2) / r2) >> 6 , floored at 0, capped at 0x3f

   and adds v to R, G and B alike through 0x002d3d02, leaving LIGHT
   alone - so these are GREY, unlike the ghosts' blue-white.

   THE SQUARE IS MOSTLY EMPTY. v only clears zero while r2 is under
   about size / 2, so a blob of 315 is a bright core a dozen texels
   across inside a square wider than the texture. The sphere map then
   stretches that core, which is why they read as ovals rather than
   discs and why one can pass for a star.

   The row step wraps at 0x40000 but the COLUMN step does not: within a
   row the original walks straight past the right edge into the next
   one, and a blob near the end of the texture runs up to size * 4 bytes
   beyond it, into the direction table the pool puts next.

   AND IT DRAWS 30 NUMBERS FROM THE LCG BEFORE Sky_AddStars DRAWS ANY.
   Leaving the blobs out therefore does not just lose them, it puts all
   500 stars somewhere else - which is how a missing pass this quiet
   shows up as a sky that is wrong everywhere [verified: with the blobs
   in, eight consecutive texels across one blob's core matched the
   running demo exactly, core included].

   Sky_GeneratePlasma reads the sine table at 0x228b6c through six
   pointers, in pairs, one pair a channel:

       t = (((s1 >> 1) + s2) >> 8) + 0x40 , floored at 0
       c = ((t >> a) + 0x10) >> b

   with (a,b) of (2,2) for blue, (3,3) for green and (2,3) for red.
   The six pointers advance by their own stride per pixel AND again
   per row, wrapping at the table's 0x8000 - two independent strides
   per pointer is what stops the field repeating visibly.

   THE FIELD IS DELIBERATELY VERY DIM: blue reaches 14 of 0x3f, red
   7 and green 4. Sky_AddStars then adds 15 on top, so the stars are
   the only bright thing in the background. Each is a five-pixel
   plus - a centre and four arms at +3,+3,+4 - and the centre
   ALTERNATES between 0x0f0f000f and 0x04040004, giving two
   magnitudes. Positions come from Math_Rand, the demo's own LCG, so
   the field is deterministic and reproducing the generator
   reproduces the sky.

   SUBSTITUTING FPLASMA GETS BOTH THE BRIGHTNESS AND THE HUE WRONG.
   Fplasma means (15, 12, 8) in 6-bit and peaks at 55; the generated
   field means about (7, 4, 8) and is blue-dominant. And because
   Draw_Flare ADDS to whatever is already there, too bright a sky
   makes the sun's own colour clip and shift hue rather than reading
   as a flare.

   THE CARRY OUT OF THE LOW BYTE IS LOAD-BEARING in the sampler. A
   negative u makes (u & 0xff) large, which carries into the row, so
   the table is NOT simply addressed [v + 128][u + 128] - build it
   that way and the sky shears by one row down the left half.

   Sky_BuildDirTable (0x002d3990) precomputes the mapping once, as
   65536 longwords. For x and y each stepping 0x100 from -0x8000:

       r     = isqrt(x*x + y*y + 2*0xf000000)
       nx    = (x << 15) / r
       ny    = (y << 15) / r
       entry = (ny & ~0xff) * 4 + (nx & ~0xff) / 64

   THE 0xf000000 IS A Z TERM, which is what makes this a sphere map
   rather than a flat radius. Sky_BuildSquareTable adds it into
   every entry of g_SquareTable and both axes draw from there, so
   the root is sqrt(x*x + y*y + z*z) with z fixed near 22434. nx
   and ny are that unit direction's components times 32768, and the
   packing is a byte offset into the texture: (ny >> 8) * 1024 for
   the row and (nx >> 8) * 4 for the column, both components
   landing inside -128..127.

   PER FRAME, the three screen corners (-0x5000,-0x3200),
   (+0x5000,+0x3200) and (+0x5000,-0x3200) go through 0x002d4132
   rotated by the camera, and their differences from the third
   divide by 0xa0 and 0x64 to give per-column and per-row steps.
   0xa000 of texture space over 0xa0 pixels is 256 a pixel, so the
   map is 1:1 in 24.8 when unrotated. The pan is the camera angle's
   bits 15..22, four bytes a step, which is one texel - the sky
   slides about one texel every eight frames, and that slide is
   what reads as a camera move.

   THE PAN IS ADDED TO THE TABLE ENTRY, NOT SUBTRACTED, and it is two
   components in one longword: bits 16..23 of twice the camera angle
   become the low byte and a second angle at 0x002e0ad4 lands in the
   high half, so multiplying by four shifts whole 1024-byte texture
   rows as well as columns [verified, 0x002d2e64]. That second angle
   is zero throughout this scene, so only the horizontal term acts.
   Getting the sign wrong samples 272 texels away, and blue carries
   the error because blue has three times the range of red and green
   in the generated field.

   The inner loop is four instructions of work a pixel:

       D6.w  = (x_acc & 0xff00) | (y_acc & 0xff)
       D6    = g_pSkyDirTable[D6.w]      SIGN-EXTENDED word index
       D6   += the camera pan
       D6   &= 0x3ffff
       pixel = g_pSkyTexture[D6]

   The (x & ~0xff) | y packing is the same trick
   Logo_DrawZoomedText uses. MASKING RATHER THAN CLAMPING MEANS THE
   SKY TILES, and 0x3ffff is 0x40000 - Fplasma's size exactly,
   which is also why its edges match seamlessly.

   THE TWO POINTERS ARE SUB-BLOCKS, NOT CHUNKS, and reading them
   the other way round is easy: g_pSkyTexture at 0x002f1104 holds
   the texels and g_pSkyDirTable at 0x002f1108 the lookup, both
   0x40000. g_SpacePool carves one allocation into seven, and the
   three after them at 0x1000, 0x1000 and 0x400 are the flare
   ghosts at 32x32x4, 32x32x4 and 16x16x4 - which independently
   confirms the ghost geometry of section 6.

10. What Is In The Reconstruction

   src/part_sun.c carries the scene. Four of its pieces are shared
   primitives already in src/gfx.c, because Draw_Flare has seventeen
   callers across the demo:

   +------------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Gfx_Flare              | Draw_Flare        0x002d4452         |
   +------------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Gfx_Isqrt              | Math_Isqrt        0x002ca766         |
   +------------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | g_ColRampWarm          | 0x002eef90                           |
   +------------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | g_RayArcs              | g_pRayArcs        0x002f102c         |
   +------------------------+--------------------------------------+

   The sky needs no archive - it is generated - so src/part_sun.c
   depends on nothing but the executable. src/dat.c exists for the
   textures that are chunks.

   src/part_sun.c carries all eight passes of section 2, the occlusion
   counter and both Draw_Flare calls included.

   TWO DELIBERATE DIVERGENCES. Sky_AddNebulae stops at the end of the
   texture where the original can overrun into the direction table, and
   the flare's span is clipped in output pixels rather than demo ones,
   which is exact at scale 1 and a sub-pixel matter above it.

   A THIRD IS OPTIONAL. RELIC_MODE_SUBPIXEL re-evaluates section 7's
   projection without its truncation and places the sun, both flares
   and the terminator at the result, so they advance 0.128 and 0.384
   of a pixel a tick instead of stalling and jumping. Measured at
   scale 12, the terminator goes from frozen for seven ticks and then
   60 screen pixels in a single frame to a steady 4.6 a tick. The
   drawn centre can therefore sit up to a pixel right of the demo's
   own, and that difference IS the truncation being removed.

   The mode is forced off at scale 1.0, which is what keeps that the
   exact reference - see reconstruction.txt section 5. Three things it
   deliberately does not cover: the sky pan, which the demo steps a
   whole texel at a time through its own direction table; the lens
   flare's ghosts; and the glare band, whose offset is wrapped
   through a signed byte before the polar lookup, so carrying a
   fraction there would change the wrap rather than smooth the slide.

11. Open Questions

   o  Why the halo appears to move with the sun and to pulse
      [observed]. Planet_BuildHaloRing is called once at scene entry
      by all five of its callers, and Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo centres
      it on the planet, which mode 3 never moves - so the sprite
      cannot move or change. The likeliest reading is that the
      shadow sweeping across the ring changes which part of it is
      visible, which would read as both motion and a pulse; that is
      a prediction, not a finding.

   o  Which of g_SpacePool's two 0x40000 slots receives Fplasma.
      Space_LoadAssets loads six chunks through 0x0021fa94 from the
      name table g_SpaceChunkNames - Fspacedata, Fwadepic, Fplasma,
      Fshiptextures, Fcrust, Fearth - and the hand-off into slot 0
      is not read. Fplasma is the only chunk whose size and format
      match what the sampler needs.
