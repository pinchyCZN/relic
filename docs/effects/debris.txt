PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                     DEBRIS FALLING ONTO THE PLANET


Abstract

   The third and last phase of Space_RunReturn: the planet seen from
   close orbit with burning debris falling onto it. It follows the
   portal burst of effects/return.txt and ends the return module.

   Its parts are almost all primitives the earlier space scenes
   already built. What is new is one streak routine, one light-plane
   blend, and three small entity tables driven by a script.

1.  Where It Sits

   Space_RunReturn 0x002c8ddc runs three phases and this is the
   last:

       0x002c8df4   Space_RunDeparture   the flight and the ripple
       0x002c8e0a   Space_RunPortalRun   the swirl and the burst
       0x002c8e1e   Debris_RunScene      this

   Debris_RunScene 0x002cc3a2 sets scene mode 5 at 0x002e0bf6, a
   fuse of 0x1c2 = 450 fields at 0x002e0a92 and the light base
   0x7f/0x5f/0x3f, calls Debris_SetupScene once and then
   Debris_DrawFrame once a field until the fuse or 0x00237230 ends
   it [verified].

1.1.  The Black Screen Before It

   THE BLACK FRAMES BETWEEN THE BURST AND THIS SCENE ARE THE LOADER,
   NOT AN EFFECT. Space_RunReturn frees Fplasma at 0x002c8e0e and
   re-loads it at 0x002c8e12 - Dat_FreeChunk then Dat_LoadSplit on
   the name at 0x002e0cf3 - before calling Debris_RunScene. Nothing
   draws and no counter advances while that runs.

   The free and the re-load are of the SAME chunk. The portal run
   writes into that buffer, so the scene needs a clean copy and
   cannot simply keep the one it has [verified].

2.  The Frame

   Debris_DrawFrame 0x002ce046 latches the camera once and then
   makes five calls [verified]:

       0x002ce068   Debris_BlendScorch       section 2.5
       0x002ce072   Warp_FullscreenRotate    section 2.1
       0x002ce076   Debris_StepBolide        section 2.2
       0x002ce07a   Debris_DrawSparks        section 2.4
       0x002ce07e   Debris_DrawImpacts       section 2.3

   0x002e0b2a and 0x002e0b2e are copied from 0x002e0b32 and
   0x002e0b36 at the top, and the camera word at 0x002e0bec is
   0x002e0b2a >> 5. Everything below reads the latched pair, so the
   whole frame is drawn against one camera.

2.1.  The Planet Is The Same Buffer, Rotated

   The limb is Warp_FullscreenRotate 0x002d3e94 over the buffer at
   0x002d32d4 - THE SAME SOURCE THE OTHER SPACE SCENES USE. The
   quadrant reads mirrored onto the right of the screen because of
   the rotation, not because a second asset was authored for it
   [verified]. orb.h already holds the routine.

   What it reads is built by Debris_BuildPlanet 0x002d3136, 256 x
   256 longwords, in SIX passes [verified]:

       1  0x002d315a   the sky resampled through the direction
                       table, bias 0x118, mask 0x3ffff, plus
                       0x10000000 - +0x10 on R alone
       2  0x002d31a4   Fearth stamped 0x5c x 0xf0 at +0x8c00, a
                       zero longword skipped as a hole, same red
       3  0x002d31ca   the limb glow, 100 x 250 at +0x1380c,
                       ramp 0x002e17c8 - NOT the earth's
       4  0x002d3256   Texture_GlowLight(0x7f, 0x73, -2)
       5  0x002d326c   a running smear, NOT a halve - see below
       6  0x002d3288   copied to the second buffer, and 0x002d32d4
                       and 0x002d32d8 take the pair

   PASS 3 IS NOT THE EARTH'S PASS 3, in two ways that both bite.
   It reads a DIFFERENT RAMP - 0x002e17c8 against 0x002e13c8 at
   0x002d30b8, 0x400 further on - and it transforms the longword
   with the B2.B1.00.B0 rotate and >> 2 after masking 0xfcfcfcfc,
   where the earth's takes (v & 0xfefefefe) >> 1 in source order.
   So this one cannot write LIGHT and the earth's can. Taking the
   earth's ramp address costs R 1.07 -> 4.06 and B 0.92 -> 2.72
   [observed].

   The walk in pass 1 is rotated - the index is 0x8080 plus a word
   that starts at 0x7f80 and wraps - but that works out to the
   identity for every texel except the first 128.

   PASS 4 IS THE WHOLE OF THE LIGHT PLANE. None of the other passes
   can write byte +2: the sky's own is zero, ALL 22080 OF FEARTH'S
   ARE ZERO, and the limb ramp's rotate at 0x002d3228 zeroes it the
   same way the burst's front ramp does. Texture_GlowLight writes
   byte +2 and nothing else, and pass 5 then halves it along with
   the colour.

   PASS 5 IS NOT A HALVE. Its moveq #0,D0 sits OUTSIDE the loop -
   the dbf target is 0x002d3278 - so D0 carries from texel to texel
   and the pass is a running smear along the texture's memory order:

       acc = ((acc + texel) & 0xfefefefe) >> 1

   which preserves magnitude rather than dividing it. Read as a
   halve it costs R 4.06 -> 17.59 and B 1.78 -> 10.23 [observed],
   because nothing doubled the colour first.

2.1.1.  Texture_GlowLight

   0x002d221a, a clipped circle whose every texel is shaded from
   two chord angles, two sphere-normal lookups and two Fplasma
   samples. Called once, (r 0x7f, cx 0x73, cy -2) [verified].

   Per row, with dy running from -r:

       half  = the span half width - section 2.1.2
       cya   = g_SphereChordAngle[(dy << 8) | r]

   and per texel, with dx running from -half:

       cxa   = g_SphereChordAngle[(dx << 8) | half]
       n     = g_SphereNormalTable[(cya << 8) | cxa]
       n     = n with its LOW BYTE less 0x5a
       n     = g_SphereNormalTable[n]
       n     = n with its low byte less 0x16, times 8, masked word
       v     = ring[n * 4]
       n     = n with its low byte less 0x16, masked word
       v     = v + ring[n * 4 + 1], a BYTE add that wraps
       byte +2 = v - 0xf, floored at zero when that goes negative

   EVERY INDEX IS A SIGNED WORD. g_SphereChordAngle takes it raw
   with no bias, g_SphereNormalTable scaled by 2 from a base of
   0x0026f97a + 0x10100, and the ring scaled by 4 from Fplasma +
   0x20200.

   THE RING IS FPLASMA. *g_pRingTexture holds it at exactly its
   0x40000 bytes, which is why Space_RunReturn frees and re-loads
   that chunk before the scene - section 1.1. The two readings are
   one fact.

   The read at 0x002d22b0 is a 68020 MEMORY INDIRECT POST-INDEXED
   mode, extension word 0x79b7 - base suppressed, index not
   suppressed, I/IS 7 - so it resolves to *g_pSpanHalfWidth plus
   the index plus 0x8000, NOT to a pointer fetched from an array
   at g_pSpanHalfWidth + index. Ghidra renders the index inside
   the brackets, which reads as pre-indexed and is not what the
   encoding says.

2.1.2.  The Span Half Width, And Its Off-By-One

   Span_BuildHalfWidth 0x002ca14c fills the 0x10000 byte table
   every glow in the demo clips its rows with, indexed
   (dy << 8) + r + 0x8000:

       i = 0 .. 0x7fff   r = i & 0xff, dy = (i >> 8) - 0x80
                         byte = Math_Isqrt(r * r - dy * dy)
       then 128 rows     each copied from the row 0x100 BEFORE it

   THE MIRROR IS OFF BY ONE. The upper half is not dy's own chord
   but the one a row back, so dy = 0 is served by dy = -1 and
   dy = +64 by dy = -65 [verified]: table[0][63] is 62 where
   isqrt(63 * 63) is 63, and table[64][113] is 92 where
   isqrt(113^2 - 64^2) is 93 but isqrt(113^2 - 65^2) is 92.

   It is load bearing beyond clipping, because Texture_GlowLight
   indexes g_SphereChordAngle with the half width. Correcting it
   took LIGHT from 3.62 to 3.09 [observed].

2.2.  The Bolides, And The Script That Places Them

   Debris_StepBolide 0x002ce288 walks the block at 0x002ce3f4,
   stride 0x18, SIX LONGWORDS A RECORD. A NEGATIVE FIRST LONGWORD
   ENDS THE WALK - there is no count anywhere [verified].

       +0x00   the impact point's x, a plain long
       +0x04   its y; -1 marks the record dead
       +0x08   x offset from that point, 16.16
       +0x0c   y offset, 16.16
       +0x10   size, 16.16
       +0x14   a start delay in fields; non-zero skips the record

   THE RECORD HOLDS WHERE THE BODY LANDS, NOT WHERE IT IS. The
   screen position is the point at +0x00 plus the high word of the
   offset at +0x08, so a body whose offsets decay toward zero eases
   into its impact point [verified].

   The VBL hook's mode 5 block at 0x002d5a3a does the easing, and
   all three terms decay the same way - the value less itself >> 5,
   so 31/32 of it survives each field:

       +0x14 non-zero   decrement it and do nothing else
       otherwise        +0x10 -= +0x10 >> 5
                        and while +0x10 is still >= 0x10000,
                        +0x08 -= +0x08 >> 5 and +0x0c likewise

   So the body slows as it falls and stops moving once it is under
   one unit across, while continuing to shrink.

   Debris_StepBolide draws it with Draw_FlareTurbulent through the
   ramp at 0x002f0c50, at a radius of the size plus Math_Rand(0xa)
   clamped to 0x7f - which is why a falling body shimmers.

   WHEN THE SIZE'S HIGH WORD REACHES 1 THE RECORD IMPACTS. It writes
   -1 to +0x04 to kill itself, arms the scorch at 0x002cc4b8 with
   0x40, reads a triple of words from 0x002ce5a8 indexed by
   0x002cc4ba, advances that index and calls Debris_SpawnImpact.

   The impact SITES ARE SCRIPTED and the index is shared with the
   scorch's position table - one impact consumes one entry of both.

   The six scripted bodies, from 0x002ce4e4 [verified]:

       +----------+-----------+--------+--------+-------+
       | lands at | offset    |  size  | delay  | flag  |
       +==========+===========+========+========+=======+
       | 120, 25  | -220, 120 | 200.0  |      0 |     0 |
       | 110, 30  | -320, -20 | 127.0  |    150 |     0 |
       | 150, 40  | -320,-200 | 300.0  |    230 |     0 |
       | 170, 20  | -300, 240 | 320.0  |    290 |     0 |
       | 110, 10  | -320, 100 | 127.0  |    400 |     0 |
       | 110, 40  | -200, 240 | 300.0  |    480 |     0 |
       +----------+-----------+--------+--------+-------+

   The offset is the SECOND column, not a screen position: body
   one begins at (120-220, 25+120) = (-100, 145) and eases in.
   Every one of the six starts off the left of the screen.

2.3.  The Impacts

   Debris_SpawnImpact 0x002ce3aa takes the first of six slots at
   0x002e1374 whose lifetime word is zero and writes seven words:

       +0x00   lifetime 0xfa = 250 fields
       +0x02   x        the bolide's impact point
       +0x04   y
       +0x06   size     0xf at spawn
       +0x08   the triple read from 0x002ce5a8
       +0x0a
       +0x0c

   TWO CLOCKS, AS IN THE BURST. The size at +0x06 is grown by one a
   field by the mode 5 block at 0x002d5a80, and the lifetime at
   +0x00 is counted down by the DRAW. A port that draws once a field
   keeps them in step; one that does not will not.

   Debris_DrawImpacts 0x002ce1e8 draws two things from the one slot.
   While size >> 2 is below 0x32 - the first 185 fields - it draws
   Draw_MeteorStreak 0x002d4552 with the shape at 0x002e22e2, at x
   plus the word at +0x0a. Then it draws the bloom with
   Draw_GlowBlob at (size >> 2) >> 3 plus 0x19, clamped to 0x28,
   through the ramp at 0x002f07d8.

   THE STREAK AND THE BLOOM ARE ONE SLOT, not two entities: the
   streak is simply what the slot looks like early in its life.

2.3.1.  The Streak

   Draw_MeteorStreak 0x002d4552 ADDS TO LIGHT AND NOTHING ELSE.
   The slot hands it a radius of size >> 2, x plus the slot's
   +0x0a, the slot's +0x0c as a column step and its +0x08 as a
   per-row x step, with the ramp at 0x002e22e2 [verified].

   The per-row step is saved to 0x002d4668 before D5 is reused as
   radius * 2, and added to the centre EVERY ROW with the centre
   held in 8.8 - which is what leans the streak.

   THE POLAR TABLE IS WALKED LINEARLY. The pointer starts at
   g_PolarTable + 0x10000 + ((dy << 8) | dx) * 2 and advances 2
   PLUS the column step each column, so a non-zero step runs
   through the table faster than the span does and stretches the
   shape sideways. It also halves the row's half width. Clamping
   the walk per row would square the streak off; the overrun into
   the next row is the effect, the same lesson the panorama
   taught.

   Per texel the walked word's low byte is a distance, that
   indexes g_BlobFalloff at the row's radius << 8, the falloff
   indexes the ramp, and the LOW BYTE of that longword >> 2 less
   radius * 2, floored at zero, is added to LIGHT.

   The six scripted triples at 0x002ce5a8 give the first impact a
   step of 0 and a lean of 64, which in 8.8 is a quarter pixel a
   row; only the fifth has a non-zero step [verified].

2.4.  The Sparks

   Debris_DrawSparks 0x002ce19e walks 0x32 = 50 slots of 8 bytes at
   0x002e11e4: lifetime, x, y, size. A zero lifetime skips the slot.
   The radius is size >> 1 plus Math_Rand(0xa) and the blob goes
   through the ramp at 0x002f0a28, a different ramp from the
   impacts' [verified].

   The random term is added per field rather than stored, so a spark
   flickers instead of fading smoothly.

   Debris_SpawnSpark 0x002ce34a takes the first free slot and
   scatters it about the body by the body's OWN radius - a random
   0x14 less 0xa, times that radius, over 32 - so a big body throws
   its sparks wide and a small one keeps them close [verified].

   THE TRAIL'S LENGTH IS A DRAW COUNT, NOT A FIELD COUNT, and that
   is this scene's third two-clock divergence. Spawning is one a
   draw per live body and the lifetime counts draws, so the demo's
   trail persists about 2.8 times longer in fields than a port
   drawing every field manages. Hardware shows it plainly: of
   the demo's 21 live sparks only 9 are on screen and the rest run
   back along the body's path to x = -214, well out of frame,
   while ours are 28 live packed into the last 25 fields of that
   path [observed].

   The cost is measurable. Our sparks are the single largest
   remaining LIGHT error at tick 233 - drawing none at all
   takes LIGHT from 2.24 to 1.85 and empties the two worst blocks
   of the error map - not because they should not be there, but
   because the same trail is compressed into a shorter arc and so
   lies denser.

2.5.  The Scorch

   Debris_BlendScorch 0x002ce084 is gated on 0x002cc4b8, which an
   impact sets to 0x40. 0x002cc4ba picks an (x, y) pair from the
   table at 0x002ce578, stored as two longwords with the second
   shifted left 8 and added to give one pixel offset.

   It covers 64 by 64 pixels AND WRITES THE LIGHT BYTE ONLY. Each
   pixel is a weighted mean of the frame's own LIGHT and a mask byte
   from 0x002f1144, the two weights read as a pair of words from
   0x002f1140 and the sum taken >> 5. The 0x8080 added to A5 is the
   same patch one buffer along.

   IT IS INVISIBLE IN A PPM CAPTURE. R, G and B are untouched, so
   any comparison of this scene has to be raw to raw.

   IT WRITES THE TEXTURE, NOT THE FRAME, and it is gated twice:
   0x002cc4b8 must be non-zero and the byte at 0x002ce15e indexed
   by it must be non-zero too, so the blend runs on only some of
   the 0x40 an impact arms. Per texel of the 64 x 64 patch an
   8-byte record from *g_ScorchWeights gives a source byte offset
   into the OTHER buffer and two weights:

       LIGHT = (otherLight * w0 + maskByte * w1) >> 5

   and the two buffers are then SWAPPED, so the blend ping-pongs
   and the mark spreads a little further each time it runs. On the
   way out it zeroes LIGHT on the 2 x 2 at the patch centre,
   +0x8080 in, which is the seed the spread grows from. The
   Math_Rand(0x40) at 0x002ce122 is discarded and only advances
   the generator.

   ITS TWO TABLES ARE BUILT, NOT BAKED. Scorch_SnapshotMask
   0x002d32b8 copies the LIGHT byte of all 0x10000 texels of the
   texture into *g_ScorchMask - at the end of Debris_BuildPlanet
   and again on every impact, so marks already burned in are part
   of the next impact's snapshot. Scorch_BuildWeights 0x002cc2d0
   fills *g_ScorchWeights, 8 bytes a cell [verified]:

       off = (0x20 + sinTerm) * 0x100 + (0x20 + cosTerm)
       w   = clamp((dist + Math_Rand(0xa) - 0xf) * 2, 0, 0x1f)
             stored as w then w ^ 0x1f

   where the angle is the cell's own from g_PolarTable, sin and
   cos are taken at angle << 5, and both are scaled by the cell's
   distance LESS ONE. THAT MINUS ONE IS THE WHOLE MECHANISM: the
   sample lands one texel nearer the centre than the cell, so the
   zeroed seed walks outward a texel per blend.

   The weights summing to 0x1f against the blend's >> 5 means
   EVERY PASS MULTIPLIES LIGHT BY 31/32, which is what fades the
   mark as it spreads.

   THE BLEND RATE IS A SECOND TWO-CLOCK DIVERGENCE. The gate runs
   it on 25 of the 0x40 counts an impact arms, once per DRAW.
   Tick 186 is 42 fields past its impact with only 14 draws
   taken, so the demo had run 8 gated passes where a port drawing
   every field has run 18. 31/32 to those powers is 0.774 against
   0.566, a ratio of 0.73 - and the mark reads ~44 against the
   demo's saturated 63 there, which is 0.70 [observed]. The
   arithmetic is right; the rate is ours.

4.  The Reconstruction

   src/part_debris.c, registered as part 2.16. IT NEEDS RELIC.DAT -
   the planet's overlay is Fearth, and without -dat the part reports
   ready=0 and draws nothing.

   Most of what it needs was already in src/: Draw_GlowBlob,
   Draw_FlareTurbulent and Turbulence_BuildTable in gfx.c and
   Warp_FullscreenRotate in orb.h. New here are the bolide script,
   the three entity tables, the ember ramp and the scene's own
   planet build.

   DEBRIS_BUILDPLANET IS NOT EARTH_BUILDTEXTURE and the two are kept
   separate. They share the remap bias 0x118, the mask 0x3ffff, the
   0x5c x 0xf0 stamp at 0x8c00 and the limb's 100 x 250 walk, but
   they differ twice [verified]:

       the limb's x origin   0x2a0 here, 0x280 at 0x002d3026
       a red tint            BOTH of this one's first two passes
                             add 0x10000000 to the longword, which
                             is +0x10 on R alone. The earth's two
                             passes add nothing.

   The red tint is most of the scene's look. Without it the planet
   renders as the ordinary blue earth and R scores 19.63 against
   hardware; with it, 5.23.

   Scored against hardware at our tick 11, raw to raw [observed]:

           R 1.07   G 0.95   LIGHT 3.09   B 0.92

   with 5089 of 16000 pixels byte identical, and against hardware
   at our tick 186 - 40 fields past the first impact -

           R 0.46   G 0.47   LIGHT 1.78   B 0.46

   with 10141 of 16000 byte identical, and against hardware at
   our tick 233

           R 0.86   G 0.79   LIGHT 2.24   B 0.74

   [observed]. TICK 233 IS FIELD 233 ON THREE INDEPENDENT
   COUNTS: its warp counter reads 0x4d1 against a staged 0x3e8,
   its first impact's size of 102 is 87 fields past a spawn at
   146, and its second body's offset and size are bit for bit
   what the recurrence gives at 83 steps from a start at 151.

   The first is tick 10 or 11 of the scene: body one's offset has
   decayed from 0xff240000 to 0xff5fd866, a ratio of 0.737, and
   (31/32)^n = 0.737 gives n = 9.6.

5.  Open questions

   -  WHAT THE IMPACT'S +0x08 TRIPLE IS FOR. Debris_SpawnImpact
      stores three words read from 0x002ce5a8 and Debris_DrawImpacts
      loads +0x08 into D5 before the streak block. Where D5 is
      consumed is not read, and +0x0c reaches Draw_MeteorStreak as
      D4 without its meaning being established.

   -  HOW MANY BLEND PASSES A PORT SHOULD RUN. Section 2.5's rate
      is the burst's question again: the demo ties the spread to
      its draw and does not hold a field rate, and a port drawing
      every field fades the mark about a third further. Matching
      the pass count instead is worse overall - scored against
      tick 186 it costs LIGHT 1.78 -> 5.73 - because the bloom
      and the streak both key off the field-driven size. Nothing
      may be tuned to a capture to settle it.

   -  THE STREAK HELPS BUT IS NOT PROVED. Drawing it improves both
      points - LIGHT 2.37 to 2.21 at tick 233 and 2.17 to 1.78
      at tick 186 - so its sign and rough magnitude are right
      [observed]. Nothing yet separates it from the bloom and the
      scorch it sits inside, so an error in its shape would not
      show.

   -  WHAT IS LEFT OF THE FRAME'S LIGHT. The texture is right -
      64 of 64 sampled texels of its LIGHT plane are byte exact
      against the demo, and R, G and B are 61, 63 and 61 of 64
      with nothing off by more than 2 [observed]. The frame still
      scores LIGHT 3.09 where its other channels are near 1, so
      what remains is drawn per field rather than baked: the
      sparks and the falling bodies, whose positions are seeded
      from a different RNG.

   -  WHAT 0x002e0c7e CONTRIBUTES TO THE GLOW. g_FlareGrow is read
      into D5 at 0x002d229e and D5 is never used again in the
      routine, so it is taken as dead and not transcribed.

   -  OUR LIGHT PEAKS AT 64 AGAINST THE DEMO'S 63, so something in
      the frame adds to LIGHT without saturating. One pixel, but it
      is the same class of fault the burst's front ramp had.

   -  THE TWO LIMB GLOWS TRANSFORM THEIR RAMP DIFFERENTLY, and only
      the clamp is in doubt. Earth_BuildTexture takes the halo ramp
      longword as (v & 0xfefefefe) >> 1 IN SOURCE ORDER at
      0x002d310a - every byte, LIGHT included - where this scene
      rotates it to B2.B1.00.B0 and takes >> 2 at 0x002d3228. So
      part_earth.c's byte handling is correct for its own routine.

      What neither matches is the add: both demo routines do a plain
      add.l with no saturation, and part_earth.c clamps each byte at
      CHAN_MAX. The debris build clamps at 0xff instead, which is
      nearer but still not a long add. Whether a carry between bytes
      ever happens here is not established.

   -  WHAT 0x002d32b8 AND 0x002d3136 DO. Both are called once each -
      the first on impact, the second from the scene's setup - and
      neither is read.
