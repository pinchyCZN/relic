PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                    THE ORB FORMATION AND ITS LIGHTNING


Abstract

   Part 2.10. The camera circles the ship while six lightning beams
   charge an orb held in the claws. This note covers the beams: how a
   beam is aimed, how it grows, and the three glow stages that make it
   read as a flow rather than a switch. The scene's phase script and
   its camera path are in parts.txt 3.2, and the cut that follows it is
   effects/orb_underside.txt.

1.  The Six Beams

   Six two-vertex blocks in the executable, 28 bytes each, back to
   back from g_BeamGeomHullToSpike [verified]. Each is a longword
   nverts-1 then two twelve-byte vertices, in the scene's own
   (horizontal, depth, vertical) order:

   +---+--------------------------+--------------------------+
   | # | from                     | to                       |
   +===+==========================+==========================+
   | 0 | (0, 66560, -43520)       | (0, 43520, 30720)        |
   | 1 | (57600, -33280, -43520)  | (37632, -21760, 30720)   |
   | 2 | (-57600, -33280, -43520) | (-37632, -21760, 30720)  |
   | 3 | (0, 43520, 30720)        | (0, 0, 51200)            |
   | 4 | (37632, -21760, 30720)   | (0, 0, 51200)            |
   | 5 | (-37632, -21760, 30720)  | (0, 0, 51200)            |
   +---+--------------------------+--------------------------+

   The first three leave the saucer's rim - vertical -43520, on a
   radius of 66560 at 0, 120 and 240 degrees - and land on the small
   disc's three spikes at vertical 30720. The second three run from
   those same three points to one place at (0, 0, 51200), which is
   where the orb forms in the claw. THE SECOND GROUP'S START POINTS
   ARE THE FIRST GROUP'S ENDPOINTS EXACTLY, so the charge visibly
   hands off rather than starting again.

   A beam is a scene object of type 2 - the type word is at the
   slot's +0x2c - and Scene_SortAndDrawAll projects its two vertices
   through Scene_TransformProject like any hull vertex, rejecting one
   that will not project by writing 0x7fffffff [verified,
   0x002d29b6]. Each slot's +0x34 points at the longword holding that
   beam's strength: g_BeamPhaseA for the first three, g_BeamPhaseB
   for the second three.

   THE PROJECTION IS IN WHOLE PIXELS, not a fixed point fraction. The
   divide is by comp1 plus 500 and the screen centres added after it
   are 0x50 and 0x32 - 80 and 50, the middle of the 160x100 frame
   [verified, 0x002d2a18].

2.  The Three Stages

   Beam_Draw tests the strength against 0x20 and does one of two
   things, but it draws the trail before it looks at all [verified,
   0x002ca800]. So the stages overlap:

       strength        what is drawn
       below 0x10      the trail alone, so an ungrown beam is dark
       0x10 to 0x1f    the trail and a travelling head
       0x20 and over   the trail and a struck bolt

   g_BeamPhaseA is seeded -0x32 and climbs one a tick, so the first
   three beams stay dark for 50 ticks, grow over the next 16, and
   strike on the 67th. g_BeamPhaseB is seeded 0 when its phase
   starts. NEITHER IS CLAMPED - 0x20 is only the threshold tested
   here, and the counters climb past it; the demo reads 88 and 34
   where both are long since struck [observed, frame 348].

   ONE PER VERTICAL BLANK, NOT ONE PER DRAWN FRAME. Anim_SceneTick
   adds one to both strengths every tick [verified, 0x002d5f14], and
   the demo renders every second tick - so the head crosses in 16
   blanks, 0.32 s of a PAL frame, moving two sixteenths between the
   frames that are actually shown. Checked live: g_BeamPhaseA read 1
   at frame 261 and 24 at frame 284, exactly 23 over 23 [observed,
   on hardware]. A reconstruction that steps state at 50 Hz and draws
   every one of those steps gets the same duration and a smoother
   head, which is the intended enhancement and not a speed change.

2.1.  The Travelling Head

   Beam_DrawGrowing walks a point from the beam's origin out to
   (strength - 0x10)/16 of the way along, and draws ONE BIG GLOW
   there - radius 0x1e plus a random ten, so thirty to forty pixels
   on a hundred-row frame [verified, 0x002ca9a0]. There is no line
   behind it and nothing joins the two ends.

   THAT IS WHY A BEAM DOES NOT SWITCH ON. A glow head runs out along
   where the bolt will be, and only when it arrives does the
   lightning appear.

   The glow is Draw_FlareTurbulent, NOT Draw_GlowBlob - section 3.

2.2.  The Trail

   The head seeds a particle at its own position with its own radius
   every tick, so the trail is as wide as the head. Particle_Spawn
   takes the first free of 51 eight-byte slots at the object's +0x48
   and writes {life 0xa, x, y, size}; Particle_UpdateAndDraw walks 50
   of them, subtracts two from each life and draws it with
   Draw_GlowBlob and g_RampSpark [verified].

   So five are alive at once and they fade over five ticks. Because
   Beam_Draw runs the trail unconditionally, the sparks the head shed
   on its way out are still fading after the bolt has landed.

2.3.  The Struck Bolt

   Beam_BuildBoltPath lays down eight nodes between the two ends and
   Beam_Draw joins them with nine Draw_LineAA segments - eight to the
   nodes and one more to the real endpoint, because the eighth node
   stops short of it [verified, 0x002ca800].

   THERE IS NO RANDOM NUMBER IN THE BOLT'S SHAPE. Two sine lookups
   displace each node, one walking the table at half the rate of the
   march along the line and one at an eighth, so they never come back
   into step and the result reads as irregular rather than as a wave.
   Both are scaled by the line's own pixel length, so a bolt keeps
   its shape at any distance instead of flattening. g_BoltSeed and
   g_BoltPhase advance two and 200 a tick, which is all the motion
   there is.

   Each node also gets a Draw_GlowBlob whose radius falls out of
   (0x10000 - d/2) scaled by the line length, with four as the floor
   [verified, 0x002ca9e4]. FOR EVERY BEAM IN THIS SCENE THE FLOOR
   WINS: the formula only exceeds four for a line over about 130
   pixels, and none of the six is that long on screen.

   AND AT FOUR THE HALO IS INVISIBLE. The polar table's distance
   starts at 3 rather than 0 (section 5), so a radius-4 blob reads
   g_BlobFalloff[4 << 8 | 3] = 192 at its centre and 255 one pixel
   out. Through the bolt's linear ramp that is 0x1f under a line
   which has already written 0x3f, and nothing at all beyond. So a
   STRUCK BOLT IS A BARE THREE-PIXEL ANTIALIASED LINE and all of the
   effect's width comes from the trail and the head. A reconstruction
   that draws visible halos there is drawing something the demo does
   not.

3.  The Turbulence

   Draw_FlareTurbulent is the head's glow and is a different routine
   from Draw_GlowBlob [verified, 0x002d466c]. The circle is the same
   - the span half width out of g_pSpanHalfWidth, the falloff out of
   g_BlobFalloff[radius << 8 | dist] - but the ramp index carries two
   more reads:

       i  = g_BlobFalloff[radius << 8 | dist]
       i += (*g_pTurbulenceA)[dist]
       i += (*g_pTurbulenceB)[dist + (angle >> 2)]
       LIGHT += g_RampBeamHead[i]

   Turbulence_BuildTable builds the one table both cursors read: 1024
   words of a random term four times as wide as either sine, added to
   two sine walks taken at different rates and shifted down ten
   [verified, 0x002cc4bc]. Structure at two scales with grain on top.

   ONLY 512 WORDS ARE EVER A CURSOR and the table is twice that long.
   A read reaches dist + angle/4 words past the cursor, up to 318, so
   the second half is the overrun a wrap would otherwise fold.
   Nothing masks the read index.

   Anim_SceneTick walks the two cursors OPPOSITE WAYS AND AT
   DIFFERENT SPEEDS - B back two words, A forward one - so one table
   read twice never repeats its pairing until both wrap [verified,
   0x002d5f28]. That is the whole of the churn.

   TWO THINGS ABOUT THE ADD. It goes into byte +2, the LIGHT
   accumulator, and leaves R, G and B exactly as they were. And it is
   a plain byte add, NOT taken through g_LightSatLut the way
   Draw_GlowBlob's is - so the head alone can push LIGHT past 0x3f
   and Ham8_BuildTables has the levels to show it. The demo reads
   0x4b where a saturating draw would sit flat at 0x3f [observed,
   on hardware at frame 285].

   The ramp is indexed by a SIGNED word and the turbulence walks off
   both of its ends on purpose. Read live around g_RampBeamHead: the
   128 bytes below hold 0x3f, its own plateau value, so turbulence
   toward the centre cannot overbrighten; the 256 above hold zero, so
   turbulence at the rim cuts the glow off hard.

4.  The Attach Points

   Seven places a beam ends, twelve bytes each at g_BeamAttachPoints,
   the array running right up to the beam geometry [verified]: the
   three saucer-rim points, the three spikes, and the one in the claw
   where the orb forms. They are the beams' own endpoints, so nothing
   has to be kept in step.

   SIX OF THEM GET A PULSING FLARE and that is what phase 3 adds.
   Space_BuildObjects gives each a TYPE 1 scene slot - 25 to 27 the
   rim, 31 to 33 the spikes, with three meshes in the slots between -
   and phase 3 reveals all nine the tick after the first group
   strikes. So the attach points light up WITH the strike rather than
   easing in. The claw point waits for the second group.

   Scene_SortAndDrawAll's type 1 branch draws one [verified,
   0x002d29ea]:

       radius = table[g_FlareSizeStep++ & 7] * 2048 / depth

   clamped to 0x7f, through Draw_Flare with g_pRaySpikes and
   g_ColRampAmber - so it is a coloured flare WITH rays, added to all
   four channels, not a light-only glow like the rest of the beam.
   Being divided by the projection's own depth, it shrinks with
   distance.

   THE PULSE IS A TABLE, NOT A WAVEFORM, and the rim and the spikes
   are given different ones:

       rim     240 320 160 120 160 112 400 160
       spike   176 160 208 160 176 208 144 192

   g_FlareSizeStep advances once per flare DRAWN and the index is
   masked to 7. With six live the pattern rotates by six a frame, so
   no two neighbours flicker together and none of them repeats on a
   short cycle.

5.  The Polar Table Is A Closed Form

   g_PolarTable's two bytes are, for every one of its 65536 entries
   [verified, checked entry by entry against the image]:

       low  byte = isqrt(dx*dx + dy*dy) + 3, clamped to 255
       high byte = atan2(dy, dx) * 128 / pi, to within one

   THE +3 IS A FLOOR, not an offset to undo. dist(0, 0) is 3, so
   g_BlobFalloff[r << 8 | 3] is the brightest a blob's centre ever
   gets, and at small radii that is most of the way down the ramp -
   which is why the bolt's node halos do not show (section 2.3).

   BEING CLOSED FORM IS WHAT LETS THE RECONSTRUCTION SCALE. Indexed
   in whole demo units the falloff steps once every `scale` pixels
   and a blob comes out as a staircase of square blocks. Computing
   the same function with four fraction bits and interpolating
   g_BlobFalloff between its entries removes that, and it is
   resampling the demo's own data rather than inventing a smoother
   curve - reconstruction.txt 2. Scale 1 discards the fraction and is
   the table lookup exactly, byte for byte over 301 frames.

6.  The Orb Before It Forms

   The claw attach point is not given a flare like the other six: it
   is given a TYPE 3 slot, which is the orb, and Space_BuildObjects
   builds it immediately after the second beam group [verified,
   0x002d11f0]. It is the last slot of all, so PHASE 6 reveals it -
   measured at frame 352, tick 343 [observed, on hardware].

   While g_OrbNearMode is zero the type 3 branch draws the orb as TEN
   GLOW BLOBS ON A SPHERE about that point [verified, 0x002d2b3c]:

       p = (0x2800, 0, 0) rotated by (A + i*0x333, B + i*0x514)
       drawn at the claw point + p, size 0x1e, with g_RampBolt

   The two base angles are g_OrbSpinA and g_OrbSpinB, which
   Anim_SceneTick advances 0x64 and 0xa a tick right beside the beam
   strengths [verified, 0x002d5f00] - so the satellites turn on the
   same clock as everything else in the scene. BECAUSE EACH SATELLITE
   TAKES ITS OWN PAIR OF ANGLES, and the two steps are coprime with
   neither period, they do not sit on a ring: they scatter over the
   sphere and the pattern never repeats within the scene.

   Then Orb_DrawCentreFlare puts THE BIG PULSING LIGHT at the centre.
   It is the attach points' effect exactly - the same
   g_FlareSizeStep counter masked to 7, the same g_pRaySpikes and
   g_ColRampAmber - with its own and much larger table:

       orb     400 480 320 440 360 400 480 360

   against the rim's 240..400 and the spikes' 144..208. So the claw
   holds the brightest pulse in the scene, beating in step with the
   six on the arcs.

   THE RADIUS IS PERSPECTIVE, not a constant. Scene_ProjectSized
   scales the size by the same depth it divided the position by -
   (size << 11) / depth, clamped at 0x7f - which is how every type 1
   and type 3 glow in the scene is sized [verified, 0x002d2cb2].

7.  One Sort Over Everything

   Scene_SortAndDrawAll keys EVERY live slot and sorts the lot
   together [verified, 0x002d28ca]: the hull parts, the beams, the
   attach flares and the orb. The key is the slot's own centroid taken
   through the object angle and both camera angles, keeping only the
   depth, and it is NOT translated by the camera.

   THAT IS WHY THE ORB LOOKS HELD INSIDE THE CLAWS. There is no depth
   buffer, so order is everything: a claw part nearer than the claw
   centre paints over the glow behind it, and the orb reads as being
   inside the cage and showing between its arms. Drawing the glow
   after the ship instead puts all of it on top, and the orb then
   looks painted onto the front of the claws - which is the single
   most visible difference the ordering makes.

   A beam's centroid is the MEAN of its vertices, Obj_SetCentroid
   writing it [verified, 0x002cfeba]; a flare's and the orb's are the
   point itself, Space_BuildObjects writing those straight into the
   slot without calling it.

   SO THE INTERLEAVING IS PER OBJECT AND COSTS NOTHING. There is no
   per-triangle sorting anywhere, and none is needed: a claw is its own
   object and a beam is its own object, so one key each is enough to
   put a near claw over a far beam. What it CANNOT do is split one
   object across another - a beam that runs from outside the cage to
   its centre is wholly in front of a given claw or wholly behind it.
   At 160 by 100, with claw arms a few pixels wide, that is not
   visible.

7.1.  Why A Claw Is Keyed From Its Unbent Pose

   A claw is centroided ONCE, by Mesh_InstanceAtAngle when
   Space_BuildObjects instances it, from the UNBENT arm at its hub
   angle - and Strut_SetClawPose re-poses all six every tick and never
   centroids again [verified, 0x002cffea]. The key is therefore the
   extended arm's and does not follow the claws as they open.

   THAT IS WHAT MAKES THE LOWER ARCS LAYER. The six centroids sit on a
   circle of radius 16912 at c2 38059, sixty degrees apart [observed,
   on hardware], and their keys spread about 1700 -
   wide enough for a beam's key to fall between them.

   TAKING IT OFF THE POSED ARM INSTEAD IS THE TRAP. Closed, the six
   collapse to within 2230 of the hub axis, their keys land within 219
   of each other, the sort cannot separate them, and every claw paints
   over every beam - the three lower arcs vanish behind the whole cage
   while the upper three, whose keys are far outside that band, are
   unaffected. The symptom is one arc looking right and two looking
   wrong, which reads as a beam problem and is a centroid one.

8.  The Claws Change Texture

   Strut_RetextureToTex3 re-runs the UV generation for all six claws
   with pass {mode 1, scale 0x96, 0xc0 by 0x40} off the REST pose, the
   slot's +0x4 pointed at +0x8 for the duration and put back after
   [verified, 0x002cf550].

   EVERY ONE OF THOSE IS WHAT THE CLAWS ALREADY HAD. Same pass word,
   same scale, same extents, same rest-pose source. THE ONLY THING
   THAT DIFFERS IS THE TEXTURE - g_ShipTexSlots[3] in place of [2] -
   so nothing blends and nothing eases: one tick has the old texture
   and the next has the new.

   IT HAPPENS AT THE PHASE 5 TO 6 BOUNDARY [verified, 0x002cc7e0], the
   same tick g_SceneObjectCount goes to 38 and the orb appears. The
   claws change colour exactly as the orb arrives, which is why the
   two read as one event.

   g_ShipTexSlots is [count, slot0..slot3] - the first longword is the
   count, 4, and NOT a slot - and the sub-blocks are 0x40000, 0x40000,
   0x20000 and 0x20000 of Fshiptextures, so [3] is the chunk's last
   0x20000 at offset 0xa0000 [verified].

9.  The Shell Around Each Arc

   THE THREE MESHES PHASE 3 ADDS ARE SHELLS AROUND THE THREE ARCS -
   a faint blue transparent skin, spiked along the same path the arc
   takes, that the lightning wriggles inside. They are Fspacedata
   objects 5, 6 and 7, built from slot triples 15/16/17, 18/19/20 and
   21/22/23 [verified, 0x002d1010 onward].

   WHAT MAKES THEM TRANSPARENT IS A REWRITTEN MODE WORD.
   Mesh_MakeAdditiveEnvelope - which an earlier pass named
   Uv_WriteFlatTable - stores 1 into every face of each of the three
   and copies a 24-byte record into the slot's UV buffer [verified,
   0x002d155c]. Mode 1 is Rast_AdditivePoly, so these three objects
   are the only additive geometry in the scene; everything else is
   mode 2 and textured. formats/geometry.txt 3.1.

   The 24 bytes are six longwords, (127, 0, 0, 0, 64, 127), which
   Poly_DrawFaceList loads into A0..A5 as three pattern-space (u, v)
   pairs - (127,0), (0,0), (64,127) - THE SAME FOR EVERY FACE.

   Rast_AdditivePoly interpolates that coordinate across the triangle
   and per pixel [verified, 0x002cb3aa]:

       i  = g_pFlatPattern[(uv + g_FlatScroll)          & 0x7fff]
       i += g_pFlatPattern[(uv + 2 * g_FlatScroll) + 0x7f & 0x7fff]
       *(u32 *)pixel += g_pFlatPalette[i]

   TWO TAPS OF ONE PATTERN, 0x7f and a scroll apart, summed into a
   256-entry palette of whole four-byte pixels which is then ADDED. So
   it adds to R, G, LIGHT and B together - the only thing in the scene
   that does; every glow here touches byte +2 alone.

   AND THE SHELL FLOWS BECAUSE THE PATTERN SCROLLS. g_FlatScroll is
   0x002e0afc and anim mode 0x12 takes 0x100 off it a tick [verified,
   0x002d6008]. MODE 0x13 TAKES THE SAME 0x100 OFF IT - measured
   0x8200 then 0x8000 two frames apart [observed, on hardware] - so
   the envelope flows in BOTH shots. MODE 6 IS THE ONE THAT DOES NOT
   TOUCH IT: hardware reads 0x5900 and still 0x5900 four frames
   later, so the value is static for the whole of phase 1 and where in
   phase 1 a reading is taken does not matter.

   That makes 0x5900 the seed for the whole of 2.10, and stepping it
   over phase 2 to 6's 280 ticks lands on 0x4100 - within two ticks of
   the 0x3f00 measured independently for 2.11, so the scroll runs
   continuously across the cut.

   BOTH TAPS MOVE, AND BY DIFFERENT AMOUNTS: the first is at
   (uv + scroll) and the second a further scroll beyond it, so two
   sampling points slide through the 32768-byte table at different
   rates. That is what makes the surface flow rather than sit, and it
   is finer than the HAM8 result can show.

   g_pFlatPattern is Fspacedata slot 65 at 32768 bytes and
   g_pFlatPalette slot 66 at 1024 - 256 four-byte pixels. THE PALETTE
   IS A BLUE RAMP WITH R ALWAYS ZERO [verified], which is the glass
   look: its low entries are nearly black with a little blue, and it
   climbs to G 0xc6, LIGHT 0xc7, B 0xfe at the top.

   EACH SHELL IS THREE FACES AND FOUR VERTICES [verified] - a thin
   spike laid along its arc, not a tube.

   THE ACCUMULATOR PACKING. The original carries its two coordinates
   across two registers so one add steps both [verified, 0x002cb622]:
   D7's low word holds v as 8.8 and its high word u's fraction, D2's
   low word holds u's integer, and addx.w adds u's step plus the carry
   out of the v add. The index takes its high byte from D7's low word
   - bits 8 to 15 of v, so v's integer - and its low byte from D2:

       index = ((v_int & 0xff) << 8 | (u_int & 0xff)) & 0x7fff

   so v's bit 7 falls off in the mask, which is what makes the pattern
   128 rows of 256 rather than 256 of 256.

   AND THE GRADIENTS ARE WHOLE-UNIT, which the reciprocal table
   settles: g_pRecip holds 0x8000 / n [verified - 1638 times 20 and
   1560 times 21 are both 32760]. After the multiply, the doubling and
   the swap, dudx is du / width in whole units; dvdx takes asr #7
   instead and is dv * 256 / width in 8.8. One affine pair per
   triangle, computed at the knee, not per span.

   src/rast.c's Rast_AddTri carries the whole of that shading path:
   the geometry, both sub-blocks, the coordinate template, the two
   taps, the scroll, the wrapping byte sum, the palette and these
   gradients.

   THE DRAW ORDER IS NOT THE PROBLEM, which took measuring both sides
   to establish. The demo's three shells are slots 29, 30 and 31 and
   they sort INTERLEAVED THROUGH THE HULL - keys 2885, 1604 and -313
   against a hull spanning 8591 down past -313 - so hull parts drawn
   after them paint over them [observed, on hardware, read out of
   the sort list at g_SceneSortList]. Ours do the same: keys
   1603, 1600 and 1589 at positions 5, 6 and 7 of the list, with more
   than twenty hull parts drawn afterwards.

   READING THAT LIST NEEDS THE RIGHT BASE, and the two bias-forced
   hull pieces are what anchor it: slots 0 and 1 carry the subtractions
   that push them to the far end, so they are always the list's first
   two entries. The list itself is the slot array plus 0xcd0.

   THE RASTERISER IS RIGHT AND THE COUNT IS WRONG, which per-triangle
   coverage separates. Instrumented, the port calls Rast_AddTri NINE
   times and writes 864 pixels; the demo reaches Rast_AdditivePoly's
   span build THREE times and leaves 249 blue-shifted pixels. Per
   triangle that is 96 against 83 [observed, on hardware] -
   close enough that the span walk, the coordinate and the palette are
   all behaving. The whole difference is that nine triangles are drawn
   where three should be.

   AND THE SIX ARE REJECTED AS OFF-FRAME, NOT AS FACES. Each shell
   reports three faces - its count word reads 2 - and all three carry
   mode 1, so Mesh_MakeAdditiveEnvelope ran and nothing drops them at
   the face level. What drops them is Rast_AdditivePoly's own early
   outs, which reject a triangle whose x or y extent lies wholly
   outside the frame, and those sit BEFORE the scratch word a watch can
   see. So the earlier count of three was counting survivors, not
   dispatches.

   THE PROJECTION IS RIGHT AND SO IS THE REJECTION, which comparing
   the corners settled. For the shell on object 6 the demo's four
   project to (80,-77), (47,-60), (69,-54) and (77,-2) and ours to
   (80,-78), (46,-61), (69,-55) and (78,-3) - a pixel apart at worst
   [observed, on hardware].

   EVERY y IS NEGATIVE: that shell sits entirely above the frame, so
   Rast_AdditivePoly's max-y-below-zero test drops all three of its
   faces. Object 7 lands at x 173 to 189, wholly off the right edge,
   and goes the same way. ONLY OBJECT 5 IS ON SCREEN, and its three
   faces are the demo's three survivors. The port rejects the same six
   - add_span clips them - so both sides draw the same three triangles
   from the same object.

   SO THE EARLIER 249 AGAINST 1456 WAS NOT COVERAGE. It counted
   blue-shifted pixels over a brightness threshold, and the demo's
   shell is faint enough that most of its pixels fell under it. Both
   sides cover the same area.

   THE PATTERN IS A SMOOTH TWO-DIMENSIONAL FIELD, checked along both
   axes [verified]: neighbouring bytes differ by a few, and so do bytes
   256 apart. So a SMALL index step reads a smooth ramp and a step of
   hundreds reads unrelated values. That is what separates a shell that
   shimmers from one that speckles.

   THE RATE, FROM THE DEMO'S OWN FORMULA, for the three faces of the
   one shell that is on screen - recip(n) being 0x8000/n:

       face   dudx (whole)   dvdx (8.8)   index step per pixel
       0                 6         0.11                      6
       1                 1        -7.05                  -1791
       2                -6       -25.40                  -6662

   And the port measured on one row: the first span steps u by -8 and v
   by nothing, so its index steps -8 - which matches face 0; the second
   steps u by -2 and v by SEVEN, so its index steps +1790 - which
   matches face 1 [observed, on hardware].

   SO BOTH SIDES AGREE ON THE RATES, and that is the problem. By the
   demo's own arithmetic two of the three faces advance v about seven
   whole rows a pixel, which on this pattern can only read as noise -
   yet the demo's output over that box is a smooth 0x0a to 0x3e.

   THE RATES, OFF THE HARDWARE. Halting at 0x002cb4c6, where the
   gradients are stored, the registers hold dudx = -3 in its low word
   and dvdx = 68 [observed, on hardware] - so v advances
   68/256, A QUARTER OF A ROW A PIXEL, and the index creeps. Not the
   seven rows a pixel the earlier reading gave.

   AND THE FORMULA IS CONFIRMED by feeding it the same registers'
   inputs - dyTotal 116, dyMid 11, width 41, and the SORTED corners'
   coordinates (0,0), (127,0), (64,127):

       t    = (dyMid * recip(dyTotal)) >> 7                    = 24
       dudx = (dun * recip(width) * 2) >> 16                   = -3
       dvdx = (dvn * recip(width)) >> 7                        = 68

   both exact [verified]. The earlier derivation failed only on which
   sorted corner carries which coordinate; the hardware settles that
   too.

   SO THE GRADIENTS ARE ONE AFFINE PAIR PER TRIANGLE, taken at the
   knee, u in whole units and v in 8.8 - not a per-span division, which
   is what src/rast.c had and what made v advance whole rows.
   Rast_AddTri now computes them the demo's way.

   THE SPAN LOOP ITSELF IS CONFIRMED. Halted at 0x002cb622 the
   registers read D5 low word 0xfff9 and D6 low word 0xffe3, so that
   span advanced u by -7 whole units and v by -29 in 8.8 - an eighth of
   a row across the whole span. D7's low word held 0x0296 and D2's low
   byte 0x7d, giving index 0x027d, which is the (v << 8) | u packing
   this note already carries. A6 read 0x640eb6 and A0 0x648eb6, so the
   palette is the pattern plus 0x8000 and the two sub-blocks are
   adjacent in the chunk [observed, on hardware].

   AND THE GRADIENT IS SMALL ONLY FOR SOME ORDERINGS. dv_num is
   ((dv2 * t) >> 8) - dv1 with t in 0..0xff, so the first term is
   always small and dv_num is about -dv1. The template gives two
   corners v=0 and one v=127, so dv1 is zero - and the gradient a
   fraction of a row - exactly when the v=127 corner sorts LAST in y,
   and 127 when it sorts into the middle. The demo's sampled triangle
   sorted to (0,0) (127,0) (64,127) and gave -29; ours sort both ways
   and the middle ones give thousands.

   THE UV BLOCK IS PER FACE.
   Mesh_MakeAdditiveEnvelope walks a table at 0x002d1590 with its
   source cursor advancing - six move.l (A4)+,(A3)+ inside the dbf -
   so face n takes the n'th 24-byte block [verified, 0x002d157c]. The
   three blocks are permutations of one triple:

       face 0    (127,0)  (0,0)    (64,127)
       face 1    (127,0)  (64,127) (0,0)
       face 2    (64,127) (0,0)    (127,0)

   The table is exactly three blocks; 0x002d15d8 starts an unrelated
   one of screen corners. Because the numbers are the same three pairs
   in every block, using block 0 for all three faces looks harmless -
   and is not. It puts the v=127 corner in the MIDDLE of the y sort on
   two faces of every three, where dv1 is 127 instead of 0, and the v
   gradient comes out two orders of magnitude too steep.

   THE RECIPROCAL IS SIGNED AND 0x002f111c IS A POINTER. The access is
   muls.w ([0x2f111c,-,D6w*0x2]) and extension word 0x63b5 carries
   I/IS=101, memory indirect POST-indexed: the longword at 0x002f111c
   is fetched first and the index added to what it points at. Read as
   a table base the entries are the zeros that pointer holds until the
   run fills it. The table it points at is 0x7fff / n and runs both
   ways - entry -18 is 0xf8e4, -1820 - so a negative width yields a
   negative reciprocal, and taking abs(width) flips the shading of
   every leftward span.

   WITH ALL THREE IN PLACE THE GRADIENTS MATCH THE HARDWARE at a
   matched tick 510 [observed, on hardware]:

       +-----------------+-------------+-------------+
       | dyt / dym / w   | demo        | port        |
       +=================+=============+=============+
       | 50 /  1 / -18   | -7 /   -29  | -7 /   -29  |
       | 50 / 14 / -17   | -7 /  -527  | -7 /  -470  |
       +-----------------+-------------+-------------+

   The second row's inputs differ by the one frame between the two

   [open] THE PORT RASTERISES THREE ADDITIVE TRIANGLES WHERE THE DEMO
   RASTERISES TWO. The demo's third face projects two corners onto the
   same column, x 64 twice, so the width subtraction at 0x002cb48a

   the demo's (41,66) (59,67) (64,115): x agrees but for that one

   gates the third triangle and belongs with the camera, not here.

   THE PER-PIXEL ADD IS ONE LONGWORD. add.l D4,(A5)+ at 0x002cb63e
   adds the palette entry to the whole four-byte pixel at once, so a
   channel carries into the next and nothing clamps or saturates
   [verified]. The port adds per byte and clamps to its own 6-bit
   channel, which is a divergence and the reason it cannot wrap the
   way the demo can.

   THE PATTERN SUB-BLOCK THE PORT READS IS FOUR TIMES THE GUEST'S.
   Sixteen consecutive bytes from relic.dat read

       16 0e 0d 16 1b 16 24 2e 37 35 27 27 51 96 ad b2

   and the guest's pattern at A6 holds

       05 03 03 05 06 05 09 0b 0d 0d 09 09 14 25 2b 2c

   which is the first list shifted right by two, exactly, on all
   sixteen [verified live, on hardware]. Left unshifted every index is
   four times too large, so both of Rast_AddTri's taps land on palette
   entries far brighter than the demo's.

   BOTH BLOCKS ARE CONDITIONED IN PLACE BY Flat_ConditionTables
   [verified, 0x002cff42], which Space_LoadAssets runs once behind the
   flag at 0x002e0c84. Neither conditioned form appears anywhere in
   relic.dat, so searching the file for the bytes the guest holds finds
   nothing and reads as the blocks being built from scratch; they are
   not, they are these two sweeps over the loaded chunk.

   The pattern is one byte at a time, 0x8001 of them:

       pattern[i] = file[i] >> 2

   The count is one past the 32768-byte block, so the palette's first
   byte is shifted too; the palette's own pass discards that byte.

   The palette is 193 longwords, NOT 256, and it is a permutation as
   well as a shift - lsl.l #8 then lsr.w #8 drops byte 0 and leaves a
   ZERO in byte 2:

       entry = ((b1 & 0xf8) >> 2, (b2 & 0xf8) >> 2, 0, (b3 & 0xf8) >> 2)

   SO THE CONDITIONED PALETTE CARRIES NO LIGHT AT ALL - byte 2 is
   always zero and the envelope adds colour only, never a glow. The
   mask keeps five bits of each byte before the shift, and indices
   never reach the unconditioned tail above 192 because the two taps
   sum to at most 126.

   Verified byte for byte against the guest [observed, on hardware]:

       +-------+----------------+----------------+
       | entry | file bytes     | conditioned    |
       +=======+================+================+
       |    33 | 00 10 11 19    | 04 04 00 06    |
       |    34 | 00 03 0a 30    | 00 02 00 0c    |
       |    35 | 00 04 08 32    | 00 02 00 0c    |
       |    36 | 00 0d 10 23    | 02 04 00 08    |
       +-------+----------------+----------------+

   and the guest holds exactly the right-hand column. src/geom.c keeps
   its own conditioned copies rather than writing to the mapped image.

   THE ENVELOPE'S ADD IS RIGHT UNSCALED, and the earlier claim that
   the background behind it was two to four times too bright was an
   artefact of scoring a PPM against a raw capture - across the HAM8
   resolve. Resolving the demo's side first, over the 2306 pixels the

       | port, envelope off  |  37.6 |  54.1 |  76.0 |

   [observed]. The port's background is about HALF the demo's, not

   The shells are DRAWN ONLY WHEN Ship_SetAdditiveLit IS SET, which no

   TREATING THE TEMPLATE AS 8.8 RATHER THAN WHOLE UNITS makes it

10.  Measuring A Glow

8.  Measuring A Glow

   READ THE BACK BUFFER, NOT THE FRONT. The HAM8 resolve CONSUMES byte
   +2 in place: the front buffer is post-resolve, with final RGB and
   LIGHT zeroed, and the back buffer still holds the raw channels.
   Measured on one frame of the underside shot - the front has not one
   of 16000 pixels with a non-zero byte +2, and the back has 2507, to
   a maximum of 60 [observed, on hardware].

   SO A FRONT-BUFFER CAPTURE PROVES NOTHING ABOUT A GLOW, and it reads
   as the demo simply not drawing one. It also mismatches on RGB,
   because relic_ppm's raw output is pre-resolve and the front buffer
   is not. Any LIGHT agreement scored against a front buffer came from
   a frame caught before the resolve reached it.

   A CAPTURE THAT DROPS BYTE +2 SHOWS NO GLOW AT ALL. Every glow in
   the demo - this flare, the bolt lines, the sparks - adds only to
   LIGHT, so a comparison made on R, G and B cannot see any of them,
   and two frames can agree closely while one draws a flare the other
   has never heard of.

   So amiga_fb's ppm is for geometry and its raw is for glow, and
   tools/amiga/fbcmp.py scores LIGHT separately and says so when it
   has not been given it.

   TWO OTHER WAYS THE MEASUREMENT LIES:

   The demo renders every second VBL, so stopping the emulator on the
   wrong parity catches the front buffer part drawn. It reads as a
   plausible dark frame rather than as an error - check a neighbouring
   frame before believing a dark one.

   The live offset is per HUNK, not per run. g_BlobFalloff and
   g_PolarTable are in CODE_00 and almost everything else here is in
   CODE_03, so one derived offset does not cover both; applying
   CODE_03's to a CODE_00 address returns plausible garbage. Both of
   those tables are load-time data anyway and are better read out of
   the image.

11.  The Centre Flare, Its Ghosts, And The Horizon Band

   ONE MISSING SLOT ACCOUNTS FOR THREE MISSING THINGS. The demo's
   2.10 and 2.11 show a flare at the orb, a row of ghost discs
   running out of it, and a bright horizontal band across the whole
   frame. We draw none of them, and all three are Orb_DrawCentreFlare
   at 0x002d2c00 [verified].

   IT IS A SCENE SLOT, NOT A RENDER STEP, which is why it is easy to
   miss: Scene_RenderFrame (0x002cd892) is only Scene_DrawTexturedSky,
   Scene_SortAndDrawAll, a call to 0x002ce81c that is two bytes of
   rts, and Scene_DrawHorizonBand. The flare is reached from the
   draw-kind dispatch INSIDE the sort, at 0x002d2b9c, so it lands in
   depth order with the hull and the beams.

11.1.  What Orb_DrawCentreFlare does

   1.  The orb's world position, three longwords at 0x002ee894. The
       third is g_OrbDrift, so it is not at the origin.

   2.  A base size from g_OrbFlareSizes, eight longwords at
       0x002ee9e8 indexed by (0x002e0b08) & 7 [verified]:

           0x190  0x1e0  0x140  0x1b8  0x168  0x190  0x1e0  0x168
            400    480    320    440    360    400    480    360

       so the flare breathes on an eight frame cycle.

   3.  The projection at 0x002d2c7a, which is Scene_TransformProject's
       with two differences: THE NEAR PLANE IS 0x32, not 0xc8, and it
       carries the size through the same divide.

           depth  = y + 0x1f4,  and nothing is drawn at depth <= 0x32
           sx     = (x << 8) / depth + 0x50
           sy     = (z << 8) / depth + 0x32
           radius = (size << 11) / depth,  clamped to 0x7f

       That radius form is the one src/part_release.c already uses.

   4.  sx and sy are kept at 0x002e0b3e and 0x002e0b42, and g_SunRange
       0x002e0b62 is FORCED TO 0x7f00. That matters: g_SunRange is
       what Flare_Draw gates and shifts on, so the orb borrows the
       sun's machinery by planting the sun's number.

   5.  Draw_Flare 0x002d4452 - which is already src/gfx.c's Gfx_Flare
       - with the ray table at *(0x002f1028) and the colour ramp at
       0x002ef430. THOSE RESOLVE TO THINGS WE ALREADY HAVE: the ramp
       is slot 2 of the block at 0x002eeaf0, g_ColRampAmber, and the
       table is g_RaySpikes - read live, its bumps are five positive
       entries shaped 1,3,6,3,1, where g_RayArcs is negated and reads
       back 0xffxx [observed].

   6.  Flare_Draw 0x002cf2aa, which is already Space_DrawFlareGhosts:
       the four ghosts reflected through (0x3c,0x3c) at -2, -1/2, -1
       and +2. This is the row of discs.

11.2.  The band is the flare's, not a horizon

   Step 4 above also writes the band's two inputs, and that is the
   whole of the coupling: g_HorizonBandRow 0x002e0bea takes sy, and
   g_HorizonBandTarget 0x002e0b6e takes (radius - 0x38) * 2 << 8.

   Scene_DrawHorizonBand 0x002d2530 then draws TEN ROWS OF 160,
   starting at g_HorizonBandRow - 7:

       g_HorizonBandOff 0x002e0bf8 non-zero  ->  nothing
       g_HorizonBandLevel 0x002e0b6a eased half way to the target
         each call, so the band lags the flare
       D5 = (level >> 8) - 0x64, negated when positive, >> 2, - 0x1e
       at or below -0x32 nothing is drawn - it fades, not switches
       per pixel: the polar table byte, & 0xff, >> 1, eor 0x3f - a
         horizontal falloff - plus D5; negatives skipped

   THE AMOUNT IS NOT A PLAIN ADD. 0x002d3d34 saturating-adds it to R,
   G and B at 0x3f and saturating-SUBTRACTS twice it from the light
   byte at 0, so the band brightens the colour and takes the additive
   glow back out. Ham8_BuildTables cannot then resolve the same glow
   twice.

   SO SECTION 7 IS WRONG WHERE IT SAYS THE BAND IS GATED OFF for this
   phase. It is not gated at all; it is dark in our port only because
   nothing sets its inputs.

11.5.  The pulse counter, and why a single frame cannot judge it

   g_FlareSizeStep 0x002e0b08 picks the entry of eight every flare in
   the scene is sized from, so its phase decides every radius at once.
   TWO KINDS READ IT AND ONLY ONE ADVANCES IT [verified]: a flare slot
   reads it and adds one at 0x002d2a4e, Orb_DrawCentreFlare reads it
   at 0x002d2c0a and leaves it, and Anim_SceneTick adds one a tick at
   0x002d6320 in the same breath as the object angle's 0x14. Both are
   now in src/orbscene.c; the centre flare advancing the counter was
   ours and is gone.

   A SINGLE FRAME CANNOT JUDGE THE PHASE, and that is worth knowing
   before anyone tries. Holding everything else still and shifting
   only the phase swings the whole-frame absolute error against the
   hardware from 763324 to 1513811 - a factor of two - so any one
   frame's score says more about where the counter happens to sit
   than about whether the code is right [observed]. The counter's own
   value is the measurement to use, not the pixels.

   IT IS TICK STATE AND NOT FRAME STATE, which matters more here than
   the phase does. The demo renders once a tick, so the slot advances
   and Anim_SceneTick's one happen together and the strobe is in demo
   time. In the port frame() and step() run at different rates, and a
   counter the DRAW carries advances per presented frame: the flicker
   then follows the display. Worse, splitting it - some advances in
   the draw and Anim_SceneTick's in the step - makes the step per
   frame alternate, and the strobe stutters, pausing and then
   rushing. So src/orbscene.c keeps the base in step(), seeds a
   running index from it in OrbScene_Register, and lets the draw only
   read: two frames inside one tick then draw the same sizes, which
   is what the scene state says they should.

   Driven at 17 ms a frame against 20 ms ticks the base goes 0, 7,
   14, 21, 28, 35, 35, 42 - seven a tick, and a repeat where the host
   outran the clock [observed].

   EXACTLY ONE FRAME A TICK MAY COUNT ITS SLOTS, and that is the
   second half of it. A tick can draw twice or not at all, so the
   step is armed and the first frame after it is the one whose slot
   count the next step folds in. Without the arm the first tick's
   advance depended on whether a frame or a step came first - one at
   20 ms a frame, seven at 17 - and six of the eight phases apart is
   a different flare size for every slot in the scene for the rest of
   the run. With it the base reads 2094 at tick 300 of 2.11 at 10,
   17, 20, 25 and 33 ms a frame alike [observed].

   AND THE RATES DO NOT MATCH, which is why no starting value would
   align them. Read off the hardware at the frame of 11.3 the counter
   runs 39 up over ten ticks, 3.9 a tick; ours runs seven - one for
   the tick and one for each of six attach points [observed]. So the
   demo draws about three flare slots a frame where we draw six, and
   the phases drift apart whatever they start at. The absolute values
   cannot be compared: the demo's counter is global and carries in
   from the scenes before.
