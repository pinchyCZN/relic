PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                       THE APPROACH, AND THE WARP


Abstract

   maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes (0x002cd102) is two scenes in one
   routine, and it is what SpaceModule_Part0 runs straight after the
   first logo. Scene one is the planet and sun again with the shadow
   gone and a light growing in front of them; scene two is the
   approach - an object arriving out of a fullscreen ripple, then the
   whole scene drawn together.

   Almost everything that puts pixels on the screen here is already
   in effects/planet_sun.txt. This note covers what is NEW: the
   sequencer's two scenes, the portal glow, the ripple warp and the
   sparks.

1.  Where It Runs

   SpaceModule_Part0 runs it fourth, after Space_RunSunScene and the
   first Logo_Run - see parts.txt section 3. The two scenes are
   timeline 2.3's second half and 2.4 to 2.5.

   NEITHER HANDOVER HERE IS A BLANK SCREEN, unlike the temple's and
   the stills module's - effects/temple.txt 11.9. Scene one DRAWS AND
   PRESENTS TWICE before it calls Space_DisplayOn 0x002d5410
   [verified, 0x002cd226 to 0x002cd23a], so its display comes up
   already carrying a frame, and the logo's copper list stays up
   until that moment. Measured either side: the sun scene's last
   present and the logo's list are two fields apart, and the logo's
   list and scene one's display-on are one [observed].

   Both scenes end on the music, not on a frame count: Music_ArmCue
   then a loop polling Music_PollCue. Scene one is cut by cues 4:0
   and 4:16, scene two by 8:0 [verified].

2.  Scene One: The Planet, Lit

   THE SAME BACKDROP AS 2.3 WITH THE SHADOW REMOVED [observed]. The
   frame routine is Space_DrawSunFrame (0x002cd838) and it is
   Space_SunSceneFrame step for step with one substitution
   [verified]:

   +----+--------------------------+----------------------------+
   | #  | Space_SunSceneFrame 2.3  | Space_DrawSunFrame         |
   +====+==========================+============================+
   | 1  | Scene_DrawTexturedSky    | Scene_DrawTexturedSky      |
   +----+--------------------------+----------------------------+
   | 2  | Scene_DrawShadowSweep    | maybe_Portal_DrawGlow      |
   +----+--------------------------+----------------------------+
   | 3  | Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun  | Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun    |
   +----+--------------------------+----------------------------+
   | 4  | Sprite_StampSaveUnder    | Sprite_StampSaveUnder      |
   +----+--------------------------+----------------------------+
   | 5  | Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo  | Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo    |
   +----+--------------------------+----------------------------+
   | 6  | -                        | maybe_Light_DrawIfOnScreen |
   +----+--------------------------+----------------------------+
   | 7  | Sprite_RestoreSaveUnder  | Sprite_RestoreSaveUnder    |
   +----+--------------------------+----------------------------+
   | 8  | Flare_Draw               | Flare_Draw                 |
   +----+--------------------------+----------------------------+

   So the shadow's slot carries the portal glow instead, and one step
   is added at 6. Neither routine sorts and draws the object list,
   even though the sequencer counts 22 objects.

   The setup, all [verified] from the sequencer:

       Ham8_BuildTables(0x3f, 0x3f, 0x3f)     base colour WHITE
       planet world      (-200, -50)          0x002e0c54 / 0x58
       sun world         (720, 66)             g_SunWorldX / Y
       g_SunRange        0x6ff9                and 0x002e0b66
       g_HorizonNow      0xb300                and g_HorizonTarget
       camera angle      0                     0x002e0ad8
       g_PortalPhase     0, then 2 for loop two
       g_PortalGlowX     -180                   0x002cd63e
       g_SceneObjectCount 0x16, g_SceneSortSkip 0

   THE BASE COLOUR IS THE DIFFERENCE THAT MATTERS. 2.3 passes
   (0,0,0) so its light byte darkens; here it is (0x3f,0x3f,0x3f) and
   the same byte brightens toward white - effects/planet_sun.txt
   section 1. The sun is also on the other side of the frame: at
   world x 720 against 2.3's -400.

   THE MOTION IS Anim_SceneTick MODE 2, and unlike 2.3's two
   instructions it is a dozen [verified]:

       FUN_002d68da()
       if g_LightProjX > -0x65 and 0x002e0b32 >= -0xaca:
           maybe_Camera_MoveRotated()
       unless g_LightProjX > -0x65 and 0x002e0b32 < -0xaca:
           0x002e0ad8  += 6000        the camera angle
       g_SunWorldX     += 3           the sun drifts 3, not 2
       0x002e0ae0      += 3           the light's position, and
       0x002e0ae4      += -6            which cell is which is not
       0x002e0ae8      += -5            established - see below
       FUN_002cec64()
       if g_PortalPhase != 0 and g_PortalPhase < 0xfe:
           g_PortalPhase += 2
           g_PortalGlowX += 2

   THE TWO SKIPS HAVE DIFFERENT TARGETS, and only one of them stops
   the sky. A g_LightProjX below -0x65 branches to the angle step, so
   the pan is skipped and the sky still turns. The yaw gate branches
   PAST the angle step, so once g_CameraYaw passes -0xaca both stop
   together and the camera is done for good - which is what an
   observer describes as the camera settling onto the light instead of
   tracking what it had been following [observed]. Treating either
   skip as both holds the sky still for ticks where the demo moves
   it.

   THE PAN TRACKS THE OBJECT'S PROJECTED X [verified]. Mode 2 reads
   it, biases it and hands the negated result to
   maybe_Camera_MoveRotated:

       D1 = 0                           the pitch delta, explicitly
       D2 = g_LightProjX + 0x64
       skip everything if D2 < 0
       skip it and the sky's angle step too if g_CameraYaw < -0xaca
       D0 = -(D2 >> 5)
       maybe_Camera_MoveRotated

   SO THE FURTHER RIGHT THE OBJECT HAS GOT, THE FASTER THE CAMERA
   YAWS THE OTHER WAY. The yaw runs one direction and accelerates -
   measured in the reconstruction as -3 at tick 1, -185 by 60 and
   -1592 by 180 - until it passes -0xaca and the gate stops it for
   good. The backdrop travels steadily left while the object crosses
   to the right and the camera never comes back, which is what a
   viewer sees.

   g_LightProjX IS 12.4 FIXED, so the >> 5 is a halving in pixels.
   The pitch delta is zero, which is why the backdrop only slides
   sideways.

   THE OBJECT COMES BACK LEFT AT THE END, which is the pan and the
   closing depth working against each other rather than anything
   reversing. Over the scene's last 26 ticks g_LightProjX falls from
   387 to 268, so the drawn x goes 104 to 96 and the object shrinks
   with it [observed, on hardware, one sample a tick]. The scene then
   hands over - effects/portal.txt section 1.

   THE OBJECT'S HEIGHT IS NOT THE PAN RATE, which a single
   measurement makes it look like: the step came out -5 with the
   height near 160 and 160 >> 5 is 5, and the projected x plus 0x64
   happened to sit in the same range. Taking the height gives a pan
   that REVERSES when the height passes zero, which the demo never
   does.

   THE PAN DRAGS THE SKY TOO, AND DOMINATES IT.
   maybe_Camera_MoveRotated accumulates a heading in g_CameraHeading
   and feeds the same deltas through it into g_CameraAngle, which is
   what the sky's pan reads [verified]:

       g_CameraHeading += (D0 * sin(-pitch)) >> 6
       D0 = (D0 * cos(-pitch)) >> 14
       h  = (g_CameraHeading >> 8) & 0x1fff
       g_CameraAngle  += ((D0*cos h) - (D1*sin h)) >> 1
       g_CameraAngle2 -= ((D0*sin h) + (D1*cos h)) >> 1

   ONLY AN asr #1 FOLLOWS THE HEADING ROTATION, not an asr #14, so
   the term runs to thousands a tick where mode 2's own step is
   0x1770 - and the other way. Part 2.3 has the step alone and its sky
   creeps one texel every eight frames; here the camera term swamps
   it and drags the starfield briskly the opposite way. A
   reconstruction that pans the sky from a formula in the tick gets
   2.3 right and this scene exactly backwards.

   AND THE CAMERA TURNS FAR ENOUGH TO REJECT THE BACKDROP. Once the
   rotated depth goes non-positive the sun and then the planet are
   behind the eye, and the demo draws neither - Scene_ProjectPlanet
   returns -1 and Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun just returns. A
   reconstruction that CLAMPS the depth instead produces coordinates
   in the millions and walks its sprite blitters off the frame, which
   is a read access violation rather than a visible glitch.

3.  Scene Two: The Approach

   Purple light and two phases [verified]:

       Ham8_BuildTables(0x1f, 0x00, 0x2f)     base colour PURPLE
       g_ApproachRange   0xe60, counting down
       sun world         (-2400, -500)
       planet world      (-4000, -500)
       camera            (-79288, 52360, 2800)   0x002e0b8e / 92 / 96
       g_SceneMode       1

       loop  Space_DrawApproachFrame   while g_ApproachRange > 0
       loop  Space_DrawFullFrame       until music cue 8:0

   Space_DrawApproachFrame (0x002cd4d0) is four steps:

       Warp_FullscreenRipple
       Scene_SortAndDrawAll        ONLY while g_ApproachRange < 0x961
       maybe_Explosion_DrawFlash
       maybe_Flare_StepSparks
       g_ApproachSpin += 4

   THE RIPPLE STANDS WHERE THE SKY WOULD BE, so this phase does not
   draw the starfield at all - section 5. No sun and no flare either.
   The sorted object list is gated on range, so the first stretch of
   the phase has no geometry in it.

   THE ASTEROID IS TEXTURE MAPPED, and it opens the scene [observed].
   That places it on the POLYGON path and not the voxel one: it comes
   through Scene_SortAndDrawAll's sorted list and the span texture
   mapper, where the light of section 4 is an octree raycast. So the
   two things arriving in this scene are drawn by two entirely
   separate renderers, and the asteroid's appearance is also what the
   range gate is timing - the list is skipped until g_ApproachRange
   drops under 0x961.

   Space_DrawFullFrame (0x002cd55c) is the only frame routine in the
   demo that draws everything: sky, sun, save-under pair, planet and
   halo, the sorted objects, the flare, and
   maybe_Scene_DrawHorizonBand.

   ITS MOTION IS Anim_SceneTick MODE 1 [verified]:

       g_RipplePhase   += -500
       g_ApproachRange -= 0xc, floored at zero
       while g_ApproachRange < 0xa00:
           0x002e0b26 += 0x14
           0x002e0b92 += -0x50 ; 0x002e0b8e += 0x68
           g_FlatScroll -= 0x100
           and once the range reaches zero, FUN_002d57bc()

   So the ripple's phase winds BACKWARDS 500 a tick while its
   amplitude decays 0xc a tick: 0xe60 to zero in about 300 ticks, six
   seconds at 50 Hz. The camera only begins to move once the range is
   under 0xa00, two thirds of the way in.

3.1  What a viewer sees when the pan stops, and where it lives

   THE WHOLE SEQUENCE AFTER THE CAMERA SETTLES IS SCENE TWO. A viewer
   describes, in order: a white flash covering the screen, the object
   gone, a circular light spreading out and turning purple, the whole
   screen going wavy, and glowing chunks travelling outwards
   [observed]. Every one of those is a step of
   Space_DrawApproachFrame:

   +--------------------------------+------------------------------+
   | What is seen                   | What draws it                |
   +================================+==============================+
   | the whole screen goes wavy     | Warp_FullscreenRipple,       |
   |                                | step 1 - section 5           |
   +--------------------------------+------------------------------+
   | the ship and debris            | Scene_SortAndDrawAll,        |
   |                                | step 2, gated on the range   |
   +--------------------------------+------------------------------+
   | a white flash, then a circular | maybe_Explosion_DrawFlash,   |
   | light spreading and turning    | step 3 - its radius peaks at |
   | purple                         | 118 of a 160-wide screen     |
   +--------------------------------+------------------------------+
   | glowing chunks going outwards  | maybe_Flare_StepSparks,      |
   |                                | step 4 - section 6           |
   +--------------------------------+------------------------------+

   THE PURPLE IS THE SCENE'S BASE COLOUR. Scene two passes
   Ham8_BuildTables (0x1f, 0, 0x2f) where scene one passes white, so
   the same light byte that brightens toward white in scene one tints
   toward purple here - section 2. The flash does not change colour so
   much as the scene it lands in does.

   AND THE OBJECT IS GONE BECAUSE THE SCENE CHANGED, not because
   anything removed it: maybe_Light_DrawIfOnScreen is step 6 of scene
   ONE's frame routine and has no counterpart in scene two's.

   NONE OF THIS IS IN THE RECONSTRUCTION. src/part_approach.c is scene
   one only. Scene two needs three things it does not have: the
   ripple's source image, which is still open - section 5 - the whole
   sorted-object pipeline, and whatever seeds g_SparkList.

   THE PURPLISH STARBURST SCENE ONE DOES HAVE is section 4.1's portal
   glow, and the reconstruction's parameters match the demo's
   [observed]:

       phase 0x7c    g_PortalPhase 0x7c, g_PortalGlowX -58
                     -> field at (144, 52), flare at (144, 62)
                     the reconstruction matches at its tick 61
       phase 0xc0    g_PortalPhase 0xc0, g_PortalGlowX 10
                     -> field at (76, 52), flare at (76, 96)
                     the reconstruction matches at its tick 95

   BOTH HALVES OF IT RUN IN THE DEMO. A breakpoint on
   maybe_Portal_DrawGlow's entry halts, and one on its field write at
   0x002cd6de halts too, so the field inks as well as the flare.

   ITS TWO COLOUR RAMPS ARE BUILT AT RUN TIME, NOT STORED, and that
   is the trap. Ramp_BuildAll writes them at 0x002ef8d0 and
   0x002efd70, which are uninitialised buffers inside a CODE hunk -
   every byte of both is zero in the executable. Reading them with an
   image address yields a ramp of zeros, so the field and the flare
   add nothing at all while still passing every inking test and
   counting thousands of inked pixels. The starburst is simply
   invisible, and nothing about the arithmetic looks wrong.

   Ramp_BuildAll's parameters for them [verified]:

   +------------+----------+------+------+------+----------+--------+
   | Address    | Used for | R    | G    | B    | D7       | Hump   |
   +============+==========+======+======+======+==========+========+
   | 0x002ef8d0 | the      | 0x3f | 0x2f | 0x3f | 0x6e0000 | 0x96   |
   |            | field    |      |      |      |          | TWICE  |
   +------------+----------+------+------+------+----------+--------+
   | 0x002efd70 | its      | 0x3f | 0x2f | 0x3f | 0x500000 | -      |
   |            | flare    |      |      |      |          |        |
   +------------+----------+------+------+------+----------+--------+
   | 0x002ef430 | the      | 0x3f | 0x2f | 0x20 | 0x780000 | -      |
   |            | flash    |      |      |      |          |        |
   +------------+----------+------+------+------+----------+--------+

   BLUE AT 0x3f AGAINST GREEN AT 0x2f IS THE PURPLE, and the field's
   hump is applied twice, which pushes its blue further still. The
   flare's parameters are the ones gfx.c already builds as
   g_ColRampMagenta; only the field's needed adding.

   Built rather than read, the field's peak over the surrounding sky
   goes from R33 G30 B35 against R17 G18 B25 - a haze indistinguishable
   from the backdrop - to R139 G137 B143, about eight times the sky,
   with the rays of its flare clearly structured.

   THE FIELD'S OWN CONTRIBUTION IS STILL MODEST. At the halted write the value
   being added is 0x02020002 - two of sixty-three per channel - with
   the phase at 0x80 and so a5 at zero, which is the field's most
   generous setting. It is a haze, and what reads as a starburst is
   the Draw_Flare of radius 0x34 on top of it.

   THE FIELD IS ALSO PATCHY BY CONSTRUCTION. Its two sine indices are
   quantised - (ang * 2) << 8 masked to 0x1fff leaves only sixteen
   distinct values, and the other is masked to sixty-four - and either
   term being negative skips the pixel, so whole rows can go uninked.
   Watching row 46 across eleven frames caught no field write at all
   while the flare wrote on every one of them.

   The object covers the glow with no bleed because the glow is step 2
   and the object step 6.

4.  The Light, And Its Stages

   THE LIGHT IS NOT ONE EFFECT. An observer describes it as visibly
   staged and as a combination of several things [observed], and the
   code agrees: there are two separate pieces, one of which is itself
   a pipeline of four.

   THE SPROUT OBJECT IS PROBABLY THE ASTEROID, NOT THE PORTAL. The
   names in the database say Light because that was the first reading;
   four things now point the other way [observed]:

   o  A viewer sees an asteroid fly across this scene and DISAPPEAR
      PART WAY ACROSS, which is what maybe_Light_DrawIfOnScreen's gate
      does - it rejects outright rather than clipping, so the object
      vanishes mid-flight rather than sliding off the edge.
   o  The object TUMBLES. maybe_Sprout_Animate spins it 0x3c of 0x2000
      a tick, a revolution in about 137 ticks.
   o  It is a solid shaded voxel mass, which reads as a rock rather
      than as a glow.
   o  The camera pans to follow it and then settles, and the same
      viewer describes exactly that.

   The portal that the ship emerges from is then section 4.1's
   interference field, which is a different effect entirely. Both the
   names and this reading carry maybe_ until a frame diff settles it.

   4.1  The portal glow

   maybe_Portal_DrawGlow (0x002cd642) adds a fullscreen polar
   interference field over the picture and then draws one flare. The
   per-pixel arithmetic is on its plate comment.

   Two facts shape it. It is GATED ON g_PortalPhase and returns
   immediately while that is zero, so it draws nothing in scene one's
   first loop and everything in the second. And its field is a
   PRODUCT OF TWO SINE LOOKUPS over g_AtanTable's angle and distance
   bytes, with both terms skipped when negative - a product, so an
   interference pattern rather than a ramp, and the double skip means
   only where both are positive inks at all.

   THE FIELD AND THE FLARE ARE CONCENTRIC [verified]. Both centre on
   column 0x56 - g_PortalGlowX, row 0x34. The field reaches it by
   seeding D2 = -0x56 + g_PortalGlowX and stepping it per column, so
   its zero falls at that column rather than at that offset, and its
   row index is seeded -0x3400 and carried in the high byte of a word,
   putting row 0x34 at zero. The flare is passed the same point
   outright.

   READING DRAW_FLARE'S ARGUMENTS THE WRONG WAY ROUND SEPARATES THEM,
   and the mistake is available because all three are plain longs. The
   call passes D1 = g_PortalPhase >> 1, D2 = 0x56 - g_PortalGlowX and
   D3 = 0x34; D1 is the RADIUS, not the row. Draw_Flare's own bounds
   tests settle it - it rejects on D2 - D1 >= 0xa0 and on D3 - D1 >=
   0x64, the screen's width and height, so D2 is x and D3 is y
   [verified, 0x002d4452]. Taking D3 for the radius pins it at a
   constant 0x34 and slides the flare down the screen as the phase
   ramps, which is the one failure that looks like two independent
   lights drifting apart.

   ITS PHASE IS WHAT STAGES IT. g_PortalPhase ramps 2 to 0xfe in twos
   under mode 2 and the ramp only runs once the value is non-zero, so
   the sequencer arms it and the animator carries it [verified].
   g_PortalGlowX rides along at the same rate and in the same
   direction, which is why the centre is a function of the phase
   alone: x = 0x56 - g_PortalGlowX = 268 - g_PortalPhase.

   THE RAMP ARMS AT TICK 136 AND THE SCENE ENDS AT TICK 231, so the
   phase never reaches its 0xfe ceiling - it stops at 192 [observed].
   g_LightWorldX is an exact tick counter for the scene, seeded -0x2d
   and stepped +3 every tick with nothing gating it, which is what
   makes both numbers readable. On hardware, at frame 200 it
   reads 570 against a phase of 140 and at frame 400 it reads 648
   against a phase of 192; both give an arming tick of t - (phase -
   2) / 2 = 136. At frame 450 the scene id has cleared to 1 with
   g_LightWorldX still 648, fixing the last tick at 231.

   AND TICK 136 IS THE (4,0) CUE. The phase is staged at 0x002cd262,
   between the two loops, so the arm is not a tick at all - it is the
   first loop's poll answering. In FIELDS that lands 147 into the
   scene's 241, from the cue fires either side of it [observed]. The
   arming tick read on hardware above is 136, and that reading was
   taken under a breakpoint, so the two do not have to agree.

   THE RECONSTRUCTION ARMS ON THE CUE, because its own tick is a
   field. That puts the arm on its tick 148 and leaves the phase at
   196 at the handover, against the demo's 192; arming on 136 instead
   left it at 220, and since the glow's centre is 268 - phase, that
   was 28 columns too far along.

   SO THE GLOW IS A LATE EVENT, not an opening one, and that is what
   puts it where the camera has turned to rather than beside the sun.
   Its brightness term |2 * g_PortalPhase - 0x100| is subtracted from
   every pixel, so nothing inks until the phase approaches 0x80. The
   peak is at exactly 0x80, tick 199, with the centre at column 140;
   by the scene's end the centre has travelled to column 76 and the
   term has climbed back to 128, half dark. Ticks 0 to 136 show no
   glow at all.

   EVERY COUNTER HERE STEPS ONCE A FIELD, bloom or no bloom. A
   logging watch on g_PortalPhase and on g_LightWorldX over the
   hundred fields that follow the (4,0) cue records one write a field
   each, +2 and +3, with no field missed - and that window carries
   the phase from 4 to 182, so the glow is inking through most of it
   [observed]. Nothing in the scene is on a wall clock and nothing
   drops.

   LANDMINE: COMPARING A DEMO COUNTER AGAINST A BREAKPOINT-ARMED
   FRAME COUNT SAYS OTHERWISE. Read that way the scene looks like it
   steps once per RENDERED frame and stalls as the bloom grows - 198
   ticks in 198 frames and then 26 in 200 - but it is the frame
   number that moves under a halt, not the demo. The same trap gave
   the city three wrong shot lengths; see effects/city.txt 5.1.

   That this is the portal is [inferred] - from the timing and from
   an observer reporting a light in this scene that the ship comes
   out of. The arithmetic is [verified]; the role is not.

   4.2  The staged billboard

   maybe_Light_Project (0x002cec64) is what turns the light's world
   position into a screen one, called by mode 2 immediately after
   moving it [verified]:

       D0 = g_LightWorldX        0x002e0ae0, -45 stepping +3
       D1 = g_LightWorldZ        0x002e0ae4, 1205 stepping -6
       D2 = g_LightWorldY >> 5   0x002e0ae8, 160 stepping -5
       maybe_Camera_Rotate2Axis
       if D1 <= 0: return, writing nothing
       g_LightDepth = D1
       g_LightProjX = (D0 << 12) / D1
       g_LightProjY = (D2 << 12) / D1

   THE SECOND OF THE THREE IS THE DEPTH. The third is the vertical
   screen axis and its crossing zero at tick 32 only means the light
   passes the middle of the frame. The results are 12.4 fixed point,
   which is why the gate takes them >> 4, and the divisor is the depth
   rather than a constant focal length - so unlike the sun this is
   real perspective.

   THE DEPTH IT TESTS IS THE TRANSFORMED ONE, AND THE DISTINCTION IS
   THE WHOLE END OF THE SCENE. g_LightWorldZ falls 6 a tick from 1205
   and so goes negative at tick 201, thirty ticks before the scene
   ends. The test is after maybe_Camera_Rotate2Axis, not before it,
   and by tick 201 the camera has yawed far enough that the rotation
   mixes a large positive x back into the depth: at tick 206 the world
   triple is (573, -31, -870) and the transformed depth is about 571
   [observed, on hardware]. Gating on the raw value instead freezes
   the object at its last position for the rest of the scene, which
   reads as the object running off to the right and stopping rather
   than as a projection that has stopped being computed.

   maybe_Camera_Rotate2Axis (0x002d4396) is a yaw in the (x, depth)
   plane then a pitch in the (depth, y) one, through g_SinTable and
   g_CosTable. NEITHER ANGLE IS ZERO IN THIS SCENE - the yaw is what
   the pan drives, and it reaches -0xaca - so the transform is never
   the identity here. The products come down by 8 then 6, so by 14.


   maybe_Light_DrawIfOnScreen (0x002cec14) is step 6 of scene one's
   frame, with no counterpart in 2.3. It is a visibility gate, not a
   draw [verified]:

       if g_LightDepth <= 0:  nothing
       x = (g_LightProjX >> 4) + 0x50   reject if negative
       y = (g_LightProjY >> 4) + 0x32   reject if x >= 0xa0
       r = 0x6400 / g_LightDepth        THE FULL VALUE
       h = r >> 1
       reject if y >= 100 - h or y - h < 0
       draw at (x, y - h) with scale r

   THE RADIUS IS A PERSPECTIVE DIVIDE, r = 0x6400 / depth, so the
   object grows as it closes - and mode 2 closes the depth 6 a tick
   from 1205. The vertical test rejects it outright until the whole
   disc fits rather than clipping it, so it appears whole rather than
   sliding in.

   THREE THINGS HERE ARE EASY TO GET WRONG AND EACH IS VISIBLE. The x
   is CENTRED ON 80 - the gate's own two tests are the drawn x against
   -1 and against the screen width - so dropping the 0x50 puts the
   object eighty pixels left. The scale handed over is the FULL
   0x6400 / depth: 0x002cec4a keeps it in D2 while 0x002cec4c halves
   only a COPY for the bounds test, so using the halved value draws
   the object at half size. And the y handed over is y - h, the
   object's TOP rather than its centre, so passing y sets it half a
   radius low.

   What it gates is maybe_Light_DrawStages (0x002d6eb0), which draws
   nothing itself. It works out a framebuffer offset of D0 * 4 +
   D1 * 0x280 - 0x280 being 160 longwords - latches eight parameters,
   three of them taken up by 8 as a triple, and calls five routines
   [verified].

   THE FIVE ARE NOT FIVE EFFECTS. maybe_Sprout_RotateCorners rotates
   and scales four corner values into 0x002e0a42, 46, 4a and 4e, and
   the other four are THE SAME CODE AS EACH OTHER, each filling the
   span between one adjacent pair of those corners. So the light is
   one rotated shape filled in four sectors [verified]:

   +--------+----------------------------+---------------------+
   | Sector | Span between, 24.8         | Node slots          |
   +========+============================+=====================+
   | 0      | 0x002e0a4a - 0x002e0a4e    | +0x20 and +0x24     |
   +--------+----------------------------+---------------------+
   | 1      | 0x002e0a4e - 0x002e0a42    | +0x28 and +0x2c     |
   +--------+----------------------------+---------------------+
   | 2      | unread                     | +0x30 and +0x34     |
   +--------+----------------------------+---------------------+
   | 3      | unread                     | +0x38 and +0x3c     |
   +--------+----------------------------+---------------------+

   Each sector walks rows off a ROW-POINTER TABLE at g_pFrameBuffer,
   stepping 0x280 a row, and per pixel makes an INDIRECT CALL through
   one of its two node slots - which of the pair depends on the sign
   of 0x002e09f2, so they are the two directions. The row count is
   the high word of g_SproutScale and the per-pixel step is
   0x2000 / g_SproutScale.

   SO THE PER-PIXEL WORK IS A PROPERTY OF THE DATA, NOT THE CODE.
   What a pixel does lives in the node being drawn, and the nodes are
   built from relic.dat - section 4.3.

   4.3  Sprout, the octree

   maybe_Sprout_LoadAndBuild (0x002d6a74) runs once in scene one's
   setup and is the only caller of any of this; maybe_Light_DrawStages
   likewise has one caller. THE WHOLE SUBSYSTEM EXISTS FOR THIS ONE
   OBJECT [verified].

   It loads a chunk through Dat_LoadSplit, allocates
   hdr[8] * 0x5a + hdr[4] * 0x27 bytes, and calls
   maybe_Sprout_BuildNode for the tree. That builder recurses with
   EIGHT CHILDREN, a child-presence bitmask in the first encoded byte,
   and the extent halving at each level - an octree [verified].

   THE ENCODING IS PROVEN AND CLOSES IN CLOSED FORM. Each node's
   first encoded byte decides it: zero is a LEAF of seven bytes -
   three colour components taken >> 4 and three normal components -
   and non-zero is a BRANCH of one byte, that byte being a
   child-presence mask with one child following per set bit, recursed
   with the extent halved. Walking Fsprout that way consumes it
   exactly [verified]:

       10652 leaves * 7 + 3862 branches * 1 = 78,426
       78,426 + 12 bytes of header          = 78,438

   which is the chunk's size to the byte. Max depth is 6. SO THE
   HEADER'S +4 IS THE BRANCH COUNT and +8 the leaf count.

   WHAT THE VISITORS READ IS SETTLED TOO. Every node carries eight
   visitor code pointers at node+0x20..0x3c, reached by a branch
   visitor's jsr ([0x24,A0]); a leaf's colour sits at node+0x40 and
   its three normals at node+0x44..0x46; a branch's split planes are
   the words at node+0x40 and node+0x44.

   THE TWO NODE SIZES ARE THE BUILDER'S OWN adda.l's [verified]:
   0x27 for a leaf, 0x5a for a branch. A LEAF'S STORED POINTER IS THE
   CURSOR MINUS 0x20 while a branch's is the cursor itself, which is
   how two different layouts present one interface - visitors at
   pointer+0x20, payload at pointer+0x40.

   +----------+-------------+--------+--------------------------------+
   | Node     | From the    | Bytes  | What                           |
   |          | cursor      |        |                                |
   +==========+=============+========+================================+
   | branch   | +0x00..0x1f | 0x20   | eight child pointers, absent   |
   |          |             |        | ones left as 0xffffffff        |
   +----------+-------------+--------+--------------------------------+
   | branch   | +0x20..0x3f | 0x20   | eight visitor code pointers    |
   +----------+-------------+--------+--------------------------------+
   | branch   | +0x40..0x59 | 0x1a   | thirteen words of split planes |
   +----------+-------------+--------+--------------------------------+
   | leaf     | +0x00..0x1f | 0x20   | eight visitor code pointers    |
   +----------+-------------+--------+--------------------------------+
   | leaf     | +0x20       | 0x04   | colour, 0xRRGG00BB             |
   +----------+-------------+--------+--------------------------------+
   | leaf     | +0x24..0x26 | 0x03   | three normal components        |
   +----------+-------------+--------+--------------------------------+

   THE COLOUR NEEDS NO CONVERSION. It is built as 0xRRGG00BB by three
   move.b / lsr.b #4 / lsl.l #8 groups, which is the framebuffer's own
   byte order with a zero light byte.

   AND THE ALLOCATION FORMULA PAIRS THE COUNTS WITH THE WRONG SIZES.
   maybe_Sprout_LoadAndBuild reserves hdr[8] * 0x5a + hdr[4] * 0x27 -
   leaves at the branch size and branches at the leaf size - which is
   1,109,298 bytes where the tree needs 763,008. IT OVER-ALLOCATES BY
   346,290, about 1.45 times, so the fault is harmless and never
   bites. It is worth knowing because deriving the node sizes from
   that formula gets them swapped, and the layout above comes from the
   builder's own adda.l's instead.

   THE ROOT'S COORDINATE SPACE IS [-0x1000, +0x1000] [verified].
   maybe_Sprout_LoadAndBuild enters the builder with

       A3 = A4 = D1 = D2 = -0x1000      the four bound coordinates
       A5 = 0
       D3 = 0x2000                       the extent

   AND THE SPAN WALK USES THE SAME SPACE: each sector filler starts
   its ray interval D6 and its row coordinate D3 at -0x1000 as well,
   so the tree's cube and the traversal's parameters are one
   coordinate system and nothing converts between them. 0x2000 halves
   six times to 0x80, which is the depth the chunk walk reports.

   THE FIVE COORDINATES ARE CONFIRMED AGAINST LIVE MEMORY. A child's
   are derived from its parent's by, with j the loop counter running 7
   down to 0 so that child slot k has j = 7 - k:

       A5' = A5 + (extent >> 1) - (j bit 1 ? extent : 0)
       A3' = A3 + (j bit 0 ? 0 : extent)
       D1' = D1 + (j bit 0 ? extent : 0)
       A4' = A4 + (j bit 2 ? extent : 0)
       D2' = D2 + (j bit 2 ? 0 : extent)

   the extent being halved once on entry. The root is
   A3 = A4 = D1 = D2 = -0x1000, A5 = 0, extent 0x2000, so the root's
   thirteen split words must read 0 then four triples of (-0x1000, 0,
   +0x1000), and child 0's must read -0x800 then (-0x1000,-0x800,0),
   (0,0x800,0x1000), (0,0x800,0x1000), (-0x1000,-0x800,0). BOTH READ
   EXACTLY THAT, all twenty-six words [observed, on hardware at the
   scene's start]. The derivation is not a reading of the
   disassembly - it is a prediction the running demo confirmed.

   SO THE FOUR TRIPLES ARE ONE PER SECTOR, and decoding every walk
   routine of all four sectors confirms it - each tests exactly one
   triple and nothing else [verified]:

   +--------+------------+---------------------------------+
   | Sector | Its triple | At the node                     |
   +========+============+=================================+
   | 0      | A3         | +0x42, +0x44, +0x46             |
   +--------+------------+---------------------------------+
   | 1      | A4         | +0x48, +0x4a, +0x4c             |
   +--------+------------+---------------------------------+
   | 2      | D1         | +0x4e, +0x50, +0x52             |
   +--------+------------+---------------------------------+
   | 3      | D2         | +0x54, +0x56, +0x58             |
   +--------+------------+---------------------------------+

   split[0] - A5 - is tested by every sector and always against the
   ROW coordinate. So A5 is the shared row axis and each sector's ray
   interval is bounded by its own triple: four quadrants, four ray
   axes, one row axis. That is why there are five coordinates for
   three dimensions, and it was a prediction from the builder's layout
   before it was a reading of the tables.

   tools/sprout/walkdecode.py established it and its `triples` mode
   re-derives the table above from the executable.

   AND THE SECTORS ARE ONE ALGORITHM WITH THE COLUMNS ROTATED.
   maybe_Sprout_SetSector1Visitor is maybe_Sprout_SetSector0Visitor
   with both nibbles of the occupancy index passed through
   g_SproutColumnRotate first - a 16-byte table whose bit permutation
   is bit0 -> bit1 -> bit3 -> bit2 -> bit0, a single 4-cycle. So the
   four column bits are the corners of the (x,z) square in ring order
   0, 1, 3, 2, and each sector steps the ring one quarter turn, which
   is the same quarter turn its angle is rotated by.

   4.4  It is a voxel raycaster

   EVERY NODE IS ENTERED THROUGH A CODE POINTER, and which pointer
   decides what happens [verified]. A branch's eight are chosen by
   maybe_Sprout_SetSector0Visitor and its three siblings out of six
   jump tables, indexed by the child-occupancy byte split as & 0x33
   and & 0xcc - so THE TRAVERSAL IS SPECIALISED TO WHICH CHILDREN
   EXIST, with separate tables for the cases where either half of the
   mask is empty. A leaf's eight are the fixed set
   maybe_Sprout_SetLeafVisitors installs.

   A branch visitor computes a midpoint, compares it against the split
   planes at +0x40 and +0x44, picks a child, skips it when the pointer
   is negative, and descends with

       jsr ([0x24,A0])

   a MEMORY-INDIRECT jsr, which is 68020-only - the demo's stated
   requirement, corroborated.

   A leaf visitor shades and writes - maybe_Sprout_ShadeLeaf0
   (0x002d7172) carries the arithmetic. BOTH ITS TABLES ARE
   PROCEDURAL, so nothing here needs an asset [verified]:

       A4   maybe_Sprout_BuildProductTable, 256 x 256 signed words
            A4[(a << 8) | b] = (a * b) >> 7
            biased +0x10000 so a signed word indexes it

       A5   maybe_Sprout_BuildShadeRamp, 256 longwords
            c = max(d, 0)
            t = c; four times t = (t*t) >> 7
            v = min(0x3f, (c >> 4) + ((t*t) >> 5))
            entry = (v, v, 0, v)
            biased +0x200 so a signed dot product indexes it

   So a normal component is pasted into the low byte of its light
   term, A4 turns the pair into one term of a DOT PRODUCT, the three
   are summed, and A5 maps that to a grey whose LIGHT byte is zero -
   then the voxel's own colour is added. FOUR SQUARINGS AND A FIFTH IN
   THE SUM IS AN EXPONENT OF 32, so A5 is a sixteenth of the diffuse
   plus a Phong specular. That specular is what makes the light read
   as a lit solid rather than a flat blob.

   A LANDMINE IN A5's BUILDER. Six instructions from 0x002d6a0a clamp
   a doubled, biased copy of the index into 0xa..0xff, and then
   0x002d6a20 zeroes the low byte before it is stored - so the third
   channel is always zero and that arithmetic has no effect. Every
   live entry reads (v, v, 00, v), and d = 112 and 113 read 0x0d and
   0x0e, which the formula above reproduces exactly [observed, on
   hardware at the scene's start].

   AND IT DOES NOT RETURN. The leaf visitor restores SP from
   0x002e09e8 - the traversal uses the stack pointer as a working
   register, which is why each sector filler hands over the address of
   its step variable - and branches back into the span loop. So the
   loop's apparent call per pixel is: DESCEND THE OCTREE TO A LEAF,
   SHADE IT, JUMP BACK. The light is a voxel raycast, one ray a screen
   pixel, not a splat or a polygon.

   4.4.1  What the reconstruction does instead

   src/sprout.c CASTS A GENERIC RAY and does not transcribe any of the
   ninety-six routines. The decision it runs is the demo's, decoded
   from sector 0's all-four-columns case - col1, then col0 or col3 by
   the midpoint, then col2, narrowing the interval at each step - but
   an absent column is skipped with a test rather than with a
   different routine, and the node's bounds are tracked during the
   descent rather than precomputed per node. The first leaf the ray
   meets wins, which is the pixel their visit order produces.

   THE SPECIALISATION IS AN OPTIMISATION, NOT SEMANTICS, which is why
   that substitution is legitimate where substituting the shading
   would not be - rule 16 against rule 12. The shading, the data
   format and the coordinate conventions are all transcribed exactly;
   only the dispatch is ours.

   IT PRODUCES A COHERENT SILHOUETTE. Casting a 40 x 40 grid of rays
   down the sector-0 axis hits 771 of 1600 and they form one solid
   rounded mass with no interior gaps - `relic_ppm -sprout 40` prints
   it. That is the check that the descent reaches geometry and that
   its guards reject something: nothing hit would mean a broken
   descent, everything hit would mean guards that never fire.

   A GUARD'S SENSE IS THE NEGATION OF ITS BRANCH, and getting that
   backwards is the trap here. cmp.w (off,A0),Dn computes Dn - split
   and every one of these branches jumps to the SKIP, so col1's
   `blt -> skip` means it draws when near >= lo + h, not when
   near < lo + h. Two of the four read the obvious way round are
   inverted, and the symptom is a band of holes punched through the
   middle of the object rather than an outright failure.

   4.4.2  The light drawn

   src/part_approach.c DRAWS IT. The projection and the gate above,
   then Sprout_Draw for the four sectors. At scale 1 it writes 47
   pixels at tick 0 and 97 by tick 50, its radius growing 10 to 14 as
   the depth closes, and the gate stops it around tick 90 when the
   light crosses the frame's right bound - lx + 0x50 < 0xa0.

   TWO OF THE FOUR SECTORS DRAW AT A TIME, which is what the quadrant
   test implies: only the sectors whose rotated frame faces forward
   have cos > 0 and a positive span. Whether two sectors cover the
   whole sprite is NOT established - the corner pair bounding sectors
   2 and 3 is still inferred from the ring - so the coverage is the
   first thing a frame diff against the emulator should settle.

   4.4.3  Where the drawn object is still wrong

   THE FOUR SECTORS TILE SIDE BY SIDE, NOT TOP TO BOTTOM. The filler's
   `lea ([0x2f101c,-,D0w*0x4]),A1` is memory indirect POST-indexed -
   extension word 0x05b5, base suppressed and I/IS 101 - so it loads
   g_pFrameBuffer and THEN adds the index, four bytes a pixel. The
   corner is therefore a column offset and all four sectors share the
   same starting row. Read as pre-indexed it looks like a row-pointer
   lookup, the strips stack vertically, and the object comes out with
   bands of it see-through [observed in the reconstruction].

   WHAT IS CONFIRMED AGAINST THE DEMO: the four rotated corners read
   2048, -4864, -2304 and 4608 in a live scene and the reconstruction
   computes the same four; g_SproutSlope reads -3276 where sector 2's
   formula gives -3304, the difference being table rounding
   [observed, on hardware].

   WHAT IS STILL WRONG: the object draws about a tenth of the pixels
   it should and reads as sparse rather than solid. The leading
   explanation is the interval width. g_SproutSlope is a tangent in
   13-bit fixed, so a sector near 68 degrees has a slope of 20275 -
   two and a half times the node's whole extent of 8192 - and the
   column guards then reject almost everything, because col1 wants
   mid < lo + 2h and a midpoint that far out never satisfies it. A
   sector near 20 degrees, slope -3276, stays inside the node and
   behaves. Whether the fault is the guards, the interval or the
   per-sector slope is not settled.

   4.4.5  What a live capture settles, and what it breaks

   BREAKING AT maybe_Light_DrawStages' ENTRY confirms the gate exactly
   [observed, on hardware run forward to the asteroid]:

       D0 = 0x4c = 76      the screen x
       D1 = 0x27 = 39      the y, already the TOP - y minus the half
       D2 = 0x16 = 22      the scale, the FULL 0x6400 / depth
       D3 = 0x0b = 11      the half, which only bounded the test

   THE LIGHT DIRECTION IS A COMPUTED UNIT VECTOR. At that instant
   g_LightDirX, Y and Z read 28, 0 and -124, whose length is 127 -
   1.0 in the product table's 8.7 - and g_SproutLightX/Y/Z read the
   same values shifted up a byte, exactly as maybe_Light_DrawStages
   does it.

   THE CORNERS AND THE SECTORS FOLLOW THE RECONSTRUCTION. The four
   rotated corners read -4096, 768, 3840 and -1024, so sectors 0 and 1
   have positive spans - 4864 and 3072, nineteen and twelve pixels -
   and sectors 2 and 3 do not draw. Breaking past sector 0's guards
   confirms it runs. g_SproutSlope reads 5048 there, and the spin was
   0x2d0 = 720, which is 31.6 degrees: tan 31.6 times 8192 is 5048 to
   the unit. Sector 1 is 90 degrees off that, -58 degrees, slope about
   -13300 - wider than the node, which is section 4.4.4's asymmetry
   measured rather than inferred.

   THE LEAF VISITOR IS CONFIRMED AS TRANSCRIBED. Breaking on the
   move.l D1,(A1) that writes the pixel gives D1 = 0x0a090008 - R 10,
   G 9, LIGHT 0, B 8, a dark grey-brown - with A4 at 0x007f2180 and A5
   at 0x00802380, which are the product and ramp bases biased exactly
   as maybe_Light_DrawStages sets them [observed]. So the pixel really
   is ramp[shade] plus the leaf's own colour with a zero light byte,
   and the shading pipeline needs no correction.

   THE 0x00008000 PIXELS IN THE FRAMEBUFFER ARE NOT THE OBJECT. A
   light byte of 0x80 sits outside the valid 0 to 0x40 range and no
   leaf can produce it; they belong to another pass, the save-under
   sentinel being the obvious candidate. Reading framebuffer rows and
   assuming the object is wherever the bright run is leads straight to
   that mistake - take its position from the registers at
   maybe_Light_DrawStages instead.

   THERE ARE EIGHT LEAF VISITORS, ONE PER SECTOR AND SLOPE SIGN, and
   maybe_Sprout_SetLeafVisitors installs them in that order: 0x2d8442
   and 0x2d7172 for sector 0 positive and negative, then 0x2daaa2 /
   0x2d97cc, 0x2dd10a / 0x2dbe3c, 0x2df74a / 0x2de48e. Breaking on the
   wrong one of a pair never fires, because the sign decides which the
   filler dispatches through.

   WHAT IS STILL WRONG IS THE WIDE-SLOPE SECTOR'S COVERAGE. Measured
   live, sector 0 ran at slope +5048 with the spin at 720 and sector 1
   at -10906 with the spin at 840; the reconstruction's own formula
   gives +5048 and -10923 for those angles, so the slopes are right
   and the demo genuinely traverses with an interval wider than the
   node. With the interval that wide the reconstruction's guards admit
   only one or two columns over much of the span, and that strip comes
   out sparse where the demo's is solid.

   Sector 1 is the LEFT strip and sector 0 the RIGHT at that instant -
   corners -4096, 512, 3840 and -768 give spans of 3328 and 4608, x
   offsets -16 and -3 - and it is the left, wide-slope strip that is
   sparse in the reconstruction.

   4.4.6  Watching the framebuffer, which is the method that works

   A WRITE WATCH OVER ONE FRAMEBUFFER ROW GIVES THE WHOLE PASS ORDER
   AND THE WRITER OF EVERY PIXEL. The log carries the PC, so each
   write is attributable. Row 44 of one frame, in order [observed]:

   +------------+----------+----------------------------------------+
   | live PC    | image    | What wrote it                          |
   +============+==========+========================================+
   | 0x004963f6 | 0x2d2eb2 | Scene_DrawTexturedSky, every pixel     |
   +------------+----------+----------------------------------------+
   | 0x004920ca | 0x2d1b86 | the planet sprite                      |
   +------------+----------+----------------------------------------+
   | 0x00492140 | 0x2d1bfc | its halo, slightly brighter            |
   +------------+----------+----------------------------------------+
   | 0x0049e018 | 0x2daad4 | A SPROUT LEAF VISITOR - sector 1, the  |
   |            |          | positive-slope one                     |
   +------------+----------+----------------------------------------+
   | 0x00497a7a | 0x2d4536 | Draw_Flare                             |
   +------------+----------+----------------------------------------+

   THE DEMO'S OBJECT ROWS ARE SOLID CONTIGUOUS RUNS. On that row the
   Sprout writes land on pixels 74 to 88 - fifteen in a row with no
   gaps - and every one of them comes from the SAME visitor. One
   sector covered the whole run; the other wrote nothing on that row
   at all.

   THAT IS THE CONSTRAINT THE RECONSTRUCTION FAILS. Its rows have
   gaps, so somewhere a ray that should reach a leaf does not. Since
   the middle position of the decision carries no guard, the walk
   always descends something unless the child is ABSENT - so a gap
   means the traversal is asking for a child the tree does not have,
   which points at the column-to-slot mapping or the order rather than
   at the guards.

   AND IT IDENTIFIED THE SENTINEL. 0x00008000 is written by 0x2d1a94,
   the save-under stamp, and 0x2d1e5a writes the sky back over it -
   so those pixels were never the object, and reading a framebuffer
   row without attributing the writes is what made them look like it.

   THE HARNESS THIS WANTS. Capture the demo's state at one frame - the
   spin, the scale, the projected position, the four corners, the
   slope - feed those exact values to the reconstruction's own
   Sprout_Draw, and diff its pixel run against the watch log for the
   same row. Same inputs, same row, two outputs: that replaces
   reasoning about the guards with a measurement that says which pixel
   first differs.

   4.4.7  Two order sets, one per slope sign

   THE FILLER DISPATCHES THROUGH node+0x20 FOR A POSITIVE SLOPE AND
   +0x24 FOR A NEGATIVE ONE, and those are different routines - not
   the same code with a flag. Each sector therefore has TWO orders and
   two sets of guards [verified]:

   +--------+---------------------------+---------------------------+
   | Sector | positive slope            | negative slope            |
   +========+===========================+===========================+
   | 0      | col0 -> (col1|col2) -> 3  | col1 -> (col0|col3) -> 2  |
   +--------+---------------------------+---------------------------+
   | 1      | col2 -> (col0|col3) -> 1  | col0 -> (col2|col1) -> 3  |
   +--------+---------------------------+---------------------------+
   | 2      | col3 -> (col2|col1) -> 0  | col2 -> (col3|col0) -> 1  |
   +--------+---------------------------+---------------------------+
   | 3      | col1 -> (col3|col0) -> 2  | col3 -> (col1|col2) -> 0  |
   +--------+---------------------------+---------------------------+

   THE GUARDS ARE MIRRORED and the middle decision is INVERTED:

       positive   first  near <  lo+h and mid >= lo
                  last   far  >= lo+h and mid <  lo+2h
                  middle the far-clipping arm is mid >= lo+h
       negative   first  near >= lo+h and mid <  lo+2h
                  last   far  <  lo+h and mid >= lo
                  middle the far-clipping arm is mid <  lo+h

   USING ONE SET FOR BOTH SIGNS FAILS SILENTLY AND TOTALLY on half the
   rotations. Measured as the longest unbroken run of hits along a
   fifteen-pixel span, the negative set applied to a positive slope
   gives 0 or 1 of 15 where the correct set gives 8 to 14 - a whole
   quadrant of the object gone, which is exactly what a viewer sees as
   quadrants disappearing as it turns.

   +-----------+--------------------+-------------------+
   | Slope     | one set for both   | a set per sign    |
   +===========+====================+===================+
   | -12000    | 9 of 15            | 9 of 15           |
   +-----------+--------------------+-------------------+
   | -6000     | 10 of 15           | 10 of 15          |
   +-----------+--------------------+-------------------+
   | 0         | 1 of 15            | 14 of 15          |
   +-----------+--------------------+-------------------+
   | +6000     | 1 of 15            | 11 of 15          |
   +-----------+--------------------+-------------------+
   | +12000    | 0 of 15            | 8 of 15           |
   +-----------+--------------------+-------------------+

   THESE FIGURES ARE RUNS AGAINST A SPAN WIDTH, which measures the
   object's shape as much as the traversal - section 4.4.10. They are
   comparable to each other, not to a solid span.

   4.4.8  The sun bleeds over the object, because the flare is later

   THE FLARE IS DRAWN AFTER THE OBJECT AND ADDS INTO IT. The frame's
   steps 7 and 8 - the save-under restore and Flare_Draw - both run
   after step 6's object, and Draw_Flare accumulates rather than
   overwrites. A write watch on one row of the overlap shows it
   exactly [observed, on hardware at the crossing]:

   +------------+----------+---------------------------------------+
   | live PC    | image    | What it wrote on row 47               |
   +============+==========+=======================================+
   | 0x004a0680 | 0x2dd13c | Sprout leaf, sector 2 positive -      |
   |            |          | pixels 75 to 101                      |
   +------------+----------+---------------------------------------+
   | 0x004a1a04 | 0x2de4c0 | Sprout leaf, sector 3 negative -      |
   |            |          | pixels 72 to 74                       |
   +------------+----------+---------------------------------------+
   | 0x00497a7a | 0x2d4536 | Draw_Flare, pixels 52 to 111, OVER    |
   |            |          | the object                            |
   +------------+----------+---------------------------------------+
   | 0x0049297a | 0x2d1e36 | the save-under restore, outside it    |
   +------------+----------+---------------------------------------+

   AND THE ARITHMETIC SETTLES THAT IT IS ADDITIVE. At one address the
   leaf left 0x07050005 and the flare left 0x0e0b000a, which is that
   plus the flare's own 0x07060005. At another the leaf left
   0x43430043 and the flare left 0x4d4b004a - a channel of 0x4d where
   the shade ramp itself clamps at 0x3f, so the sum deliberately
   exceeds the ramp's ceiling and saturates.

   THAT IS THE OVERSATURATION A VIEWER DESCRIBES. Drawing the flare
   before the object instead lets the voxels cover it like any opaque
   sprite, and the object passes in front of the sun as a silhouette
   rather than being burnt through by it.

   TWO SECTORS ABUT WITH NO GAP on that row - 72 to 74 then 75 to 101
   - which is the solid-run constraint of section 4.4.6 holding across
   a sector boundary as well as within one.

   4.4.9  The object controls how much sun bleeds past it

   THE SAVE-UNDER PAIR MEASURES THE SUN'S OCCLUSION THROUGH THE
   FRAMEBUFFER, not geometrically, and the object is drawn between the
   two [verified]:

       step 4   Sprite_StampSaveUnder    the sentinel into the sun's
                                         cells
       step 5   Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo  overwrites some
       step 6   the object               overwrites some
       step 7   Sprite_RestoreSaveUnder  counts the survivors, puts
                                         the saved pixels back
       step 8   Flare_Draw               sized by what step 7 counted

   SO THE OBJECT DIMS THE FLARE BY ERASING SENTINELS. Clipping the
   sun's edge destroys a few cells and the flare is barely reduced;
   sitting squarely in front destroys most of them and it nearly shuts
   out - which is what a viewer sees, and it is not a draw order but a
   measurement.

   THE SENTINEL IS IDENTIFIED BY ITS HIGH BIT, NOT BY THE WHOLE PIXEL.
   0x80 is outside the valid 0 to 0x40 light range, so the bit alone
   is proof - and it has to be, because the halo drawn between the
   stamp and the count is ADDITIVE and shifts the other channels while
   leaving that bit set. Testing the pixel for equality counts every
   cell the halo touched as occluded, and the two symptoms are exactly
   what a viewer reports: the range stays low so the bloom arrives
   late, and those cells are never restored, so one sentinel pixel per
   cell survives into the picture as a GRID OF BLACK DOTS - black
   because a light byte of 0x80 against part 2.3's black base resolves
   to nothing.

   THE RESTORE PUTS BACK ONLY WHERE THE SENTINEL SURVIVED. Where it
   did not, something drew over the cell and that drawing has to
   stand. The demo's own restore shows it: on a row where the object
   covered pixels 72 to 101 it wrote back only outside that run
   [observed]. Restoring every cell instead erases the occluder - in
   the reconstruction that showed up as 12 of 80 pixels differing from
   part 2.3's reference, which is how it was caught.

   THE RECONSTRUCTION STAMPS ONE PROBE PIXEL PER CELL. The demo's cell
   is a pixel; above scale 1.0 ours is a block, and the sentinel goes
   in its top-left only. Anything covering the cell covers that pixel,
   so the test is unchanged and the stamp is invisible.

   DIVERGENCE: IT HAD TO MOVE OUT OF THE TICK AND INTO THE FRAME.
   There is nothing to measure until the frame is drawn, so the ease
   now follows the drawn frame rather than the 50 Hz tick. The demo's
   frame is a fixed 25 Hz so its ease is deterministic; ours follows
   the host's rate.

   Measured across the crossing, the range that sizes the flare runs
   0x50fd with the two apart, 0x219f as they overlap, 0x1133 with the
   object squarely in front, and back to 0x52f7 once it has passed.

   4.4.10  A span is a bounding extent, not a run

   NEITHER THE DEMO NOR A RECONSTRUCTION FILLS A SECTOR'S SPAN. The
   span is the screen width the sector's quadrant can reach; how much
   of it the object occupies depends on the geometry along each ray.
   Comparing a longest unbroken run against the span's width therefore
   measures the object's shape, not a defect.

   CAPTURED AT A FRAME CHOSEN FOR A STEEP SECTOR - spin 5520, scale
   36, corners 6144, 1536, -6400 and -1792, so sector 2 draws with
   slope about 14100 at 60 degrees and sector 3 shallow at -4741
   [observed, on hardware]. On row 46 of that frame the demo wrote:

       sector 2, steep     pixels 99 to 106    8 consecutive
       sector 3, shallow   pixels 96 to 98     3 consecutive

   Solid where they meet, and sector 2 filled EIGHT of its eighteen
   pixel span. The rest of the span has no geometry along those rays,
   which is what a rounded object inside a square bound looks like.

   GIVEN THE SAME SECTOR, SLOPE, ROW AND SPAN the reconstruction
   produces a run of 10 against the demo's 8 - marginally long, not
   short. A run measured against a span width instead reads as 8 or 9
   of 15 at steep angles, which is the object's shape showing through
   the metric.

   WHAT WOULD STILL BE WORTH DOING is a row-by-row diff: feed
   Sprout_Draw the captured state and compare its whole row against
   the watch log rather than a run length. Two pixels of difference on
   one row is within what the estimated row coordinate and the
   one-frame lag between g_SproutAngle and g_SproutSpin could explain,
   so it needs the exact comparison to mean anything.

   4.5  The geometry

   THE SHAPE IS THE OCTREE'S BOUNDING SQUARE, rotated and scaled.
   maybe_Sprout_RotateCorners reads four (x, y) pairs at 0x002e0a22 -
   (-128,-128), (+128,-128), (+128,+128), (-128,+128) [observed] - and
   writes

       dst[i] = (g_SproutScale * (c*x + s*y)) >> 14, low byte masked

   to 0x002e0a42, 46, 4a and 4e. Only c*x + s*y is computed, never
   c*y - s*x, because what the fillers need is an EXTENT per corner,
   not a point.

   THE FOUR SECTORS ARE THE FOUR QUADRANTS [verified]. Each has a
   two-line setup that is the same code with the angle rotated a
   quarter turn:

   +--------+------------+---------+---------------------------------+
   | Sector | Setup      | Rotation| Span between                    |
   +========+============+=========+=================================+
   | 0      | 0x002d6f98 | 0       | 0x002e0a4a - 0x002e0a4e         |
   +--------+------------+---------+---------------------------------+
   | 1      | 0x002d6fc4 | -0x800  | 0x002e0a4e - 0x002e0a42         |
   +--------+------------+---------+---------------------------------+
   | 2      | 0x002d6ff8 | -0x1000 | from 0x002e0a46                 |
   +--------+------------+---------+---------------------------------+
   | 3      | 0x002d702c | +0xe800 | unread                          |
   +--------+------------+---------+---------------------------------+

   The table is 8192 entries a turn, so 0x800 is exactly a quarter and
   +0xe800 reduces to +0x800: the four rotations are 0, 3, 2 and 1
   quarter turns and together they cover the circle.

   EACH SETUP COMPUTES A TANGENT, (sin << 13) / cos in its own rotated
   frame, and only when cos > 0 - that `cos > 0` IS the quadrant test.
   The filler then rejects the sector unless the tangent lies within
   +/-0x10000, which is |tan| < 8. So one span-walk serves every
   orientation: whichever quadrant the square has turned into, exactly
   the sectors whose rotated frame faces forward draw, and each steps
   its own major axis.

   4.6  The traversal

   A NODE'S VISITOR IS A FRONT-TO-BACK RAY STEP [verified]. The
   registers carry a ray interval and the node's split words at +0x40,
   +0x44 and +0x46 are its three subdivision planes:

       D0, D2   the interval's near and far ends
       D3       the cross-axis coordinate
       A0       the node, A1 the framebuffer longword

   Per occupied column, in the sector's own order:

       reject the column unless the interval straddles +0x44 and +0x46
       D1 = (D0 + D2) >> 1                the midpoint
       D2 = D1                            NARROW THE INTERVAL
       if D3 >= (0x40,A0): A0 += 8        take the far y-slab, two
                                          pointer slots on
       D4 = the column's child pointer
       if D4 < 0: skip                    absent - the builder's
                                          0xffffffff
       A0 = D4 ; jsr ([0x24,A0])          descend

   maybe_Sprout_Walk0_Cols01 (0x002d7264) is the clearest instance -
   columns 0 and 1 occupied, visited 1 then 0.

   THE TABLES ARE SIXTEEN ENTRIES, NOT 256. The index is built from
   the occupancy byte as

       (occ & 0x33) packed into bits 0..3     the children with y = 0
     | (occ & 0xcc) packed into bits 0..3     the children with y = 1

   so the two y-slabs are OR'd and the index is four bits of WHICH
   (x,z) COLUMN IS OCCUPIED. Nothing is lost: the y choice is made at
   run time by the +0x40 compare, and an absent child is caught by the
   pointer test. Two further table pairs cover the cases where one
   slab is empty, which the selector's two tst/bne pairs choose
   between. So there are roughly 96 routines across six tables rather
   than one per occupancy byte, and what they encode is the ORDER and
   the column set.

   EACH WALK ROUTINE IS A BINARY DECISION TREE, not a flat list of
   guarded columns [verified, entry 15 of sector 0's table]. Its
   branches fall into two kinds, and only the displacement tells them
   apart:

       blt +2        the y-slab skip - take the far slab, A0 += 8
       the long ones the decision proper

   Entry 15, the all-four-columns case, reads:

       if D0 < split[0x44] and mid < split[0x46]:
           descend col1, clipping the FAR end
       mid = (D0 + D2) >> 1
       if mid < split[0x44]:
           descend col0, clip far
           then if D2 >= split[0x44] and mid >= split[0x42]:
               descend col2, clip NEAR
       else:
           descend col3, clip near
           then if D2 >= split[0x44] and mid >= split[0x42]:
               descend col2, clip near

   SO THE MIDPOINT COMPARISON CHOOSES WHICH COLUMN THE RAY ENTERS,
   and col2 appears on both arms because either order can reach it.
   That is a front-to-back ray walk written as straight-line code: the
   comparison that would pick a child at run time has been unrolled
   into the branch structure, one routine per occupancy pattern.

   A LINEAR READ OF THESE BYTES MISCOUNTS THEM. Decoding the blocks in
   address order without following the branches makes entry 15 look
   like five column descents including col2 twice. The duplicate is
   the tell that two arms were conflated.

   A RECONSTRUCTION DOES NOT NEED THE SIXTEEN ROUTINES. What they
   unroll is one algorithm: narrow the interval to the midpoint,
   compare the midpoint against the node's split planes to decide
   which column the ray enters first, descend the occupied ones in
   that order, and pick the y-slab by comparing the row coordinate
   against split[0]. Rule 16 wants that, not the decompiler's shape.

   ALL FOUR SECTORS ARE DECODED, so one implementation serves them
   all: take the sector's own triple - section 4.3 - run the decision
   above against it, and pick the y-slab against split[0]. The four
   setters differ only in how many times they put the occupancy
   nibbles through g_SproutColumnRotate: none, once, twice, three
   times, the same quarter turn per sector that the angle gets.

   THE SLOT COUNTS A LINEAR DECODE REPORTS ARE INFLATED. Entry 14 of
   sector 1 lists eight descents in address order for a four-column
   node, because a column reached from both arms of a decision appears
   once per arm. The branch map is what tells them apart.

   THE BUILT-TREE LAYOUT DOES NOT BLOCK THE PORT. Section 4.3's open
   question is about the demo's own node sizes, which matter for
   reading its memory and not for reproducing the picture: the chunk
   encoding is proven, so a reconstruction parses Fsprout into its own
   nodes and chooses its own layout.

   WHAT THIS COSTS TO RECONSTRUCT. The specialisation is an
   optimisation, not semantics: a few hundred routines that differ
   only in which children they visit and in what order. A C version
   reads the occupancy byte and walks the children generally - rule 16
   wants the maths, not the decompiler's shape. The shading tables at
   A4 and A5 and the encoded chunk are the parts that must be read out
   of the data.

   THE CHUNK IS Fsprout, whose magic is the four bytes SPRT. Its
   header reads 3862 at +4 and 10652 at +8, which by the above is
   3,862 leaves and 10,652 branches - 1,109,298 bytes of tree built
   out of a 78,438-byte chunk, a fourteen-fold expansion [verified].
   See formats/relic_dat.txt.

   THE DECOMPILER CANNOT FOLLOW THIS SUBSYSTEM. Parameters arrive in
   D1, D2, D3 and A3 to A6 with no stack frame at all, so Ghidra
   renders them as unaff_ and extraout_ pseudo-variables and its
   control flow for these routines is a sketch - the listing is the
   only reliable form. The per-pixel operations are reached solely
   through a node's slots, so identifying them means following the
   builder's allocation cursor instruction by instruction.

   The light's position is 0x002e0ae0 to 0x002e0ae8, set to (-45,
   1205, 160) by the sequencer and stepped by (+3, -6, -5) a tick by
   mode 2. g_LightProjX, g_LightProjY and g_LightDepth are the
   projected form the gate reads, and FUN_002cec64 - called by mode 2
   immediately after moving these - is the only candidate for the
   transform between them [inferred].

   WHICH OF THE THREE IS THE DEPTH IS NOT ESTABLISHED, and the step
   rates constrain it. If it were the third, 160 falling by 5 reaches
   zero after 32 ticks and maybe_Light_DrawIfOnScreen's first test
   would stop drawing the light entirely - under two thirds of a
   second into a scene whose portal ramp alone runs 126 ticks. So
   EITHER the third cell is not the depth, OR the depth is a projected
   quantity that FUN_002cec64 computes from all three rather than
   passing one through. The second reading also explains why the
   transform exists at all.

5.  The Fullscreen Ripple

   Warp_FullscreenRipple (0x002cd760) resamples a source image over
   the whole 160 x 100 screen with a per-pixel radial displacement.
   Its arithmetic is on its plate comment.

   THE AMPLITUDE IS g_ApproachRange AND THE CALLER COUNTS IT DOWN TO
   ZERO [verified]. At range 0 the cosine term contributes nothing,
   the displacement is constant and the pass degenerates to a copy -
   so the warp settles out of its own accord as the phase ends,
   without anything switching it off.

   THE DISPLACEMENT IS PLUS OR MINUS EIGHT PIXELS, and three
   independent numbers agree on it: the -0x20 pointer bias is eight
   longwords, (d >> 13) spans 0 to 16, and the source row is 176
   longwords - 160 with eight of slack each side [verified].

   IT IS NOT Warp_FullscreenAffine. That routine (0x002d3f5c) maps
   one texture over the screen through three projected corners, which
   is linear and cannot ripple; its callers pass a Fshiptextures
   sub-texture, so it is a flat-mapped hull close-up. The two are
   different effects that both cover the screen.

   g_CosTable IS g_SinTable PLUS A QUARTER TURN. g_SinTable is at
   0x00228b6c and g_CosTable at 0x0022ab6c, 0x2000 apart, which is
   2048 longwords of an 8192-entry turn - one table with two labels
   [verified]. The entries are 2.14 fixed point, 0x4000 being 1.0,
   confirmed by the peak at index 0 and by the slope at the
   quarter-turn zero crossing: 12.5 a step against 16384 * 2*pi /
   8192 = 12.57. The ripple's base in the instruction is g_CosTable -
   0x20, so its index carries a bias of -8 entries.

6.  The Sparks

   maybe_Flare_StepSparks (0x002cd5da) walks g_SparkList, seven
   records of five longwords - x, y, dx, dy, life. A live record
   steps by its velocity, loses 0x80 of life, and draws a Draw_Flare
   through Math_Rand [verified]. There is no gravity and no drag.

   What the sparks are is open - see section 9.

7.  What Is Shared With 2.3

   Everything that draws the backdrop. Scene_DrawTexturedSky,
   Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun, the Sprite_StampSaveUnder /
   Sprite_RestoreSaveUnder pair, Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo, Flare_Draw
   and maybe_Scene_DrawHorizonBand are the same routines 2.3 uses,
   and the sequencer rebuilds the same assets through
   Planet_BuildHaloRing, Planet_BuildSpriteAndMask and
   Flare_BuildGhostSprites.

   What differs is parameters - the base colour, the sun and planet
   world positions, the camera - plus the three new routines above
   and the sorted object list.

   SO THE RECONSTRUCTION SHARES THE BACKDROP RATHER THAN COPYING IT.
   src/space.c holds those routines and src/part_approach.c holds this
   scene - reconstruction.txt section 1.2.

   WHAT src/part_approach.c DRAWS TODAY: the backdrop with scene one's
   own parameters and white base, the save-under pair, both flares,
   and the portal glow of section 4.1 transcribed per pixel. It
   carries mode 2's motion, so the sun drifts at 3, the camera angle
   steps 6000 and the portal phase ramps in twos from tick 136.

   IT HAS NO SEQUENCER, so it has no end. The demo's scene two stops
   at tick 231 with the phase at 192 (section 4.1); the part keeps
   stepping, and past tick 262 the phase sits on its 0xfe ceiling with
   the brightness term saturated, leaving a radius-127 flare parked at
   column 14 that the demo never shows. Ticks beyond 231 are outside
   the scene, not a rendering of it.

   WHAT IT DOES NOT DRAW YET: the light itself. The part tracks its
   world position and reports it, because that much is verified, and
   draws nothing for it - there is no radius until FUN_002cec64 is
   read, and inventing one would be exactly the substitution rule 12
   bans.

   src/sprout.c CARRIES THE DATA AND THE SHADING. It parses Fsprout
   into its own nodes and builds both tables, and its init REFUSES
   unless the walk reproduces the header's two counts and lands on the
   payload's last byte - so the encoding of section 4.3 is checked by
   the port every run, not just once. It reports 3862 branches, 10652
   leaves and depth 6.

   Its shade ramp was checked against the running demo: entries 112 to
   119 of the live table and of this code agree to the byte, and the
   (v, v, 00, v) packing holds throughout [observed, on hardware at the
   scene's start].

   THE WALK OF SECTION 4.6 IS NOT WRITTEN. That is what remains
   between this and a drawn light; the per-sector column order is read
   for sector 0 only.

   The only asset in the port that is NOT in the executable is this
   one, so src/sprout.c is also the first thing needing relic.dat.

8.  The Mouse Look

   A CONTROL OF OURS, NOT THE DEMO'S. A host may swing a scene's
   camera with the pointer, and the view springs back when the button
   is let go. The line this sits on is reconstruction.txt section 2:
   it resamples the demo's own camera and adds no data.

   SIX SCENES READ IT, and the mapping is Space_Look in src/space.c,
   shared rather than copied. A part that does not call it is
   unaffected.

      2.3b   the asteroid approach - this document
      2.4    the portal - effects/portal.txt section 1
      2.7    the window's long pan - parts.txt section 2.7
      2.10   the orb forming - effects/orb.txt
      2.11   the orb from underneath - effects/orb_underside.txt
      2.13   the orb release, PHASES 1 AND 2 ONLY -
             effects/orb_release.txt

   THE DRAG ARRIVES AS A GESTURE AND NOTHING MORE. The boundary it
   comes over, and the 180 ms swing home a release gets, are
   reconstruction.txt section 3.3; a host names what the pointer did
   and never what it is for. WHAT BELONGS HERE IS THE MAPPING -
   Space_Look is the springy reading of that gesture, and turns a
   deflection into the camera pair below.

   THE OFFSET IS ZERO UNLESS A HOST SETS ONE, and src/main_ppm.c
   reports no gesture at all, so the reference renders and the
   hardware comparisons are untouched.

   WHAT FULL DEFLECTION IS WORTH is 0x400 of the camera's 0x2000
   revolution - 45 degrees - and two things set it:

   o  It has to carry the framing off the frame, because the bare
      starfield behind the scene is the point of the control. The
      sky's screen corner is 0x5000 by 0x3200 against
      Sky_BuildDirTable's z term of sqrt(2 * 0xf000000), so this
      scene's half fields are about 0x3c5 across and 0x2a4 down, and
      45 degrees is just past both. At full deflection the sun's
      projected x goes from 55 to 308 and the object's from 106 to
      414 [observed].

   o  It has to stop short of the depth singularity. The sun's
      rotated depth passes through zero near +70 degrees of total
      yaw, and Space_Project rejects only AT zero - so a yaw parked
      just inside that point projects to six figures and FX_MUL
      overflows on the way to the blitters. The demo's own camera
      never reaches it because it only ever yaws negative, as far as
      -0xaca.

   THE READ SITES ARE ALL IN THE FRAME ROUTINES, never in the step:

      Space_SetCamera      the sun, the planet, and both flares
      Space_SkyPan         the starfield, geared the way
                           maybe_Camera_MoveRotated gears it with the
                           camera level - the cos 0 and the >> 1 of
                           section 2, so four camera units to a
                           texture cell
      maybe_Light_Project  this scene's object, projected a SECOND
                           time
      cam[3] and cam[4]    the vessel, which carries its own camera
                           pair into Scene_TransformProject rather
                           than reading Space_SetCamera. Ship_LookCam
                           in src/ship.c hands back a COPY with the
                           offset added, so the tracker's own camera
                           is left alone; the portal and the window
                           both go through it

   THE STEP MUST NOT SEE IT, in any of the three. Mode 2's camera tracks
   the object's own projected x and its gate never reopens once the
   yaw is past -0xaca; mode 1's tracker accumulates into the same
   camera the sky's two angles and the one-off bake are taken from.
   An offset reaching either would bend the scene's path for good and
   leave the handover carrying a camera the demo never had. That is
   why each drawn thing is transformed a second time rather than the
   camera being moved: the approach projects its object twice, once
   undragged for the gate and once through the offset for the draw,
   and the portal hands the ship a copy of the camera triple.

   THE FOUR SHIP SHOTS TAKE IT WHOLE, where the first two cannot.
   Nothing in 2.7, 2.10, 2.11 or 2.13's first two phases is baked -
   each samples the sky live every frame - so the starfield, the sun,
   the flares, the vessel, the beams and the satellites all swing
   together. The beams and the satellites follow because OrbScene
   projects through whatever camera it is handed, so passing it the
   offset copy is the whole of it.

   TWO SCENES TAKE IT ONLY IN PART, and in both the reason is a
   phase that is not a camera shot at all. The window's hold and its
   pull-back through the surround ignore the offset; so does the
   release's phase 3, which has no sky - the hull fills the frame -
   and which pins the orb to the frame's centre, so a drag there
   would swing the vessel inside a backdrop and around an orb that
   both stayed put.

   THE RELEASE PROJECTS ITS ORB A SECOND TIME, for the same reason
   the approach projects its object twice. g_sx, g_sy and g_sr are
   the step's and feed its own gating, so the frame runs
   orb_project_at again through the offset camera and draws from
   that copy. Without it the orb would hang still while the ship
   swung out from under it.

   WHAT DOES NOT FOLLOW the pointer: 2.11's flash, which
   Scene_RenderFlash puts at a fixed screen place; the portal glow,
   which is drawn
   at its own screen offset, and anything baked at a handover. The
   portal's first loop is the clearest case - its backdrop is the sky
   baked once between the two scenes, so for those 307 ticks the ship
   swings over a still picture. The second loop samples the sky live
   and the whole frame follows.

9.  Open Questions

   o  THE GLOW'S INPUTS MATCH AND ITS PICTURE DOES NOT. Held at the
      same field of the demo - g_PortalPhase 144 and the patched
      0x002cd63e reading -38, both of which the reconstruction
      reproduces exactly - the demo's blob has its brightest column
      at 135 and 168 pixels over R+G+B 150, where ours has 119 and
      266 [observed]. So it sits about fifteen columns further along
      its travel and is half again as big, with the same phase and
      the same offset driving it. The error is inside
      Space_PortalGlow, not in what stages it.

   o  What seeds g_SparkList. The velocities are not set in
      maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes or in the frame routines, so a
      writer has not been found. Whoever sets them decides whether
      the sparks are the asteroid breaking up or the portal.

   o  What the ripple's source image is. A0 reaches
      Warp_FullscreenRipple from its caller, and a 176-longword
      stride is not the framebuffer's 160.


   o  SECTORS 2 AND 3's corner pairs. Their walk tables are decoded -
      section 4.6 - but which pair of g_SproutCorner..3 bounds each of
      those two spans is read for sectors 0 and 1 only.

   o  FUN_002cec64, which mode 2 calls straight after moving the
      light's world position and is the only candidate for the
      transform into g_LightProjX, g_LightProjY and g_LightDepth.
      Without it the light's screen position and its perspective
      radius cannot be computed.

   o  FUN_002d68da, mode 2's first call, and what
      maybe_Camera_MoveRotated does to the camera when the light is
      in frame.

   o  WHICH TICK EACH MUSIC CUE FALLS ON. Both scenes are cut by cues
      rather than by counts, so no cue's tick is readable from the
      code - see subsystems/music.txt. Scene two's two are measured
      on hardware instead, section 4.1; the rest are unknown.

   o  Which phase is the asteroid and which the ship. The range gate
      suits something arriving from far off and the handover to a
      sun-lit frame suits the ship reaching the planet, but neither
      is confirmed - hence maybe_ on the sequencer.
