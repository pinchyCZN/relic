PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                            THE ORB RELEASE


Abstract

   The scene after the explosion: a long pass around the underside of
   the ship, then the ship letting the sphere go. Space_RunRelease at
   0x002cbef8, modes 0xd, 0x10 and 0x11.

   THE WHOLE SCENE IS ONE ROUTINE and it is NOT part of Space_RunPart,
   which returns before it. SpaceModule_Part0 calls the two in
   sequence [verified, 0x002c83fa].

   Its effects are mostly ones the demo already has. The flare is
   Draw_Flare with a different ramp, the crack shells are
   Rast_AdditivePoly and Draw_LineAA - the electric arcs' own two
   primitives - and the opening and closing white-outs are the same
   LIGHT washes used elsewhere. What is new is the crack mask.

1.  The Three Phases

   Space_RunRelease is a linear script, each phase a render-and-tick
   loop polling g_QuitRequested [verified, 0x002cbef8]:

    +----+------+---------------------------+-------+---------------------+
    | ph | mode | skip table                | fuse  | render              |
    +====+======+===========================+=======+=====================+
    |  1 |  0xd | g_ExplosionSceneSkipTable | 0x320 | Scene_RenderRelease |
    |  2 | 0x10 | g_ReleaseMidSkipTable     | 0x096 | Scene_RenderRelease |
    |  3 | 0x11 | g_ReleaseEndSkipTable     | -     | 0x002cdaac          |
    +----+------+---------------------------+-------+---------------------+

   THE SKIP TABLES NARROW AS IT GOES, and that is the release itself:

       phase 1   draws  0-7, 9-14, 38
       phase 2   draws  3, 4, 9-14, 38
       phase 3   draws  3, 4, 9-14

   ONLY THREE OF THE SIX CLAWS ARE EVER DRAWN. The claws are slots 12
   to 17 and no table in this scene reaches past 14, so 15, 16 and 17
   are skipped throughout. Confirmed live: g_SceneObjects slot 15's
   +0x40 reads 1 and slot 12's reads 0 [observed, on hardware]. A
   claw missing from the near side is the demo's own table, not a
   fault - see formats/geometry.txt 6.1 for the slot map.

   So the hull sheds a group of slots at a time and the last phase
   drops slot 38 as well. Phase 1 reuses the explosion's own table
   [verified, 0x002cbf9a]; the other two are its own, and the five
   tables are contiguous from 0x002ccb80.

   THE SETUP REBUILDS EVERY OBJECT. Space_RunRelease +0x22 calls
   Space_BuildObjects, so the claws come back on their ORIGINAL
   texture and the pass the orb sequence swapped them to does not
   survive into this scene; nothing here swaps them again.

   AND THE SKY PANS WITH THE CAMERA, so the stars move as it orbits -
   Scene_RenderRelease draws it from the camera's own angles, not
   from a fixed pan.

   THE LIGHT BASE IS THE ORB SEQUENCE'S, NOT THE EXPLOSION'S.
   Ham8_BuildTables is handed (0x3f, 0x3f, 0x4f) at the routine's first
   instruction [verified], the same triple the claws and the orb ran
   under, so the scene reads blue rather than the explosion's paler
   blue-white.

2.  It Opens White, And It Ends White

   FLASH_WASHFROMFUSE KEYS ON THE FUSE, NOT ON A LEVEL [verified,
   0x002cdbe0]:

       amt = g_SceneFuseTicks - 0x2ee
       if amt < 0: do nothing
       amt <<= 1, capped at 0x3f
       every pixel: LIGHT = g_LightSatLut[LIGHT + amt]

   Phase 1 seeds the fuse 0x320, which is 50 above the 0x2ee
   threshold, so the scene OPENS saturated and fades down over its
   first 50 ticks. Phase 2's fuse of 0x96 is far below it and the wash
   never fires again.

   THAT IS THE OTHER HALF OF THE EXPLOSION'S ENDING. Neither scene
   holds a whole white-out: the explosion's wash saturates and stays
   there for its last seventy ticks (effects/explosion.txt 4) and this
   one fades that same white away. A port that renders either part on
   its own sees half a transition.

   THE END IS A HARD FILL, NOT A WASH. After phase 3 the routine calls
   Space_FillWhite with 0x3f3f3f3f and ticks twice more [verified,
   0x002cc096], so the last frames are a flat fill of every channel.
   Flash_SetLightFromOrbDepth ramps into it from g_OrbDepth below 0xdc,
   SETTING rather than adding: LIGHT = (0xdc - g_OrbDepth) * 4, low
   byte only.

3.  The Flare

   THE PURPLE SUN FLARE IS Draw_Flare, the routine the explosion's ray
   burst already uses, with a different ramp and a radius that grows as
   a square [verified, 0x002cda5c]:

       r = ((g_FlareGrow >> 8) ^ 2 / 2) * 4 + 0x1e
       if r <= 0x23: draw nothing
       if r >  0x7f: r = 0x7f

   g_FlareGrow is seeded 0x7f00, so r starts clamped at 0x7f and the
   flare is at full size from the first frame. Phases 1 and 2 centre it
   on g_OrbScreenX / g_OrbScreenY with g_ColRampMagentaWide; phase 3
   centres it on (0x50, 0x32) with g_ColRampPale, and zeroes
   g_FlareGrow on entry [verified, 0x002cdafc] - which is what makes
   the flare subside before the cracks appear.

   THE TWO RAMPS ARE WHY IT CHANGES COLOUR. Nothing else about the call
   differs.

4.  The Cracks

   CRACK_DRAWSHELLS IS LIGHT ESCAPING FROM INSIDE THE SPHERE
   [verified, 0x002cdc3c], and IT RUNS IN EVERY PHASE, by two routes.

   IN PHASES 1 AND 2 IT IS A SCENE OBJECT. Scene_SortAndDrawAll draws
   the orb's slot by setting g_OrbScreenX, Y and Radius from the sorted
   object and calling Orb_DrawTexturedSphere and then Crack_DrawShells
   [verified, 0x002d2bd2]. The slot is drawn only when its depth is at
   least 0x1f4 and its projected radius under 0x50.

   IN PHASE 3 THE SLOT IS GONE and the render calls the same pair
   directly, through Orb_ProjectAndFlare - which is why
   g_ReleaseEndSkipTable drops slot 38. The two routes draw the same
   thing; the direct one just lets the phase order it after the warp.

   THE SAME 0x50 RADIUS GATE GUARDS BOTH.

   THE MASK IS TWO SINE FIELDS, NOT A TEXTURE. For each of
   g_CrackVertCount points in g_CrackVerts:

       a = g_SinTable[((x >> 2) + g_CrackPhaseA) & 0x1fff]
       b = g_SinTable[((z >> 2) + g_CrackPhaseB) & 0x1fff]
       keep the point only if both a and b are >= g_CrackThreshold

   A dropped point is marked 0x7fffffff and every edge touching it is
   skipped, so g_CrackThreshold opens and closes the whole network as
   one value.

   NOTHING IN THIS SCENE MOVES IT. Space_RunRelease zeroes it at setup
   and none of the three mode handlers touches it [verified,
   0x002cbf30 and the handlers in section 5]; the only animator is
   mode 0xe, which belongs to the scene after this one. So the cracks
   here are held fully open and ALL the motion comes from the two
   phases sliding the sine fields over the surface.

   A SURVIVING POINT IS ROTATED AND THEN SCALED BY THE ORB'S OWN
   PROJECTED RADIUS, g_OrbScreenRadius >> 5, so the rig tracks the
   sphere's apparent size rather than sitting in world space. Every
   coordinate is offset by g_OrbScreenX / g_OrbScreenY at draw time.

   EACH EDGE BECOMES A QUAD AND A LINE. Pass two walks g_pCrackEdges -
   a count then index pairs - sorts each pair by depth, clips the near
   end against z = 0, and then draws:

       Rast_AdditivePoly twice, A1 = A2 = A3 = 0x7f, A4 = 0x40, A5 = 0
       Draw_LineAA between the two QUARTER points

   THE QUADS ADD THROUGH THE CONDITIONED FLAT TABLES, the same pair
   the ship's mode-1 faces use - Flat_ConditionTables builds them out
   of the geometry chunk's tail [verified, 0x002cff42]. A scene that
   draws the orb WITHOUT the ship still needs them: the conditioning
   runs once and latches, so with no chunk loaded it leaves an
   all-zero pattern behind and every quad adds nothing at all. The
   white lines keep drawing, which makes it read as the rays being
   gone rather than as a missing load.

   THE LINE IS AT THE QUAD'S INNER EDGE, NOT ITS FAR END. The movem at
   0x002cde14 pops the quarter points back into D0 to D3, so the line
   lies on the sphere's surface where the shell attaches [verified].
   Drawing it at the outer pair puts the glowing crack out in space
   with the shell hanging off it the wrong way round.

   THESE ARE THE ELECTRIC ARCS' OWN PRIMITIVES with the same gradient
   setup; only the vertices differ. The quad's corners come off the
   endpoints shifted right two, so its width tracks the point's own
   coordinate - which is why the shells along one crack are different
   sizes. The line is what makes the crack itself read as glowing.

   THE CRACK NETWORK IS AN IFF TDDD OBJECT LINKED INTO THE
   EXECUTABLE, 102 points and 88 edges, loaded once by
   Crack_LoadGeometry [verified, 0x002cc244]. The modeller file is a
   FLAT sketch - every point's z is zero - whose x and y are read as
   longitude and latitude and wrapped onto a sphere at load time.
   formats/tddd.txt has the chunk layout and the arithmetic.

   THE AUTHORED GRID IS 0..126 BY 2..128, integers only, and every one
   of the 88 edges joins a point below index 58 to one at or above it.

   THE ORB IS A TEXTURE-MAPPED BALL AND IT ARRIVES BRIGHTEST FIRST.
   Orb_DrawTexturedSphere [verified, 0x002d1fdc] is Draw_GlowBlob's
   skeleton - the same rejects, the same g_pSpanHalfWidth chord, the
   same span walk - with the glow's add replaced by a texture fetch
   that writes a WHOLE 4-byte pixel. Two hops through
   g_SphereNormalTable, less g_SceneAngleB then less g_SceneAngleA,
   give the texel.

   THE TEXTURE IS Fcrust, 0x40000 bytes of relic.dat - 256 x 256
   four-byte pixels, loaded by name at SpaceModule_Part0 +0x4e
   [verified, the string at 0x002e0d13]. ITS LIGHT BYTE IS ZERO IN ALL
   65536 TEXELS [verified], which is what leaves byte +2 free for
   everything drawn over it. It is bright polygonal plates separated
   by near-black crack lines - a crust, which is what the demo calls
   it.

   THE SEAM ACROSS THE EQUATOR IS THE DEMO'S OWN, not a mapping
   fault. g_SphereNormalTable steps 512 a row and 1024 across dy = 0,
   so the row at the orb's centre is a visible discontinuity whatever
   the rotation. A demo capture at the same centre and radius -
   (0x4f, 0x32) radius 0x1b - carries the same line in the same place
   [observed, on hardware], and the two spheres agree on the dark
   upper cap, the cyan patch and the bright band below it. Smoothing
   it would be a divergence.

   THE DEMO'S CAPTURE CANNOT SHOW THE CRACK LINES. Draw_LineAA writes
   byte +2 only and a PPM of the render target drops that channel, so
   the lines are absent from the comparison while the sphere is not.

   AND ONE THRESHOLD IS THE WHOLE REVEAL:

       D5 = (g_FlareGrow >> 8) >> 1
       draw the texel only if its B channel less D5 is >= 0

   g_FlareGrow falls 0x64 a tick from 0x7f00, so D5 runs 0x3f down to
   0 by tick 326.

   IT REVEALS THE PLATES BEFORE THE CRACKS, not the other way about.
   B is the texture's widest channel and the crack lines are its
   darkest part, so the brightest plate highlights clear the threshold
   first and the network fills in last [observed]:

       +------+------+-------------------+
       | tick |   D5 | texels drawn      |
       +======+======+===================+
       |    0 | 0x3f |  0.9%             |
       |   77 | 0x30 |  4.9%             |
       |  158 | 0x20 | 24.2%             |
       |  240 | 0x10 | 68.5%             |
       |  326 |    0 |  100%             |
       +------+------+-------------------+

   Texels with B under 8 - the crack lines - are 12.3% of the texture.

   SO THE TEXTURE'S CRACKS ARE DARK AND THE GLOW IS DRAWN OVER THEM.
   Crack_DrawShells' additive quads and lines land on the same network
   the texture paints black, and that is what reads as light escaping
   from inside the sphere. Nothing animates the texture itself.

   ORB_PROJECTANDFLARE IS WHAT PUTS THE ORB ON SCREEN [verified,
   0x002d1f52]. Phase 3's render calls it with the world point (0, 0)
   at depth g_OrbDepth:

       g_OrbScreenX = (x << 8) / depth + 0x50
       g_OrbScreenY = (y << 8) / depth + 0x32
       g_OrbScreenRadius = 0x3200 / depth

   With x and y both zero the orb sits at the frame's centre and only
   the radius moves, so the shot is the sphere growing toward the
   camera.

5.  The Rates

   ONE HANDLER PER MODE, all in Anim_SceneTick [verified, 0x002d6058,
   0x002d60cc and 0x002d6162]:

    +---------------------------+----------+----------+----------+
    | per tick                  | 0xd      | 0x10     | 0x11     |
    +===========================+==========+==========+==========+
    | g_FlareGrow               | -0x64    | -0x64    | -        |
    | g_ApproachSpin            | +0x14    | +0x14    | +0x14    |
    | g_FlatScroll              | -0x200   | -0x200   | -0x200   |
    | g_CrackPhaseA             | +0x25    | +0x25    | +0x25    |
    | g_CrackPhaseB             | +0x53    | +0x53    | +0x53    |
    | g_SceneAngleA             | +2       | +2       | +1       |
    | g_SceneAngleB             | -        | -        | +2       |
    | Strut_AnimateClaw(0)      | -        | TWICE    | -        |
    | g_OrbDrift                | -        | +vel     | -        |
    | g_OrbDriftVel             | -        | +5       | -        |
    | g_OrbDepth                | -        | -        | -2       |
    | g_WarpParamA              | -        | -        | +0xa     |
    | g_WarpParamB              | -        | -        | +1       |
    | cam z (0x002e0b96)        | -        | -        | +0x1e    |
    | g_CameraYaw               | -        | -        | -0xa     |
    | g_SceneFuseTicks          | -1       | -1       | -        |
    +---------------------------+----------+----------+----------+

   THE CLAWS OPEN AND NEVER SNAP. Mode 0x10 calls Strut_AnimateClaw
   with D0 = 0, and D0 is what licenses the latch - so only the
   opening stage runs, where the orb sequence passed 1 and got the
   clamp (effects/orb_clamp.txt 2). It calls it TWICE a tick, so the
   joints travel at double the rate they opened at.

   AND THEY START FROM ZERO, NOT FROM THE CLAMP'S SHUT POSE.
   Space_RunRelease +0xb4 calls the reset at 0x002cca5a, and all
   three joints read zero live at tick 419 [observed, on hardware] -
   not the (562, 68, 562) the clamp leaves behind. From zero, joint 0
   reaches the -0x1ee limit in 123 ticks of phase 2's 150, so the
   claws are fully open with time to spare.

   THE ORB IS SORTED AMONG THE HULL, NOT DRAWN AFTER IT. Its slot
   body sits inside Scene_SortAndDrawAll's loop [verified,
   0x002d2bac], so the hull paints over the orb and the orb over the
   hull by depth. Drawing it after the ship puts it on top of
   everything, which shows as the sphere floating in front of a claw
   that should be in front of it.

   VERIFIED AT TWO TICKS against hardware [observed]:

       +------+-------------------+-------------------+
       | tick | demo              | port              |
       +======+===================+===================+
       |  419 | 73, 63   r33      | 73, 63   r32      |
       |  642 | 123, 56  r51      | 123, 55  r51      |
       +------+-------------------+-------------------+

   AND THE ORB LEAVES UNDER CONSTANT ACCELERATION: g_OrbDrift gains
   g_OrbDriftVel and the velocity gains 5, both from zero, which is
   the same shape as the clamp's snap run the other way.

   PHASE 3 HAS NO FUSE. It ends when g_OrbDepth falls to 0xc8, and the
   depth starts at 0x258 and drops 2 a tick, so the phase is
   (600 - 200) / 2 = 200 TICKS and the scene is 800 + 150 + 200 =
   1150 in total.

   THREE LENGTHS FALL OUT OF THOSE RATES:

   -  THE FLARE OCCUPIES THE FIRST 40% OF THE LONG SHOT. g_FlareGrow
      starts 0x7f00 and falls 0x64 a tick, and the radius formula
      drops below its own 0x23 cutoff once g_FlareGrow is under
      0x200 - at tick 320 of phase 1's 800.

   -  THE CRACKS RUN THE WHOLE OF PHASE 3. g_OrbScreenRadius is
      0x3200 / g_OrbDepth, so it climbs 21 to 64 across the phase and
      never reaches the 0x50 that would gate Crack_DrawShells off.

   -  THE CLOSING RAMP IS THE LAST TEN TICKS.
      Flash_SetLightFromOrbDepth does nothing until g_OrbDepth is
      below 0xdc, which is tick 190 of 200.

6.  The Camera Is An Orbit

   CAMERA_ORBITSTEP IS CALLED FIRST BY MODES 0xd AND 0x10, so it
   advances once a tick [verified, 0x002d5704]:

       target = Vec_Rotate2Axis((0, -0x2328, 0), g_CamOrbitB, g_CamOrbitA)
       target.z += 0x1b58
       g_CamOrbitA += 8 ; g_CamOrbitB -= 4 ; both masked to 0x1fff

   So the camera sits 9000 out and 7000 up and CIRCLES THE ORIGIN.
   Phase 1 runs 800 ticks at 8 a tick, which is 6400 of the table's
   8192 - about 78% of a turn, and that is the long pass around the
   underside. Space_RunRelease seeds the angles 0x446 and 0x872.

   THE ORB IS NOT AT THE ORIGIN. Its slot reads a world position of
   three longwords from 0x002ee894 - (0, 0, g_OrbDrift), seeded
   0xc800 - and Scene_SortAndDrawAll's projector takes that DOWN BY
   16 before it transforms [verified, 0x002d2bac and 0x002d2c7a]. The
   drift is what carries the orb away once mode 0x10 accelerates it.

   THE SLOT PROJECTOR IS NOT THE HULL'S. It rotates by the latched
   heading, subtracts the camera, rotates by yaw and pitch, adds
   0x1f4 to the depth, and then:

       radius   = (size << 11) / depth        ; size is 0x96 for the orb
       screen x = (x << 8) / depth + 0x50
       screen y = (z << 8) / depth + 0x32

   Phase 3 is the exception and the only place the frame centre is
   the answer: the slot is gone by then and Orb_ProjectAndFlare
   projects (0, 0) at g_OrbDepth directly. Using that for phases 1
   and 2 pins the orb to the middle while the ship swings past it,
   which reads as the SHIP being offset when it is the orb that is
   wrong. Read live the orb is at (73, 63) radius 33 [observed, on
   hardware].

   PHASE 3'S BACKGROUND IS THE SHIP'S HULL, NOT THE SKY. Its render
   opens with Warp_FullscreenAffine and never calls
   Scene_DrawTexturedSky [verified, 0x002cdaac], so that shot has no
   stars in it at all - the saucer fills the frame behind the falling
   orb. The texture is Fshiptextures' FIRST 256x256 block, held in
   g_ShipTexSlots[1]: the slot points at 0x006561a0 and the bytes
   there match block 0 of the chunk at the same offset [observed, on
   hardware]. Slots [1], [2] and [3] are three 0x40000 buffers
   back to back and SpaceModule_Part0 loads Fcrust over [2], so the
   middle third of Fshiptextures is replaced for this scene.

   IT TURNS AND ZOOMS. Three corners go through
   Warp_ScaleRotateCorner - scaled by g_WarpParamB >> 8, then rotated
   by g_WarpParamA - and the across and down deltas come off them;
   each pixel copies a whole longword from the texture at
   (v & ~0xff) | (u >> 8 & 0xff).

   BOTH ACCUMULATORS RUN IN 24.8 AND THE INDEX TAKES U'S INTEGER
   PART. The demo's ror.l #8 on the u terms puts the fraction in the
   TOP byte so that addx.w can fold its carry straight into the
   integer, which leaves the integer sitting in the low byte for
   move.b to lift out [verified, 0x002d3fe0 and 0x002d4008]. Reading
   u's low byte as though it were a plain integer samples the
   FRACTION instead, and the hull comes out as streaking blocks of
   colour rather than a surface. Mode 0x11 runs g_WarpParamA up 0xa a
   tick and g_WarpParamB up 1 from a seed of 0xaa, so the hull
   rotates slowly and grows as the orb approaches.

   PHASE 3 RESETS THE CAMERA; IT IS A NEW SHOT. Space_RunRelease
   +0x13c to +0x164 puts the target at (0, 0, 0x251c) and the
   orientation at yaw 0, pitch -0x800, and mode 0x11 then runs the
   target's z up 0x1e a tick and the yaw down 0xa - Camera_OrbitStep
   and Camera_TrackTarget are not called at all [verified]. That is
   the view from below with the ship overhead and the orb falling
   toward the lens.

   MEASURED 154 TICKS IN [observed, mode 0x11 live]: yaw -1540, which
   is exactly 154 steps of -0xa from zero, and pitch still -0x800.
   The latched camera z reads 14090 against a target of 14120,
   because the render latches BEFORE the tick runs and so trails it
   by one step.

   AND PHASE 3'S RENDER TAKES THE SECOND OBJECT ANGLE STRAIGHT FROM
   g_SceneAngleB [verified, 0x002cdaf2], where Scene_RenderRelease
   derived it from the pitch. Different render, different rule.

   AND THE SETUP SEEDS THE CAMERA'S ORIENTATION: g_CameraYaw 0x37a0
   and g_CameraPitch -0x286 at Space_RunRelease +0x5e and +0x68
   [verified]. Starting them at zero points the camera away from the
   origin and EVERY SHIP FACE IS THEN REJECTED as unprojectable, which
   reads as the ship being absent rather than as a camera fault - the
   orb survives it only because its own placement does not go through
   that transform.

   THE SECOND OBJECT ANGLE COMES OFF THE PITCH, NOT FROM
   g_SceneAngleB. Scene_RenderRelease +0x64 takes the latched
   g_CameraPitch, shifts it right five and inverts the LOW BYTE only,
   and writes that to 0x002e0bee, which the object transform and
   Crack_DrawShells both read. Measured live, a pitch of -305 gives
   -247 [observed, on hardware].

   THE TARGET IS THE CAMERA'S POSITION, NOT ITS LOOK-AT. The render
   latches it into the camera and Camera_TrackTarget builds the yaw
   and pitch from two addends the handlers set. The same trap as the
   orb pan's - effects/orb_underside.txt 3.

   AND WHICH ADDEND GOES ON WHICH AXIS DECIDES THE YAW RATE.
   Camera_TrackTarget adds 0x002e0b4e to the TARGET'S X and
   g_CamAim12A to its Z [verified, 0x002d57c6 and 0x002d57d8], and
   modes 0xd and 0x10 put 0x1f4 in the first and -(g_OrbDrift >> 4)
   in the second. Swapping them leaves the camera's POSITION correct
   to within a few units and its yaw accumulating about 30% slow,
   which swings the whole scene out of frame and back. Measured at
   tick 419: 12749 against the demo's 12753 once the two are the
   right way round, and the orb then projects to (73, 63) radius 32
   against (73, 63) radius 33 [observed, on hardware].

7.  Open questions

   -  THE BLUE HALO is not identified. g_ColRampPale on phase 3's
      flare is the candidate, but nothing has been measured against a
      frame.

   -  0x002d5704 in the setup, 0x002d4258 (the rotate in
      Crack_DrawShells' first pass) and 0x002cde4e.

   -  WHAT g_pOrbTexture POINTS AT. The orb's texture is fetched a
      longword at a time from it plus 0x20200, the same bias the ring
      uses on Fplasma, but the chunk behind the pointer has not been
      identified.
