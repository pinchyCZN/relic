PCDEMOS RELIC                                              Analysis Note


                    THE RECONSTRUCTION, AND ITS RULES


Abstract

   What src/ is for, how it is built, and the one judgement that
   separates this from an emulator.

   RELIC IS NOT BEING EMULATED. An emulator already reproduces it
   exactly, pixel for pixel, and there is no reason to write another
   one. This is a REIMAGINING: the demo's own code and its own data,
   run at a resolution and precision its authors could not reach, to
   answer what RELIC would have looked like on an Amiga whose chunky
   graphics were fast and high resolution.

   That licence is narrow and section 2 draws its edge.

1.  The shape of the program

   THE CORE KNOWS NOTHING ABOUT A HOST. Everything under src/
   except main_*.c renders into a Relic_Frame and stops. A host
   picks that up and puts it somewhere.

   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | File             | What it is                                 |
   +==================+============================================+
   | relic.h          | types, the 4-byte pixel, Relic_Frame       |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | image.h/.c       | the executable, addressed by IMAGE ADDRESS |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | gfx.h/.c         | the shared primitives - section 1.1        |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | host.h           | the boundary, and the music clock          |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | demo.c           | the frame-at-a-time object                 |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | parts.h/.c       | the part registry                          |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | space.h/.c       | the space backdrop - section 1.2           |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | sprout.h/.c      | the light's octree and its shading         |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | part_*.c         | one file per part                          |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | main_sdl.c       | host: a window                             |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | main_ppm.c       | host: PPM files, links only the CRT        |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | agent.h/.c       | the debug agent - subsystems/agent_mcp.txt |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+

   THE DEMO READS THE ORIGINAL FILES. image.c carries the segment
   map from formats/hunk.txt and resolves an image address, so a
   plate comment that names g_TerminalFrames at 0x00349e60 is
   fetched here by that same address. There is no intermediate asset
   format to keep in sync, and no converter of ours for a wrong
   reading to hide behind.

   1.1  Why there is a gfx.c at all

   A handful of routines and tables carry the demo's entire visual
   language. Distinct functions touching each, out of the database:

       g_SinTable       40      the sprite blitter   17
       g_PolarTable     24      g_BlobFalloff        15
       the RNG          19      Draw_GlowBlob        10
       Vbl_SetHook      18      Math_Isqrt           10
       g_LightSatLut    10      Ham8_BuildTables      9

   A program of some 940 functions runs everything it draws through
   about a dozen pieces. So ANYTHING A SECOND PART NEEDS MOVES INTO
   gfx.c rather than being written twice.

   1.2  The space backdrop

   SEVERAL SCENES SHARE ONE BACKDROP AND THE DEMO SHARES THE CODE FOR
   IT. Scene_DrawTexturedSky, Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun, the
   Sprite_StampSaveUnder and Sprite_RestoreSaveUnder pair,
   Scene_DrawPlanetAndHalo, Flare_Draw and
   maybe_Scene_DrawHorizonBand are called by the sun scene, by both
   scenes of maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes and by Space_RunPart, with
   different parameters each time - effects/approach.txt section 7.
   So space.c holds them and the part files hold the scenes.

   THE STEP ORDER IS NOT IN space.c. The demo has four frame routines
   that are permutations of the same steps - one substitutes the
   portal glow where another has the shadow sweep, one leaves the sun
   out entirely - so each gets its own C function in its own part
   file, per rule 17, and space.c exports only the steps.

   What a scene owns rather than shares: the base colour it passes
   Ham8_BuildTables, the sun and planet world positions, the camera
   angle and its rate, and any step with no counterpart elsewhere.

2.  What may be enhanced, and what may not

   The effects are RESOLUTION-PARAMETRIC: a frame carries its own
   size and a 16.16 scale against the demo's units, and every effect
   decides for itself what that scale means to its own terms. That
   is a judgement per effect and it is wanted - it is the whole
   point of the exercise.

   THE LINE: ENHANCE THE SAMPLING OF THE DEMO'S OWN DATA; NEVER
   SUBSTITUTE DATA OF OUR OWN.

   +-----------------------------------+--------------------------+
   | Allowed                           | Banned                   |
   +===================================+==========================+
   | interpolating between entries of  | inventing a palette, a   |
   | one of the demo's tables instead  | ramp or a colour that is |
   | of nearest-sampling it            | not located              |
   +-----------------------------------+--------------------------+
   | scaling a geometry constant with  | tuning a constant until  |
   | the resolution                    | it matches a screenshot  |
   +-----------------------------------+--------------------------+
   | carrying a value at higher        | changing a value because |
   | precision than 6 or 8 bits        | the original looks wrong |
   +-----------------------------------+--------------------------+
   | smoothing a gradient the demo     | adding an effect the     |
   | quantised                         | demo does not have       |
   +-----------------------------------+--------------------------+

   This does not loosen CLAUDE.md rule 12. Rule 12 bans inventing
   data, and it still does. What changes is that a HIGHER-PRECISION
   READING of located data is not an invention.

   SCALE 1.0 STAYS EXACT AND IS THE REFERENCE. A part whose output
   at scale 1.0 has drifted is broken, whatever it looks like zoomed
   in. tools/ctest.py enforces that by rendering through the PPM
   host and diffing against the verified Python in sim/, byte for
   byte.

   So every enhancement has to be expressible as a parameter that
   can be turned off, not baked into the arithmetic.

   THE TEMPLE'S TILES ARE SAMPLED BELOW THEIR OWN RESOLUTION, and
   that is a candidate for enhancement once the scenes are done. The
   forty tiles in Faztectextures are 64 by 64 apiece - see
   formats/relic_dat.txt - and the ruins draw them visibly blockier
   than that, so at least one axis is being decimated on the way to
   the screen. Sampling the tile at its own resolution is squarely
   inside section 2's rule: it resamples the demo's own data more
   finely and invents nothing. It is NOT to be done before the
   scenes are transcribed, because the decimation is the demo's
   behaviour and scale 1.0 has to keep reproducing it.

   2.1  Where the judgements already are

   The lightning showed the shape of the problem before any of this
   was written down. Beam_BuildBoltPath scales its jitter and its
   node radius by the bolt's own pixel length, so both follow the
   resolution for free. Draw_LineAA's three-pixel width and the
   radius floor of 4 do not - they are absolute. At 1x they are a
   large fraction of the frame and the bolt reads as a rope; at 8x
   they are hairlines and it reads as beads on a thread.

   Three pixels is three DEMO pixels, so the width follows the
   scale. Every effect carries one or two calls of that kind and
   each is written down where it is made.

   2.2  The table trap

   SCALE THE PIXEL GEOMETRY, NEVER THE TABLE DOMAINS. g_BlobFalloff
   is indexed [radius << 8 | dist] with the radius masked to 0x7f,
   so a radius-30 blob at 8x would run off the end of it at 240.
   Divide the pixel offset by the scale BEFORE the lookup instead:
   that resamples the demo's own table and keeps every index inside
   the range the demo uses.

3.  One frame at a time, because of the browser

   THE WHOLE DEMO IS ONE FUNCTION THAT RENDERS ONE FRAME AND
   RETURNS. Nothing under src/ outside main_*.c may loop waiting for
   anything, block, sleep or read input.

       native   while (running) tick();
       web      emscripten_set_main_loop(tick, 0, 1);

   A browser owns its own event loop. A `while (1)` inside the demo
   never gives control back and the page hangs; Asyncify can rewrite
   around that, at a cost in size and speed, but designing for it is
   free and retrofitting is not.

   THE ORIGINAL IS FULL OF BLOCKING WAITS, which is the work this
   implies. Space_RunPart is a sequence of `render ; present ; test
   a flag ; branch back`, and SpaceModule_Part0 blocks on g_IntroDone
   until the opening text has finished typing. Every one of those
   becomes a state machine that advances by one step per frame.

   A FRAME IS A PAL VERTICAL BLANK, AND THE HOST'S REFRESH IS NOT
   ONE. Vbl_Server (0x0021f9b0) is the VERTB handler every timed
   thing in RELIC counts, so a tick is 1/50 s and nothing else
   [verified]. Hosts therefore do not call Demo_Frame; they call
   Demo_Advance with the milliseconds that have passed, and it steps
   the state zero or more times - zero on a 60 Hz display one frame
   in six - then draws exactly one picture.

   STEPPING AND DRAWING ARE SEPARATE CALLS. A part that carries
   per-tick state says so by setting Relic_Part.step, which advances
   that state and draws nothing. Catching up by calling frame() per
   tick instead is the obvious reading and it feeds back: once a
   frame costs more than the 20 ms it stands for, one call draws two
   or three pictures, which makes the next delta larger still, and
   the demo settles at whatever rate it can draw rather than at 50
   Hz. The sun scene ran at a quarter speed that way. Split, its
   tick rate holds at 50.0 from 1000 presented frames a second down
   to 13.

   CALLING Demo_Frame ONCE PER PRESENTED FRAME LOOKS RIGHT AND RUNS
   THE DEMO AT THE HOST'S RATE. An unsynced SDL loop manages a few
   hundred frames a second, so the intro's cursor blinked at about
   twice its real 3.1 Hz - fast enough to see, but only against the
   original. Worse, the rate then varies by machine, so a part's
   timing stops being reproducible. Catching up after a stall is
   capped for the same reason: a dragged window must not
   fast-forward the demo to make the time back.

   AUDIO IS NOT RENDERED INSIDE THE FRAME CALL. SDL pulls audio
   through its own callback - a separate thread natively, Web Audio
   under emscripten - so it is a second entry point, not something
   the frame function does. The frame function must not assume it
   knows how much audio has been consumed; it asks the clock in
   section 4.

3.1.  Fast forward skips the drawing, not the state

   THE OBVIOUS LOOP IS THE SLOW ONE. Calling Demo_Frame twenty times
   a pass gives twenty renders, and every part here is a per-pixel
   loop - on the temple or the city at any real window size that
   costs more than running at speed, so the fast forward comes out
   slower than the demo. Demo_Skip steps instead: the state advances
   N times and the picture is drawn at the end.

   THE TAIL OF THE RUN IS STILL DRAWN, AND THAT IS NOT A NICETY.
   The temporal filters - Temple_BlurFrame, Space_BlurFrame and the
   stills module's - keep their history in frame(), not in step(),
   and each averages against the frame before it. Landing on one
   drawn frame leaves that history empty and the picture arrives at
   half its light, which is the same trap as scoring a one-frame
   render. Measured on the ark against hardware, drawing the last N
   of a run [observed]:

       +----+-------+-------+-------+-------+
       |  N |     R |     G | LIGHT |     B |
       +====+=======+=======+=======+=======+
       |  1 | 16.53 | 14.50 | 14.70 | 12.89 |
       |  4 |  2.80 |  2.48 |  2.20 |  2.26 |
       |  8 |  1.99 |  1.70 |  1.38 |  1.45 |
       | 16 |  2.01 |  1.70 |  1.37 |  1.44 |
       | 40 |  2.01 |  1.70 |  1.38 |  1.44 |
       +----+-------+-------+-------+-------+

   Eight is converged - 16 and 40 differ from it only in the
   rounding - so SKIP_DRAW is 8.

   A PART WITH NO step() CANNOT SKIP AT ALL, because its frame() IS
   its state. Four are built that way - the three logos and the
   terminal - and they tick through Demo_Frame like everything else,
   which is affordable only because they are the cheap ones.

   THE HANDOVER ENDS THE PASS, as it does in Demo_Advance: the next
   part starts from its own tick 0, so the count still in hand is
   not its to spend. At part 2.12b's 792 the pass steps the twelve
   ticks that are left, hands over, and draws the remaining eight
   into the new part [observed].

3.2.  The frame's width is rounded up to a multiple of four

   TWO THINGS STACK HERE AND EITHER ALONE IS HARMLESS.

   FX_DIV TRUNCATES, so the scale that fits a 1280 x 720 window is
   471859 and not 7.2 - near enough to the eye, but 160 times it is
   1151 rather than 1152. The frame comes out a pixel short in both
   axes and the host stretches it over the window to make the
   difference up.

   AND THREE BYTES A PIXEL TIMES AN ODD WIDTH IS AN ODD PITCH. A
   host handing a frame to a texture passes the row length as a
   pitch, and 1151 x 3 is 3453 - not a multiple of four, which is an
   alignment no driver is obliged to take. What it reads as the next
   row then starts a few bytes early, and the picture skews by a
   little more on every row.

   The two coincide: the windows where the truncation bites are
   exactly the ones that land on an odd width. 16:9 does it - 1280 x
   720, 1920 x 1080 and 1024 x 640 all give a width ending in 1 or 7
   - and 16:10 does not, so 1280 x 800, 800 x 500 and 640 x 400 come
   out clean. THAT IS WHY RESIZING MOVED IT rather than curing it,
   and why the demo's own default window was in the bad case.

   Rounding the width up costs one column of scene at the right and
   makes every pitch four-aligned. The height is left alone: nothing
   indexes a frame by column.

3.3.  The pointer arrives as a gesture, not as a camera

   A HOST REPORTS WHAT THE USER DID; A PART DECIDES WHAT IT MEANS.
   src/host.h carries five things and interprets none of them: the
   pointer's button together with how far it has moved since that
   button went down, as a 16.16 fraction of the window's half width
   and half height; a running count of wheel notches; a running count
   of "next"; the keys a host is holding, under generic names; and a
   one-frame request for rest.

   THE KEYBOARD'S TWO CHANNELS ARE SHAPED LIKE THE POINTER'S. A count
   of "next" is the wheel's form - limited nowhere, because how many
   things there are to cycle through is a part's business - and the
   held set is the drag button's, a state the host maintains and a
   part reads. Neither says what it is for, so a host binds a key
   without learning what the key does. DEMO_HOLD_A and DEMO_HOLD_B
   are named for nothing on purpose.

   THAT IS THE WHOLE BOUNDARY, AND IT IS NOT NAMED AFTER A CAMERA.
   Naming it for one part's use of it is what makes a host look as
   though it knows the demo's insides, and it does not scale: a
   second part wanting different behaviour from the same gesture
   would have to be routed by the host, or spell its own drag
   bookkeeping out again. Two readings of the one gesture exist and
   neither is the host's business:

      Space_Look           springs it back on release and maps full
                           deflection to 0x400 of camera yaw -
                           effects/approach.txt section 8
      src/part_credits.c   keeps it where it was left and maps it to
                           an orbit about a measured pivot -
                           section 7

   WHAT A HOST STILL OWNS is the four things that are genuinely about
   a window: which button starts a drag, the press point the
   deflection is measured from, the division by the window's half
   size, and ending the drag when focus is lost - the button-up is
   then delivered elsewhere and never arrives, and the gesture would
   read as held for good. A HELD KEY IS LOST THE SAME WAY and is
   cleared with it.

   A RELEASED DRAG EASES HOME IN THE CORE, on a 180 ms time constant
   in src/demo.c, because how the gesture feels is the demo's and a
   second host would otherwise have to reproduce it. A time constant
   rather than a fraction a frame, so the swing takes the same time
   whatever the host presents at; Demo_Advance already carries the
   host's elapsed milliseconds, so nothing new crosses the boundary
   to drive it.

   A MAPPING IS PULLED BY A PART, NEVER PUSHED BY THE HOST, which is
   what keeps one part's control out of another's. The orbit advances
   only when src/part_credits.c asks for it, so a drag during the
   approach cannot wind it, and the wheel count and the rest request
   are latched on entry for the same reason - a notch spun during
   another scene is not left waiting as a backlog.

   ZERO AND UP UNLESS A HOST SETS ONE. src/main_ppm.c sets none of
   the three, so the reference renders and the hardware comparisons
   are untouched.

3.4.  Anything that accumulates belongs to step()

   THE DEMO HAS ONE CLOCK AND THE PORT HAS TWO, and that is the whole
   of this. RELIC renders once a tick, so a counter the demo advances
   inside its render advances once a tick by construction; there is
   no other rate for it to follow. Section 3 split the two on purpose
   - step() runs at 50 Hz whatever the host manages and frame() once
   per presented frame - which is what keeps the demo's speed right
   on a display that is not 50 Hz.

   SO TRANSCRIBING BY LOCATION PUTS THE COUNTER ON THE WRONG CLOCK.
   The faithful place in the listing is the render; the faithful
   place here is step(). A counter left in the draw advances with the
   display: faster in a small window, slower in a large one, and
   unevenly when the two rates do not divide.

   WORSE THAN EITHER IS SPLITTING IT. A counter advanced partly in
   the draw and partly in the step gains a different amount on a tick
   that drew twice than on one that drew once, so what should be a
   steady beat stutters - a pause, then a rush. That is what the orb
   scene's flare strobe did until g_FlareSizeStep moved wholly into
   step(): effects/orb.txt 11.5 has the measurement.

   THE ONES FOUND SO FAR, all now in step():

      src/orbscene.c       g_FlareSizeStep, the flare strobe
      src/part_valley.c    the birds' wing phases and their drift
      src/part_explosion.c the ring scroll's two phases

   A COUNT THE DRAW PRODUCES NEEDS AN ARM. Where the tick's advance
   depends on something only a draw can know - how many slots read
   the counter - exactly one frame a tick may supply it, because a
   tick can draw twice or not at all. src/orbscene.c arms that in
   step() and takes the count from the first frame after. Without the
   arm the first tick's advance depended on whether a frame or a step
   came first, which set the flare phase for the whole scene from the
   host's frame rate.

   STILL IN THE DRAW, AND KNOWN: Space_EaseRange and Space_StepBand
   both ease once per call - g_sun_range by a quarter, g_band_now by
   a half - and every caller is a frame routine, in 2.1, 2.3b, 2.4,
   2.7 and the orb scenes. Over the same 1400 ticks of scene time
   they run 186 times at 33 ms a frame, 307 at 20 and 615 at 10
   [observed].

   IT SHOWS IN THE ORB SCENES AND NOWHERE ELSE SO FAR. Rendered at a
   matched tick, 2.3b, 2.4 and 2.7 come out byte for byte the same at
   10, 17, 20 and 25 ms a frame: their target holds still long enough
   that easing it oftener lands on the same value. 2.11's band does
   not - its target is the flare's radius, which moves every tick -
   so about 500 pixels across the band's seven visible rows differ at
   17 ms on roughly one tick in three [observed]. They are harder to
   move than the others because the count they ease toward,
   Space_CountSaveUnder, measures the drawn frame: the measurement
   belongs in the draw and only the easing should move.

   THE TEST, for anything new: does a file-scope value get +=, ++ or
   -- inside a frame routine? If it does, and it is not a render
   buffer being filled, it is on the wrong clock.

3.5.  A page's handles on the audio, and why it polls

   A BROWSER IS A SECOND HOST AND NOT A SECOND BOUNDARY. src/host.h
   is what the demo offers a host; this is what ONE host offers the
   page wrapped around it, and it lives in src/main_sdl.c with the
   window and the keys rather than anywhere under the core. Four
   calls, all on two values the m and +/- keys already move:

      Host_SetMute(int)     Host_Muted(void)
      Host_SetVolume(int)   Host_Volume(void)

   THE PAGE POLLS AND IS NEVER TOLD. A control pressed in the demo
   has to reach the page's checkbox and slider, and the obvious way
   - calling out of C when a key is pressed - couples this file to a
   function name in a document it should know nothing about, and
   needs a guard around every such call. Reading the two getters on
   a timer costs nothing, keeps the traffic one way, and leaves the
   demo's own keys the single source of truth.

   MUTE AND LEVEL ARE KEPT APART HERE, where the keyboard's + also
   clears mute. A page has two controls and one should not move the
   other; the poll shows whatever the keys then do.

4.  The timeline runs on the music

   0x0021ff3c arms a position in the module and 0x0021ff64 polls
   whether the replayer has reached it [verified]. Scenes END ON THE
   MUSIC, not on a frame count - maybe_Space_RunSunScene and several
   steps of Space_RunPart loop on exactly that.

   So the clock is an interface from the first commit, even with
   nothing playing:

       Music_Arm(order, row, fallback)  ORIGIN 0x0021ff3c
       Music_Reached(elapsed)           ORIGIN 0x0021ff64
       Music_BreakTimeline()            ours - see below
       Music_Tick()                     monotonic replayer ticks
       Music_Advance(ms)                a silent clock steps here

   EVERY GATE CARRIES THE LENGTH TO USE WHEN THE MUSIC CANNOT
   ANSWER, and elapsed is the PART'S OWN ticks since the arm rather
   than the music clock's - because the hosts that need the fallback
   are exactly the ones that never advance a music clock.

   music_silent.c was the first implementation. It plays nothing and
   advances at six ticks a row, fifty ticks a second - Protracker's
   defaults, which are also PAL's frame rate, which is why a demo of
   this era can treat the two as one clock. The timeline runs true
   without a note being played.

   NO AUDIO CODE IS BEING REVERSE ENGINEERED. Player 6.1 is a
   documented third-party replayer and reproducing it is not what
   this project is for - subsystems/music.txt.

4.1.  ibxm sits behind the same four calls

   src/music_ibxm.c is the second implementation and the one that
   builds. It keeps the silent clock for when no module is loaded,
   so the timeline is unchanged until one is, and hands the mixing
   to ibxm when one is - a public-domain ProTracker, S3M and XM
   replayer, src/third_party/ibxm, carried as it came.

   THE CUE IS A DIRECT TRANSLATION, NOT A STAND-IN. ibxm exports
   replay_get_sequence_pos and replay_get_row, which are exactly the
   two words 0x0021ffa2 and 0x0021ffa6 the demo's own replayer
   compares an armed pair against at 0x00220dec [verified], and
   seq_pos indexes module->sequence, so it is a song position and
   not a pattern number. Music_Reached is therefore the demo's own
   test, and the measured lengths became its fallbacks.

EVERY ARM IS ANSWERED, BY A POLL OR BY A WAIT. 24 sites arm a
   cue, 18 poll one at Music_PollCue and 6 block on it at
   Music_WaitCue, and 18 + 6 is 24 [verified, read off every site].
   The arm immediately before each answer carries the pair, D0 the
   order and D1 the row. Seventeen of the eighteen polls are wired;
   the waits are section 4.5.

       +---------------+----------+--------+----------------------+
       | poll          | cue      | arm    | the port's phase     |
       +===============+==========+========+======================+
       | 002ce90c sun  | 0x02,0x18| 002ce8f8| part 1 ends         |
       | 002cd250      | 0x04, 0  | 002cd220| NOT REPRESENTED     |
       | 002cd280      | 0x04,0x10| 002cd270| part 3 ends         |
       | 002cd3d6      | 0x08, 0  | 002cd39a| part 4 ends         |

       | 002cc8bc      | 0x1e,0x34| 002cc8ac| part 9's pan ends   |
       | 002cc90a      | 0x20, 0  | 002cc8fa| part 9's clamp ends |
       | 002cc158 eart | 0x29, 0  | 002cc148| the earth's phase 1 |
       | 003138a6 sand | 0x01,0x10| 0031388a| part 13 ends        |
       | 0036deae      | 0x05, 0  | 0036de92| the temple's shot 1 |
       | 0036c144      | 0x09,0x32| 0036c136| the descent         |

   THE ONE THAT IS NOT WIRED HAS NOWHERE TO GO.
   maybe_Space_RunPlanetScenes runs three stages and our part 3 is
   named for two of them - "Planet lit, AND THE LIGHT" - so the
   boundary 0x04,0 would separate is inside it. Representing that
   cue means splitting the part first.

   ELEVEN OF THEM ARE THE SAME SHAPE: one cue, armed on the part's
   first tick and ending the part. They share Relic_Gate and
   Parts_GateStep in parts.c rather than each spelling it out. The
   temple's three, the return flight's two and the earth's are the
   phase-internal ones and stay hand written.

   EVERY FALLBACK IS WHAT THE PART'S LENGTH ALREADY WAS, so a silent
   host and a broken timeline reproduce the old behaviour exactly -
   checked part by part, all 21 lengths unchanged.

   LANDMINE: A GATE MUST BE ABLE TO LENGTHEN A PART, NOT ONLY SHORTEN
   IT. Parts_GateLength first returned the gate's tick or the
   fallback, which quietly abandons any cue that has not landed by
   the fallback - and the demo's poll is a LOOP, drawing until its
   cue arrives however long that takes. Measured against our own
   music, the city's cue fires 331 ticks after its part had already
   ended on the fallback and the destruction's 375, so both scenes
   ran early and the explosion's bang arrived seven seconds late.
   While a live cue is still pending the gate now reports
   PARTS_GATE_OPEN, which handover reads as open-ended and a phase
   test reads as not yet; Music_CueLive is the test for whether a cue
   can still answer at all, and it is false on a silent host, which
   is what keeps the fallbacks exact there.

   WHICH TURNS A CUT INTO A HOLD. A part whose picture arrives early
   now waits on its cue instead of handing over, so the error shows
   as a scene running long rather than as the next one starting
   wrong, and the sound lands where it belongs either way. The cure
   for the length itself is the upstream deficit: our picture reaches
   the city 331 ticks ahead of the music, and the temple at 6922
   ticks is the largest unmeasured span between Cmodule2's start and
   there.

   A PART THAT ENDS ON A CUE CANNOT HAVE A FIXED LENGTH, so
   Relic_Part gained an optional length() beside its ticks and the
   handover prefers it. The temple's and the return flight's report
   where their last cue actually fell.

   THE CUE IS ONLY BELIEVED WHEN TWO THINGS HOLD.

   -  THE TIMELINE IS INTACT. A cue is an absolute song position, so
      it means nothing once the run stops being continuous from part
      0: Demo_GotoPart, Demo_SeekTick and a long Demo_Skip all call
      Music_BreakTimeline and the fallbacks serve the rest of the
      run. Skipping therefore costs music sync, which is accepted.

   -  AUDIO IS ACTUALLY BEING PULLED. LANDMINE: ibxm's position only
      advances inside replay_get_audio, so a host that loads a
      module and opens no device leaves it at 0 for ever and a cue
      gate would HANG a part rather than fail. Music_Reached counts
      Demo_Audio calls and takes the fallback unless the count has
      moved since the arm. main_sdl.c is the only host that pulls,
      so every relic_ppm render takes the fallback and is
      reproducible.

   THE TUNES ARE P61 AND ARE DEPACKED ON THE WAY IN. Cmodule1 and
   Cmodule2 of relic.dat are Player 6.1's own packed format - a
   sample-data offset, then 39 patterns and 30 samples each
   [observed] - which ibxm does not read. Music_Load tries
   P61A_Test first and depacks when it answers, so the archive's
   chunk goes straight in and there is no converted file anywhere.

   src/third_party/prowizard/p61a.c IS LIBXMP'S DEPACKER, MADE
   SELF-CONTAINED. Its two functions are carried unchanged; what
   this project supplies is the shell - a reader over a buffer
   where libxmp passes a file handle, a growable writer where it
   passes a FILE, and the two tables prowiz.h held. No disk is
   touched at any point.

   IT PRODUCES REAL PROTRACKER MODULES [observed]:

       +----------+-----------+--------+--------+-----------+
       | chunk    | depacked  | magic  | orders | duration  |
       +==========+===========+========+========+===========+
       | Cmodule1 |  859190   | M.K.   |   42   |  206.4 s  |
       | Cmodule2 |  966814   | M.K.   |   43   |  272.7 s  |
       +----------+-----------+--------+--------+-----------+

   AND THE CUE ORDERS CHECK OUT, which is the test that the depack
   is right rather than merely plausible: the five the demo arms
   are 31, 9, 31, 36 and 36, and every one falls inside a song of
   42 or 43. A depack that came out with twelve patterns would be
   wrong whatever it sounded like.

   NOT THE COMPOSER'S FILE. P61con strips unused patterns and
   samples and re-encodes effects, so what comes back plays the
   same and is not the original - the titles come back blank
   because P61 does not keep them.

   THE HOST'S SIDE IS A QUEUE, NOT A CALLBACK. Demo_Audio is called
   from main_sdl.c's own loop and topped up to about 125 ms, which
   is what stops fast forward outrunning the device - the top-up
   stops at its target however many ticks a pass ran. Volume is
   applied where the samples are queued and not in the mixer, so
   muting never costs the replayer its place.

   WHICH MAKES THE PULL THE TRANSPORT CONTROL. Because the position
   moves only inside Demo_Audio, the host steers the song by whether
   it pulls:

      pause       stop pulling and stop the device. The song freezes
                  where it stands and resuming carries on from it.
      step        pull ONE field, AUDIO_RATE / 50 pairs, and discard
                  them. The tick and the song move together, so the
                  picture cannot walk away from the score.
      fast fwd    keep pulling at the device's own rate. The ticks
                  outrun it and the sync is lost, which is accepted.

   A STEP THAT PULLS NOTHING IS THE BUG THIS PREVENTS: the song sits
   at the tick the first pause landed on while the picture advances,
   and every cue gate after it answers against a stale position.

4.2.  One tune an act, and a third one for the typing

   THE ACT LOADS THE TUNE. Demo_RunAct1 0x0021f7f4 loads the chunk
   named at 0x2378ec and Demo_RunAct2 0x0021f82a the one at
   0x2378fc, and those two names are Cmodule1 and Cmodule2
   [verified]. Act one is SpaceModule_Run(0) - everything to the
   orb's fall - and act two is the other six module calls, so the
   switch falls between the Earth shot and the sand, which is
   part 2.13 in the running order.

   CMODULE3 IS NAMED AT 0x0023790c AND IS NOT THERE. Nothing
   references it and relic.dat does not carry it - a third tune
   that did not survive, left in the name table.

THERE IS A THIRD MODULE AND IT IS IN THE EXECUTABLE.
   g_IntroModule 0x002c8084, file offset 0x000a8db4 of hunk CODE_02,
   is a P61 module Demo_Run points the replayer at from 0x0021f78a
   and starts on FIELD 32 - before anything has been loaded
   [verified]. Cmodule1 does not take over until field 275, when
   Terminal_Tick raises g_IntroDone and SpaceModule_Part0 calls
   Music_StartModule again at 0x002c836c [observed, both frames off
   a breakpoint on a clean run].

   SO THE TYPING HAS MUSIC, JUST NOT CMODULE1'S. The clicking under
   the terminal is the embedded module's own first rows; nothing in
   the terminal pokes Paula, and its one audio-looking write - #1 to
   g_SceneDisplayUp at 0x0033bcfc - goes to a word no code reads.
   TERM_MUSIC_AT 242 is right for Cmodule1 because the terminal's own
   script ends there; what the port is missing is the intro module,
   so it plays Cmodule1 under the typing instead.

   LANDMINE: THE NAME TABLE READS AS IF THERE WERE ONLY TWO TUNES.
   0x002378ec lists Cmodule1 to Cmodule4 and the archive holds 1 and
   2, which invites the conclusion that the intro is silent. The
   intro's tune is not a chunk at all.

   THE 32 IS THE DEMO'S START-UP AND IS NOT PART OF THE TIMING. Two
   measured numbers place it: Cmodule1 takes over on field 275, and
   the terminal's own script reaches that point on ITS tick 242, so
   the terminal's tick 0 is field 33 - one field AFTER the replayer
   started. The music and the first character arrive together. A port
   with no start-up therefore begins the intro module at its own tick
   0; starting it at 32 puts the clicking two thirds of a second
   behind the typing.

   LANDMINE: THE TERMINAL DOES NOT STOP THERE. The same branch
   winds the cursor back 0x10 and carries on, so the typing loops
   until the act moves on. The flag is raised once; the text is not
   the clock.

   Demo_GotoPart follows the act and music_follow the tick, which
   is what lets a part start its own tune partway through itself.
   Skipping into any part picks up that part's tune, so jumping
   about does not leave the wrong one playing. TERM_MUSIC_AT carries
   the measured 32.


4.3.  P61's speed word, and what the depacker does with F

NO CIA TIMER IS PROGRAMMED, AND THE TEMPO IS STILL HONOURED.
   Nothing in 0x0021ff00 - 0x00222000 writes a CIA timer register -
   the only 0xbf access anywhere in the replayer is 0x00bfe001, the
   LED and audio-filter bits - and no scene calls a per-frame music
   routine either: every call in from the demo's code is
   Music_ArmCue, Music_PollCue or 0x0021ff50 [verified]. P61 counts
   the tempo out in software instead.

   LANDMINE: "NO TIMER" DOES NOT MEAN "ONE TICK A FIELD". The intro
   module settles it, because its row 0 carries F0F beside F40 -
   speed 15 at 64 BPM. Ignoring the tempo would make its rows 15
   fields long; honouring it makes them 15 / (64 * 2 / 5) = 29.3.
   The demo takes 30 [observed, row 1 on field 61 and row 2 on 91].
   Its rows 3 and on carry F02 beside F80, predicting 1.95 fields a
   row against a measured 1.94. So P61 implements the full
   ProTracker model and IBXM MATCHES IT TO ABOUT ONE PER CENT on
   this module. Stripping the tempo commands, which an earlier
   reading of the missing CIA suggested, is wrong.

   THE SPEED WORD IS g_P61Speed 0x002217b0, seeded to 6 by P61_Init.
   LANDMINE: its static xref list shows that init as the only
   writer, because the replayer's own stores reach it through A3 and
   Ghidra cannot resolve them. It does change, and reading it live is
   the only way to see the module's speed.

   P61 IS NOT A SUPERSET OF PROTRACKER. P61con compresses a
   ProTracker module; the effect set is ProTracker's, re-encoded.
   p61a.c takes the effect as c1 & 0x0f and the parameter as c2
   straight through, with three special cases and no handling of F at
   all: effect 8 is folded to 0, and 5, 6 and 0x0a get their
   parameter's sign unpacked. So there is nothing in the F
   translation to correct, and the depacked Cmodule1 is
   self-consistent - 42 orders, all 39 patterns referenced, and its
   only F parameters anywhere are 0, 2, 3, 6, 100 and 126.

   WHICH IS HOW THE INTRO MODULE WAS FOUND. g_P61Speed reads 15
   through the terminal's first rows and no F0F exists in Cmodule1,
   so what was playing could not have been Cmodule1 - and the live
   module's header matched neither archive chunk but did match the
   executable at file 0x000a8db4. Section 4.2.

CMODULE1 ITSELF STILL RUNS FAST IN OUR PORT. From its real start
   at field 275 the demo reaches cue 1 at 02.1a on field 1138, 863
   fields later, where ibxm takes 546 from a standing start. Both
   sides read speed 2 at order 0 and 6 from order 1, and 84 rows in
   500 fields in the speed-6 stretch, so the gap is in the head of
   the module. UNMEASURED, and the intro module's agreement says it
   is worth measuring the same way: P61's speed and row sampled
   through Cmodule1's first two orders, against ibxm's.


4.4.  The tunes are parsed once, and the queue is latency

   MUSIC_PRELOAD PARSES ALL THREE AT THE HOST'S START-UP. Switching
   act used to depack a Cmodule inside the frame that asked for it -
   822 KB through a byte-at-a-time reader - so the main loop stopped
   topping the device up, the queue ran dry, and the handover
   arrived with a thud and the next tune behind where it belonged.
   g_tune owns the three parsed modules for the run; a switch is a
   new_replay and a rewind.

   A HOST WITH NO AUDIO MUST NOT CALL IT, which is why it is not in
   Demo_Init: relic_ppm would pay the whole depack on every render
   for a tune it never plays. Without it Music_Start finds nothing,
   Music_Playing stays false, and every gate runs on its fallback -
   which is what keeps a PPM render reproducible.

   THE QUEUE DEPTH IS HEARD AS DELAY. Everything reaches the device
   AUDIO_TARGET behind the mix, and at an eighth of a second that is
   6 PAL fields - enough that the typewriter audibly trails the
   typing it is supposed to land with. Three fields, 60 ms, still
   leaves two frames of slack at 60 Hz, and nothing stalls the loop
   long enough to drain it now the tunes are parsed up front.

   OPEN: THE SWITCH STILL STEPS. Music_Start drops the live replay
   and builds another, and the samples already queued end wherever
   the old tune left them, so the join is a discontinuity rather
   than a ramp. A few milliseconds of fade either side would settle
   it; nothing in the demo needs one, because Paula just carries on.


4.5.  Holds and waits, and how a span is measured

   A SCENE CAN RUN FOR SECONDS BEFORE IT MOVES. Space_RunSunScene is
   entered on field 273 - the field Cmodule1 starts - and g_SunWorldX
   0x002e0c28 holds its -400 entry value until field 388, where it
   begins stepping +2 a field. It fits -400 + 2 * (field - 388)
   exactly at both 500 and 600 [observed]. So 115 fields pass with
   the music running and the render target black while the scene
   builds its tables. A port with no tables to build has to hold that
   span deliberately or its sun leads the score by two and a third
   seconds.

   THE DEMO DOES NOT MISS A VBL, and the belief that it did was an
   artifact of measuring under warp - which starves the scene of
   cycles while the music keeps absolute time, so it manufactures
   this exact symptom. g_VblTotal 0x00237236 is the demo's own field
   count and it stays a constant 29 behind the emulator's PAL field:
   29 at 10368 and 29 at 22067, across the heaviest scenes it has
   [observed]. g_SunWorldX steps 2.00 units a field over 500 to 600,
   not the 1.11 a warp run showed.

   SO A FIELD COUNT AND A MUSIC POSITION ARE INTERCHANGEABLE, and our
   tick may be compared against the demo's field directly. Over the
   sixteen spans between consecutive cue fires that lie within one
   module, our ibxm agrees with the demo's own replayer to 20813
   fields against 20817 - four fields in 437 seconds, and every
   single span within eleven [observed]. relic_ppm -cues dumps the
   row timing that measurement is made from.

   WHAT IS BANNED IS A WARP-DERIVED NUMBER, which describes neither
   the scene nor the music. A span taken that way is re-measured, not
   adjusted.

   AND THE HOLDS ARE CUES TOO. Music_WaitCue 0x0021ff50 is the
   blocking half of the pair: where Music_PollCue tests g_MusicCueHit
   and returns either way, this SPINS on it, with bit 6 of 0x00bfe001
   - the fire button - as the only escape [verified]. That is what
   the six arms with no Music_PollCue after them are for. They are
   not dead; they are waited on.

       +---------------------------+----------+------------+
       | scene                     | arm      | waits at   |
       +===========================+==========+============+
       | Space_RunSunScene         | 002ce8d8 | 002ce8de   |
       | maybe_Space_RunWindowScene| 002ccfea | 002ccff0   |
       | Space_RunEarth            | 002cc1dc | 002cc1e2   |
       | Temple_RunShot1           | 0036df90 | 0036df96   |
       +---------------------------+----------+------------+

   SO A SCENE IS OFTEN A HOLD AND THEN A RUN, and the two cues share
   the one armed slot in turn: the window scene arms (0x0c,0), waits,
   and only then arms the (0x15,0) that ends it. Its photograph is
   held for 480 fields that way - 479 by our own replayer, 0a.00 to
   the armed 0c.00, with g_PullBackVel 0x002e0be2 leaving zero as it
   releases - and the
   pull-back that follows matches ours step for step, g_PullBackScaled
   reading -5851 ten steps in on both sides [observed].

   A PORT THAT RUNS THE SCENE STRAIGHT THROUGH IS THEREFORE SHORT AT
   THE FRONT AND LONG AT THE BACK, which is what makes a held still
   leave early and everything downstream arrive late.

   LANDMINE: A POLL INSIDE A PART'S OWN ADDRESS RANGE ENDS A PHASE,
   NOT THE PART. Space_RunPart holds three of them and spans parts 8,
   9 and 10, and only the first sits before part 9's 0x002cc840. The
   other two are +0x32c and +0x370 into it - they close the camera
   pan and the claw clamp in turn - and part 10 has no cue at all.
   Ending the PART on the first of them drops the clamp and the
   flash, so the shot cuts mid-pan straight to the side-on
   explosion, and part 10 then finds its cue already passed and
   lasts no time at all. Pair every poll with the part whose address
   range CONTAINS it before wiring it.

   WHERE THE GATES STAND, measured against our own music: parts 1, 4,
   6 and 8 reach their cue within 1 to 28 ticks of their fallback, so
   the holds have brought them into line. Part 9's two phases answer
   at 1419 and 292 against fallbacks of 1394 and 285.

   THE VALLEY WAS THE SAME MISTAKE AGAIN. Valley_RunScene spans the
   valley and the city, and its first poll 0x00314426 is inside the
   valley's own 0x00314304 to 0x0031460c - it closes the OPENING, the
   loop VY_OPEN_TICKS stands for, and the part's own comment said as
   much. Ending the part there released no rock, ran no impact and
   cut straight to the city. Its second poll 0x00314858 sits past all
   three of the city's shot thresholds - 0x0031460c, 0x003146e0 and
   0x003147fa - so THAT one does end its part.

   THE EARTH'S HOLD IS A WAIT, NOT A CONSTANT: Space_RunEarth arms
   (0x29,0x3e) at 0x002cc1dc and blocks on it at 0x002cc1e2, which is
   what EA_HOLD stood in for.

   A WAIT CAN BE NEARLY NOTHING AND STILL MATTER. Temple_RunShot1
   arms (0x0a,0) at 0x0036df90 and blocks at 0x0036df96, returning at
   0x0036df9c - measured, the block begins on field 15242 and ends on
   15255, THIRTEEN fields [observed], because the descent's own cue
   had already carried the music to 0x0a. It is a RESYNC POINT rather
   than a pace: in a clean run it costs nothing, and it absorbs
   whatever the picture has gained when the run is not clean. That is
   why a scene can drift and the one after it still land right.


4.6.  Seeking the replayer, and what a jump is worth

   ONE PRIMITIVE SERVES A SEEK AND A FAST FORWARD: advance the
   replayer and drop what it hands back. ibxm produces one tick of
   audio a call, so Music_DrainTicks counts calls and Music_DrainTo
   keeps calling until the position has passed a pair - the same two
   floors Music_Reached tests.

   LANDMINE: replay_set_sequence_pos RESETS THE TEMPO. ibxm says so
   itself, and it is fatal here: RELIC's modules set their speed and
   tempo in their first two orders - F02 with F7e, then F06 - so a
   jump to order 31 starts at the default 6 and 125 BPM and plays
   every row after it at the wrong rate until the next F command.
   Music_SeekTo therefore rewinds the tune and DRAINS from its start,
   which costs only mixing: a whole module is a fraction of a second
   once the pre-parse of section 4.4 has done the depack.

   A MARK CARRIES WHERE THE MUSIC STANDS. Relic_Mark gained tune,
   order and row, and a jump drains the replayer to them, so the run
   from that mark is the run a full play-through would have had. ONLY
   THE BOUNDARIES A CUE DECIDES CAN SAY: a part that follows a
   counter-ended one - a logo screen, the debris - has no position of
   its own to state, so its mark carries tune -1, seeks nothing, and
   reports that it did not.

       +------+---------------------+------+-------+
       | mark | scene               | tune | pair  |
       +======+=====================+======+=======+
       |  p0  | terminal            | intro| 00.00 |
       |  p1  | planet and sun      |   0  | 00.00 |
       |  p2  | logo one            |   0  | 02.18 |
       |  p4  | asteroid            |   0  | 04.10 |
       |  p5  | logo two            |   0  | 08.00 |
       |  p7  | logo three          |   0  | 15.00 |
       |  p9  | the clamp           |   0  | 19.00 |
       | p10  | ship blows up       |   0  | 20.00 |
       | p14  | the temple          |   1  | 01.10 |
       | 14b  | the descent         |   1  | 05.00 |
       | 14c  | the orb             |   1  | 09.32 |
       | p15  | ship departing      |   1  | 1f.28 |
       | p16  | debris              |   1  | 24.24 |
       | p19  | planet destroyed    |   1  | 26.2e |
       | p20  | the ark             |   1  | 27.3c |
       +------+---------------------+------+-------+

   EACH PAIR IS THE PREVIOUS PART'S OWN ARMED CUE, not a figure
   measured for the mark - so a span that changes cannot leave one
   stale, and nothing here is a number of ours.

   A SEEKED JUMP LEAVES THE TIMELINE INTACT, which is the whole point
   of it: Music_BreakTimeline would put every gate after the jump on
   its fallback, and the fallbacks are what the jump was meant to get
   away from. A jump that could not seek breaks it as before.
   Demo_MusicLine reports which happened and prints the position it
   actually landed on, so the claim is checkable rather than trusted.

   LANDMINE: LEAVING IT INTACT IS NOT ENOUGH - LANDING HAS TO MEND
   IT. The break was one way: nothing set g_timeline back, so a
   single jump to a mark that could not seek put the WHOLE SESSION on
   fallbacks, and every accurate seek afterwards reported itself in
   sync and then drifted exactly as though it had never seeked.

   It reads as a scene running fast rather than as a broken flag,
   which is what makes it worth writing down. The return flight is
   the clearest case: its swirl waits on (0x24,0) at 1443 ticks into
   the part, and on the fallback it ends at 362 instead - so the
   burst arrives 322 ticks early and its bang lands seconds late. The
   state says so if asked. g_PortalOpen grows one per four ticks, so
   90 opens is 360 swirl ticks and not 684, and the burst's grow has
   left its 160 seed by the tick the swirl should still have been
   running. Music_SeekTo now sets g_timeline when it lands.

   AND FAST FORWARD DRAINS WITH THE TICKS. Demo_Skip steps state
   without drawing and used to leave the replayer behind at the audio
   device's own rate, which desynchronised by construction. It now
   drains a tick of music for every tick it skips, and no longer
   breaks the timeline - the music costs nothing next to a part's
   step(), which is what makes the skip slow in the first place.


5.  Subpixel placement, and the DIVERGENCE marker

   THE FRACTION IS ALREADY THERE. Everything the demo places on
   screen is placed from fixed point and then truncated at the last
   step. Scene_ProjectAndDrawSun is the clearest case:

       g_FlareScreenX = (wx << 8) / 0xfa0 + 0x50

   That is 24.8 fixed point, and the eight fractional bits exist
   until the framebuffer index throws them away. The same is true of
   the logo's zoom accumulators, the texture mapper's u and v, and
   every sprite centre in the sun scene.

   So subpixel placement needs NO DATA OF OUR OWN. It means not
   discarding a fraction the demo computed, which is exactly what
   section 2 permits: enhance the sampling of the demo's own data,
   never substitute data of our own. An interpolated sprite edge is
   a better reading of the demo's own number; an invented one is a
   rule 12 violation.

   WHAT IT COSTS is that a blit stops being a copy. A sprite at
   x = 61.6 covers two destination columns with weights, so
   blit_masked and blit_additive become weighted writes, and a
   masked edge needs the mask interpolated too or the cutout gets a
   hard step back.

   SCALE 1.0 MUST NOT CHANGE. Rule 20 makes scale 1.0 the reference
   every other scale is checked against, and tools/ctest.py enforces
   it byte for byte. So subpixel is a MODE, and it is off at scale
   1.0 by construction: below one destination pixel per demo unit
   there is nothing to interpolate into. It engages above 1.0, where
   the fraction has somewhere to go.

   FRAMERATE IS A SEPARATE AXIS AND SHOULD STAY SEPARATE. The
   simulation tick is a PAL vertical blank and nothing else sets the
   rate - section 3. Running the simulation faster would change
   every effect that integrates per frame, the diffusion fields
   worst of all, and would break the music sync the timeline hangs
   on.

   The split that works is fixed timestep, interpolated
   presentation, and it is in place - section 3. Demo_Advance steps a
   fixed 50 Hz timestep and hands the part the leftover fraction of a
   tick as Relic_Frame.subtick, 0 to 0xffff; that fraction is the
   interpolation alpha. The simulation stays at 50 Hz, reproducible,
   and only what the viewer sees is smoothed.

   RE-EVALUATE, DO NOT BLEND, WHERE THE MOTION IS A FORMULA. A part
   whose positions are a function of the tick can simply be asked for
   the fractional tick, which costs no latency and no second copy of
   anything. Part 2.3 does that: it re-evaluates its projection at
   tick + subtick, and the sun, both flares and the shadow's
   terminator follow the clock instead of the tick boundary - see
   effects/planet_sun.txt section 10.

   Blending two bracketing states is the fallback for whatever cannot
   be re-evaluated, and it is there that the latency and the second
   copy are owed, which is why the choice belongs per part - a
   handful of sprite centres are cheap either way, a 40,960-cell
   diffusion field is not.

   HOW TO MARK IT. Rule 18 already keeps a deliberate divergence;
   the marker is spelled DIVERGENCE and sits with ORIGIN and OURS at
   the head of a routine, saying what the demo does and what we do
   instead:

       /* ORIGIN 0x002ceaf8
        * DIVERGENCE: the demo truncates the sprite centre to whole
        * pixels. Above scale 1.0 this keeps the 24.8 fraction and
        * weights the two covered columns. Scale 1.0 truncates as
        * the original does, so ctest still matches. */

   A routine with DIVERGENCE still needs its ORIGIN: the divergence
   is from something, and the address is what lets a reader check
   what.

   RELIC.DAT IS READ BY src/dat.c AND IS OPTIONAL. Every part gets
   what it can out of the executable; only textures live in the
   archive. A host passes its path or does not, and a part that
   wanted a chunk says so rather than failing - the sun scene draws
   a black background and reports it. That keeps the no-dependency
   PPM host buildable and runnable with nothing but the executable.

6.  Building it

   TWO BUILD SYSTEMS, NEITHER GENERATING THE OTHER. There is no
   CMake: a generated solution is a file you cannot edit in the IDE
   and keep, and emcc does not need one.

   Windows - src/relic.sln, hand written, v141, SDL2 from
   src/third_party/SDL with direct relative paths:

       relic       the SDL2 host
       relic_ppm   the no-dependency host; links only the CRT

   Keep relic_ppm buildable. It is what runs on a machine with no
   SDL and what tools/ctest.py drives.

   Web - tools/build_web.sh, which GLOBS src/*.c, so adding a part
   means editing one file, the .vcxproj.

   RUN THE WEB BUILD EVEN WHEN NOT SHIPPING ONE. emcc is clang with
   -std=c89 -pedantic; MSVC's /TC is far laxer and has no C89
   switch at all. The web build is the only thing that actually
   holds the C89 line. It globs src/*.c but skips main_ppm.c and
   agent.c, the two files that are not part of the core.

   6.1  Driving it without a window

   relic_ppm -agent holds the demo on one frame and takes verbs on
   a named pipe: go to a part, step it a tick at a time, read a
   named variable, write the frame out as a PPM. It is the way an
   effect gets looked at while it is being transcribed, and its
   render path is byte-identical to the batch -p/-t/-n mode.

   Debug|Win32 only, and 22 of the effect files carry /Gh /GH
   there. subsystems/agent_mcp.txt is the protocol, the limits and
   what the map file can and cannot tell you.

   A START TICK IS PLAYED UP TO, NOT JUMPED TO. -t N runs the part's
   step() from zero before it renders anything, and the skip keys do
   the same through Demo_SeekTick. An accumulated value - an eased
   altitude, a warp scale, an LCG rolled a thousand times - exists
   only as the sum of every tick before it, so entering at tick N
   with that state still at its initial value puts a handover keyed
   on the tick N ticks late. The cost is one step() per skipped tick,
   with no drawing, and the result is byte-identical to playing
   through: tick 2364 of the temple renders the same file either way
   [verified].

   THE SKIP KEYS MOVE BY MARK, NOT BY PART. A part is one scene in
   most cases and then a mark and a part are the same thing, but a
   module holding several shots declares the ticks the others start
   at, and [ and ] stop at each. The temple declares one at tick 2356
   for the descent, which is otherwise forty seconds of the ruins
   away. parts.h carries the table and the window title names the
   mark it is on.

7.  A part of our own, past the end

   src/part_credits.c SHOWS THE DEMO'S GEOMETRY AND IS NOT THE
   DEMO'S. RELIC stops on the ark; this part is a turntable for
   looking at what Fspacedata holds, and it is last in the registry so
   every index below it is a scene the demo actually has.

   SECTION 2 STILL APPLIES TO IT. The model, its closed claw pose and
   its spin rate of 0x14 a tick are the demo's, read from the geometry
   chunk and from Anim_SceneTick's mode 1. What is ours is the
   framing, and the rule that keeps that honest is that the camera is
   MEASURED, not chosen: Ship_Bounds walks the drawn objects, honours
   the skip table and reports the box's centre and its largest
   half-extent across all three components, and the rest distance and
   both ends of the dolly's travel come out of that. The one number
   of our own is how much of the frame the model fills at rest.

   THE RADIUS SPANS ALL THREE COMPONENTS BECAUSE THE VIEW CAN ORBIT.
   Taking it across only the two the object spin turns is right for a
   fixed camera, and wrong as soon as a drag can swing the third onto
   the view axis, where it decides how close the model may come
   before it clips and how far before it overflows.

   THE PIVOT IS THE MODEL'S CENTRE, WHICH COSTS TWO ROTATIONS.
   Scene_TransformProject subtracts the camera BEFORE it rotates, and
   that one ordering is behind both of them:

   o  The spin turns vertices about the geometry's origin before the
      subtraction, so a camera parked at the measured centre would be
      left behind as the model turned and the model would circle the
      frame. The part turns the centre by the same angle first.

   o  The camera distance has to go in through the INVERSE of the
      camera rotation. Put straight into cam[1] it leaves the pair of
      camera angles rotating the scene about the eye, which is a pan;
      taken back through the inverse first, the same pair orbits the
      pivot. At rest the two are identical, which is what makes the
      change checkable - the undragged frame does not move a pixel.

   A DRAG ORBITS AND STAYS WHERE IT IS LEFT, against the springy
   reading the space scenes take of the same gesture - section 3.3. A
   drag across the whole window is one turn, so full deflection is
   worth half of the 0x2000 revolution. The turn is latched on the
   press edge and added to, never integrated a frame at a time, which
   is what stops the speed depending on the host's frame rate. Yaw
   runs free and wraps; pitch stops at a quarter revolution, straight
   down over the model, because past that the picture mirrors and the
   yaw stops meaning anything.

   A DRAG TURNS THE NEAR FACE WITH THE POINTER, and that is a
   NEGATIVE yaw. The rotation carries a vertex in front of the pivot
   the opposite way to one behind it, so the sign that moves a whole
   scene right is the sign that turns a centred model left.

   THE TURNTABLE STOPS WHEN A HAND IS PUT ON IT. An orbit that sticks
   is pointless against a model that keeps turning out from under it,
   so the first drag takes the spin over and the rest request gives
   it back.

   THE WHEEL IS A DOLLY, NOT A LENS. Each notch takes a fifth off the
   distance, so the model closes on the camera and the perspective
   opens up with it - what walking toward something does, and the
   reason nothing of a backdrop needs to follow.

   THE DISTANCE IS THE STATE, NOT THE NOTCH COUNT, and that is what
   puts the limits in the right unit. Both stops are distances
   measured off the model, and a count cannot express either, because
   what a count is worth depends on the size of whatever has loaded.
   A count clamped against the stops also keeps winding past one and
   then hands that much back as dead travel on the way out. So the
   part holds the distance, clamps it to the band on every step, and
   works from the difference between the host's running count and the
   one it last saw.

   THE TWO STOPS, BOTH DERIVED:

   o  Winding IN stops where the model's own front would reach
      Scene_TransformProject's near plane, SHIP_NEAR_CLIP. Closer
      than that and faces vanish into the camera one at a time.

   o  Winding OUT stops where the depth would overflow the rotate.
      This one is a LANDMINE, because it does not look like a limit
      at all: Scene_TransformProject rotates against a 0x4000
      amplitude table and shifts by 14, so a coordinate reaching
      (1 << 31) / 0x4000 - that is 0x20000 - overflows the product
      and reads back negative, every vertex falls behind the near
      plane and the model disappears. Keep winding and the product
      wraps a whole cycle of 0x40000 and the model REAPPEARS, larger
      than it was, as though the zoom had gone round. The stop is
      0x20000 less the model's radius, since the radius is what the
      far side of the box adds to the depth.

   THE OVERFLOW IS OURS, NOT THE DEMO'S. Nothing in RELIC comes near
   it - its camera distances are small - so SHIP_DEPTH_MAX carries no
   ORIGIN. It is a consequence of a viewer being allowed to stand
   further back than any scene does.

   THE FAR END IS THE TIGHTER ONE. Against a measured radius of 5488
   the rest distance is 35123, the near stop 5688 and the far stop
   125584, which is about eight notches in and six out [observed].
   Filling more of the frame at rest trades one for the other.

   A PART MUST UNDO WHAT THE PART BEFORE IT LEFT. The ship's claw
   pose, claw texture, additive flag, flat scroll, billboard and
   extras are module state in src/ship.c and each scene sets only the
   ones it needs, so a part wanting the plain vessel cannot rely on
   the defaults still being there. Ship_ResetPose restores all six.

   WHAT IS STILL WRONG, AND IS THE DEMO'S OWN DOING: each object's
   face order is sorted once when the geometry loads, so orbiting to
   the far side of an object that is not convex can paint its back
   faces over its front ones. Backface culling covers most of it. The
   demo never had to care, because its camera never left one side.

7.1.  Two things to look at, and the cycle that picks between them

   THE PART HOLDS A LIST, NOT A MODEL. Tab or Enter walks it, shift
   walks it backwards, and host.h's count is read as a DIFFERENCE
   rather than as an index, because the count arrives already running
   from whatever a host did with it elsewhere. The index is latched
   on arrival, so the part always opens on the vessel however far the
   count has wound.

   EACH THING IS RESET AS IT COMES UP. A turn and a distance made on
   the vessel mean nothing on the orb, whose dolly is a radius rather
   than a distance, so the switch runs the same reset the middle
   button does.

   THE ORB NEEDS NO CAMERA AT ALL. Orb_DrawTexturedSphere takes a
   screen place, a radius and two angles, so the drag goes straight
   onto the angles and the wheel straight onto the radius - none of
   section 7's measured framing applies to it, and none of the
   overflow landmine either. Its rest radius is the vessel's framing
   number, which puts both at 40 of the frame's 50 half-rows.

   A REVOLUTION OF THE ORB IS 0x100, NOT 0x2000, and that is a trap
   worth stating as a fact because nothing about the call says so.
   Crack_DrawShells takes its angle and shifts it LEFT BY FIVE into
   an ordinary 0x2000 angle, and Orb_DrawTexturedSphere applies the
   other to the LOW BYTE of a texture coordinate; both therefore wrap
   at 0x100. A drag handed straight over in the transform's units is
   32 times too fine, and reads as the orb spinning eight times for
   half a window of pointer travel. Shifting the deflection down by
   the same five makes a drag across the window worth half a turn.

   THE SPIN STOPS WHILE A HAND IS ON IT, as it does for the vessel -
   an orbit that sticks is pointless against a thing that keeps
   turning out from under it. THE CRACKS AND THE SHELLS CARRY ON,
   because it is the pose a drag takes over and not the animation.

   THE SIZE STOPS AT THE RELEASE'S OWN GATE. Scene_RenderRelease
   drops Crack_DrawShells above a projected radius of 0x50 and the
   rays go with it, so the viewer stays under that and keeps them.
   This costs nothing: 0x50 is a RADIUS, so the orb may be the
   frame's full width before they would cut out.

   THE COOL-DOWN IS ONE ACCUMULATOR, WALKED BOTH WAYS. g_FlareGrow is
   seeded at 0x7f00 and stepped down by 0x64 a tick through the
   release, and Orb_DrawTexturedSphere's reveal is (it >> 8) >> 1 - a
   floor on each texel's blue, so the sphere is sparse while the
   number is high and solid at zero. Holding the key walks it back up
   on the same step, which takes the orb apart at the speed the
   release puts it together. Nothing is ramped at a rate of our own.

   THE ORB IS OPTIONAL AND THE VESSEL IS NOT. Orb_Init wants Fcrust
   and Fspacedata, so a run without relic.dat loses the orb and the
   cycle has one thing in it. Failing the part instead would lose the
   vessel too, which needs only the executable.

7.2.  The ark's body, and measuring something with no bounds call

   TWO VESSELS EXIST AND THEY ARE NOT VARIANTS. src/ship.c draws the
   space vessel out of Fspacedata and seven parts use it; the temple,
   the ark and the departure draw a different body entirely, an
   image-resident object set behind src/objset.h. The viewer carries
   both, and the ark's needs no relic.dat at all.

   THE ARK'S AND THE TEMPLE'S ARE THE SAME MESH, stored twice and
   byte-identical - objset.h. The temple's copy is halved in place
   and the departure's is not, and the two sets name different glow
   blobs, but the body is one body. The rising ship is not a
   half-modelled stand-in for the flying one.

   Temple_DrawObjectSet TAKES THE FIVE Ship_Draw TAKES, and its
   projection subtracts the camera before it rotates exactly as
   Scene_TransformProject does, so section 7's two rotations place
   the eye unchanged and the orbit needs nothing new.

   THERE IS NO Ship_Bounds FOR AN OBJECT SET, so the extent is
   measured through the one public projection instead of being
   chosen. A screen offset is (x << 8) / (depth + 0x1f4), so
   projecting every vertex from a distance far enough that nothing
   clips and inverting that gives the half-extent in object units.
   It is taken over eight yaws and from the FRAME'S CENTRE, which is
   where the set's origin lands: the widest yaw is the one that has
   to fit, and a body sitting off its own origin is then already
   counted. Section 7's rule survives into a part of the engine that
   never offered it.

   NEITHER OF THE VESSEL'S STOPS CARRIES OVER. The near one is the
   set's own TE_OBJ_CLIP less the 0x1f4 the depth is biased by, not
   SHIP_NEAR_CLIP. The far one does carry: obj_rot shifts by 14
   against the same 0x4000 amplitude, so the same 0x20000 overflow
   described in section 7 waits at the same distance.

   THE THRUSTERS ARE NOT DRAWN HERE, and the set is copied and its
   two glow addresses zeroed to say so rather than left to fail
   quietly. They could not work as they stand: the temple's set sizes
   its glows from part_temple's own tick, which nothing outside it
   advances, and the ramp their colour comes from is built in that
   module's init. A part reached without the temple having run would
   draw them in black. Temple_SetGlowCounter drives only the
   departure's style, whose set is private to src/part_return.c.

7.3.  The city's two burns, which are not objects at all

   THE FOURTH THING TO LOOK AT IS A PICTURE. Part 2.18's first shot
   is a photograph of a city with a burn front crossing it, and
   every routine behind it already lives in src/stills.h, shared
   with the valley. The part needed NOTHING exported: the constants
   are Farmdata offsets and the loader is fifteen lines, so not one
   function was duplicated or made public for it.

   FARMDATA IS SPLIT FOUR WAYS and only two parts decode - the
   picture and the scorched layer. The height map and the 1BPP mask
   are used where they lie, as pointers into the chunk.
   effects/city.txt 3 holds the table.

   THE RESTART IS THE SHOT'S OWN ENTRY. Stills_SetThreshold puts the
   front back, the pool empties and the haze is reseeded - the same
   sequence 0x0031460c runs once. Here a key runs it, and so does
   arriving at the picture, so it always opens unburnt.

   THE DRAG IS THE DEMO'S OWN SCROLL. Stills_SetScroll takes a row
   offset, and 153 rows through a 100-row frame leaves EXACTLY 53 of
   travel - which is the whole of what the demo uses too. The
   pointer spans that range and rests halfway up it.

   IT GOES NOWHERE SIDEWAYS, and that is data, not a decision: the
   picture is 160 wide, which is the frame's own width, so there is
   nothing at either edge to scroll into. A horizontal drag could
   only wrap or show black, and section 2 does not allow either.

   ONE HELD KEY, READ TWO WAYS. The orb eases on it as a STATE and
   the city restarts on its EDGE, which is what a neutral boundary
   buys: host.h still names nothing.

   THE STREET SHOT IS THE SAME EFFECT AND NOT THE SAME LOOP. Shot
   two's stills are baked into the image rather than the archive and
   are FRAME SIZED, so it has no travel at all - and scrolling it
   would be wrong rather than empty, because the 153-row buffer it
   is padded into carries the AERIAL shot's bottom rows behind it.
   It also hazes twice, once at double the threshold under the
   debris and once straight over, where the aerial shot hazes only
   after them.

   SO ITS DRAG SCRUBS THE BURN INSTEAD. The value is the demo's own
   threshold and the span is the one its loop covers, so the pointer
   samples the shot rather than driving something invented; the
   front is latched on the press and the step pauses while a hand is
   on it, which is the turntable rule again.

   A BURN STOPS WHERE ITS SHOT ENDS. Stills_StepThreshold floors at
   zero, but each loop runs a fixed count and leaves the front well
   above that - 110 after the aerial shot's 230 fields, 72 after the
   street's 150. Running on would burn further than the demo ever
   shows. Both end states are near-white, which is where the demo
   leaves them before it cuts to its flash.

7.4.  The temple takes the look too, because it is a raycaster

   THE TEMPLE IS NOT A MESH. It is a voxel raycaster over a 64x64
   grid, one column cast per screen x, and its camera is a position
   plus TWO SCALARS: g_heading, a yaw in tenths of a degree, and
   g_horizon, a row offset that serves as the pitch. Both come off
   splines in Temple_EvalCameraSpline, which is where the look is
   added - one place for every shot that casts terrain, the long
   approach and the orb's charging alike.

   NOTHING HAD TO BE TAUGHT TO TAKE A NEW VALUE. All 3600 headings
   are planned at init, so a turn needs no rebuild, and the shake
   already moves both angles every field during its phase.

   BOTH SIGNS RUN AGAINST Space_Look's, and that is not an
   inconsistency to tidy away. A rising heading and a rising horizon
   turn the view the opposite way to that one's yaw and pitch, so
   matching its signs would have the scene push away from the hand
   where every other part carries the ground with it. The rule is
   the FEEL, not the arithmetic.

   TWO NUMBERS SET HOW FAR A FULL DRAG LOOKS, and nothing else does:
   TE_LOOK_YAW in the heading's own tenths of a degree, 3600 to a
   turn, and TE_LOOK_PITCH in frame rows out of TE_H.

   THERE IS NO SKY AND SO NOTHING TO PROTECT. A ray that reaches its
   last step without a hit leaves its column at the fog colour, a
   flat grey, so looking anywhere unintended costs a grey frame and
   never a broken one.

   WHAT IS OUT THERE IS A VALLEY RINGED BY MOUNTAINS, fully
   populated in every direction - the temple, a second structure and
   ridges all round. It repays a look of about this size and tilted
   down; further round, the ridges are silhouettes.

   THE SHOT IS NOT RESOLUTION-PARAMETRIC, and that is the real limit
   on it. The module renders into its own 160x100 buffer and the
   frame is filled from it by nearest neighbour, so a look here
   stays at the demo's own resolution where a space scene's sharpens
   with the window. Section 20's rule stops at this module's edge.

7.5.  Flying the temple's ground, with a camera it never had

   THE CAST COMES OUT WHOLE, BEHIND THREE CALLS. draw_terrain reads
   five globals and writes the module's own buffer, so a terrain-only
   init, a draw that stages those five, and a heading-to-step helper
   are the entire surface - objset.h. Everything else of that module
   stays where it is.

   THE INIT HAD TO BE SPLIT, AND THAT IS NOT TIDINESS. te_init ends
   by selecting the temple's object set, and the credits holds a set
   of its own with the thrusters off; wrapping the whole of it would
   have given the ark its thrusters back, silently, depending on
   which part initialised last. The terrain half touches no set.

   THE CAMERA IS OURS AND IS SAID TO BE. The demo flies this ground
   on a spline and never freely, so there is nothing to be faithful
   to: the view turns while the pointer is down and walks while the
   key is, and stops dead on either. No physics, because none is
   being reproduced.

   LOOKING NEVER STEERS THE WALK, AND THAT IS THE MODULE'S DOING.
   g_heading is a bearing and g_horizon only a screen row, so the
   two are independent and forward is defined at any pitch, straight
   down included. The degeneracy a look-vector camera suffers at the
   poles cannot arise here, so the pitch is clamped for the view's
   sake and never for the walk's. The walk is therefore flat by
   construction - it reads the heading alone - and height belongs to
   the wheel, stepped LINEARLY where the other characters' dollies
   are ratios, because a ratio keeps a dolly even in apparent size
   and a height has no such property.

   THE CLAMP IS ASYMMETRIC because its two ends are not worth the
   same: down is terrain and up is the fog colour and nothing else.

   THE TWO WALK KEYS ARE ONE SIGNED STEP, forward and back along the
   same bearing, so holding both is a stand rather than a race
   between them. Backing up is what makes a bearing-only camera
   usable: with no strafe, the alternative to a step back is half a
   turn and a step forward, and then another half turn to face what
   was being looked at.

   EVERY RATE HERE IS OURS, which section 7 allows for a viewer's
   framing, and none of them stands in for something readable.

   NEITHER OF ITS TWO RANGES DEPENDS ON THE WINDOW. The gesture
   arrives as a 16.16 fraction of the window's half size and the
   horizon is in the module's own fixed 100 rows, so both ends are
   normalised and a resize changes nothing. The property that makes
   this module awkward - that it renders at 160x100 and is scaled up
   - is what makes its look window-independent.

   WHERE IT OPENS IS A POINT ON THE DEMO'S OWN TRACK, chosen rather
   than computed, because the track's start faces unlit ground a few
   units away. The pose is the demo's; which moment of it is ours.

   FLYING OFF THE GRID IS CLAMPED, not undefined - a ray off the map
   holds at the edge cell - and flying UNDER it hits the cast's own
   branch and fills the frame with one dark red. Both are the demo's
   handling, not a guard of ours.

8.  Open questions

   o How much of the demo's blocking structure survives translation
     to a state machine before it stops resembling the original?
     Space_RunPart is one long straight-line sequence of waits and
     that shape is worth keeping if it can be.
   o Does a higher-precision light channel want more than 6 bits?
     Ham8_BuildTables quantises to 0x3f because the chipset does;
     carrying 8 or 16 and quantising at the end is allowed by
     section 2 but has not been tried.
   o Nothing has been decided about how the viewer will present
     parts that are shaped but not lit, beyond a status flag.
   o Jumping straight to the ark draws its thrusters in black, since
     the glow ramp is built in the temple's init and the ark's does
     not call it - section 7.2. Harmless in a run from the start.
   o Has the released orb's colour been put against hardware?
     src/part_release.c and src/part_credits.c draw the same hue from
     the same texture, so they agree with each other, and neither has
     been compared with a capture - section 7.1.
