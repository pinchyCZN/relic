RELIC

    
An Amiga AGA demo by Nerve Axis, and the winner of the Amiga
      demo competition at Assembly 1998 in Helsinki, 7-9 August. It
      wants a 68020, the AGA chipset, a PAL display and four megabytes
      of fast RAM, and its authors say in their own release notes that
      it is 33,495 lines of hand-written assembly.
      pouet.net


    
This is not an emulator and not a capture. Every part here is a
      transcription of the original's own code, in ANSI C, reading the
      1998 executable and its data file at run time - the meshes, the
      textures, the sine tables, the camera splines and the music are
      all taken out of those bytes as the demo starts. Where the demo
      hard-codes a number, so does this; where it builds a table, this
      builds the same table.


    
The one deliberate difference is resolution. The
      Amiga renders 160 x 100 chunky pixels and encodes them to HAM8;
      the effects here are parametric, so the same arithmetic runs at
      whatever the window is. Scale 1.0 stays exact and is the
      reference every other scale is checked against.


    
WHAT IT RUNS

    
The demo's own order: a terminal typing itself, a sun, the
      approach to a vessel, a portal, a window over a planet, the orb
      forming and the ship beneath it, an explosion, the release, the
      Earth, sand, the temple, the return, debris, a valley, a city
      burning, the planet destroyed and the ark leaving.


    
The clock is the music's, not the frame counter's. Scenes end
      on a position in the module rather than after a count of fields,
      which is how the cuts land where they did in 1998.


    
THE CREDITS PART IS OURS AND THE DEMO HAS NO SUCH
      THING. Past the end it hands over to a gallery of the demo's own
      things with controls on them: the vessel, the cracked orb, the
      ark, the burning city from above and from the street, and the
      temple valley to fly through. Tab moves
      between them.

A drag in the demo's own scenes only swings the
      view and never touches what the demo is doing; letting go eases
      back to the recorded camera. The gallery is the exception - its
      orbit holds where you leave it.
